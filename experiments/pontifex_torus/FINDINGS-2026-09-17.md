# Pontifex experimental evolution — 2026-09-17

These are exploratory findings, not confirmatory evidence. The sequence matters:
early experiments exposed failure modes of a direct convergence head and motivated
the newer intervention-geometry / torus direction rather than being silently
replaced by it.

## 1. RED-1: cheap direct-convergence probe

The first 240-example synthetic run compared a best single encoder, a simple mean,
and a nonlinear convergence head over within-space bilateral similarity signals.
Across three seeds the held-out delta

`AUPRC(C) - max(AUPRC(best single), AUPRC(mean))`

was `+0.086`, `-0.508`, and `+0.030` (median `+0.030`). The positive median was
interesting, but the seed variance was immediately too large for a strong claim.

A contamination control initially contained a confound (different MLP initialization
for the contaminated condition); that was fixed before subsequent interpretation.

## 2. Scale and diversity exposed fragility

The sample-scale smoke study did not reproduce a stable gain:

| N | median delta | mean delta | fraction positive |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | -0.472 | -0.405 | 0% |
| 300 | -0.453 | -0.393 | 0% |
| 1000 | -0.364 | -0.241 | 20% |

At N=1000 one seed was strongly positive (`+0.292`), but four were negative. Partial
or complete corruption of one channel did not produce a clean, monotonic diagnostic.

The diversity smoke study (N=480, K=3) was similarly seed-sensitive. With 24
semantic families one seed had positive `delta_interaction = C - learned weighted
mean` (`+0.160`), while another was strongly negative (`-0.800`). This is evidence
that the crude nonlinear head is not yet a reliable mechanism for the distinctive
Pontifex claim.

These adverse results are retained as useful falsification pressure. They motivate
moving from a single learned head over scalar features to a structured response
field induced by shared interventions.

## 3. Real SciFact: simple learned fusion can help

A no-reencode real-data toy used precomputed Cohere `embed-english-v3.0` SciFact
embeddings plus a lexical TF-IDF channel. On 38 held-out queries:

| condition | macro AUPRC | Recall@10 |
|---|---:|---:|
| best single (dense) | 0.7047 | 0.8105 |
| simple mean | 0.6024 | 0.8039 |
| learned linear fusion | **0.7302** | **0.8368** |
| nonlinear MLP | 0.0093 | 0.0263 |

The linear fusion improved AUPRC by about `+0.0254` over the best single channel.
The nonlinear MLP collapsed and should be treated as an optimization failure, not
as a meaningful test of the newer torus hypothesis.

This result is useful mainly because it establishes that real, precomputed semantic
spaces can be queried cheaply and that learned cross-signal structure can matter.

## 4. Sampled prebuilt latent spaces are practical

The Pyserini MS MARCO TCT-ColBERT-v2-HNP artifact is a real FAISS `IndexFlatIP` with
8,841,823 vectors of dimension 768. The index file is about 27.16 GB. Reading it
normally exceeded runner RAM, but opening it with FAISS mmap succeeded.

A deterministic set of 1,000 MS MARCO PIDs was extracted without re-encoding into
a compact feature store of about 2.86 MB. This validates the infrastructure pattern:
large public latent spaces can remain external/memory-mapped while experiments
materialize only a shared fixed sample.

## 5. Torus v0: occlusion size behaves like a useful lens

The first torus smoke uses MiniLM as the inner semantic space and BGE-small as the
outer space. Eighty held-out/synthetic texts generate 1,920 intervention rows from
six positions per text and occlusion sizes `1, 2, 4, 8`.

The target is deliberately modest: predict the outer space's within-space response
to an occlusion from the inner space's response and intervention coordinates.

On wholly held-out texts:

| map | RMSE |
|---|---:|
| size-agnostic periodic map | 0.04032 |
| + occlusion-size lens | 0.03857 |
| torus/local interaction map | **0.03476** |

Thus the lens reduces RMSE by about 4.3% relative to the size-agnostic map, and the
cheap torus interaction basis reduces it by about 13.8%.

The unseen-scale test trains on occlusion sizes `1, 2, 8` and predicts size `4` on
held-out texts:

| map | RMSE on unseen size 4 |
|---|---:|
| size-agnostic | 0.04928 |
| lens | **0.04424** |
| torus interactions | 0.04594 |

So explicit occlusion scale helps even when the tested scale was not observed in
training. In this first run the simpler lens generalizes better than the richer
interaction basis on the unseen scale, which is a useful constraint rather than a
failure.

Most importantly for active cartography, with only 10% of the training response
field the torus condition achieved RMSE `0.03602`, versus `0.04195` for the
size-agnostic condition and `0.03476` for the torus with 100% of training rows.
That is preliminary evidence that useful cross-space geometry can emerge before
exhaustive traversal.

The neighbor-overlap diagnostic in the unseen-scale slice was undefined in this
smoke because its fixed `k=10` exceeded the number of points available per text;
those zeros are an instrumentation artifact and are being corrected before the
larger run.

## 6. Current interpretation

The day's experiments do **not** validate the original neural convergence-head
claim. They instead provide a coherent empirical path toward a stronger and more
structured hypothesis:

1. direct nonlinear convergence is fragile under scale/diversity;
2. simple real-data fusion can improve over a strong single channel;
3. precomputed latent spaces can be sampled cheaply without re-encoding;
4. occlusion size carries transferable information across semantic spaces;
5. a local periodic/deformation representation predicts cross-space intervention
   responses with fewer observations than a size-agnostic map in the first smoke.

The next experiments therefore focus on occlusion/context lenses, held-out scales,
active selection of interventions, and smooth local deformation. MaleCNS enters
only after the geometric substrate has earned its keep, as a candidate operator for
updating that deformation field rather than as a mandatory premise.


## 7. Sequential accumulation and return to the first text

A follow-up protocol tested the shared-space interpretation directly. For each of five
seeds, a single torus map was updated with eleven distinct texts in sequence. Each
text received 16 consecutive forward passes; the geometry was snapshotted after every
text. After T11, the protocol returned to T1 for another 16 passes. Thirty texts per
seed were held out from all updates.

The surprising result is that T1 did **not** degrade across the ten intervening texts.
Its mean RMSE improved from `0.05688` immediately after its first 16 passes to
`0.04230` after T2..T11, a mean change of `-0.01458`; all five seeds improved.
Thus, in this toy, subsequent texts transfer useful structure back to the first text
through the shared geometry rather than merely overwriting it.

Returning to T1 after those ten intervening texts produced a second improvement:
mean T1 RMSE fell from `0.04230` to `0.03305`, a further mean change of
`-0.00925`, again improving in all five seeds.

The return is not free of interference. Across T2..T11, the revisit changed mean RMSE
by `+0.00176` (worse on average; two of five seeds improved). The aggregate RMSE
over all eleven seen texts changed by `+0.00093`. Held-out RMSE, however, changed
from mean `0.04439` before the return to `0.04329` after it
(`-0.00110` on average; three of five seeds improved).

The geometry remains active rather than converged to an inert point. The mean L2
geometry displacement caused by the T1 return was `0.01388`, comparable to the
displacements caused by later new texts. The run stores every geometry snapshot,
making rollback and branching from earlier states possible in later experiments.

The cautious interpretation is therefore:

1. repeated exposure to different texts can create **positive backward transfer** to
   an earlier text through a shared learned geometry;
2. revisiting an earlier text can substantially improve that text again;
3. the revisit may introduce mild interference with other already-seen texts;
4. held-out behavior does not show a corresponding average penalty in this five-seed
   run, but the effect is variable and needs larger validation before treating it as
   robust.

This motivates checkpointed geometry search: preserve each state, evaluate on a
validation set, and branch or roll back rather than forcing a single irreversible
training trajectory.
