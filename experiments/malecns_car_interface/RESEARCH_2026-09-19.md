# Research pass — 2026-09-19

## 1. Explicit MaleCNS / connectome-derived embodied control

This pass distinguishes projects that actually place MaleCNS-derived circuitry in a control loop from projects that merely visualize the connectome or borrow insect inspiration.

### Explicit driving / vehicle control

- **Fly Self Driving** — https://github.com/suanmiao/fly-self-driving
  - Explicitly uses the MaleCNS connectome as a recurrent network for pixel-to-steering simulated street driving.
  - Repository reports 58/60 held-out streets completed across three sets with no vehicle collisions, with controls including rewired/linear/MLP baselines.
  - Relevance: this remains the closest public direct prior art to the present car-control line and strengthens the requirement for matched random/rewired/conventional controls.

- **Flyhard** — https://github.com/MarkUnthank/flyhard
  - Explicitly uses a MaleCNS-retaining model in CARLA-linked vehicle-control work.
  - Public status describes a bounded pilot for physically operating a steering wheel through a simulated fly body; the broader full-driving goal remains larger than the reported pilot.
  - Relevance: direct prior art for embodied actuation and for separating “connectome drives controller” from “connectome drives a body that manipulates vehicle controls.”

- **InsideTheFlyBrain parallel-parking demonstration** — https://insidetheflybrain.site/
  - Public project page claims a MaleCNS-based closed-loop parking demonstration with optic-lobe / descending-pathway framing.
  - Relevance: worth tracking, but the claim should not be treated as independently validated evidence without reproducible code/results matching the description.

### Other embodied MaleCNS control

- **Fly Brain Codex** — https://github.com/monomyth/fly-brain-codex
  - MaleCNS-derived controller for a simulated six-joint robot arm and gripper.
  - Relevance: supports the broader prior-art category “MaleCNS-derived embodied control,” but is not road driving.

- **flyByWire** — https://github.com/Flybywirerh/Flybywire
  - Uses a real toy drone camera and MaleCNS activity visualization, but its current public description says the brain observes and does **not** steer the drone yet.
  - Relevance: negative boundary example. It should not be cited as prior art for demonstrated MaleCNS drone control.

- **Fly Arena / digitalfly / flyverse**
  - These projects connect MaleCNS data to simulated fly bodies or hybrid controllers.
  - Relevance: useful embodied-neuroscience engineering references, but generic insect embodiment is not equivalent to demonstrated car/drone/mobile-robot control.

## 2. Autonomous-driving datasets and benchmarks

### PAVE — production autonomous-vehicle evaluation

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14185  
Repository: https://github.com/KITE-Lab/PAVE_Dataset

PAVE is unusually relevant because it is collected in production autonomous-driving mode rather than only human driving. The paper reports over 100 hours, 32,727 key frames, four synchronized cameras, high-precision GNSS/IMU, past/future ego trajectories and scene annotations. The public repository currently describes academic subsets and warns that the available archives are legacy releases while a corrected release is being prepared.

**Use classification**

- training: useful for non-commercial research, subject to its academic license;
- offline perception: useful;
- offline trajectory/planning evaluation: highly useful;
- closed-loop control: not by itself, because it is logged real-world data;
- safety-event analysis: useful for behavior/trajectory analysis, but not a crash-regulator database;
- external real-world comparison: useful because the frames are from production autonomous-driving mode.

**Access/cache implication**

The public archives are tens of GB and non-commercial-license constrained. Do not download them opportunistically in hourly runs. Pin an exact archive version/checksum and cache it persistently only when the experiment is ready to use it. Start with a deliberately small extracted slice where licensing permits.

### Open-loop versus closed-loop correlation

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00066

The 2026 NAVSIM-versus-Bench2Drive cross-benchmark study reports that open-loop metrics can rank methods differently from closed-loop driving, although aggregate NAVSIM PDM Score correlates strongly in the small matched sample. Ego Progress was the strongest single open-loop predictor in that study, while collision-only metrics did not explain the closed-loop ranking by themselves.

**Implication for this project:** offline replay metrics are useful as a cheap gate, but the main claim of driving competence should remain closed-loop. CARLA/Bench2Drive-style route completion, infraction, timeout and recovery metrics should not be replaced by ADE/FDE alone.

### Waymo Safety Impact

Official source: https://waymo.com/safety/impact/

Waymo's current downloadable safety hub reports 220.6 million rider-only miles through March 2026 and exposes CSVs for miles by geography, SGO-linked crashes and benchmark comparisons.

**Use classification**

- training: no;
- offline perception: no;
- closed-loop benchmark: no direct replay benchmark;
- safety-event analysis: yes;
- external real-world comparison: yes, with methodology/geography matching.

**Adaptation:** use the published outcome definitions and exposure-normalized crash-rate methodology as an eventual external reporting template once this project has enough real/simulated exposure. Do not compare raw crash counts across systems without denominator and operating-domain matching.

### NHTSA Standing General Order crash data

Official source: https://www.nhtsa.gov/es/node/103486

The current page exposes ADS, Level-2 ADAS and “Other” CSVs covering the amended reporting regime from June 16, 2025 through August 17, 2026 and states that the data are updated monthly.

**Use classification**

- training: no;
- offline perception: no;
- closed-loop benchmark: no;
- safety-event analysis: excellent for failure-mode mining;
- external comparison: only with strong caveats because reporting obligations, exposure and operational domains differ.

**Adaptation:** mine narratives and structured crash-scene fields into a failure-mode taxonomy, then reproduce selected scenarios in CARLA without leaking the original outcome to the agent.

### Tesla / production-system safety evidence

Recent public reporting in September 2026 includes Tesla-provided European supervised-driving collision comparisons. These are useful as examples of how production systems report safety, but they remain company-reported comparisons and should not be treated as a directly interchangeable benchmark against Waymo rider-only Level-4 operation or NHTSA counts.

**Project rule:** record source, automation level, supervision state, road class, exposure denominator and reporting definition before any Tesla/Waymo comparison.

## 3. New cheap scalar/vector channels worth adding

### Camera contamination / visibility health

Candidate scalars:

- blur score;
- glare/saturation fraction;
- fog/haze confidence;
- lens-obstruction fraction;
- dropped-frame rate;
- exposure instability.

**Cost:** essentially free on the phone once the camera is already running.  
**Rate:** 5–30 Hz depending on computation.  
**Real-car availability:** direct.  
**Simulator emulation:** image-space blur, glare, haze, occlusion and frame-drop transforms.  
**Ablation:** raw camera alone vs raw camera + health scalars under controlled corruption.  
**Why useful:** these signals tell the coordinator when an entire visual colony may be jointly unreliable.

### Road-wetness / standing-water risk

Candidate scalars:

- wiper state / duty cycle from CAN where available;
- precipitation intensity from a cached/online weather feed;
- camera-derived wet-road probability;
- specular-water / standing-water visual score;
- wheel-slip disagreement under wet conditions.

**Cost:** free to near-zero when existing camera/CAN/data connection is available.  
**Rate:** CAN/camera real time; weather feed slower.  
**Real-car availability:** yes, with vehicle/profile dependence.  
**Simulator emulation:** CARLA weather plus image effects and wheel-friction changes, exposing only the physical sensor consequences.  
**Ablation:** remove each scalar and measure failure/recovery on wet-road/flood-like scenarios.  
**Privacy/safety:** weather service location queries should be coarse and cached where possible.

### Per-modality compute health

Candidate scalars:

- inference latency;
- queue depth;
- thermal throttling state;
- CPU/GPU/NPU utilization;
- time since last successful model output;
- model confidence entropy.

**Cost:** free telemetry.  
**Rate:** 1–30 Hz.  
**Real-car availability:** phone/system APIs.  
**Simulator emulation:** deterministic delay/load injection.  
**Ablation:** sensor values alone vs sensor values + compute-health reports under induced overload.  
**Why useful:** a coordinator should distinguish “the world is ambiguous” from “my visual organ is late.”

## 4. Immediate experiment implication

The strongest immediate consequence of this research pass is methodological: keep a two-stage evaluation stack.

1. **Cheap logged-data gate** — comma2k19/PAVE-like synchronized sensor slices for sensor-specialist calibration, corruption tests and offline prediction.
2. **Closed-loop gate** — CARLA/Bench2Drive-like route execution with progress, collisions, violations, timeout/recovery and sensor-degradation stress.

Waymo/NHTSA/Tesla evidence belongs in the third layer: external safety context and failure-mode design, not as a substitute for an executable benchmark.
