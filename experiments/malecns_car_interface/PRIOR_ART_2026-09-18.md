# MaleCNS Vehicle and Robotics Prior Art — 2026-09-18

This is the first daily prior-art sweep for the MaleCNS car-interface experiment line.

The search criterion is strict: prioritize projects that explicitly use the Drosophila MaleCNS / male fly connectome as computational substrate, wiring, or weights. Generic insect-inspired robotics is recorded only as background and is not treated as direct prior art.

## Direct prior art found

### 1. Fly Self Driving — explicit MaleCNS autonomous driving

Repository/project:
https://github.com/suanmiao/fly-self-driving

The project states that it runs the measured MaleCNS connectome as a recurrent network and trains it to steer a simulated street from a low-resolution windscreen input. The project reports held-out driving scenarios with parked vehicles, oncoming traffic and overtaking.

Relevance: **direct and high**.

This is the closest prior art found so far to the current experiment line because it explicitly places MaleCNS-derived connectivity in a driving task.

Important distinction from our proposed line: our current experiment is centered on a reality-bounded, replaceable sensor/actuator contract intended to map directly onto an ordinary car plus phone/OBD/external cheap sensors, with modular sensor ablations, bodily feedback, multi-agent radio communication, active sensing and sim-to-real stress.

Follow-up: inspect training mechanism, readout/adapters, whether synaptic weights are fixed or trained, whether OBD/IMU/GPS-like channels are absent, whether the environment exposes simulator-only state, and what exact held-out protocol produced the reported 58/60 result.

### 2. Ommatid — MaleCNS driving a physical hexapod robot

Repository/project:
https://github.com/FutureJJ/ommatid

The project describes a real fruit-fly connectome, with FlyVis optic-lobe input and a MaleCNS spiking model, controlling a physical six-legged robot. It maps visual input into fly-like retinotopic channels and reads descending neurons associated with steering, forward motion, backward motion and stopping.

Relevance: **direct embodied-control prior art, but not driving**.

This is important because it demonstrates a body-transfer framing very close to our broader hypothesis: a biological connectome can be re-embodied in hardware it did not evolve to control.

Follow-up: compare their descending-neuron readout and proprioceptive feedback with our thin input/output adapters and curriculum strategy.

## Strongly related scientific background, but not direct MaleCNS car/drone prior art

### NeuroMechFly v2

Paper:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02497-y

NeuroMechFly v2 provides embodied sensorimotor control for adult Drosophila, with vision, olfaction, ascending motor feedback, navigation and connectome-constrained visual circuits. The paper explicitly discusses autonomous artificial agents and robots as an application.

Relevance: **strong methodological background**.

It is not the same as taking the 2026 MaleCNS whole-CNS connectome and putting it into a car, but it gives a mature embodied-control methodology and useful baselines for sensorimotor integration.

### Drosophila central-complex navigation connectome

Paper:
https://elifesciences.org/articles/66039

The central-complex connectome identifies circuit motifs for head-direction representation, vector-based navigation and context-dependent action selection.

Relevance: **biological mechanism background**.

This is particularly relevant to route following, heading control and the question of whether navigation should be injected through a thin adapter or mapped into biologically meaningful navigation circuits.

## Search result: cars vs drones

The most important result of this first sweep is asymmetrical:

- **cars:** direct explicit MaleCNS prior art exists;
- **physical robots:** direct explicit MaleCNS prior art exists;
- **drones:** suggestive recent claims exist, but a sufficiently strong primary technical source was not yet verified in this round.

## Novelty implications for our experiment line

The novelty claim must therefore not be “first use of MaleCNS to drive a car.”

More defensible questions are whether this is the first systematic study of MaleCNS control under a **reality-bounded retrofit interface** designed to transfer from simulator to an ordinary car through:

- phone camera + IMU + GNSS;
- OBD-II telemetry;
- optional on-device YOLO;
- optional cheap active echolocation;
- optional low-cost LiDAR;
- ambient RF / Wi-Fi context;
- explicit multi-agent communication and transferable internal credits;
- staged one-actuator / one-signal curriculum;
- systematic ablations of each sensor and transducer;
- local LLM as an optional semantic transducer rather than a privileged controller;
- realistic latency, dropout, noise and missing-sensor stress.

Those differences should be tested experimentally rather than merely asserted as novelty.

## Daily-search rule

At least one hourly run per calendar day should perform a fresh prior-art sweep. Each sweep should:

1. search papers, preprints, repositories, project pages and recent discussions;
2. distinguish explicit MaleCNS/connectome use from generic fly-inspired robotics;
3. re-check unverified drone / vehicle claims for primary evidence;
4. inspect newly found projects for architecture, training, sensor interfaces and results;
5. record implications for novelty, baselines and experiment design;
6. avoid treating social posts, summaries or project pages as equivalent to peer-reviewed or reproducible primary evidence.
