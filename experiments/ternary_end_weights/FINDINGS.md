## Round 1 — deterministic synthetic controls

The encoder/decoder is exact relative to the quantized integer. The main storage results are ratios to the fixed-width baseline; values below 1 are smaller than fixed width under that accounting convention.

| distribution | bits | zero rate | trits / fixed cells | capacity ratio | naive 2-bit ratio | binary length-prefix ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| uniform | 4 | 6.98% | 1.037 | 1.644 | 2.074 | 1.287 |
| uniform | 8 | 0.39% | 1.003 | 1.590 | 2.007 | 1.253 |
| Gaussian | 4 | 24.90% | 0.932 | 1.478 | 1.865 | 1.182 |
| Laplace | 4 | 44.83% | 0.768 | 1.217 | 1.535 | 1.018 |
| centered mixture | 4 | 57.95% | 0.658 | 1.043 | 1.316 | 0.908 |
| sparse uniform | 4 | 53.82% | 0.640 | 1.014 | 1.279 | 0.890 |
| sparse uniform | 8 | 50.51% | 0.562 | **0.891** | 1.124 | **0.812** |
| sparse uniform | 16 | 50.31% | 0.528 | **0.837** | 1.057 | **0.716** |

Two points are already clear.

First, counting ternary cells alone is misleading. Gaussian and Laplace controls use fewer ternary symbols than fixed binary cells at 4 bits, but both lose once the alphabet is normalized by `log2(3)`. The uniform negative control is worse under every ternary accounting view.

Second, high exact sparsity can be sufficient for a capacity-normalized win over fixed width: the 50%-sparse control reaches ratios 0.891 at 8 bits and 0.837 at 16 bits. But even there, naive two-bit storage of each trit still loses, and the deliberately simple binary variable-length representation is smaller than the ternary-capacity-normalized representation. The synthetic win therefore supports **variable-length trailing-zero suppression**, not yet a special advantage from the third state.

## Gates after Round 1

- **G0 — exactness: PASS.** Exhaustive 4-bit round-trip and concatenated-stream boundary tests pass.
- **G1 — negative-control sanity: PASS.** Uniform 8-bit `capacity_ratio = 1.590 > 1`; the accounting does not manufacture a win by equating trits with bits.
- **G2 — real-model capacity win: NOT YET EXECUTED.** Requires the pinned SmolLM2 artifact.
- **G3 — binary-software win: NOT YET EXECUTED on the registered model.** The synthetic 50%-sparse control still loses under naive 2-bit packing at 4/8/16 bits.
- **G4 — simple binary variable-length competitor: NOT YET EXECUTED on the registered model.** Synthetic diagnostic: wherever the sparse control beats fixed width by capacity normalization, the simple binary competitor is smaller still.
- **G5 — native ternary boundary: PASS.** No result here is interpreted as beating direct one-trit representation of a native `{-1,0,+1}` weight alphabet.

## Interpretation and limits

Round 1 establishes that the proposal is a valid self-delimiting code and identifies the mechanism that can make it shorter: zero weights and quantized magnitudes with removable trailing zeroes. It does **not** establish that ordinary LLM weights have enough of that structure.

The strongest synthetic result is deliberately not treated as confirmation. A 50%-exact-zero control was included to show a regime where the hypothesis ought to have a chance; it is not a model of SmolLM2 or of dense transformer weights.

The binary length-prefix comparator is also not an optimal compressor. Its purpose is adversarial: if even this simple binary scheme captures the same gain more cheaply, the experiment cannot attribute the benefit to ternary delimiting.

## Claim boundary

These results may support the claim that `{0,1,END}` can losslessly delimit a variable-length binary payload and can beat fixed-width payload cost under sufficiently sparse quantized distributions, depending on the physical cost model.

They may **not** be used to claim that ternary hardware is more area-efficient or energy-efficient; that LLM inference becomes faster; that the scheme beats modern entropy coding, sparse formats, or optimized INT4/INT8 layouts; that SmolLM2 passes any gate before its pinned execution is recorded; or that this is a novel coding-theory construction.
