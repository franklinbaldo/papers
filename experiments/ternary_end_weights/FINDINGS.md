## Round 1 — deterministic synthetic controls

The encoder/decoder is exact relative to the quantized integer. The main storage results are ratios to the fixed-width baseline; values below 1 are smaller than fixed width under that accounting convention.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| uniform | 4 | 6.98% | 1.037 | 1.644 | 2.074 | 1.287 |
| uniform | 8 | 0.39% | 1.003 | 1.590 | 2.007 | 1.253 |
| Gaussian | 4 | 24.90% | 0.932 | 1.478 | 1.865 | 1.182 |
| Laplace | 4 | 44.83% | 0.768 | 1.217 | 1.535 | 1.018 |
| centered mixture | 4 | 57.95% | 0.658 | 1.043 | 1.316 | 0.908 |
| sparse uniform | 4 | 53.82% | 0.640 | 1.014 | 1.279 | 0.890 |
| sparse uniform | 8 | 50.51% | 0.562 | **0.891** | 1.124 | **0.812** |
| sparse uniform | 16 | 50.31% | 0.528 | **0.837** | 1.057 | **0.716** |

Counting ternary cells alone is misleading. Gaussian and Laplace controls use fewer ternary symbols than fixed binary cells at 4 bits, but both lose once the alphabet is normalized by `log2(3)`. The uniform negative control is worse under every ternary accounting view.

High exact sparsity can be sufficient for a capacity-normalized win over fixed width: the 50%-sparse control reaches ratios 0.891 at 8 bits and 0.837 at 16 bits. But even there, naive two-bit storage of each trit still loses, and the deliberately simple binary variable-length representation is smaller than the ternary-capacity-normalized representation. The synthetic win therefore supports **variable-length trailing-zero suppression**, not a special advantage from the third state.

## Round 2 — pinned SmolLM2-135M

The registered model run covered 211 matrix-like tensors and 134,479,872 weights. No supported numeric dtype was skipped.

| bits | quantized zero rate | avg trits | cell ratio | capacity-equivalent bits | capacity ratio | naive 2-bit ratio | binary length-prefix ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 19.9545% | 3.8334 | **0.9584** | 6.0758 | **1.5190** | 1.9167 | 1.2084 |
| 8 | 1.1251% | 7.9899 | **0.9987** | 12.6637 | **1.5830** | 1.9975 | 1.2487 |
| 16 | 0.00444% | 15.8690 | **0.9918** | 25.1517 | **1.5720** | 1.9836 | 1.1793 |

The raw *symbol-cell* count is slightly below the fixed binary bit count at all three widths, most visibly at 4 bits: about 4.16% fewer ternary symbols than fixed binary bits. That is the strongest surviving form of the original intuition.

It does **not** survive hardware-neutral capacity accounting. A trit-normalized payload costs 1.519 times the 4-bit reference and 1.583 times the 8-bit reference. Naively representing each trit with two binary bits is worse again. The simple binary variable-length comparator is also smaller than the ternary-capacity-normalized representation at every width, although it too loses to ordinary fixed width on this dense model.

The model result therefore rejects the storage-compression hypothesis in its registered form. SmolLM2 does not contain enough post-quantization zero/trailing-zero structure for the delimiter state to repay the information capacity consumed by a third symbol.

## Gates

- **G0 — exactness: PASS.** Exhaustive 4-bit round-trip and concatenated-stream boundary tests pass.
- **G1 — negative-control sanity: PASS.** Uniform 8-bit `capacity_ratio = 1.590 > 1`; the accounting does not manufacture a win by equating trits with bits.
- **G2 — real-model capacity win: FAIL.** SmolLM2 `capacity_ratio` is 1.519 at 4 bits and 1.583 at 8 bits; neither is below 1.
- **G3 — binary-software win: FAIL.** SmolLM2 `packed_2bit_ratio` is 1.917 at 4 bits and 1.997 at 8 bits.
- **G4 — simple binary variable-length competitor: FAIL.** There is no real-model width at which the proposed capacity-normalized representation beats fixed width; moreover the registered binary length-prefix comparator is smaller than the ternary-capacity-normalized representation at every tested width.
- **G5 — native ternary boundary: PASS.** No result is interpreted as beating direct one-trit representation of a native `{-1,0,+1}` weight alphabet.

## Interpretation

The experiment found a real mechanism but a negative application result.

`{0,1,END}` is a valid self-delimiting representation. It can omit trailing zero magnitude digits without changing an already-quantized integer, and it can reduce the *number of symbols* when zeros or powers of two are common. In a deliberately sparse synthetic regime it can even beat fixed width after `log2(3)` capacity normalization.

For the registered dense LLM, however, that structure is insufficient. The only positive statement left is hardware-conditional: **if a physical ternary storage/compute cell could be built with cost close enough to one binary cell that cell count, rather than information capacity, dominates, the 4-bit SmolLM2 result gives a small symbol-count advantage worth measuring.** This experiment supplies no evidence that such a cost assumption is true.

The next defensible direction is therefore not to call this a better compression format. It is either:

1. test the same delimiter idea on deliberately sparse/pruned LLMs, where the synthetic results predict a threshold crossing; or
2. move the question to actual ternary-device area/energy/latency and ask whether the raw 4-bit cell-count reduction can compensate for ternary-cell complexity.

A third direction — native ternary-weight models — should use direct ternary weight representation as the baseline, not this `0/1/END` encoding.

## Claim boundary

These results support the claim that `{0,1,END}` can losslessly delimit a variable-length binary payload and can reduce symbol count for quantized values with suitable zero/trailing-zero structure.

They do **not** support the claim that this representation compresses ordinary dense LLM weights better than fixed INT4/INT8 storage; that it beats a simple binary variable-length encoding; that ternary hardware is more area-efficient or energy-efficient; that LLM inference becomes faster; that the scheme beats modern entropy coding or sparse formats; or that this is a novel coding-theory construction.
