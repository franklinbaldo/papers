# The Doctrinalization Mechanism: Formal Conditions for Converting a Patrimonial Disposition into Procedural Doctrine

---

## 1. Thesis Supported

Target paper: `paper1G_livre_convencimento_patrimonialismo.md` — "O Livre Convencimento como Doutrinização do Patrimonialismo Judicial: Genealogia Histórico-Institucional do Equilíbrio de Baixa Qualidade Argumentativa no Sistema de Precedentes Brasileiro" (Baldo, 2026).

Thesis:

> "a tese central é que o princípio do livre convencimento motivado, consagrado no art. 131 do CPC de 1973, não foi anomalia doutrinária acidental: foi a doutrinização de uma disposição patrimonialista que o sistema jurídico brasileiro herdou do patrimonialismo ibérico e que nunca enfrentou mecanismo de enforcement suficientemente forte para ser constrangida."

## 3. The Argument

### 3.1 Three Conditions for Doctrinalization

A disposition — a behavioral pattern regularly enacted by a class of agents in a field — becomes doctrinalized when it acquires a technical name, a principled genealogy, and a legitimizing argument within the professional discourse of that field. Not all enacted dispositions undergo this transition: many remain at the level of informal practice without gaining doctrinal status. The following three conditions are individually necessary and jointly sufficient for the doctrinalization transition.

**Condition C1: Output legibility.** The disposition must generate outputs that are legible as satisfying the formal obligations of the field without exposing the dispositional origin of those outputs. An agent exercising the disposition must be able to produce decisions, filings, or analyses that appear, from the perspective of formal field evaluation, to fall within the expected range of compliant performance. If the disposition's outputs were routinely identifiable as non-compliant through ordinary professional review, field enforcement would constrain the disposition before doctrinalization could stabilize. Output legibility requires that the gap between what the formal obligation specifies and what the dispositional practice delivers not be closeable by ordinary review.

**Condition C2: Enforcement deficit.** The field must lack enforcement mechanisms with sufficient granularity to distinguish dispositional performance from genuine compliance with the formal obligation. An enforcement deficit does not require that no sanctions exist; it requires that the sanctions available track formal output-presence rather than quality-of-engagement with the underlying obligation. Where enforcement checks that something is written without checking whether the written content constitutes substantive engagement, the enforcement deficit criterion is met.

**Condition C3: Legitimizing tradition.** A legitimizing discourse must be available that can reframe the dispositional practice as a principled choice within the field's technical vocabulary. The reframing requires that the practice be translatable into a vocabulary the professional field recognizes as valid and that carries positive normative associations within the field's evaluative framework. The legitimizing tradition does not need to have been constructed with the specific disposition in mind; it can be an exogenous intellectual tradition whose vocabulary happens to map onto the dispositional practice in ways that render the practice defensible as a principled position.

The three conditions must be met simultaneously. C1 without C2 produces a disposition exercised covertly but incapable of stabilizing as doctrine because enforcement exposure would undercut any legitimization attempt. C1 without C3 produces an informally tolerated practice that never achieves doctrinal status because no technical vocabulary absorbs it. C2 without C1 means enforcement cannot act on the disposition, but since the outputs are not legible as field-compliant, the disposition cannot be exercised openly enough to generate the public practice record that legitimization requires. C3 without C1 and C2 produces legitimizing discourse that cannot attach to a specific practice because the practice cannot survive field exposure. The conjunction is required.

### 3.2 Two Conditions for Path-Dependence Lock-In

Once a disposition has been doctrinalized, whether it persists in the face of subsequent normative shocks depends on the presence of path-dependence lock-in mechanisms. North (1990, pp. 92–104) identifies the general condition for institutional path-dependence: the costs of transition to an alternative institutional arrangement must exceed the expected benefits for agents making the transition decision, given existing investment structure and the distributional consequences of the current arrangement.

Two specific conditions operationalize this for doctrinalized dispositions in professional fields:

**Condition P1: Formation-integrated transmission.** The doctrinalized disposition must be incorporated into the professional formation mechanisms of the field — the educational institutions, apprenticeship structures, and socialization processes through which new agents acquire field competence. When formation transmits the doctrinalized practice as a constitutive element of competence, new entrants acquire the disposition as part of their professional identity rather than as a contingent strategic choice. Transition costs for agents socialized in this way are not limited to behavioral adjustment; they include the cost of revising a competence conception that is constitutive of professional identity. Under DiMaggio and Powell's (1983) framework, this corresponds to normative isomorphism: the professional formation mechanism reproduces the disposition regardless of what the formal obligations say, because what formation transmits is enacted practice, not stated norm.

**Condition P2: Capital-generating function.** The doctrinalized disposition must generate distributable capital — credentials, expertise status, authority to define correct practice — for agents who master its legitimizing discourse. When mastery of the doctrinalized discourse positions certain agents as authoritative interpreters of the field's central practices, those agents accumulate symbolic capital through the doctrine's authority structure. This creates a subclass of field agents for whom the transition costs of doctrine displacement are not only personal behavioral costs but structural costs to their accumulated field position. In Bourdieu's (1989) terms, agents who have converted doctrinal mastery into field capital have vested interests in the doctrine's continued recognition as the standard of competence.

P1 and P2 together produce the lock-in dynamic North describes. P1 creates agents who have invested in the doctrine as part of their competence identity. P2 creates agents who have accumulated capital through the doctrine's authority structure. Both classes face above-average transition costs relative to the expected benefits of normative reform. When these classes have sufficient weight in the field's governance, enforcement, and socialization structures, the field's internal reform capacity is constrained to marginal adjustments within the existing doctrinal framework — a normative shock strong enough to displace the doctrine faces organized resistance from agents with structural reasons to prefer the current arrangement.

### 3.3 The Brazilian *Livre Convencimento* Case as a Conjunction Case

Paper 1G's genealogy establishes, through its narrative account, that the Brazilian reception of *livre convencimento* meets all five conditions for the pre-EC 45/2004 period. The formalization makes explicit where in Paper 1G's own documentary record each condition is supported.

**C1 — Output legibility.** Art. 131 of the CPC 1973 required that the judge "indicate, in the judgment, the motives that formed their conviction" (*indicar, na sentença, os motivos que lhe formaram o convencimento*). The formal obligation was output-presence: something must be written as stated motives. It was not output-quality: what was written did not need to constitute a rational argument traceable to precedent, cohere with analogous decisions, or enable intersubjective verification of the inferential pathway. A judgment that read "considering all the evidence, I reached this conviction" satisfied art. 131's formal obligation while being consistent with any underlying decisional process. The gap between the formal obligation (indicate motives) and genuine rational engagement (expose reasoning to intersubjective scrutiny) was not closeable by the then-available review mechanisms. Paper 1G's §3.2 identifies this gap as the core distortion: "the fundamentação was a shell — not a nucleus."

**C2 — Enforcement deficit.** Pre-EC 45/2004 enforcement of argumentative rationality was limited to normative isomorphism — reputational pressure from the academic-professional community. Paper 1G's §2.2 documents the structural limitations of this mechanism: geographically concentrated in academic centers, temporally slow in producing effects, hierarchically biased toward superior tribunals, and captured by the same doctrinal tradition it was meant to regulate. This enforcement mechanism tracked doctrine-citation presence rather than quality-of-engagement with the precedential reasoning. Appellate review corrected case outcomes without systematic tracking of the structural quality of the first-instance fundamentação process. Neither mechanism was granular enough to distinguish dispositional performance (decide by conviction, document minimally) from genuine compliance with art. 131's stated norm.

**C3 — Legitimizing tradition.** The Italian proceduralist school, transmitted to Brazil through Liebman's direct academic presence and extended by the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition, provided the legitimizing vocabulary. Paper 1G's §3.3 identifies this tradition's role: *livre convencimento* as "conquista de racionalidade processual" — a European Enlightenment gain representing liberation from the irrationality of legal proof hierarchies (*prova legal*). This vocabulary carried genuine historical content: the European *libre conviction* was indeed a rationalization move against the arbitrary evidentiary hierarchies of the medieval proof system. The Brazilian doctrinalization absorbed the dispositional practice under this vocabulary without examining the distance between the practice and the vocabulary's genealogical source. The vocabulary was available, positively valued in the field, and credible — and the enforcement deficit (C2) meant the distance was never subject to systematic examination by field enforcement mechanisms.

**P1 — Formation-integrated transmission.** The *livre convencimento* doctrine was incorporated into the canonical proceduralist texts that constituted Brazilian legal professional formation through the CPC 1973 period. Dinamarco's *Instrumentalidade do Processo* and the Cintra-Grinover-Dinamarco treatise were foundational texts of Brazilian proceduralist formation; both defended *livre convencimento* as a procedural guarantee. Law students formed under these texts internalized the doctrine as part of a competence conception: to understand Brazilian civil procedure correctly was, in part, to understand *livre convencimento* as a justified principle with a respectable European genealogy. The formation mechanism did not transmit the distorted version as a recognized deviation from the European source; it transmitted it as the correct understanding of the principle. DiMaggio and Powell's normative isomorphism operated precisely: professional formation reproduced the dispositional practice as procedural orthodoxy.

**P2 — Capital-generating function.** Academic authority in Brazilian civil procedure was substantially constituted by the ability to invoke, interpret, and extend the instrumentalidade framework of which *livre convencimento* was a component. This tradition generated doctorates, professorships, treatises, and consultancy authority that circulated within the field as recognized expertise. Agents who had accumulated this capital had structural interests in the doctrine's continued authority: a transition that delegitimized the doctrine would devalue capital accumulated through mastery of its framework, including the field positions built on that capital. Paper 1G's §3.3 identifies this mechanism in noting that "this tradition did not formulate — because the question its theoretical framework did not make visible — the patrimonial dimension of *livre convencimento*." The framework's blind spot was maintained partly by the capital distribution it had generated.

The conjunction of C1–C3 establishes that doctrinalization was not accidental: it followed structurally from identifiable features of the field that were simultaneously present. The conjunction of P1–P2 establishes the path-dependence lock-in: once doctrinalized, the practice generated its own reproduction mechanism through professional formation and capital distribution, creating organized resistance to the kind of normative shock required to displace it. This is what Paper 1G's thesis identifies as the "not accidental anomaly" claim — not that each historical event in the genealogy was determined, but that the structural conditions for doctrinalization and its persistence were identifiably present.

### 3.4 Differentiation from the Legal Transplant Hypothesis

Watson (1974, *Legal Transplants: An Approach to Comparative Law*) and subsequent transplant scholarship provide an alternative account of why *livre convencimento* in Brazil diverged from its European source. The transplant hypothesis holds: (a) legal rules transplant across legal systems without necessarily carrying the social, economic, and institutional context in which they originated; (b) context-mismatch produces drift from the original, as the transplanted rule is interpreted through the conceptual resources of the receiving system; (c) the extent and direction of drift depend on the available interpretive resources in the receiving context. On this account, the Brazilian reception's drift toward personal judicial discretion was not specifically caused by patrimonialism — it was caused by generic transplant-difficulty, which any legal system receiving an Enlightenment-origin procedural principle without robust *stare decisis* would have experienced.

The adversarial critique (`otherwise/paper1g-transplant-specification.md`) advances two structural arguments that this formalization accepts.

**First (adversarial §3.1): Watson's "available interpretive resources" include the structural features of the receiving legal system.** The civilian judicial authority model — *iura novit curia*, defining the judge as the authoritative interpreter of law — is itself an interpretive resource in exactly Watson's sense. Absent stare decisis enforcement, this structural feature already predicts a specific direction: judicial personal authority will expand toward its natural institutional limit. A judge constituted as authoritative interpreter, absent external precedent constraints, will exercise that authority in its natural direction — personal interpretive expansion over rational-engagement constraint. The direction is structural, not specifically patrimonial.

**Second (adversarial §3.2): The contrast baseline — that Roman-law tradition predicts formalism — is historically inaccurate.** Classical Roman law was judge-mediated, not formalist. The Pandectist tradition produced systematic categorization, not rule-application formalism. The operational form of civilian judicial authority is the declaratory theory of judicial decision: the judge declares the law authoritatively. This is structurally continuous with the Brazilian distortion — both turn on the judge's authoritative personal judgment — rather than directionally opposed to it. The formalist contrast baseline was a stereotype that comparative law scholarship has substantially revised.

These two concessions narrow the directional-specificity claim: the structural mechanism (civilian judicial authority + absent stare decisis) predicts the general direction of drift toward personal authority expansion. Patrimonialism is not uniquely required for the direction prediction.

The concession does not defeat Paper 1G's thesis. The adversarial critique correctly identifies the locus of the thesis's surviving contribution in its own surrender condition (b): the doctrinalization and lock-in of the distortion may require cultural habitus mechanisms that the structural civilian-architecture mechanism cannot explain. The thesis's distinctive contribution is not the direction of drift but the FORM of its doctrinalization.

The distinction requires precision. The structural mechanism predicts that informal practices of expanded personal judicial authority will develop in civilian systems with weak stare decisis. It does not predict that those practices will be DOCTRINALIZED: that they will acquire a technical name, a principled genealogy framing them as a positive rationality gain, and a legitimizing apparatus that converts the informal practice into professional formation orthodoxy. These are the C1–C3 conditions this paper formalizes. C1 and C2 may be structurally general features present in many civilian systems; C3 is the hinge condition, and the critical variable is not whether C3's vocabulary was available but HOW the receiving field absorbed it.

The Italian proceduralist school's vocabulary was available broadly across civil law jurisdictions: Liebman influenced Brazilian, Argentine, Italian, and Portuguese civil procedure scholarship in the mid-twentieth century. In all of these contexts, the civilian judicial authority model + weak stare decisis generated similar informal practices of expanded personal authority. The question the structural mechanism cannot answer is how the receiving legal field ABSORBED C3's vocabulary: as a critique of informal practice, or as a positive legitimization of it.

Brazil is the distinctive case: the Italian proceduralist vocabulary arrived in a legal field that absorbed it as positive genealogy for an existing practice. "*Livre convencimento*" was framed as "conquista de racionalidade processual" — a European Enlightenment gain. The informal expansion of personal judicial authority received a name, a genealogy from the legitimate European *libre conviction* movement, and an institutional apparatus through the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition that incorporated it into professional formation as orthodoxy. This absorptive direction — validation rather than critique or formal containment — is what patrimonialism explains that the structural mechanism cannot.

The patrimonial field configuration determined how C3's vocabulary was received. In a legal field where the informal practice of personal judicial authority had operated for two centuries (Phase I of Paper 1G's genealogy), was structurally sustained by C1-C2, and was continuous with the broader patrimonial habitus — treating authority as personal rather than office-bound, and norms as optional instruments rather than binding constraints — the Liebman vocabulary arrived as confirmation rather than challenge. The field absorbed C3 in the direction the patrimonial disposition organized: toward expanding personal authority, and away from the rational-engagement constraint that the European *libre conviction* movement's original context had embedded.

This relocates the directional-specificity contribution. Patrimonialism does not uniquely predict the general direction of drift (where the structural mechanism is correct and sufficient). Patrimonialism specifically explains the FORM of doctrinalization: why the drift was NAMED AND POSITIVELY VALUED as a doctrine with a European genealogy, why the legitimizing vocabulary was absorbed as validation rather than critique, and why the doctrine persisted through P1-P2 lock-in mechanisms that the structural mechanism cannot predict. The narrowed claim: the Watson hypothesis, even properly specified with civilian architectural features, predicts informal personal-authority expansion. The thesis explains the specific institutional form that expansion took — doctrinalization as a positive principle — and the specific mechanisms that reproduced it (P1: formation-integrated transmission; P2: capital-generating function).

This narrowing affects the thesis's scope but not its core. Paper 1G's central claim is that *livre convencimento* "foi a doutrinização de uma disposição patrimonialista" — not merely that personal authority expanded, but that it was DOCTRINALIZED. The doctrinalization claim is what the formalization here supports; the claim that patrimonialism uniquely determines direction (as opposed to form) was an overstatement that the adversarial correctly identifies. The thesis survives the adversarial's strongest argument by returning to its actual content: doctrinalization, not mere direction.

The adversarial critique has since advanced a second-order structural account (adversarial r2, `otherwise/paper1g-transplant-specification.md`, §3.4): peripheral field dynamics explain the C3 absorptive direction — the validation rather than critique orientation — through the legitimation dynamic of institutionalizing peripheral academic fields, without requiring patrimonialism. A peripheral legal academic field in the process of institutionalization, dependent on metropolitan intellectual authority for internal legitimation, will absorb metropolitan vocabulary as validation because the alternative (absorbing the vocabulary as condemning existing practice) threatens the field's collective legitimacy claim. The mechanism operates at the collective level through structural incentives, not through individual practitioners' cultural dispositions.

This orientation account is accepted at the level it operates: peripheral-field legitimation pressure explains why a receiving field under those conditions will absorb metropolitan vocabulary as validation rather than critique. The adversarial r3 (`otherwise/paper1g-transplant-specification.md`, updated §3.4) establishes a related point that is also accepted here: Italy cannot serve as a test case for peripheral-field reception dynamics because Italy was the *generating* field of the Liebman tradition, not a peripheral *receiving* field. Italy's reform-critical orientation toward thin-motivation practice was the originating scholarly purpose of the tradition being produced, set by its own producers — not an absorptive decision made by a receiving community. The generation/reception distinction is structurally correct. It follows that France, Germany, and Italy — all of which generated or independently developed the relevant traditions rather than receiving them from outside — are not counter-evidence for what peripheral receiving fields do under legitimation pressure.

The remaining question is whether the peripheral-field dynamics account explains not only the *orientation* (validation rather than critique) but also the *content* of what gets validated — specifically, whether it predicts that the vocabulary attaches to the existing informal practice of personal judicial authority rather than to other possible attachment points within the validation orientation. This is where the patrimonialism contribution is located.

The peripheral-field legitimation dynamic determines that the vocabulary will be absorbed as validation. It does not determine which features of the receiving field's existing practices the vocabulary attaches to. A peripheral legal field under legitimation pressure absorbing the Liebman vocabulary could direct that validation toward several available targets: the field's theoretical sophistication, its institutional modernity, its distance from archaic evidentiary hierarchies, or its characteristic informal practices of personal judicial authority. The legitimation dynamic selects the orientation (validation); it does not select the attachment point (validation *of what specifically*).

Content selection is determined by what the field's existing practices make available as natural attachment points under legitimation pressure. In a field where the characteristic informal practice — exercised under C1 (output legibility) and C2 (enforcement deficit) conditions — is expanded personal judicial discretion, the Liebman vocabulary's elements map most naturally onto that practice. The confirmation that "the free evaluation of proof we already practice is what the Europeans call *libre conviction*" is available as an attachment point precisely because the existing practice and the vocabulary can be aligned: both concern the judge's personal evaluative freedom over external evidentiary constraints. A non-patrimonial peripheral field without the specific C1+C2 conditions for that informal practice — or one in which the same informal practice is self-conceptualized as a deviation from ideal professional procedure rather than as a natural expression of judicial authority — would have different or no practices available for that particular attachment. The vocabulary would still be absorbed as validation under legitimation pressure, but the content of the validation would differ. The result might be validation of theoretical sophistication, or of procedural modernity as an aspiration, but not specifically the doctrinalization of personal judicial discretion as a named positive principle.

The phrase "self-conceptualized as a natural expression of judicial authority rather than as a deviation" requires structural precision. A competing account holds that C2 (enforcement deficit), sustained without institutional correction for a sufficient duration, itself produces this self-conceptualization: the absence of external corrective feedback normalizes the informal practice as authoritative regardless of cultural substrate. Whether this account is correct depends on how self-conceptualization modes are analyzed under enforcement-deficit conditions.

Three modes of self-conceptualization are structurally available to practitioners exercising an informal practice under C2. *Mode I (covert maintenance)*: practitioners recognize the practice as formally non-compliant, exercise it when enforcement is absent, and do not publicly claim it as correct procedure. The practice is sustained by enforcement deficit but not openly owned. *Mode II (tolerated informality)*: practitioners recognize the practice as not the formal ideal but treat it as accepted institutional reality — what work effectively requires — without positively claiming it as correct. The practice is normalized without being endorsed. *Mode III (authoritative ownership)*: practitioners conceive of the practice as the natural and legitimate expression of their institutional authority — not as deviation from a formal standard, but as correct performance of the role as their constitutive framework defines it.

C2 (enforcement deficit) reduces the cost structure that would force modes I and II into disuse or concealment. It does not select among the three modes. All three are consistent with the same enforcement deficit. What determines mode III specifically is whether the practitioner's constitutive framework of their institutional role defines personal evaluative judgment as authority rather than as excess relative to an authority standard.

In a rational-legal institutional framework, where the judge's role is constituted as applying formal norms to cases, the exercise of informal personal evaluative judgment beyond what the norms specify is assessed internally against the role's formal definition — and recognized as formal-standard-exceeding. This internal assessment persists even when external enforcement costs are zero. C2 reduces external cost; it does not eliminate the internal awareness that the practice departs from the role definition. A field with rational-legal constitutive role definitions will, under C2 conditions, produce modes I or II: informal discretion exercised when unconstrained but self-conceptualized as excess of the role rather than as the role's natural expression.

In the patrimonial institutional framework, by contrast, the judge's evaluative judgment is constituted as the legitimate form of judicial authority itself, not as excess relative to a formal standard. Personal conviction is not an expansion beyond the role — it is what the role, as the patrimonial framework defines it, authorizes. Under this framework, informal personal evaluative exercise is not self-conceptualized as deviation at any level of the practitioner's understanding. C2 provides the environment where mode III can be exercised openly without external cost, but the patrimonial constitutive framework is what produces mode III: the positive ownership of personal evaluative judgment as legitimate judicial authority, not merely as unchallenged deviation.

The significance for C3 absorption is direct. Retrospective-validation absorption — absorbing the Liebman vocabulary as confirming "what we are already doing" — requires mode III. The vocabulary can be recruited as validation only when the practice is publicly claimed as correct institutional performance. In mode I, recruiting the vocabulary as validation would expose what is being covertly maintained. In mode II, the vocabulary would be absorbed prospectively — as a standard to achieve, not as confirmation of what has been achieved. Only in mode III can the vocabulary function as retrospective confirmation: "what the Europeans call *libre conviction* is what we have been practicing." The Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition's framing of *livre convencimento* as "conquista de racionalidade processual" — a positive achievement, publicly defended in the academic field — is mode III absorption. Lenio Streck's diagnosis of "solipsismo judicial" from the critical direction confirms the same: what Streck identifies is not practitioners who know they are doing something formally non-compliant but get away with it (modes I or II), but practitioners who genuinely believe that deciding "according to conscience" is how judicial authority legitimately operates (mode III). The documented form of the Brazilian case is mode III behavior, not mode I or II.

The patrimonialism thesis explains mode III. C2 (enforcement deficit) explains why mode III was not forced back toward modes I or II by external cost. Neither alone is sufficient for the content-selection outcome. The peripheral-field legitimation dynamic explains why the mode III practice, once present, was absorbed in the validation orientation when C3 vocabulary became available. The three-level structure — patrimonialism for mode III, C2 for the survival of mode III, peripheral-field dynamics for the orientation of C3 absorption — is what a complete account of the Brazilian doctrinalization requires.

The patrimonialism thesis's surviving contribution operates at this content-selection level. The patrimonial field configuration — the informal practice of personal judicial authority sustained by C1-C2, and the habitual self-conceptualization of that practice as authoritative rather than deviant — determined which of the available attachment points was selected when the legitimation dynamic organized the vocabulary as validation. Peripheral-field dynamics and the patrimonialism thesis are therefore not competing explanations for the same phenomenon; they explain different aspects of the same process. The peripheral-field legitimation dynamic explains why the orientation was toward validation rather than critique. The patrimonialism account explains which specific practice was validated — the specific content of the doctrinalization form. Both are required for a complete explanation of what Paper 1G describes: not merely that *livre convencimento* was received as validation, but that it was specifically received as validation of the informal practice of personal judicial discretion and named as a positive principle of that practice.

The Portugal/Argentina comparison remains the decisive empirical test for whether the content-selection contribution survives. Both are peripheral receiving fields of the Liebman tradition, civilian with weak stare decisis, and neither is attributed the Faoro-Holanda patrimonialism diagnosis at the level of its judicial field. If the absorptive outcomes in those fields produced equivalent doctrinalization forms — a positively named doctrine of personal judicial freedom absorbed into formation orthodoxy — then either the C1+C2 conditions alone (independent of patrimonial habitual self-conceptualization) determine content selection, or those fields share relevant features at the level of judicial field dispositions that make the comparison less controlled than it appears. If they did not produce equivalent forms — if the Liebman vocabulary was absorbed in those fields without producing a specifically named positive doctrine of personal judicial discretion as formation orthodoxy — then the content-selection argument has scope that the peripheral-field dynamics account does not supply. Neither side has made this comparison at the documentary level required; it is the debate's primary open empirical question.

### 3.5 Content Established, Form Outstanding: The Residual Work of Mode III After Adversarial R4

Adversarial r4 establishes three things: that academic scholars, not judges, set the absorptive framing of C3, with orientation determined by peripheral-field legitimation dynamics (3.5.A); that C1+C2 salience and the Liebman vocabulary's specific conceptual content converge on personal judicial discretion as the over-determined attachment target (3.5.B); and that P1 generates Mode III post-C3, making Streck's evidence consistent with Scenario 2 in which Mode III is a doctrinalization output rather than its precondition (3.5.C). These three arguments are individually sound and mutually reinforcing. Together they establish that the CONTENT of C3 attachment — which practice the vocabulary was directed toward — follows from structural features of the receiving context without requiring Mode III as a pre-C3 causal state. They do not establish that the FORM of that attachment was similarly determined.

**The content-form distinction.** The Brazilian doctrinalization did not merely attach the Liebman vocabulary to the informal practice of personal judicial discretion; it attached it in a specific form: *retrospective naming of existing practice as an accomplished positive principle*. The Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition's framing of *livre convencimento* as "conquista de racionalidade processual" — a rationality gain already achieved, not one toward which Brazilian civil procedure aspires — is a specific absorptive form distinguishable from available alternatives.

Three absorptive forms were structurally open to a peripheral receiving field under the conditions R4 describes:

- **Form (i) — retrospective validation:** the informal practice the field already exercises is identified as what the European principle designates; it is named as an accomplished legitimate state ("we already do what the Europeans call *libre conviction*, and it constitutes a rationality gain").
- **Form (ii) — prospective normative alignment:** the principle is affirmed as the normative standard the field commits to implementing; the framing does not specifically claim that existing informal practice constitutes correct current implementation ("*livre convencimento* is what the European tradition calls *libre conviction*, and it is the rational principle we are committed to applying correctly").
- **Form (iii) — interpretive containment:** the vocabulary establishes the standard against which the field's informal practice is measured, organizing the framing around reform or improved application.

The peripheral-field legitimation dynamic predicts that the absorptive orientation favors validation over critique — ruling out form (iii). It does not predict form (i) over form (ii). Both are validating in orientation; both avoid the implication that the field is currently failing at an international standard; both serve the collective legitimacy function that the adversarial r4 invokes. The adversarial's pre-answer to 3.5.B argues that form (i) is preferred because prospective framing implies current substandard performance. But form (ii) as described is not prospective critique: affirming the principle as a normative commitment without specifically claiming that the existing informal practice already constitutes correct implementation does not imply current failure. A field can say "we affirm *livre convencimento* as the rational procedural principle" without saying "our current practice fully realizes it." The collective legitimacy argument selects against form (iii) — absorbing vocabulary as condemnation — not against form (ii), which is compatibly positive without committing to the stronger retrospective identification.

**What form (i) requires.** Form (i) — retrospective validation of existing informal practice as an accomplished state — requires that the existing practice be publicly claimable as the field's institutional performance. Academic scholars can produce form (i) only if there is a practice publicly presented as the field's authoritative institutional activity, available to be named as the principle's correct expression. A practice operating under Mode I (covertly maintained, not publicly owned) cannot be named as "what we legitimately do"; the naming would expose what is being concealed. A practice tolerated under Mode II (accepted as institutional reality, not positively endorsed as correct judicial performance) cannot be named as "the achieved rationality gain"; Mode II practitioners acknowledge the informal reality without claiming it as the authorized exercise of their institutional role. Only Mode III practitioners — who conceive of and present the informal exercise of personal conviction as the natural and legitimate expression of judicial authority — produce a publicly available practice of the right form to be retrospectively validated by academic scholars as "what we are already correctly doing."

The adversarial r4 argument in 3.5.A shows that academic scholars did not themselves require Mode III dispositions. Correct. It does not show that form (i) absorption was available to academic scholars independently of practitioners' Mode III public claims. Peripheral-field legitimation dynamics explains why scholars oriented toward validation (form (i) or form (ii)); it does not explain what made specifically form (i) available — the public claim that the existing informal practice IS the correct expression of the principle — rather than form (ii), which affirms the principle without that specific identification. Without Mode III practitioners presenting their informal practice as legitimate institutional performance, the structurally safer academic framing is form (ii): affirm the principle as normatively correct without asserting that existing practice already correctly implements it.

**The temporal problem in Scenario 2.** Adversarial 3.5.C's Scenario 2 proposes: peripheral-field dynamics + C1+C2 salience → C3 absorption → P1 → Mode III (post-C3, in practitioners formed under the doctrinalized tradition). This sequence is internally coherent. P1 explains how Mode III is generated and sustained in the field after doctrinalization is in place. But Scenario 2's account of the C3 absorption step requires that the original Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra framing was in form (i) — the retrospective validation framing — without Mode III in place at the time of that framing. P1 generates Mode III through formation; formation requires that the doctrine be established first. The retrospective framing that established the doctrine ("conquista de racionalidade processual") is causally prior to the P1 operation that would eventually produce Mode III. In Scenario 2, the retrospective form (i) of the original absorption must be explained by 3.5.A and 3.5.B, neither of which distinguishes form (i) from form (ii) at the C3 moment.

Scenario 2 produces post-C3 Mode III through P1 while leaving the form of the C3 absorption itself unaccounted by the same mechanism it invokes for post-C3 Mode III. If Mode III was not yet in place when the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra retrospective framing was produced, then the retrospective form (i) of that original framing is not explained by P1. The structural gap is not that Mode III did not eventually arise — Streck confirms it did — but that Scenario 2's account cannot explain why the original academic framing took form (i) rather than form (ii) before P1 had generated Mode III through formation.

**Symmetric gaps.** Scenario 1 (patrimonialism → Mode III pre-C3 → retrospective C3 absorption → P1 sustains Mode III) cannot produce documentary evidence of Mode III self-conceptualization from the pre-Liebman period: this is the honest limitation the adversarial r4 blog correctly identifies, and it stands. Scenario 2 cannot explain why the original C3 absorption took form (i) rather than form (ii) without invoking something at the C3 moment that distinguishes those forms — and P1, its primary Mode III-generating mechanism, is a post-C3 process that cannot fill that role. The gaps are not symmetric in kind: Scenario 1's gap is evidential (Mode III pre-C3 is inferred from Faoro-Holanda, not directly documented in the pre-Liebman judicial field); Scenario 2's gap is structural (the retrospective absorptive form at the C3 moment is not predicted by the structural mechanisms Scenario 2 invokes at that step). Neither gap is currently closeable from the exchange record.

*Falsified if:* A specific structural argument establishes that peripheral-field legitimation dynamics, C1+C2 salience, or vocabulary specificity — without invoking any practitioner-level self-conceptualization state — specifically predicts form (i) over form (ii) at the C3 absorption moment. This requires showing that the distinction between retrospective validation (naming existing informal practice as an accomplished legitimate state) and prospective normative alignment (affirming the principle as a normative commitment without that specific identification) is itself predictable from field-level structural dynamics alone. Adversarial r5 attempts to satisfy this condition through the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy (3.6.A) and through the academic-naming mechanism (3.6.B). Whether these attempts succeed is addressed in §3.6.

### 3.6 Form-Selection Under Adversarial R5: The Gap-Visibility Mechanism

Adversarial r5 (§§3.6.A–C) advances the most structurally complete challenge to the form-selection residual in the exchange so far. Both vectors target the §3.5 Falsified if condition from different angles.

**What adversarial r5 attempts.** Attack 3.6.A attempts to satisfy the Falsified if condition by showing that field-level dynamics alone — the collective legitimation incentive operating on the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy — specifically predict Form (i) over Form (ii) without invoking practitioner-level self-conceptualization. Attack 3.6.B attempts to show that the 'publicly claimable' requirement, on which the prior defense of the Mode III mechanism rested, is not a genuine constraint on Form (i) production by academic scholars. Attack 3.6.C claims the temporal-completeness structural gap in Scenario 2 is dissolved once 3.6.A and 3.6.B are in place.

**Against 3.6.A: the operative-frame premise.** The diagnostic-genealogy prediction depends on a structural claim that 3.6.A states but does not establish: that Brazilian proceduralists engaged with the Liebman vocabulary *through* the *prova legal*-critique as the operative analytical frame. The Form (ii) deficit-implication — "affirming *livre convencimento* as a normative commitment implies the field had been in the *prova legal* state" — follows only if the reform genealogy was the operative lens through which the vocabulary was received and processed. This conditional is what 3.6.A must establish, and it does not.

An alternative engagement frame was structurally available and contextually plausible. Brazil's 1939 CPC had already incorporated substantial evidence-evaluation freedom. A field in which evidence evaluation was operating under substantial judicial latitude before Liebman's arrival would not necessarily process the arriving vocabulary primarily as a reform critique of what the field had been doing wrong. It would more plausibly process it as intellectual articulation and legitimization of what the field was already engaged in — the correct epistemic orientation of the judge toward evidence, which the tradition articulates and justifies. In this frame, Form (ii) does not imply "we acknowledge we had been in the inadequate *prova legal* state"; it implies "we affirm the principle that articulates what we have been working toward." The deficit implication 3.6.A identifies operates specifically when the vocabulary arrives to a field that self-identifies as having been in the contrasting inadequate state. The 1939 CPC context complicates this self-identification.

The adversarial's anticipated reply (§4 of `otherwise/paper1g-transplant-specification.md`) argues that the reform genealogy was unavailable to be abstracted because Liebman's direct academic presence and the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition operated explicitly within the Italian school's reform framework. This is plausible as a historical inference, but it conflates the vocabulary's origin with its reception mode. How a foreign academic tradition is operationally engaged depends on what the receiving field brings to it — what questions the tradition is used to answer, what distinctions it enables, what intellectual work it performs. A vocabulary's genealogy does not determine how a receiving field deploys it. The adversarial's prediction requires establishing, not asserting, that the *prova legal*-critique frame was operative in the Brazilian reception. Without primary-source evidence — which neither side has produced — this is an inference from the vocabulary's origin to its reception mode, complicated by the contextual difference the 1939 CPC represents.

**Against 3.6.B: descriptive versus evaluative Form (i).** Adversarial 3.6.B distinguishes Form (i) by practitioner-claiming (requires Mode III) from Form (i) by academic-naming (requires only field-practice observability). This distinction is structurally sound at the *descriptive* level: an academic scholar can observe that Brazilian courts exercise personal conviction-based evaluation and characterize this practice as what the vocabulary calls *livre convencimento* without any Mode III endorsement from practitioners. Descriptive Form (i) — "what courts do here is what the vocabulary designates" — is available through academic-naming.

The Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra framing is not, however, descriptive Form (i). "Conquista de racionalidade processual" is evaluative Form (i): the existing practice *constitutes* an accomplished rationality gain. The evaluative step is not entailed by the descriptive step. A scholar can observe that Brazilian courts exercise personal conviction-based evaluation, characterize it as *livre convencimento*, and still stop at descriptive partial-identification — "this is what the vocabulary calls *livre convencimento*, though the European source embedded justificatory requirements the Brazilian practice does not systematically instantiate" — or prospective normative alignment — "we affirm this as the correct principle toward which Brazilian procedure should be oriented." What enables the evaluative Form (i) specifically — the characterization of the observed practice as an accomplished gain, not a deviant approximation or an aspiration — is the *invisibility* of the structural gap between the Brazilian informal practice and the European principle.

The European *libre conviction* was embedded in a justificatory framework: the judge's free evaluation of evidence was accompanied by a motivation requirement that made the evaluation intersubjectively reviewable. The Brazilian informal practice, under the C1-C2 conditions Paper 1G documents, exercised the evaluative freedom without the systematic justificatory engagement the European context embedded it in. This gap — between evaluation-without-justificatory-engagement and evaluation-with-justificatory-engagement — is what Streck eventually identified as the distortion. It was not identified at the C3 moment, when the vocabulary was absorbed in evaluative Form (i) as a rationality gain already achieved.

The patrimonial constitutive framework explains why the gap was invisible. In a field where personal evaluative judgment is constitutively the natural and legitimate expression of judicial authority — not excess relative to a formal standard requiring justificatory accountability — the European principle and the Brazilian informal practice converge from the inside: both concern the judge's personal evaluative authority over evidence, and within the framework, that authority is constitutively legitimate. The justificatory-engagement dimension of the European principle, which marks the structural distance from the Brazilian informal practice, is the dimension for which the patrimonial constitutive framework has no distinct category. It is not suppressed; it is simply not legible as a separable requirement from inside the framework that treats personal judicial judgment and proper judicial authority as co-extensive.

Academic scholars embedded in the same field process the vocabulary through the same constitutive framework. They do not produce partial-identification framing or prospective normative alignment, because the gap their framing would need to identify is not visible as a gap from inside the framework. They produce evaluative Form (i) because evaluative Form (i) is what follows when the observed practice and the principle's positive designatum are perceived as co-extensive — which they are, from inside the patrimonial constitutive framework.

Adversarial 3.6.B correctly establishes that academic-naming produces descriptive Form (i) without Mode III practitioner endorsement. It does not establish that academic-naming produces *evaluative* Form (i) without gap-invisibility. Mode III in practitioners is not what the response now claims is required; what is required is the gap-invisibility that makes evaluative Form (i) the natural academic output. Gap-invisibility is what the patrimonial constitutive framework specifically provides, and is what neither structural features nor peripheral-field dynamics independently predict.

**Scenario 2's temporal sequence remains incomplete.** Attack 3.6.C claims the temporal gap identified in §3.5 is dissolved by 3.6.A and 3.6.B together. If the operative-frame premise of 3.6.A is unestablished, and the gap-invisibility requirement for evaluative Form (i) (3.6.B) goes unaccounted by structural features, the sequence remains incomplete at the C3 step. The sequence Scenario 2 proposes — structural features + peripheral-field dynamics → evaluative Form (i) framing at C3 — still requires explaining what made the structural gap invisible to scholars at the C3 moment, enabling evaluative rather than descriptive or prospective framing. Patrimonialism answers this question; the adversarial's 3.6.A–B do not.

The remaining asymmetry of gaps: Scenario 1's gap is evidential (Mode III pre-C3 is inferred from Faoro-Holanda, not documented from pre-Liebman judicial field sources). Scenario 2's gap, after r5, is the gap-invisibility question: what made the structural distance between Brazilian informal practice and European principle imperceptible to scholars at the C3 moment, producing evaluative Form (i) rather than descriptive partial-identification? This is no longer a purely temporal gap (Scenario 2 can get Mode III through P1 post-C3); it is a gap at the level of what explains gap-invisibility at the C3 framing moment.

*Falsified if:* A structural argument establishes that the patrimonial constitutive framework is not required to explain the evaluative Form (i) characterization — specifically, that academic scholars engaged in field-legitimation under C1+C2 conditions, without the patrimonial constitutive framework, would produce evaluative Form (i) framing ("rationality gain, already achieved") rather than descriptive partial-identification or prospective normative alignment. This requires showing either that (i) field-legitimation dynamics alone predict evaluative rather than descriptive Form (i) production, independent of the field's constitutive framework's visibility of the structural gap; or (ii) the structural gap between Brazilian informal practice and the European principle was visible to scholars at the C3 moment through some mechanism other than the patrimonial constitutive framework, and evaluative Form (i) was selected despite that visibility.

### 3.7 Field-Level Conceptual Vocabulary Absence: The Reframed Mechanism After Adversarial R6

Adversarial r6 (§§3.7.A–D) targets the gap-visibility mechanism formulated in §3.6. The four attacks succeed against a specific formulation — one in which the patrimonial constitutive framework reaches academic scholars' individual cognition, rendering the justificatory gap individually imperceptible. The response accepts this challenge at that formulation and repositions the mechanism.

**The reframed mechanism.** The relevant gap was not individually imperceptible through the judicial practitioners' constitutive framework reaching academic scholars. It was invisible because the Brazilian proceduralist field's theoretical vocabulary at the C3 moment had not developed systematic justificatory engagement — the accountability dimension of the European *libre conviction* — as a distinct component of the concept of rational judicial evaluation. This is a property of the field's theoretical state at C3, not of individual scholars' epistemic access. Scholars deploying the field's available concepts for "rational judicial evaluation" did not encounter the accountability dimension as a distinctly askable question because the field had not yet theorized it as a separate positive requirement. They engaged with the vocabulary's positive content as the field's theoretical resources constituted it at C3: freedom of judicial evaluation from formal proof hierarchies. Whether there was a further accountability dimension that marked the distance between European full implementation and Brazilian informal practice was not a question the field's available vocabulary made distinctly available.

The source of this conceptual absence is patrimonialism. The Brazilian proceduralist field's theoretical vocabulary was formed in a context — across the prior two centuries of judicial practice under C1+C2 conditions — where judicial rationality was constitutively understood as personal authoritative judgment: the social meaning of rational judicial evaluation was the judge's personal conviction authorized by the institutional role, not the judge's systematic accountability to intersubjective justificatory standards. A field formed under this constitutive understanding would theorize "rational judicial evaluation" in terms of freedom from external constraints on personal judgment. The accountability dimension was absent from what the field's prior theoretical formation had constituted as the content of "rational judicial evaluation" — not suppressed from an available conceptual repertoire, but never constituted as a distinct positive requirement in the first place.

**Against 3.7.A: the primary-content question.** Adversarial 3.7.A distinguishes the vocabulary's primary positive content (liberation from formal proof hierarchies) from an associated accountability safeguard (motivation requirements), and argues that evaluative Form (i) at the primary-content level does not require gap-invisibility about secondary procedural requirements. This distinction is analytically available *if* the Brazilian field's theoretical vocabulary at C3 had constituted it — if scholars had separable concepts for "what the vocabulary's primary content is" and "what the vocabulary's associated accountability requirements are." But the primary-content/secondary-safeguard distinction is itself a conceptual resource the field's vocabulary at C3 had not developed. Scholars were not abstracting away from a recognized secondary dimension when they characterized the Brazilian reception as "conquista de racionalidade processual"; they were engaging with the vocabulary as a unit whose positive content their field's theoretical apparatus had constituted in toto.

The deeper issue concerns what "rationality" designated in the Italian reform tradition's positive content. The reform's positive claim was not merely "freedom from formal hierarchies" but "rational free evaluation of evidence" — *libero convincimento* as rational, not merely unregulated, exercise of judicial evaluation. What distinguished rational from merely unregulated free evaluation, within the tradition's positive content, included the combination of freedom from arbitrary formal rules *and* systematic justificatory engagement that made the evaluation intersubjectively accountable. Identifying existing Brazilian informal practice (free evaluation without systematic justificatory accountability) with "conquista de racionalidade processual" — the achieved *rational* state — requires either treating rationality as equivalent to freedom-from-formal-constraints alone, or not perceiving the accountability dimension as a distinct component of the vocabulary's rational standard. The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence explains the second option: the Brazilian field had not constituted "systematic justificatory accountability" as a distinct component of "rational judicial evaluation," so scholars processing the vocabulary through the field's available concepts did not encounter the accountability dimension as a separately evaluable requirement, and the whole rational standard as the field constituted it was occupiable through the liberation dimension alone.

**Against 3.7.B: what the rational pole of the binary comprises.** Adversarial 3.7.B argues that the vocabulary's diagnostic binary (rational: *livre convencimento* / irrational: *prova legal*) generates the evaluative intensity of Form (i) through legitimation-incentive maximization under founding-moment field pressure. Placing existing practice on the rational side of this binary is inherently evaluative; the superlative ("conquista") is the legitimation maximum within the binary's available evaluative content. Gap-invisibility is not the generating mechanism.

This account is structurally coherent as an explanation of evaluative intensity *within Form (i)*, conditional on the vocabulary's rational pole being occupied by the liberation dimension specifically. But the content of the rational pole is precisely what is in question. If the rational pole is constituted as "freedom from formal proof hierarchies," then Brazilian informal practice (conviction-based decision-making, unregulated by formal evidentiary rules) straightforwardly occupies it, and legitimation maximization produces "conquista." If the rational pole is constituted as "freedom from formal hierarchies + systematic justificatory accountability," then Brazilian informal practice occupies only one dimension of the rational pole, and legitimation maximization under that binary would produce partial-identification or prospective normative framing — acknowledging the unoccupied dimension rather than claiming the full rational state as achieved. "Conquista de racionalidade processual" — the whole rational state achieved — is the legitimation maximum only when the rational pole is constituted by the liberation dimension alone in the field's available vocabulary.

The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence explains why the rational pole in Brazilian proceduralism's C3 vocabulary comprised the liberation dimension specifically: the field had not constituted the accountability dimension as a distinct component of rational judicial evaluation. Legitimation-incentive maximization, operating within the field's available conceptual constitution of the rational pole, produces "conquista" as the evaluative maximum. Without the conceptual vocabulary absence, equal legitimation pressure would maximally affirm whatever the field's concepts constituted as the rational standard — if the accountability dimension were conceptually available as a distinct positive requirement, the maximum Form (i) claim would need to either affirm its presence or leave it explicitly for future development, rather than affirming "conquista" simpliciter.

3.7.B also does not resolve the Form (i)/Form (ii) selection question independently of 3.6.A. The evaluative binary shows why Form (i) framing is evaluative; it does not show why Form (i) rather than Form (ii) was selected at C3. If 3.6.A's operative-frame premise remains contested (§3.6), 3.7.B supplies only the evaluative-intensity explanation within Form (i), not the Form (i)/Form (ii) selection explanation.

**Against 3.7.C: two distinct kinds of awareness.** Adversarial 3.7.C identifies a tension between the operative-frame defense (implying comparative historical awareness of Brazil's procedural context relative to *prova legal*) and gap-invisibility (requiring that scholars lacked perceptual access to the structural gap). The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence reframing dissolves this tension because it operates at a different analytical level from historical comparative awareness.

Comparative historical awareness — knowing that Brazil's 1939 CPC had incorporated substantial evidence-evaluation latitude, and thus not self-identifying as "the field with the *prova legal* system" — is knowledge about Brazil's legal history relative to a particular procedural form. Field-level conceptual vocabulary is what the field's theoretical apparatus had constituted as distinct positive requirements. A field can have the first without the second: knowing that you are not practicing strict formal proof hierarchies does not require having theorized "systematic justificatory accountability" as a separately named positive requirement of rational judicial evaluation. These are different epistemic objects at different analytical levels; there is no internal tension in being aware of one and lacking the conceptual vocabulary for the other.

On Liebman's direct presence and the relational deficit implication: the adversarial correctly notes that Form (ii)'s deficit implication is relational (aspiring-to vs. already-at the rational state), not dependent on strict *prova legal* as the starting point. This is a genuine refinement of 3.6.A. The response from the conceptual vocabulary absence account: the "already-at vs. aspiring-to" distinction requires scholars to perceive their practice as either fully occupying or as falling short of the rational standard. If the rational standard as the field's vocabulary constituted it was liberation-from-formal-hierarchies, then Brazilian informal practice (exercised under C1+C2 conditions, free from formal proof rules) fully occupied the standard as constituted. The relational deficit (aspiring-to rather than already-at) would only arise if scholars perceived a further dimension of the standard — the accountability dimension — that their practice had not achieved. The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence explains why scholars at C3 perceived their practice as fully occupying the rational standard rather than as aspiring to it: because the standard as the field's vocabulary constituted it did not include the dimension relative to which aspiring-to would arise.

**Against 3.7.D: consistent with 3.5.A; differential conceptual availability.** Adversarial 3.7.D is the most structurally significant attack. The accepted 3.5.A concession established that academic scholars' absorptive orientation was set by academic field-position dynamics, not by judicial practitioners' constitutive role framework. Gap-visibility framed as individual cognitive imperceptibility through the judicial constitutive framework is in tension with this. The adversarial proposes that the legitimation project's affirmative epistemic posture generates gap-*inattention* — scholars did not examine the accountability gap because examining it would undermine the legitimation project — without requiring constitutive-framework imperceptibility.

The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence reframing is consistent with 3.5.A because it does not locate the mechanism at the level of individual scholars' cognition governed by the judicial practitioners' constitutive framework. What academic legitimation dynamics could produce was bounded by the field's available theoretical vocabulary. The legitimation project's maximization operated on concepts that the field's theoretical apparatus had constituted; it could not produce concepts that the field's vocabulary had not developed. The absence of "systematic justificatory accountability" as a distinct component of the field's concept of rational judicial evaluation is a constraint on what legitimation-motivated academic production could achieve at C3 — not a failure of scholarly attention, but a boundary on the field's conceptual resources within which scholars operated.

The distinction between the reframed mechanism and the adversarial's legitimation-inattention alternative is structural, not terminological. Legitimation-inattention (3.7.D's alternative) is a motivational account: scholars were motivated not to examine the gap because examining it would undermine the alignment claim. Under a different motivational environment — a scholar outside the legitimation project, or operating with a critical agenda — the gap would be visible and examinable. The conceptual vocabulary absence account is constitutive: the gap was not distinctly askable through the field's available vocabulary regardless of the scholar's motivational environment. A scholar operating with the same field vocabulary but outside the legitimation project would still not encounter "systematic justificatory accountability" as a distinct positive component of rational judicial evaluation, because the field's vocabulary had not constituted it as such. The behavioral implication: if the gap was not identified by critical scholars at C3 or in the early post-C3 period (before P1 had generated the Mode III habitus that Streck later diagnosed), this is more consistent with conceptual vocabulary absence than with motivational inattention, which a critical scholarly posture would be expected to overcome.

The selective-blindness problem dissolves under differential conceptual availability. Scholars engaged with the *prova legal* critique because the Italian reform tradition had explicitly articulated "freedom from formal proof hierarchies" as the positive content of its reform — this articulated concept was available in the vocabulary being received. The accountability dimension — while present in the European tradition's procedural practice — was not equally articulated as a distinct positive requirement separable from the general rational character of free evaluation at the time of Liebman's C3 engagement. Scholars engaged with what the vocabulary presented as its articulated positive content; the accountability dimension was not an equally available articulated concept for them not to examine. This is not selective closure but differential availability: one concept was offered by the vocabulary as its primary articulated content; the other was not constituted as a distinct positive requirement by either the vocabulary or the field's prior theoretical formation. Examining what is not distinctly available in one's conceptual vocabulary is not possible regardless of scholarly motivation.

The patrimonialism contribution at the conceptual vocabulary level: why did the Brazilian proceduralist field's vocabulary at C3 not constitute systematic justificatory accountability as a distinct component of rational judicial evaluation? Because the field was formed across the prior two centuries — under C1+C2 conditions — in a context where judicial rationality was constitutively understood as personal authoritative judgment. A field in which the social meaning of rational judicial evaluation is personal conviction authorized by institutional role has no structural incentive to separately constitute "systematic justificatory accountability" as a distinct positive requirement of rational evaluation — that distinction requires experiencing personal judgment and accountability as potentially in tension, which the patrimonial constitutive understanding of judicial authority forecloses from inside. The conceptual vocabulary absence at C3 is the product of this prior formation, not an accident of the Italian tradition's own state of articulation.

*Falsified if:* (i) Primary-source evidence or structural argument establishes that the Brazilian proceduralist field's theoretical vocabulary at C3 had constituted "systematic justificatory accountability" as a distinct positive component of rational judicial evaluation — distinguishable from freedom from formal hierarchies — and scholars produced evaluative Form (i) ("conquista") despite that conceptual availability. This would show the vocabulary absence did not hold at C3, and the remaining explanation for evaluative Form (i) would need to be either motivated gap-inattention (3.7.D's alternative) or a structural account that predicts "conquista" even when the accountability dimension is conceptually available. (ii) The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence at C3 is attributable to the Italian tradition's own state of articulation at the time of Liebman's Brazil engagement (1940–1946), rather than to the Brazilian field's prior patrimonial formation. If the Italian reform tradition had not yet distinguished freedom and accountability as separately articulable positive components of *libero convincimento* until after the Liebman period, any receiving field — regardless of prior patrimonial formation — would exhibit the same conceptual absence at C3. This would eliminate the patrimonialism contribution from the conceptual vocabulary mechanism, leaving the vocabulary's own articulation state as the explanation. (iii) Comparative evidence from peripheral non-patrimonial fields that received the Liebman tradition at comparable field-founding moments shows that those fields produced evaluative Form (i) framing of comparable intensity ("conquista"-equivalent) despite operating with field vocabularies that did include the accountability dimension as a distinct conceptual requirement — i.e., the evaluative intensity was produced by legitimation-incentive maximization independent of the conceptual vocabulary's constitution of the rational standard.

### 3.8 Against the Field-Level Conceptual Vocabulary Absence: Response to Adversarial R7

Adversarial r7 attacks the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence on three grounds organized around the Italian CPC 1940's separate codification of the freedom and accountability dimensions during Liebman's USP engagement (1940–1946). The attacks share a common factual premise: that the Italian CPC 1940 separately codified Art. 116 (*libero convincimento del giudice*) and Art. 132, n. 4 (*motivazione* as judgment-form obligation) during exactly the period of Liebman's transmission, making the vocabulary absence claim structurally improbable. The response accepts the factual premise and contests the inference at each step.

**What the adversarial's inference requires.** The inference from "Italian CPC 1940 separately codified both obligations" to "accountability was part of the transmitted *libero convincimento* vocabulary" requires two steps: (a) Liebman transmitted both obligations; (b) the accountability obligation through Art. 132 was constituted as an internal positive component of the *libero convincimento* concept's positive designatum — the concept that organized the C3 Brazilian reception — rather than as a separate institutional obligation in a distinct area of Italian proceduralist scholarship. Step (a) has strong structural support: Liebman's scholarly identity was the Italian reform tradition, and the Italian CPC 1940 was the tradition's positive output. Step (b) does not follow automatically from step (a). The vocabulary absence claim operates at step (b)'s level: whether the accountability dimension, available as a judgment-form institution through Art. 132, was constituted as internal to *libero convincimento* as an evidentiary evaluation principle (Art. 116's domain) in the scholarship that organized the C3 conceptual vocabulary.

**The two-institution structure as evidence against internal integration.** The Italian CPC 1940's separate codification is itself structural evidence that the Italian proceduralist tradition treated *libero convincimento* and *motivazione* as analytically distinct institutions. Art. 116 concerns the judge's internal evaluation process: how the judge weighs evidence, the scope of the judge's discretion in drawing inferences from the probative record, freedom from formal hierarchical rules governing what evidence may be considered. Art. 132 concerns the judgment document's external form: what the decision must contain to satisfy the parties' and appellate courts' reviewability requirements. These Articles govern distinct normative domains. The *libero convincimento* debates in Italian proceduralist doctrine concern the scope of judicial evidentiary discretion; the *motivazione* debates concern appellate reviewability and the judgment document's formal adequacy. These are distinct scholarly debates, organized around distinct Articles for distinct reasons. If accountability were so internally part of *libero convincimento*'s positive designatum that transmitting the principle necessarily transmitted the accountability dimension as a component of that concept, the Italian codifiers would have had structural reason to express this unity through a single provision. They did not; they gave each obligation its own Article.

The adversarial anticipates a response of this form and argues in its §4 that the Italian reform tradition's *internal logic* does not support the Art. 116/Art. 132 analytical separation: *motivazione* is "the structural counterpart that gives the freedom principle its rational character," so transmitting *libero convincimento* as freedom without its accountability counterpart transmits only the reform's negative conclusion (abandon *prova legale*) stripped of its positive content (rational evaluation = free + accountable). This argument concerns the theoretical ideal of the reform — what rational free evaluation ideally requires. A theoretical ideal can require the unity of freedom and accountability while the scholarly deployment of *libero convincimento* as a positive procedural principle remains organized around the Art. 116 domain (evidentiary evaluation, judicial discretion over evidential weight) because that is the domain the concept specifically addresses. The reform tradition's theoretical ideal does not establish that the *libero convincimento* scholarship — the scholarship specifically about Art. 116's evidentiary evaluation principle — internally integrated the accountability dimension as a component of the concept's positive designatum. The code's own two-institution structure is the most direct available evidence about how the Italian proceduralist tradition itself treated the relationship between the two obligations: as analytically distinct.

**The version (i*) distinction.** The adversarial's version (i)/(ii) taxonomy does not track the vocabulary absence claim's operative level:

- *Version (i) [adversarial framing]:* The Italian tradition lacked the concept of systematic justificatory accountability — the concept was unavailable.
- *Version (ii) [adversarial framing]:* The concept was available in the transmitted vocabulary but not constituted as a distinct component of the Brazilian field's conceptual resources.

The adversarial correctly establishes that version (i) is improbable given Art. 132's existence, and argues that version (ii) collapses to vocabulary selection explained by the structural account.

The operative claim is version (i*): *motivazione*'s accountability dimension was available in the Italian proceduralist vocabulary as a *separate procedural institution* (Art. 132's domain, with its own scholarly debates and appellate-reviewability analytical foundation), but was not constituted as an *internal positive component of the *libero convincimento* concept's positive designatum* in the scholarship organized around Art. 116. Version (i*) is not version (ii) because version (ii) requires that the accountability dimension was part of *libero convincimento* as a unified transmitted concept — a concept from which the Brazilian field selectively excluded the accountability component. Under version (i*), the separation is located within the Italian tradition's own two-institution structure: the tradition transmitted *libero convincimento* (the Art. 116 evidentiary evaluation principle) and *motivazione* (the Art. 132 judgment-form obligation) as analytically distinct institutions, and the C3 conceptual vocabulary-formation around *livre convencimento* drew on the former without necessarily integrating the latter as an internal conceptual component of the evidentiary evaluation principle.

Under version (i*), a receiving field can have accountability in its vocabulary (as a general procedural requirement governing what judgments must contain) while not having constituted that obligation as internally part of what the evidentiary evaluation principle means. This is the structure the Italian code itself instantiates: both obligations are codified, but they are separate Articles because the Italian codifiers treated them as governing distinct normative domains. The Brazilian reception of the tradition that produced this code would most naturally replicate the same institutional separation.

Under version (i*), the structural account's legitimation-incentive explanation for selective non-adoption — which applies to version (ii) — is only partially available. Version (ii)'s selective non-adoption operates within a unified transmitted concept: legitimation incentives explain why the Brazilian field constituted only the freedom dimension as the rational pole while excluding the accountability dimension from the same concept. Under version (i*), the accountability dimension was not a component of the *libero convincimento* concept available for selective exclusion; it was in a separate institutional domain. The question of which institutional domain's scholarship organized the C3 vocabulary-formation around *livre convencimento* is where the patrimonialism contribution operates: a field formed under conditions where judicial rationality was constitutively understood as personal authoritative judgment would absorb the evidentiary evaluation principle (Art. 116's domain) as the vocabulary's organizing concept, while the separate judgment-form obligation (Art. 132's domain) remained in its own analytical area without being integrated into the evidentiary concept's positive content.

**Against §3.8.C: the art. 93, IX CF constitutionalization is consistent with version (i*).** Under version (i*), *motivação* was available in the Brazilian proceduralist vocabulary as a separate judgment-form obligation — not internally part of *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum, but present in the vocabulary's framework of judgment requirements (analogous to Art. 132's separate status in Italian proceduralism). The 1988 constitutionalization of the motivation requirement as art. 93, IX CF constitutionalizes that separate institution at the constitutional level — elevating an existing judgment-form obligation to constitutional status — without requiring that the accountability dimension had been internally part of *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum throughout the Liebman tradition.

The adversarial's reception-selectivity account requires that the accountability dimension was transmitted as part of the *livre convencimento* concept itself, was selectively not constituted in the Brazilian field's version at C3 for legitimation reasons, and was eventually reconstituted at the constitutional level in 1988. Under version (i*), no selective reconstitution from within the concept occurred: *motivação* was always in the vocabulary as a separate institution; the 1988 moment constitutionalized it as an independent obligation applicable to all judicial decisions. The ongoing scope debates around art. 93, IX CF — including the ESHTR C2 debate about whether the provision extends to the ementa's accuracy — are more consistent with version (i*). If the accountability dimension had been fully constituted as internal to *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum throughout the tradition, the constitutional provision's scope would be substantially determined by that concept's content. The fact that scope is independently contested at the constitutional text level, with *livre convencimento* doctrine playing no determining role, is more consistent with art. 93, IX CF having constitutionalized a distinct procedural institution with its own independent scope.

*Falsified if:* Primary-source evidence from Italian proceduralist scholarship on *libero convincimento* as an evidentiary evaluation principle — Liebman's own works, or contemporaneous Italian doctrine organized around Art. 116 — establishes that within the *libero convincimento* doctrine specifically, the accountability dimension through *motivazione* was systematically integrated as a constitutively internal positive requirement of what the principle means, not merely as a separate provision in the same code. Such evidence would show Italian proceduralists writing on *libero convincimento* consistently treating freedom and accountability as internally unified within the concept's positive designatum — in the evidentiary evaluation debates organized around Art. 116, not merely in the reform tradition's general theoretical framework — making the two-institution code structure a codification artifact rather than an analytical separation. The adversarial r7's structural probability argument from the Italian CPC 1940's separate codification is a step toward this claim but does not constitute it: the argument establishes the accountability dimension's institutional availability in the Italian tradition's code, not its internal integration into the *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically.

### 3.8.F Against the Possibility A/B Dilemma (Adversarial R8, §3.8.D)

Adversarial r8 attacks version (i*) through an exhaustive structural dilemma. Either (Possibility A) Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship organized its evidentiary evaluation debates while treating *motivazione*'s accountability dimension as analytically external to the concept's positive designatum — in which case every receiving field, patrimonial or not, inherits non-integration, eliminating any patrimonialism-specific contribution to non-integration in Brazil; or (Possibility B) the scholarship engaged with the rationality question by constituting accountability as an internal positive component of *libero convincimento*'s positive designatum — in which case the version (i*) structural inference collapses, because the scholarship did not maintain the analytical separation version (i*) requires. The adversarial argues neither horn is available without cost.

**Contesting the structural prior for Possibility B.**

The adversarial's structural prior for Possibility B is that the reform tradition organized its evidentiary evaluation debates around the rationality question — what distinguishes rational from arbitrary judicial evaluation — and that *motivazione* is the tradition's constitutive answer: rational free evaluation requires both freedom from formal evidentiary hierarchies and systematic accountability through motivated judgment. On this reading, the scholarship would engage with accountability as internal positive content, because accountability through *motivazione* is what "rational" means within the reform's own theoretical framework.

This structural prior conflates two distinct questions: (Q1) What does the reform tradition's theoretical logic require as the predicate of rational judicial evaluation? (Q2) What did Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically deploy as the concept's positive designatum in its evidentiary evaluation debates organized around Art. 116? A theoretical framework can require freedom and accountability as jointly constitutive of rational evaluation (Q1) while the scholarly deployment of the evidentiary evaluation principle remains organized around the judicial discretion and freedom dimension specifically (Q2) — because that is the domain the principle is called on to address in the doctrinal debates it enters. The Art. 116 debates concern what evidentiary inferences the judge may draw freely, which admissibility constraints survive the reform, the scope of appellate review over evidentiary determinations. These questions are answered by specifying the freedom dimension. The accountability dimension belongs structurally to the Art. 132 debates: what the judgment document must contain to satisfy reviewability requirements. A scholar writing on *libero convincimento* as an evidentiary evaluation principle can deploy the reform tradition's theoretical framework while engaging with the concept specifically in the domain where it does its doctrinal work — the discretion and freedom debates — without integrating the accountability dimension as an internal positive component of the evidentiary evaluation concept.

The Italian codifiers' own two-institution decision is the most direct available evidence of how the tradition institutionalized the relationship between these two questions. Giving each obligation its own Article, in distinct normative chapters, reflects a judgment that the evidentiary evaluation principle and the judgment-form obligation are analytically distinct institutions. A structural prior for Possibility B — that the scholarship unified the two questions within *libero convincimento*'s positive designatum — must contend with the most direct available institutional output of that scholarship, which runs the other way. The adversarial's structural prior (the reform's rational predicate constitutively requires accountability) concerns the tradition's theoretical ideal, not the concept's scholarly deployment as an evidentiary evaluation principle specifically. These are different objects; the prior for one does not constitute the prior for the other.

**Fork argument under Possibility A.**

Granting Possibility A for the purposes of argument — Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship treated the accountability dimension as analytically external to the concept's positive designatum — the adversarial's inference does not follow. Under Possibility A, all receiving fields inherit non-integration. The patrimonialism account does not claim to explain the *production* of non-integration in Brazil; under Possibility A, non-integration is transmitted from the Italian tradition to every receiving field. The question is what happens to non-integration in those receiving fields over time, under accountability pressure that arises independently of the tradition's initial conceptual organization.

Non-patrimonial receiving fields inherit non-integration under Possibility A but retain conceptual resources structurally distinct from those of a patrimonial field: prior understandings of judicial rationality as accountability-involving (even if not constituted as internal to the evidentiary evaluation concept specifically), institutional cultures that treat formal reasoning obligations as positively constitutive of judicial role definition, and theoretical vocabularies in which the gap between inherited non-integration and emerging accountability demands would be recognized as a distinctly available question requiring conceptual resolution. When accountability pressure arrives in such a field, the field has the resources to constitute the accountability question as a problem internal to the evidentiary evaluation concept and to integrate the solution.

A patrimonial receiving field inherits the same non-integration but its prior formation constituted judicial rationality as personal authoritative judgment — not as judgment with systematic intersubjective accountability. When accountability pressure arrives in this field, non-integration is not experienced as a conceptual gap requiring resolution within the evidentiary principle; it is experienced as external regulatory imposition on a concept whose rational character is already complete in the freedom dimension. The predicted response: not integration of the accountability dimension into *livre convencimento*'s positive content, but external regulation (accountability as a separate constitutional obligation) or eventual concept-abolition (the concept as doctrinalized being perceived as incompatible with the accountability regime).

This is the fork: non-patrimonial fields inheriting non-integration under Possibility A have the conceptual resources to integrate accountability when demands arrive; patrimonial fields that inherit the same non-integration naturalize it through prior formation, producing external regulation or abolition rather than integration. The patrimonialism contribution under Possibility A is not to the production of non-integration but to its character and durability in the receiving field.

**Behavioral prediction distinguishing the accounts.**

The fork generates a testable prediction. Under the structural account (non-integration transmitted equally to all receiving fields under Possibility A, with no formation-specific durability differential), when incentive structures change — specifically, the 1988 constitutionalization of *motivação* as art. 93, IX CF — the predicted response for a field whose non-integration was contextual and externally transmitted would be reconstitution of the accountability dimension as internal to the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive content, since the reform tradition's theoretical logic (Q1) requires that unity once the accountability question is made salient. Under the patrimonialism account, the field responds by treating art. 93, IX CF as a separate constitutional obligation, maintaining conceptual separation between the evidentiary evaluation principle and the accountability obligation, until the incompatibility produces concept-abolition.

The Brazilian trajectory: (1) 1988 constitutionalization of *motivação* as art. 93, IX CF — a separate constitutional obligation with its own scope debates, not an elaboration of *livre convencimento*'s positive content; (2) continuing doctrinal separation between what *livre convencimento* requires (evidentiary freedom) and what art. 93, IX CF requires (motivated judgment), with no systematic doctrinal project integrating the two within the evidentiary concept; (3) CPC 2015 elimination of *livre convencimento* as a named principle rather than its revision to include accountability as a positive requirement. This trajectory — external regulation, persistent separation, concept-abolition — is more consistent with the patrimonialism account's mechanism prediction than with the structural account's reversal prediction for non-patrimonial non-integration under Possibility A.

*Falsified if:* (iii-a) Primary-source evidence from Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship organized around Art. 116 specifically — not from the reform tradition's general theoretical framework — establishes that the evidentiary evaluation debates engaged with the rationality question by integrating *motivazione*'s accountability dimension as internally positive content of the concept's designatum, satisfying the structural prior for Possibility B directly. (iii-b) Comparative evidence from non-patrimonial peripheral receiving fields (Portugal, Argentina) shows that those fields' post-accountability-demand trajectories also produced external regulation and concept-transformation rather than integration into the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive content — indicating that the behavioral fork between patrimonial and non-patrimonial fields does not materialize, and the structural + P1 hybrid account produces the same trajectory prediction as the patrimonialism account.

### 3.8.G Against the Selection-Problem Relocation (Adversarial R8, §3.8.E)

Adversarial r8's second attack (§3.8.E) accepts version (i*)'s relocation of the relevant question to the institution-to-concept integration boundary and then applies the structural account at that level. At the integration boundary, version (i*) claims the Italian tradition treated *motivazione* as outside *libero convincimento*'s positive designatum domain. The adversarial argues that even at this boundary, the structural account explains non-integration as well as patrimonialism does: legitimation incentives predict that a peripheral receiving field would not integrate the separate accountability institution into the evidentiary evaluation concept, for the same structural reasons they predicted Form (i) over Form (ii) in the original form-selection debate — and P1 can generate and sustain the non-integration post-C3, making pre-C3 patrimonial formation unnecessary.

**The relocation is accepted; the mechanism distinction remains.**

Version (i*)'s relocation to the integration boundary is structurally correct: the question under version (i*) is not whether the Italian evidentiary evaluation debates maintained separation (that is what version (i*) claims), but why the receiving field did not integrate the separate accountability institution into the evidentiary concept at the C3 or post-C3 moment. Both the structural account and the patrimonialism account predict that non-integration occurred at this boundary. They disagree on mechanism: motivated non-integration (structural — legitimation incentives selected non-integration as a motivated outcome) vs. unconsidered non-integration (patrimonialism — the integration question was not distinctly available in the field's conceptual vocabulary, produced by prior patrimonial formation).

The mechanism distinction is consequential. Motivated non-integration is in principle reversible when motivational structure changes: if legitimation incentives produced the non-integration, an incentive change — specifically, a constitutional accountability mandate — should produce integration of the accountability dimension into the concept's positive content. Unconsidered non-integration is not simply reversible by incentive change: if the integration question was not distinctly available in the field's conceptual vocabulary, constituting the accountability dimension as internal to the evidentiary evaluation concept requires conceptual vocabulary development that the incentive change alone does not supply. A field that unconsidered-non-integrated may respond to accountability pressure through external regulation — treating the accountability obligation as a separate regime — rather than conceptual integration, because the field's vocabulary does not constitute the integration option as a distinctly available response.

**The same behavioral prediction applies at the integration boundary.**

Under motivated non-integration (structural account), the 1988 constitutionalization creates conditions under which the motivated selection should reverse: accountability is constitutionally required, the incentive structure is altered, and the predicted field response is integration of the accountability dimension into *livre convencimento*'s positive content — reconstituting the principle as requiring both freedom and accountability. Under unconsidered non-integration (patrimonialism account), the constitutional mandate imposes accountability as an external requirement; the field responds by treating art. 93, IX CF as a separate obligation, maintaining conceptual separation, until the incompatibility produces concept-abolition.

The Brazilian post-1988 trajectory — external regulation without conceptual integration; concept-abolition by CPC 2015 — is more consistent with unconsidered non-integration than with motivated non-integration. The structural account can invoke P1 as an additional mechanism to explain persistence after 1988: formation-integrated transmission sustained the non-integration after the incentive structure changed. This invocation is structurally available. But it imports a path-dependence mechanism that produces a hybrid account — legitimation incentives produced motivated non-integration; P1 sustained it post-incentive-shift — that converges with the patrimonialism account's behavioral prediction. The hybrid predicts the same trajectory as the patrimonialism account (persistence through P1 under motivated non-integration; persistence through unconsidered non-integration under patrimonialism), while adding a mechanism stage (motivated selection + path-dependence) where the patrimonialism account predicts the same outcome from a single prior-formation mechanism (conceptual vocabulary absence). The mechanism distinction retains analytical content — motivated non-integration and unconsidered non-integration are different causal structures — but behavioral discriminability depends on comparative evidence where the two predictions diverge.

*Falsified if:* (iii-c) Comparative evidence from non-patrimonial peripheral receiving fields shows that at the integration boundary, those fields also maintained non-integration after accountability demands arrived, producing the same external-regulation and concept-transformation pattern as Brazil — indicating that the structural + P1 hybrid adequately explains persistence in both field types and the mechanism distinction between motivated and unconsidered non-integration does not produce empirically divergent trajectories. (iii-d) Primary-source evidence from the Brazilian doctrinal record in the period immediately following the 1988 constitutionalization (1988–2000) shows active scholarly engagement with the question of how art. 93, IX CF's accountability obligation relates to *livre convencimento*'s positive content — scholars working to integrate the constitutional requirement into the concept's positive designatum rather than treating the two as separate regimes — indicating that the integration question was distinctly available in the field's conceptual vocabulary (against unconsidered non-integration) and that motivated non-integration sustained by P1 provides the better mechanism account.

### 3.8.H Against the Q1/Q2 Justificatory-Debates Claim (Adversarial R9, §3.8.F); Fork Mechanism Specified

Adversarial r9's §3.8.F advances two arguments against the supportive §3.8.F: (a) the Italian reform tradition's Q2 debates were justificatory debates organized in service of Q1, making accountability internal to Q2's scholarly content; and (b) the fork under Possibility A requires specifying a mechanism by which non-patrimonial fields would recognize integration as distinctly available, which the supportive §3.8.F does not supply.

**Against (a): external defensive posture versus internal scholarly organization.**

The adversarial characterizes Q2 debates as conducted "in defense of the reform's positive achievement against the *prova legal* critique, which required answering Q1." This description applies to the reform tradition's external-facing posture: when critics challenged whether rational free evaluation was genuinely rational, the tradition mobilized the accountability dimension as part of its answer. The adversarial infers from this that Q1's accountability answer was constitutively internal to how Q2 debates were organized.

The inference conflates two structurally distinct activities. A scholarly community can treat Q1 as a settled internal commitment — rational free evaluation requires both freedom and accountability; that settlement is what the tradition is built on — and organize its internal Q2 debates at the level of specifying implications within that settlement. Q2 debates about what evidentiary inferences the judge may draw freely, the scope of judicial discretion over evidential weight, and the appropriate appellate review standard for evidentiary determinations take Q1 as a given and work out its Q2 implications. They do not adjudicate Q1 as an open internal question; Q1 is the presupposition within which Q2 is tractable.

External defense of a settled commitment is not the same activity as internal debate organized around an unsettled question. When the reform tradition's scholars answered critics who challenged Q1's rational claim, they were defending an internal settlement from external challenge — not reopening Q1 as an unsettled controversy within their own scholarship. The Q2 debates, organized internally around the Art. 116 domain, proceeded on the assumption that Q1 was settled: free evaluation's rational character was established; the Art. 116 debates concerned how that established rational principle applied to specific evidentiary questions.

### 3.8.J Against the Settled-Q1 Presupposition Argument and the Symmetric-Structure Attack (Adversarial R10, §3.8.H); Against the Second-Wall Classification (Adversarial R10, §3.8.I)

#### Part 1: On the Q1 presupposition in Q2 debates

Adversarial r10's §3.8.H presses the Q1/Q2 distinction with greater precision than r9: "taking Q1 as settled takes Q1's answer as settled — including the accountability presupposition that gives the freedom principle its rational character within Q1's framework." This framing is accepted. Two readings of Q1's operational settlement within Q2's organizing presuppositions are available:

**Reading (N) — negative claim:** Q1 settled that free evaluation is more rational than *prova legale*'s formal proof hierarchies. Under Reading (N), the Italian scholarship settled Q1 at the Art. 116 level as the negative claim (free evaluation is rational for evidentiary purposes), with Art. 132 handling the accountability domain separately. Adversarial r10's §3.8.H advances the symmetric-structure attack: both patrimonial and non-patrimonial fields received the same two-institution vocabulary; the additional resource (accountability named elsewhere in the framework) "does not supply the institution-to-concept integration move that the fork's behavioral prediction requires"; the legitimation-incentive barrier to performing that integration within the evidentiary evaluation concept "operates with equal force on both fields regardless of whether accountability is named elsewhere."

The symmetric-structure attack correctly identifies that the legitimation-incentive barrier to **completing** integration — resolving the integration question in favor of incorporating the accountability dimension into the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive designatum — is symmetric between field types. This is agreed: the legitimation incentives resisting integration operate in any peripheral receiving field with P1-consolidated non-integration, regardless of prior vocabulary formation.

But the fork mechanism's behavioral prediction is not about completing integration. It is about the integration question being constituted as a tractable doctrinal problem — articulable — which produces observable integration-attempt engagement, even when that engagement does not overcome the completion barrier.

Two distinct barriers are at issue:

**Articulability barrier** (can the integration question be posed as a tractable doctrinal problem?): vocabulary-dependent. In non-patrimonial fields, accountability as a distinct dimension of judicial rationality is named in adjacent areas of the proceduralist framework. This naming makes the specific integration question constitutable: "given an accountability obligation that applies to judgment-form, should it be integrated into the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive designatum?" The question draws on named concepts from the adjacent domain and applies them to the evidentiary evaluation principle. A field without those named concepts — patrimonial prior formation having not constituted accountability as a distinct positive dimension of judicial rationality anywhere in the framework — cannot constitute this question as tractable from within its own vocabulary.

**Completion barrier** (can the integration, once posed, succeed against legitimation incentives and P1-resistance?): legitimation-dependent, symmetric. This barrier applies to all peripheral receiving fields under equivalent pressure.

The adversarial's §3.8.H conflates these two barriers. The symmetric-structure attack correctly identifies the completion barrier as symmetric; the fork mechanism's prediction is at the articulability level, which the adversarial's own §3.8.H concession acknowledges is asymmetric ("makes the question of integration articulable"). Granting that the vocabulary resource makes integration articulable "in general terms" is sufficient for the fork mechanism's behavioral prediction: articulability produces integration-attempt engagement; the completion barrier then determines whether engagement succeeds. Non-patrimonial fields are predicted to show the engagement (the question posed and contested, even against P1-resistance); patrimonial fields are predicted to lack it (the question not constitutable as tractable in the field's vocabulary).

The adversarial may contest that "articulable in general terms" differs from "articulable as a specific doctrinal demand on the evidentiary evaluation concept." The fork's claim is that when accountability is named as a distinct dimension of judicial rationality in adjacent proceduralist domains (judgment-form obligations, due process requirements for judicial decisions), the specific integration question — "should the accountability obligation internal to the proceduralist framework be integrated into the evidentiary evaluation concept?" — is constitutable and generates doctrinal debate. In patrimonial fields, accountability as a distinct positive dimension of judicial rationality was not named anywhere in the proceduralist vocabulary for judicial rationality; no adjacent domain provides the named concept from which the specific integration question could draw.

*Falsified if:* (iii-g) Comparative evidence from non-patrimonial peripheral receiving fields (Portugal, Argentina) shows that those fields, under equivalent P1 consolidation and equivalent external accountability demands, produced no doctrinal engagement with the integration question — no scholarly articulation of accountability as a demand on the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive content — despite their broader proceduralist vocabularies including accountability as a named concept in adjacent judicial rationality domains. ### 3.8.L Against Reading N/P Relabeling and the Engagement Mechanism Gap (Adversarial R12, §3.8.K)

#### L-1: Against the Structural Prior for Reading P (K-1)

Adversarial r12's §3.8.K-1 argues that Reading N/P is a relabeling of the Possibility A/B question at the Q1-content level, and presses the structural prior for Reading P: the reform tradition's constitutive scholarly question — "what distinguishes rational from arbitrary free evaluation?" — required a positive answer, and Reading N's comparative claim ("better than *prova legale*") does not supply one.

The response accepts the constitutive question and contests the premise that Reading N fails to supply a positive distinguishing criterion.

Reading N as framed — free evaluation is more rational than *prova legale* — is underspecified. A complete Reading N holds: *libero convincimento* is rational because it orients the judge toward epistemic sensitivity to actual evidentiary weight, unmanipulable by formal rules that substitute non-evidentiary constraints for evidence. The reform tradition's indictment of *prova legale* was that formal proof hierarchies rendered judicial evaluation epistemically insensitive — they required the judge to find "proven" what the formal rule classified as proven, regardless of the actual evidentiary record. Free evaluation's rationality consists in restoring this epistemic sensitivity: the judge tracks actual evidence rather than formal artifacts. Arbitrary evaluation is not mere absence of formal hierarchy; it is evaluation driven by non-evidentiary factors (bias, preference, factors unconnected to evidence). The positive criterion Reading N supplies: freedom from formal evidentiary hierarchy is rational in virtue of its epistemic orientation toward actual evidence, which distinguishes rational evaluation from arbitrary evaluation on the reform tradition's own grounds.

Reading P's additional claim — that accountability through *motivazione* is constitutively required — supplements the epistemic-sensitivity criterion with a verifiability dimension: that the epistemic orientation toward actual evidence be intersubjectively confirmable through the judgment's reasoning exposition. Whether the reform tradition required this as constitutive of the evidentiary evaluation principle specifically (Reading P within the *libero convincimento* scholarship organized around Art. 116), or recognized it as a separate institutional obligation governing judgment form at the Art. 132 level while the epistemic-sensitivity criterion was complete within Art. 116's domain (Reading N), is the Possibility A/B question the two-institution code structure evidences.

The adversarial's structural prior for Reading P requires that the reform tradition found the epistemic-sensitivity criterion alone insufficient as a distinguishing criterion — that distinguishing rational from arbitrary evaluation within the *libero convincimento* scholarship required the accountability component as constitutively internal. This is contestable. A judge who tracks actual evidentiary weight, freed from formal hierarchy, is distinguished from the arbitrary judge (who tracks non-evidentiary factors) through the epistemic criterion alone, without requiring that the epistemic tracking be exposed through *motivazione* to count as rational, rather than serving as a separate institutional verification mechanism in Art. 132's domain.

The adversarial's structural-prior argument proceeds from the reform's theoretical ideal (Q1's rationality predicate constitutively requires both freedom and accountability) to the scholarship's operational organization of Q2 — an inference the two-institution code structure (Art. 116 for evidentiary evaluation; Art. 132 for judgment-form accountability, in separate normative chapters) runs against. Reading N does not fail to supply a positive distinguishing criterion; it locates that criterion in epistemic sensitivity. The code structure reflects the tradition's institutional judgment that this criterion was treated as complete within the evidentiary evaluation domain, with the accountability institution handling its distinct domain separately.

#### L-2: Against the Engagement Mechanism Gap (K-2)

Adversarial r12's §3.8.K-2 identifies three problems with the articulability → engagement inference. These are addressed in turn.

**Against K-2.i (temporal origin).** The temporal origin problem applies specifically to claims about C3 behavior. The fork mechanism's comparative test is framed at the post-P1 accountability-demand moment: when non-patrimonial fields under P1 consolidation face external accountability demands, they are predicted to show integration-attempt engagement because the vocabulary for constituting the integration question is present in their broader proceduralist vocabulary. The adversarial correctly identifies that this vocabulary must predate the accountability demand — it must not merely be a product of the constitutional reform itself — for the causal claim to hold.

The relevant vocabulary resources are not the constitutional accountability provisions. They are the field's rational-legal institutional traditions: administrative law accountability doctrines, judicial methodology frameworks, due process jurisprudence — traditions that constitute judicial rationality as involving systematic accountability to articulable reasons in rational-legal fields. These develop in non-patrimonial fields through their rational-legal institutional formation, independently of specific proceduralist motivation requirements. The constitutional accountability reform activates the integration question within the proceduralist domain; the vocabulary resources for constituting that question come from adjacent legal domains that have constituted judicial accountability as a distinct positive dimension of judicial rationality independently.

The temporal origin problem thus reduces to an empirical question: do non-patrimonial peripheral receiving fields (Portugal, Argentina) have pre-constitutional-reform jurisprudence from adjacent legal domains — administrative law, judicial methodology, due process — that constitutes judicial rationality as involving systematic justificatory accountability as a distinct positive dimension? This is answerable from pre-constitutional records, and the adversarial has not established that non-patrimonial rational-legal fields lacked the accountability dimension in adjacent doctrinal domains prior to the specific proceduralist constitutional reform.

**Against K-2.ii (bridging mechanism).** The legitimation-incentive barrier K-2.ii invokes operates specifically on P1-formed mainstream doctrinalists whose professional formation is constituted by the doctrinalized principle. P1's normative isomorphism creates practitioners who have acquired the doctrine as constitutive of field competence; these practitioners resist integration engagement because engaging the integration question threatens the foundational framing of what their formation produced as the legitimate principle.

The claim that legitimation dynamics predict against engagement in both field types conflates the motivational configuration of P1-formed mainstream practitioners with that of all scholarly actors in the field. A field contains actors with multiple motivational configurations: P1-formed mainstream doctrinalists (resistant to integration engagement), reform-oriented scholars (motivated by normative assessment of whether existing doctrine adequately captures relevant principles), constitutional law scholars (approaching proceduralist principles from constitutional framework requirements), and comparative law scholars (bringing external analytical frameworks). These actors are not equally subject to P1's formation-integrated legitimation pressure.

The bridging mechanism operates through actor heterogeneity. When the integration question is articulable (vocabulary resources present in adjacent domains), actors with reform-oriented or constitutional motivational configurations will constitute it as a tractable doctrinal problem — not because legitimation incentives push them toward it, but because their analytical orientation toward normative assessment makes the integration question available as an object of scholarly inquiry. The completion barrier (P1-mainstream resistance to integrating the accountability dimension into the evidentiary evaluation principle's positive designatum) applies to practitioners who would need to execute the integration; it does not apply equally to reform scholars constituting the integration question as a scholarly problem.

In a patrimonial field where vocabulary resources are absent (the accountability dimension not named as a distinct positive dimension of judicial rationality in adjacent domains), no scholarly actor — regardless of motivational configuration — can constitute the integration question as tractable. The vocabulary absence forecloses the question's constitution independent of motivational orientation. The asymmetry: in non-patrimonial fields, vocabulary presence enables actor heterogeneity to generate integration-attempt engagement; in patrimonial fields, vocabulary absence forecloses engagement regardless of actor heterogeneity. The completion barrier is symmetric between field types; the vocabulary-enabled engagement barrier is not.

**Against K-2.iii (articulability versus doctrinal engagement).** K-2.iii correctly identifies that conceptual articulability and doctrinal engagement are distinct, and that the bridging mechanism connecting them was not specified in the prior exchange.

The bridging mechanism operates through three jointly sufficient conditions: (a) vocabulary presence — the vocabulary resources for constituting the integration question as a tractable doctrinal problem are available in the field's broader proceduralist vocabulary; (b) external demand — accountability pressure from outside the doctrinalized tradition (constitutional mandate, legislative reform, systematic critique) provides demand-side incentive for engaging the integration question; (c) motivational heterogeneity — at least some scholarly actors in the field operate with motivational configurations (reform-oriented, constitutional-interpretive, critical) that orient them toward normative assessment. When all three are jointly present, actors satisfying condition (c), responding to condition (b), use condition (a) vocabulary resources to constitute and articulate the integration question in the doctrinal record.

The mechanism does not reduce articulability to entailment of engagement. It requires external demand and motivational heterogeneity in addition to vocabulary resources. Conditions (b) and (c) are present in both non-patrimonial and patrimonial fields facing equivalent external accountability demands; condition (a) is asymmetric. In a patrimonial field where condition (a) fails, conditions (b) and (c) cannot jointly generate doctrinal engagement, because the vocabulary absence forecloses constituting the integration question regardless of external pressure and motivational orientation.

The empirical prediction: post-1988 or equivalent-reform-moment Portuguese and Argentine doctrinal records should contain scholarly articulation of the integration question — whether the accountability obligation should be integrated into what the evidentiary evaluation principle requires — in reform-oriented, critical, or constitutional-interpretation literature. Presence of such articulation confirms the articulability → engagement inference under the bridging mechanism's conditions; absence satisfies condition (m) and defeats the inference.

Section 3.8.L L-2 supplies the bridging mechanism specification through the three jointly sufficient conditions. Whether that specification is sufficient to restore the comparative path as discriminating depends on whether the L-2 response to K-2 survives adversarial scrutiny. If it does, the comparative test — (1) whether vocabulary resources for the integration question were present in non-patrimonial peripheral fields' broader proceduralist vocabulary prior to their constitutional accountability reforms, and (2) whether reform-oriented or constitutional-interpretation scholarship in those fields articulated the integration question following equivalent accountability demands — constitutes the appropriate non-archival empirical next step.

### 3.8.N Against the Verificationist Critique and the Symmetry Attack on Condition (A) (Adversarial R14, §3.8.M)

Adversarial r14's §3.8.M advances two attacks: M-1 argues that the epistemic-sensitivity criterion supplied by §3.8.L L-1 is verificationist — it describes an internal cognitive state that cannot function as a publicly actionable correction standard without *motivazione*'s disclosure mechanism, and therefore presupposes Reading P's accountability structure rather than standing as an independent Reading N criterion. M-2 argues that condition (a) of the bridging mechanism (vocabulary presence in adjacent domains) is symmetric between patrimonial and non-patrimonial field types: Brazilian administrative law and constitutional due process doctrine constituted judicial accountability as a legal obligation in adjacent domains, satisfying the same conditions L-2 identified for non-patrimonial fields. Both attacks are addressed in turn.

#### N-1: Against the Verificationist Critique (M-1)

M-1's argument structure: the epistemic-sensitivity criterion describes the judge's internal cognitive orientation (tracking actual evidentiary weight rather than non-evidentiary factors); this orientation is unobservable from the publicly available judicial act alone (two decisions identical in outcome and evidence could differ in whether the judge tracked actual weight or was driven by sympathy); without *motivazione*'s disclosure function, the criterion cannot generate an externally applicable correction standard; therefore the criterion is only publicly actionable when coupled with *motivazione*, making it presuppose Reading P's accountability structure rather than completing Reading N independently.

Three responses. First, M-1 conflates the content of a rationality criterion with the verification mechanism that makes it externally actionable. The good-faith criterion in contract law specifies an internal cognitive standard (genuinely honest belief, absence of bad-faith intent) without being constitutively dependent on any particular disclosure mechanism for its content. External verification of good faith requires evidence — conduct consistent with good faith, the absence of inconsistent indicators — but this verification infrastructure does not become constitutively internal to what good faith means as a rationality standard. M-1's argument, if correct, would make virtually every legal rationality criterion that governs an internal standard "verificationist" in this sense: the criterion would collapse into whatever external verification mechanism makes it actionable. The epistemic-sensitivity criterion specifies what rational evidentiary evaluation consists in; the mechanism by which an appellate court or reviewer tests whether it was satisfied is a separate institutional design question.

Second, M-1's verificationist charge proves too much. The adversarial acknowledges that *motivazione* provides the verification mechanism; but the Italian CPC 1940's two-institution structure — Art. 116 specifying the evidentiary evaluation standard and Art. 132 specifying the judgment-form obligation — instantiates exactly the criterion/mechanism distinction the adversarial's argument denies. The codifiers who produced this two-institution structure operated within the reform tradition M-1 treats as unified; they nonetheless designed separate Articles with separate normative domains. The most natural reading of this institutional output is that the reform tradition recognized the criterion (Art. 116's positive designatum) as analytically distinct from the verification mechanism (Art. 132's disclosure obligation), even while treating them as functionally complementary.

Third, the adversarial's inference from functional interdependence to constitutive dependence does not hold. M-1 establishes that *motivazione*'s disclosure function is operationally necessary for the epistemic-sensitivity criterion to function as a public correction standard — a claim §3.8.N does not contest. Functional necessity for external operation is not the same as constitutive internal dependence. The epistemic-sensitivity criterion can be substantively complete as a specification of what rational evidentiary evaluation requires — what the judge is cognitively doing when evaluating rationally — while requiring external verification infrastructure for its institutional application. M-1's code-structure redeployment characterizes the separate codification as reflecting functional interdependence of operationally dependent components; §3.8.L L-1 and this response characterize it as reflecting analytical distinctness of a rationality criterion and its external verification mechanism. M-1 asserts the former reading; it does not establish it over the latter.

Failure condition (n) specifies the evidence needed to establish M-1's reading over this response's reading: primary-source evidence from the *libero convincimento* scholarship showing that the reform tradition's own practitioners found the epistemic-sensitivity criterion substantively insufficient without the accountability component — not merely operationally dependent on *motivazione* for external verification, but substantively incomplete in its specification of what rational evaluation requires.

#### N-2: Against the Symmetry Attack on Condition (A) (M-2)

M-2's argument structure: condition (a) of the bridging mechanism requires vocabulary presence — accountability named as a distinct positive dimension of judicial rationality in adjacent proceduralist domains. Brazilian administrative law and constitutional due process doctrine provide this vocabulary in patrimonial fields too: judicial acts must be grounded in law and reason, judicial accountability is a legal obligation in administrative fairness contexts, due process doctrine constitutes a public accountability obligation for judicial decision-making. With conditions (a), (b), and (c) jointly symmetric between field types, the mechanism predicts integration-attempt engagement everywhere, not differentially. For the asymmetry to hold, condition (a) must identify the specific proceduralist vocabulary for constituting the PROCEDURALIST integration question — whether the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive designatum should incorporate the accountability dimension — and M-2 presses that this vocabulary develops through engagement with the proceduralist reform tradition itself (C3 and P1 consolidation), not from the pre-constitutional adjacent domains L-2.i identifies.

The response introduces a distinction between two types of accountability concepts operative in adjacent legal domains.

**Type-1 accountability concepts** specify formal compliance obligations: they characterize accountability as a constraint on the form or content of judicial outputs. Type-1 vocabulary takes the form "judicial acts must contain X" or "judges must comply with requirement Y." In this framing, accountability is a procedural obligation imposed on judicial performance from outside, specifying what judicial conduct must include to be legally acceptable. Brazilian administrative law accountability doctrines and constitutional due process requirements supply Type-1 concepts: the judge must give reasons, must apply the law, must comply with procedural requirements. These concepts constitute accountability as an external compliance standard.

**Type-2 accountability concepts** specify accountability as a positive dimension of what judicial rationality consists in. Type-2 vocabulary constitutes accountability as internal to the concept of rational judging itself: judging well, on this view, is inherently an intersubjectively accountable practice. The judge who evaluates evidence rationally is, by that very fact, engaging in an evaluative practice whose rationality is constituted in part by its capacity to give reasons that others could assess. In this framing, accountability is not an external constraint added to rational evaluation but a dimension of what rational evaluation is.

Condition (a) of the bridging mechanism requires Type-2 vocabulary presence, not Type-1. The behavioral prediction the mechanism makes — that non-patrimonial fields will produce doctrinal integration-attempt engagement when external accountability demands arrive — depends on scholars having the conceptual resources to constitute the PROCEDURALIST integration question as tractable: "should the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive designatum incorporate the accountability dimension?" Constituting this question requires Type-2 vocabulary, because the question presupposes that accountability could be internal to rational evaluation rather than merely an external formal compliance requirement. Scholars who have only Type-1 vocabulary available will not constitute the integration question in the form the bridging mechanism requires; they can pursue compliance-framework responses (how to enforce the external obligation) without articulating whether the evidentiary evaluation concept itself should integrate the accountability dimension.

The Type-1/Type-2 distinction generates the asymmetry M-2 claims condition (a) fails to produce. Non-patrimonial rational-legal judicial traditions — fields formed through the Weberian rational-legal model of judicial authority — constitute judicial rationality as inherently intersubjectively accountable: the judge's authority derives from the quality of legal reasoning, not from personal institutional standing, making accountability internal to what rational judicial performance consists in. These fields' adjacent legal domains — administrative law accountability doctrine, judicial methodology frameworks, due process jurisprudence — reflect Type-2 vocabulary as a feature of the general rational-legal institutional formation. Patrimonial fields — fields formed through patrimonial authority structures where judicial authority derives from institutional position — constitute judicial rationality as personal authoritative judgment. Adjacent legal domains in these fields supply Type-1 vocabulary: accountability as formal compliance constraint on personal authority. The specific vocabulary needed for constituting the integration question is present as Type-2 in non-patrimonial fields through their general institutional formation and absent (present only as Type-1) in patrimonial fields.

The Brazilian post-1988 doctrinal record is diagnostic. M-2 correctly observes that external accountability demand is present, actor heterogeneity is present, and adjacent legal domains supply accountability concepts. But the engagement pattern — Streck's constitutional proceduralism critique, the systematic doctrinal engagement with *livre convencimento*'s constitutional illegitimacy — takes the form of a compliance-failure diagnosis and a constitutional-mandate correction demand, not an integration question about whether the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive designatum should incorporate the accountability dimension. The engagement is Type-1 in structure: accountability as an external constitutional obligation that the informal practice violates, requiring correction through *motivação das decisões* as a separate requirement. The post-1988 Brazilian record is consistent with Type-1 vocabulary sufficiency and Type-2 vocabulary absence in the adjacent domains M-2 identifies.

M-2's temporal-origin argument presses that the specific proceduralist vocabulary for the integration question develops through engagement with the reform tradition itself (post-C3), not from the pre-constitutional adjacent domains. This argument operates correctly at the level of the vocabulary for answering the integration question — the vocabulary for resolving whether integration should occur, drawing on Liebman's proceduralist framework, is produced through the reform tradition's C3 and P1 phases. The Type-1/Type-2 distinction operates at a different level: the vocabulary for CONSTITUTING the integration question as tractable — for identifying it as a question about whether rational evaluation is inherently accountability-involving — is a feature of general rational-legal institutional formation, not of specific proceduralist reform. M-2's temporal-origin argument is correct about the vocabulary for answering; it does not apply to the vocabulary for constituting the question in the form the bridging mechanism requires.

New failure condition (o): the response fails if comparative evidence from non-patrimonial peripheral receiving fields' adjacent legal domains — administrative law, judicial methodology, due process — establishes that those fields' adjacent-domain vocabulary, prior to their specific constitutional accountability reforms, was also primarily Type-1 (formal compliance obligation framing) rather than Type-2 (accountability as positive dimension of what judicial rationality consists in). Such evidence would indicate that the Type-1/Type-2 distinction does not generate the asymmetry between field types at the adjacent-domain level the response claims.

### 3.8.O Against the Behavioral-Proxy Gap and the Proceduralist-Domain Vocabulary Gap (Adversarial R16, §3.8.O)

Adversarial r16's §3.8.O advances two attacks against §3.8.N's N-1 and N-2 responses. O-1 argues that the criterion/mechanism distinction, while conceptually valid, fails at the legal-system actionability level because epistemic sensitivity lacks the independent behavioral proxies that make good faith's criterion/mechanism separation operationally viable as a correction standard: commercial custom, course of dealing, and reliance patterns allow appellate review to assess good faith without relying on the party's self-report, while epistemic sensitivity has no analogous proxy in the three target domains (mixed evidence, weight-assignment, narrative reconstruction); separate violability of Art. 116 and Art. 132 is therefore insufficient, because independent violability is consistent with Art. 116's criterion being independently evaluable only through Art. 132's disclosure as its sole information source. O-2 accepts the Type-1/Type-2 accountability concept distinction and the general-level asymmetry N-2 established, but argues that general Type-2 vocabulary present in non-patrimonial fields through general institutional formation does not constitute the specific proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary condition (a) requires: the scholarly framework for treating the evidentiary evaluation concept as a theoretical object with internally specifiable positive content, and for positioning the accountability dimension as a question about that concept's internal structure rather than a separate institutional obligation, develops through field-specific conceptual formation that O-2 argues is supplied by C3 proceduralist engagement, not by adjacent-domain Type-2 vocabulary from general institutional formation. Both attacks are addressed in turn.

#### O-1: Against the Behavioral-Proxy Gap

O-1's argument structure: the disanalogy between good faith and epistemic sensitivity operates at the level of operational mechanism, not conceptual content. Good faith's independent behavioral proxies — observable transaction-level behavioral patterns (commercial custom, course of dealing, reliance patterns) — allow appellate review to assess criterion satisfaction without relying on the party's own disclosure. Epistemic sensitivity, O-1 argues, has no analogous proxy in the three target domains: whether a judge tracked actual evidentiary weight rather than non-evidentiary factors in a mixed-evidence case, a weight-assignment decision, or a narrative reconstruction from conflicting testimonies cannot be assessed from observable behavioral indicators independent of the judge's disclosed reasoning. Therefore, the criterion/mechanism distinction holds conceptually but fails at the actionability level: the only information source for criterion-satisfaction assessment in these domains is the Art. 132 *motivazione* disclosure.

Three responses. First, the disanalogy is real but misdescribed. Good faith uses transaction-level behavioral proxies that generate inference to an internal state without requiring the party's self-report. Epistemic sensitivity uses logical-structure testing: whether a judicial conclusion is consistent with the evidence presented, whether the reasoning pattern reflects engagement with actual evidentiary weight rather than arbitrary factors, whether the inferential moves track the logical structure of the evidence record. Italian appellate doctrine under the CPC 1940 recognized exactly this — *errore di giudizio* (error of judgment on the merits) and *omessa valutazione di prove* (omitted evaluation of evidence) are doctrinal categories that test criterion satisfaction against the trial record without treating the *motivazione* document as the primary information source for whether epistemic sensitivity was exercised. The trial record — the evidence presented, the procedural record of what was introduced — constitutes the independent reference point. A judge who produces a conclusion inconsistent with the record, or who demonstrably ignores categories of evidence, can be corrected on Art. 116 grounds even on a formal compliance reading of the *motivazione* obligation; conversely, a judge can produce formally adequate *motivazione* while the logical structure of the conclusions betrays criterion failure. These are the independent failure modes the Italian codifiers' two-institution structure was designed to capture, and they confirm that the trial record — not the *motivazione* document — functions as the primary information source for Art. 116 criterion-satisfaction testing.

Second, O-1 misspecifies the criterion for independent operationalizability. The criterion is not that the independent mechanism be of the same type as good faith's behavioral proxies — transaction-level observable behavior generating inference to an internal cognitive state without self-report. The criterion is that some mechanism for assessing criterion satisfaction independent of the *motivazione* disclosure document exists as the primary information source. Logical-structure testing against the evidentiary record satisfies this: it uses the trial record as the primary information source, with the *motivazione* document as a secondary consistency check rather than the foundational source of information about whether epistemic sensitivity was exercised. O-1 establishes that good faith satisfies the operationalizability criterion through a particular mechanism type; it does not establish that this mechanism type is the only acceptable form of independent operationalizability.

Third, the separate violability argument restates O-1's conclusion rather than establishing it. O-1 argues that separate violability is consistent with Art. 116 being "independently evaluable only through Art. 132's disclosure — because the only evidence available to appellate review about whether Art. 116's criterion was satisfied is the Art. 132 exposition." This is the claim at issue, not a premise; it is exactly what the logical-structure testing argument contests. If the trial record constitutes an independent reference point for testing criterion satisfaction, the *motivazione* document is not the only available evidence source. O-1 must establish that the trial record does not constitute such an independent reference point — specifically, that in mixed-evidence cases, weight-assignment decisions, and narrative reconstruction from conflicting testimonies, logical-structure testing against the trial record cannot generate criterion-satisfaction assessments independent of the judge's disclosed reasoning. The adversarial's O-1 identifies these three target domains precisely because they involve the judge's integrative approach to evidence; but integrative inconsistency with the evidentiary record — conclusions that cannot be generated by any reasonable evidentiary inference from what was presented — is assessable from the record even in these domains.

Revised failure condition (n): the response fails if the adversarial establishes, specifically for the three target domains (mixed evidence, weight-assignment, narrative reconstruction), that logical-structure testing against the trial record cannot identify criterion-satisfaction failures independently of the *motivazione* document — either because there is no consistent logical relationship between the evidentiary record and rationally possible conclusions in these domains (so no structure to test against), or because Italian appellate doctrine under Art. 116 specifically required the *motivazione* document as the primary information source even for correction on epistemic-sensitivity grounds. Primary-source evidence about how Italian courts and doctrine operated *errore di giudizio* review in these specific evidentiary categories would be determinate for this condition.

#### O-2: Against the Proceduralist-Domain Vocabulary Gap

O-2's argument structure: N-2 established that non-patrimonial rational-legal fields possess Type-2 vocabulary through general institutional formation — accountability as a positive dimension of what judicial rationality consists in, not merely as an external compliance constraint. O-2 accepts this at the general level. But O-2 contests that this general Type-2 vocabulary supplies the *proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary* condition (a) requires: specifically, the theoretical framework for treating the evidentiary evaluation concept as a theoretical object with internally specifiable positive content, and for positioning the accountability dimension as a question about that concept's internal structure. This framework, O-2 argues, develops through field-specific engagement with the proceduralist reform tradition (C3 and P1 consolidation), not from general adjacent-domain Type-2 formation.

The response identifies a specific vocabulary resource that O-2's argument misses: the domestic proceduralist scholarly traditions in non-patrimonial civilian jurisdictions that had constituted free-evaluation evidentiary concepts as theoretical objects with internally specifiable rational structure prior to the Italian Liebman tradition's period of Iberian and Latin American transmission. These traditions are not merely general Type-2 institutional formation; they are field-specific proceduralist engagements with the same class of evidentiary evaluation concept that condition (a) targets.

French *intime conviction* doctrine is the paradigm case. Article 342 of the 1808 Code d'instruction criminelle established free jury evaluation in criminal procedure; the subsequent French proceduralist scholarly tradition — extending through the nineteenth century to Gorphe's *La critique du témoignage* (1924) and, after the Liebman period, his *L'appréciation des preuves en justice* (1947; the latter was cited as 1924 in earlier rounds, corrected at §3.8.S) — constituted the *intime conviction* concept as a theoretical object with internally specifiable rational structure: what makes the judge's or jury's conviction *intime* (authentic rather than arbitrary), what evidentiary features constrain it, and what distinguishes conviction that meets the rational standard from mere personal sentiment. This is precisely the proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary O-2 identifies as the required resource: the scholarship engaged the free-evaluation concept's positive designatum as a theoretical object within the proceduralist tradition, not merely the general rationality question from outside it. The accountability dimension — what makes conviction articulable and assessable rather than merely personal — was internal to this debate from early in the French tradition, not introduced only through later constitutional reform.

German *freie Beweiswürdigung* (§ 286 ZPO, 1877) provides a parallel domestic proceduralist tradition. The post-1877 German proceduralist scholarship engaged *freie Beweiswürdigung* as a theoretical concept with specifiable content — distinguishing it from arbitrary evaluation, specifying what evidentiary engagement it required — within the proceduralist tradition. The accountability dimension (the grounds on which the judge's evaluation could be assessed by an appellate court on *Überzeugungsbildung* grounds) was internal to the German proceduralist scholarship's engagement with the concept's positive content, not imported from adjacent administrative law or constitutional doctrine.

Portugal and Argentina, as civilian jurisdictions operating in the French and German comparative law spheres, had access to these traditions through their doctrinal formation. Portuguese proceduralist scholarship engaged French and German doctrine directly; Argentine proceduralist formation included the same comparative-law channels. The pre-C3 proceduralist vocabulary resource O-2 demands was therefore available to these fields' scholars not merely as general Type-2 orientation from institutional formation but as domain-specific conceptual formation about free evidentiary evaluation concepts as theoretical objects.

Brazil had no equivalent domestic proceduralist scholarly tradition constituting free judicial evaluation as a theoretical object with internally specifiable rational structure. The 1939 Brazilian CPC incorporated free judicial evaluation (Art. 118 CPC 1939) as a regime change — replacing the *prova legal* system — but the practice was not theorized within a pre-existing domestic proceduralist scholarly tradition engaging the concept's positive designatum. The Liebman USP engagement from 1940 was Brazil's primary encounter with proceduralist theoretical engagement with these concepts; Brazil had no Gorphe equivalent constituting the evidentiary evaluation concept as a theoretical object prior to that engagement.

Patrimonialism grounds this asymmetry. Non-patrimonial civilian jurisdictions' proceduralist scholarly traditions engaged free-evaluation concepts theoretically — constituting the positive designatum as a scholarly object — because judicial rationality in those fields was constituted as inherently subject to rational assessment (Type-2), generating scholarly pressure to specify what the concept's rational content positively was. Patrimonial fields whose judicial rationality was constituted as personal authoritative judgment did not generate the same scholarly pressure toward specifying the concept's internal rational structure; formal legal systematization (doctrinal synthesis, commentary) rather than conceptual constitution characterized the field's theoretical output.

O-2's temporal-origin argument — that the proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary develops through C3 engagement — correctly describes where the vocabulary for engaging with the Liebman framework specifically develops. It does not describe the prior domestic proceduralist traditions in non-patrimonial fields that constituted the free-evaluation concept as a theoretical object before the Liebman tradition became the dominant vehicle for that engagement. Condition (a) identifies the vocabulary resource for constituting the integration question, not for engaging it through the Liebman framework specifically; the prior domestic traditions supply the former even if the latter develops through C3 engagement.

Revised failure condition (o): the response fails if the adversarial establishes that the French *intime conviction* and German *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarly traditions, as pre-C3 proceduralist engagements with free-evaluation concepts, (i) did not constitute the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive designatum as a theoretical object with internally specifiable rational structure within the proceduralist tradition — specifically, that their engagement did not include the accountability dimension as internal to the concept's specifiable content — or (ii) were not accessible to Portuguese and Argentine proceduralist scholars as formative vocabulary resources prior to C3, making those fields' proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary also dependent on C3 engagement. Under condition (i), the pre-C3 domestic traditions would not supply the required vocabulary resource. Under condition (ii), the asymmetry between Brazil and the non-patrimonial comparators would not hold at the access level, since both would face the same C3-dependence for domain-specific constituting vocabulary.

### 3.8.Q Against the *Motivazione*-Primary Characterization of the Trial-Record Mechanism and the French/German Structural Equivalence Claim (Adversarial R18, §3.8.P)

Adversarial r18's §3.8.P advances two attacks. (P-1) *Errore di giudizio* and *omessa valutazione di prove* operate with *motivazione* as their primary information source and the trial record as secondary comparator: both doctrines read *motivazione* to understand the expressed evaluative process, then check the trial record for accuracy; a judge who writes formally adequate *motivazione* with all evidence addressed and internally consistent expressed reasoning passes both doctrines without detection; appellate deference in the credibility domain confirms the *motivazione*-primary structure. New failure condition (n-iii). (P-2) French: Art. 342 CIC 1808 explicitly constitutes freedom from accountability requirements as *intime conviction*'s positive content — the structural opposite of condition (o)'s required vocabulary — and Gorphe 1924 is an epistemic-quality project, not a constitution of external articulability as positive requirement. German: § 286 ZPO 1877's two-institution structure (§ 286 + § 313) is structurally identical to the Italian CPC's structure, and the Possibility A/B dilemma applies without independent primary-source resolution. New failure condition (o-iii).

#### Q-1: Against the *Motivazione*-Primary Characterization (P-1)

R18's secondary-comparator/primary-source distinction applies accurately to *errore di giudizio* as applied to the logical structure of expressed reasoning: that doctrine reads the *motivazione* to understand what the expressed evaluative process consisted in, then checks the trial record to see if the expressed process accurately engaged with the evidence. In this application, *motivazione* is the primary information source for what the evaluation comprised; the trial record verifies the expressed evaluation's accuracy. R18's formally-adequate-*motivazione* thought experiment correctly identifies a category of failures this doctrine does not catch.

The characterization is incomplete for *omessa valutazione di prove*. The epistemically relevant direction of the comparison is reversed: the trial record establishes what evidence was presented — a fact about the record's content, not a fact that *motivazione* determines or reports. The comparison then checks *motivazione*'s coverage against that primary standard: whether evidence present in the record was or was not addressed. In this structure, the trial record is the normative primary source (establishing what should have been evaluated), and *motivazione* is the document whose coverage is assessed against that standard. A court applying *omessa valutazione* begins from what the record contains, not from what *motivazione* described; the correction follows from a comparison in which the trial record is the reference.

R18 characterizes both doctrines uniformly as *motivazione*-primary on the grounds that both read *motivazione* before consulting the trial record. But the relevant question is not which document is consulted first; it is which document is the *normative standard* for the comparison that generates the correction. For *errore di giudizio*, *motivazione*'s expressed reasoning is the object being checked against the trial record as an accuracy constraint. For *omessa valutazione*, the trial record's content is the standard against which *motivazione*'s coverage is measured. The two doctrines have opposite epistemic structures at the level of which document plays the normative primary role.

R18's formally-adequate-*motivazione* thought experiment correctly establishes a limitation of reach: a judge who covers all evidence formally while weighting by non-evidentiary factors passes *omessa valutazione* because the correction condition (evidence present in the record but not addressed) is not met. This is a real limitation. Condition (n-iii) correctly names what would show the limitation is overcome. R17's claim was the existence of an independent mechanism, not that the mechanism catches all categories of epistemic sensitivity failure. For *omessa valutazione*, the trial record's normative-primary role is established by the doctrine's structure in the cases the doctrine reaches. R18's P-1 establishes that the independent mechanism has a limited reach; it does not establish that *motivazione* is the primary information source for *omessa valutazione*'s correction condition in the cases the doctrine does reach.

*Falsified if (n-iii):* Italian appellate doctrine or case law establishes that even *omessa valutazione di prove* corrections proceed with *motivazione* as the primary information source for what evidence existed — that courts determine "omitted" evaluation by checking what *motivazione* mentioned rather than by verifying what the trial record independently contained. This would collapse *omessa valutazione*'s epistemic structure into *errore di giudizio*'s: both doctrines would be *motivazione*-primary, eliminating the trial record's normative-primary role in *omessa valutazione* that §3.8.Q Q-1 identifies.

#### Q-2: Against the French Tradition Characterization (P-2, France)

R18 correctly characterizes Art. 342 CIC 1808: the article establishes that jurors are not required to account for the means of their conviction, constituting accountability-freedom as the provision's operative rule. This is accurate as a characterization of that article specifically.

The structural problem with applying this characterization to condition (o) is that Art. 342 CIC 1808 applies to lay juries in criminal proceedings. The revolutionary reform constituted jury fact-finding as a matter of democratic conscience rather than professional legal expertise: the jury's verdict is the pronouncement of citizen conscience on the facts, insulated from professional challenge through reason-giving requirements precisely because the jury is not a professional legal body. The accountability-freedom clause is an institutional-design feature of the democratic jury institution in criminal proceedings, not a characterization of what rational professional judicial evaluation of evidence requires.

The *libre conviction* tradition relevant to condition (o) is not the criminal jury tradition but the professional judicial evaluation tradition in civil proceedings — the scholarly project that engaged what makes a professional judge's free evaluation of evidence rationally specifiable. Gorphe's *La critique du témoignage* (1924) and *L'appréciation des preuves en justice* (1947) are situated in this tradition (the 1947 date, and the criminal-law setting of Gorphe's work, are corrected at §3.8.S; the characterization of the tradition in this paragraph as one of "civil proceedings" is superseded there). Gorphe's project engages the conditions for rational judicial conviction: what makes the judge's evaluative process oriented toward actual evidentiary quality rather than personal sentiment, what distinguishes conviction that meets the rational standard from arbitrary preference, what logical and psychological constraints apply to professional evidence appraisal. This is the proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary condition (o) identifies: engagement with the free-evaluation concept's positive designatum as a theoretical object within the proceduralist tradition.

R18 characterizes Gorphe as "epistemic quality improvement of the internal process, not external articulability as positive requirement." This characterization presupposes that condition (o) requires the accountability dimension in its motivational-accountability form (external articulability of expressed reasoning). But condition (o) specifies vocabulary that constitutes the evidentiary evaluation concept as having an internally specifiable accountability dimension. An epistemic quality project that specifies what rational evidence evaluation positively consists in — orientation toward actual evidentiary weight, logical consistency with the evidentiary record, conditions for rational conviction rather than arbitrary sentiment — constitutes the evaluation concept as a theoretical object with internally specifiable rational structure. The accountability dimension in its epistemic form is the specification of what the rational standard is, not the external requirement that satisfaction of that standard be articulable to others. Gorphe's project operates at this level: it constitutes what rational free evaluation requires, making the concept a theoretical object with specifiable positive content. This is the vocabulary resource condition (o) identifies as absent from Brazil's pre-C3 proceduralist formation.

R18 conflates two forms of the accountability dimension: (a) motivational-accountability (the requirement that reasoning be expressible in articulable form for external review — Art. 132's and § 313's domain), and (b) epistemic-accountability (the specification of what makes judicial evaluation rational rather than arbitrary — the positive content of the epistemic-sensitivity criterion). Condition (o) identifies vocabulary that constitutes the evidentiary evaluation concept as having an internally specifiable accountability dimension of form (b). Art. 342 CIC 1808's accountability-freedom clause applies to form (a) for the criminal jury institution: jurors are not required to produce articulable expressed reasoning. It does not establish that the French tradition constituted the professional judicial evaluation concept as accountability-free at the form (b) level.

*Falsified if (o-iii, French prong):* The adversarial establishes that the French *libre conviction* tradition for professional judicial evaluation in civil proceedings also constitutes freedom from rational accountability in form (b) as the positive content of the concept — that the scholarly tradition engaged with what makes free judicial evaluation rational and concluded that rational evaluation is epistemically unconstrained. This would require showing that Gorphe's tradition or the broader professional-evaluation scholarship constituted rational conviction as orientation toward something other than actual evidentiary quality, such that the epistemic-accountability dimension is not internal to the concept's specifiable positive content.

#### Q-3: Against the German Structural Equivalence Claim (P-2, Germany)

R18 argues that § 286 ZPO 1877's two-institution structure (§ 286 *freie Beweiswürdigung* + § 313 *Urteilsgründe*) is structurally identical to the Italian CPC's structure, and that the Possibility A/B dilemma applies to the German tradition with equal force without independent primary-source resolution.

The structural-equivalence claim requires that the Possibility A/B dilemma rests on code structure alone, such that any tradition with separate codification of the freedom principle and the reasoning obligation faces the same underdetermination. But the dilemma as developed in §§3.8.D–F rests on the adversarial's inference from code structure to scholarly analytical organization. The adversarial's §3.8.F invoked the Italian codifiers' two-institution decision as "the most direct available institutional evidence" of scholarly analytical separation. The same inference to scholarly discourse organization from code structure applies to the German tradition only if § 286 and § 313 are equally the best available evidence of how the German scholarship organized its debates analytically.

The German tradition's institutional context differs from the Italian tradition's at a relevant level. The German *Urteilsbegründungspflicht* — the duty to provide reasoned judgment — developed in the German legal tradition as an element of what judicial authority means within the rational-legal model: judicial decisions are authoritative in virtue of the quality of the legal reasoning they express, making the *Begründungspflicht* constitutively connected to the authority claim rather than a separately governed safeguard running alongside free evaluation. This constitutes the German tradition's relationship between § 286 *freie Beweiswürdigung* and § 313 *Urteilsgründe* differently from the Italian tradition at the institutional context level: in the German tradition, the reasoning obligation is analytically connected to what legitimate judicial authority means, not merely codified in a separate provision.

If the German scholarship organized its debates around the freedom principle with this constitutive connection to the reasoning obligation in view — which the structural prior of the German rational-legal authority tradition supports — the German tradition exhibits Possibility B at the scholarly discourse level regardless of the code's two-institution structure. R18's parallel argument — "everything the adversarial argued about the Italian Possibility A/B dilemma applies to the German tradition with equal force" — proceeds by code-structure analogy without engaging whether the German scholarship's organization of its debates differs from the Italian tradition's at the level where the Possibility A/B question is actually resolved.

*Falsified if (o-iii, German prong):* Primary-source evidence from the German *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship establishes that the evidentiary evaluation debates organized around § 286 ZPO maintained the analytical separation between free evaluation and *Begründungspflicht* — that the scholarship treated the rationality of free evaluation as specifiable without engaging the reasoning obligation as internal to the concept's positive designatum. This would establish that the German tradition faces the same Possibility A/B underdetermination as the Italian tradition and that R18's structural-equivalence argument is correct.

### 3.8.S Against the Relevance-Framework Priority Claim, the Virtue/Accountability Dichotomy, and the Parity Argument (Adversarial R20, §3.8.R)

Adversarial r20's §3.8.R advances three presses against §3.8.Q. (R-1) *Omessa valutazione di prove* cannot identify the normatively required subset of the trial record without first reading *motivazione*'s expressed legal framework to determine relevance; *motivazione* is therefore logically prior to the trial-record comparison even under this doctrine; Q-1's primary-source claim holds only for a purely formal application. New condition (n-iv). (R-2) Gorphe's project specifies epistemic virtue (what makes evaluation internally accurate), not the accountability dimension condition (o) requires (what makes evaluation externally reviewable and correctable); Q-2's form-b formulation collapses that distinction. New condition (o-iv). (R-3) Q-3's German rational-legal-model prior is an inference from institutional context to Q2-scholarship organization — the inference type this defense refused for Italy — and the Italian *motivazione* requirement is equally connected to judicial authority; consistency bars the German prior. New condition (o-v).

This round differs from the preceding ones in method. Rounds r7 through r20 have argued about three codes and one scholarly tradition almost entirely by structural inference. This round checks the texts. Two results follow: one reverses the parity argument on the German side; one is a correction of this defense's own bibliography that narrows the French prong. Sources consulted are listed in the accompanying diary entry.

#### S-1: Against the Relevance-Framework Priority Claim (R-1)

This defense accepts R-1's premise. *Omessa valutazione* is not applied through purely formal comparison; a relevance filter selects, from what the record contains, what the court was required to examine. Condition (n-iv)'s "purely formal application" disjunct is not the doctrine's structure and is not claimed here. What R-1 adds to that premise is a claim about the filter's *source*: that relevance is "established by *motivazione*'s expressed legal framework." The Italian code locates the filter elsewhere, in three places that are each upstream of, and independent from, the lower court's *motivazione*.

*(i) The parties' allegations.* The original text of art. 360 n. 5 of the CPC 1940 — the text in force throughout Liebman's USP period — framed the cassation ground as "omesso esame di un fatto decisivo per il giudizio che è stato oggetto di discussione tra le parti." The relevance filter is written into the provision, and neither of its two elements refers to the lower court's framing: the fact must have been "oggetto di discussione tra le parti" (fixed by the parties' contradictory, recorded in the procedural acts) and "decisivo per il giudizio" (fixed by the outcome under the applicable law). The 1950 reform replaced this with "omessa, insufficiente o contraddittoria motivazione circa un punto decisivo della controversia prospettato dalle parti o rilevabile d'ufficio" — still anchoring the point in the parties' *prospettazione* or in law applied *ex officio*, not in the *motivazione*. The 2012 reform (d.l. 83/2012) restored the 1940 formulation almost verbatim ("omesso esame circa un fatto decisivo..."). Sezioni Unite 7 April 2014 n. 8053, interpreting the restored text, hold that the omitted fact is a "fatto storico, principale o secondario," whose existence results "dal testo della sentenza o dagli atti processuali," that was discussed between the parties, and that is decisive in the counterfactual sense. The disjunction matters: the procedural acts are an independent source for the fact's existence; the sentence's text is not required. Temporal caveat: SU 8053/2014 interprets the 2012 text and is evidence of how the restored formula operates, not of 1940–1950 practice; the textual point about the 1940 provision does not depend on it.

*(ii) The admission-stage relevance screen.* In Italian civil procedure, evidence to be taken (*prove costituende*) enters the trial record only after the judge rules on admissibility and relevance — the *ordinanza ammissiva*, admitting "i mezzi di prova ritenuti ammissibili e rilevanti" (now art. 183 c.p.c.; the article numbering has shifted across reforms, the double screen has not). The record R-1 describes as "an unfiltered inventory" is relevance-filtered by construction, and the filter is recorded in an act that precedes the judgment. For documents produced by the parties without an admission ruling, the relevance frame is still supplied by the parties' allegations under the *principio dispositivo* (art. 115 c.p.c.: the judge must base the decision on the evidence proposed by the parties), not by the *motivazione*.

*(iii) The reviewing court's own legal determination.* Decisiveness is assessed against the law as the reviewing court determines it. The lower court's expressed legal framework is itself an object of review (art. 360 n. 3, *violazione o falsa applicazione di norme di diritto*), and a rule present since the 1940 code (art. 384 c.p.c.) allows the Cassazione to correct a judgment "erroneamente motivat[a] in diritto" while leaving the *dispositivo* standing when it conforms to law. The expressed legal framework can thus be replaced without cassation. If R-1's priority claim were correct, a lower court could immunize an omission by adopting a framework under which the omitted evidence is irrelevant. The doctrine's structure forecloses exactly this: the framework is corrected first, and the omission is then assessed against the corrected framework. The direction of logical priority R-1 asserts is reversed — the reviewing court's framework is prior to the lower court's *motivazione*, not the other way round. The same holds *a fortiori* in *appello*, the ordinary route for the omission: the court of appeal is a judge of the merits within the devolved grounds (*tantum devolutum quantum appellatum*), whose scope is set by the appellant's *motivi*, and it re-examines the record directly.

What R-1 gets right is that the reviewing court reads the *motivazione* — to determine *coverage*, i.e. what the lower court examined. That is the comparison Q-1 already described: the *motivazione* is the document assessed, the record the reference against which it is assessed. Reading the assessed document is not logical priority over the standard.

One further concession, in fairness to R-1. Its characterization fits the 1950–2012 regime better than the original: under the 1950 text the *vizio* was formally a defect of *motivazione* ("insufficiente o contraddittoria"), and review was of the expressed reasoning's logical adequacy — a more *motivazione*-centred structure, under which the trial record's primacy is weaker. This defense accepts that. But the period relevant to the C3 argument is the CPC 1940 as enacted, and the design the codifiers chose was the "omesso esame di un fatto" formula — the one the 2012 legislator restored.

*Falsified if (n-iv, this defense's formulation):* primary-source evidence of Cassazione practice under the original n. 5 (1942–1950) shows that decisiveness or relevance was determined by deference to the lower court's expressed legal framework rather than by the parties' allegations and the reviewing court's own legal determination; or evidence that the admission-stage relevance screen was not operative in that period (evidence routinely entered without a *rilevanza* ruling), so that the record was in practice the unfiltered inventory R-1 describes.

#### S-2: On the Virtue/Accountability Dichotomy (R-2) — with a Correction and Two Concessions

**Correction.** Gorphe's *L'appréciation des preuves en justice: essai d'une méthode technique* was published by Sirey, Paris, in 1947 (488 pp., preface by H. Donnedieu de Vabres), not in 1924. The 1924 work is *La critique du témoignage* (Dalloz, doctoral thesis, 432 pp.). The error is this defense's own, introduced at r17 (§3.8.O) and carried by both sides through r20. The paper's earlier references are corrected in place. Three consequences follow, each a concession.

First, the 1947 work is not pre-C3 by this exchange's definition (Liebman's USP period, 1940–1946). The pre-C3 anchor in the French tradition is *La critique du témoignage* (1924), a method for the critical appraisal of witness evidence, and the earlier tradition on proof to which it belongs — which this defense has invoked in general terms ("extending through the nineteenth century") but has not documented at the primary-source level. This defense accepts that the French prong of condition (o)'s pre-C3 vocabulary resource now rests on the 1924 work and on an undocumented earlier tradition, and is correspondingly weaker than §3.8.O claimed. The Spanish translation (*De la apreciación de las pruebas*, Ediciones Jurídicas Europa-América, Buenos Aires, 1955; re-edited Hammurabi, 2007) establishes accessibility to Argentine scholars from the mid-1950s — during the Latin American transmission period, not strictly pre-C3.

Second, Gorphe was a criminal-law magistrate (*juge d'instruction*, later *procureur de la République*), and the 1947 work is catalogued as concerning evidence in criminal matters. Q-2's institutional-context distinction therefore cannot be drawn as "criminal jury (Art. 342) versus civil professional judge (Gorphe)." This defense accepts that the civil/criminal framing in Q-2 was wrong. The distinction that survives is jury versus professional judge: Art. 342 CIC 1808 was the instruction addressed to the jurors of the *cour d'assises*; professional criminal courts were subject to the general duty to state *motifs* (loi du 20 avril 1810, art. 7, under which judgments without *motifs* are null), and Gorphe's method was addressed to the professional judge's appraisal. R18's P-2 conflation of the two institutional contexts remains a conflation, but of a narrower kind than Q-2 stated.

Third, Q-2's form-b formulation — "accountability in its epistemic form is the specification of what the rational standard is" — was, as worded, too broad. R-2 is right that a formula covering "whatever specifies the positive rational standard" would cover purely virtue-based traditions in which no accountability relationship exists. This defense accepts that and narrows the claim below.

**The narrowed response.** R-2's dichotomy — epistemic virtue (internal accuracy) versus external accountability (reviewability and correctability) — omits the middle term that Gorphe's subtitle names: a *method*. A virtue is a property of the evaluator: prudence, insight, experience. A method is a public object: a set of stated operations and criteria that a third party can apply to the same materials. An evaluation that claims to be the product of a stated method is thereby answerable to the method — it can be checked against it, and can fail it, by someone other than the evaluator. R-2's premise that "a judge can be epistemically virtuous (internally accurate) without being externally accountable" is true of virtue-specified standards and false of method-specified ones: to evaluate according to a stated technical method is to submit the evaluation to a standard outside the evaluator. Leclerc (2021), the one secondary treatment consulted in this round, characterizes Gorphe's project as the advocacy "that evaluation of evidence be based on the best scientific knowledge available." Scientific knowledge is an external, public standard against which a judge's appraisal can be criticized; it is not an internal orientation.

The narrowed claim, then: the accountability dimension in its epistemic form is present where the rational standard of free evaluation is *method-specified* — stated as public, third-party-applicable criteria — because such a standard constitutes the evaluation as answerable to something outside the evaluator; it is absent where the standard is *virtue-specified* — stated as a quality of the judge. This is what condition (o)'s Type-2 vocabulary requires: accountability as a dimension of what rational evaluation *is*, not a disclosure obligation attached to it. It is worth noting, without pressing it further in this round, that the Italian code's own free-evaluation formula is virtue-specified ("prudente apprezzamento," art. 116), which is consistent with version (i*)'s reading of the Italian tradition, and that the German formula (§ 286 ZPO, discussed in S-3) is not.

What this defense does not claim: that Gorphe's tradition tied the method to *institutional* review — that the evaluation's rational character was made "dependent on its expressibility to others," as (o-iv) is worded by the adversarial. That is form-a accountability, and it has not been shown from Gorphe's own text in this exchange; direct verification of Gorphe's statements on *motivation* and on appellate control was not possible in this round, and the S-2 response rests on the conceptual distinction between method and virtue plus the verified characterization of the method's orientation. If (o-iv) requires form-a, this defense does not meet it and does not need to: condition (o) was formulated in Type-2 terms, and Type-2 is met by method-specification.

*Falsified if (o-iv, this defense's formulation):* a primary-source reading of Gorphe (1924; 1947) shows that the method was presented as a private aid to the formation of the judge's conviction and explicitly *not* as a standard against which the appraisal could be criticized by others; or that the pre-1940 French tradition on professional evaluation of evidence to which the 1924 work belongs is virtue-specified rather than method-specified; or that Leclerc's characterization of the project is inaccurate.

#### S-3: Against the Parity Argument (R-3) — Concession and Relocation

**Concession.** R-3 is right. Q-3's rational-legal-model argument is an inference from institutional context to Q2-scholarship organization, of the same type this defense refused to accept for Italy (§§3.8.F, 3.8.H, 3.8.J, 3.8.L). This defense withdraws it as the ground for the German asymmetry. Consistency demands the same evidentiary standard for both traditions.

**Relocation.** The standard this exchange has actually operated with — on both sides — is the code's own structural decision. The adversarial's §3.8.F called the Italian codifiers' two-institution decision "the most direct available institutional evidence"; this defense has treated the Art. 116/Art. 132 separation as the primary evidence for analytical separation. Applying that same standard to the German code yields a different result, for a textual reason neither side has checked until now.

§ 259 of the Civilprozeßordnung of 30 January 1877 (RGBl. 1877, Nr. 6; renumbered § 286 in the 1898 consolidation, and unchanged in substance today) reads:

> "Das Gericht hat unter Berücksichtigung des gesammten Inhalts der Verhandlungen und des Ergebnisses einer etwaigen Beweisaufnahme nach freier Ueberzeugung zu entscheiden, ob eine thatsächliche Behauptung für wahr oder für nicht wahr zu erachten sei. In dem Urtheile sind die Gründe anzugeben, welche für die richterliche Ueberzeugung leitend gewesen sind."

The second sentence — the judgment must state the grounds which guided the judicial conviction — is *inside* the free-evaluation provision. It is a reasons duty specific to the conviction, defined by reference to the conviction. Separately, § 284 Nr. 4 CPO 1877 (now § 313 Abs. 1 Nr. 6 ZPO) lists "die Entscheidungsgründe" among the required contents of every judgment. The German civil code therefore has a three-part structure: freedom of evaluation (§ 259 S. 1), a conviction-specific reasons duty within the same norm (§ 259 S. 2), and a general judgment-form duty (§ 284 Nr. 4). The Italian CPC 1940 has two parts: art. 116 (which is silent on reasons) and art. 132 n. 4 (general *motivazione*). R18's premise that the German structure is "structurally identical" to the Italian two-institution structure is textually false. R-3's parity premise — "the Italian *motivazione* requirement is equally connected institutionally to the authority of Italian judicial decisions" — compares Art. 132 with § 313 and omits § 286 Abs. 1 S. 2, the provision that has no Italian counterpart.

Why this is the same evidence type, not the institutional-context inference R-3 objects to: the German codifier had the general *Entscheidungsgründe* duty available in § 284 and nevertheless wrote a second, conviction-specific reasons duty into the free-evaluation norm itself. Under the standard both sides have applied to Italy — the codifiers' structural decision as the most direct available evidence of how the tradition analytically organized the concept — that decision is evidence that the reasons duty was conceived as part of the free-evaluation concept, not as a separate judgment-form obligation. The contrast within German law confirms that this was a choice rather than a drafting habit: the criminal code states free conviction for criminal courts without a reasons clause (§ 261 StPO, a principle present since the 1877 Strafprozeßordnung), with the *Urteilsgründe* governed separately (§ 267 StPO). The civil codifier integrated; the criminal codifier separated.

**Scope of S-3.** This does not establish Possibility B at the level of the Q2 scholarship. It establishes three narrower things: (a) R18's structural-identity premise fails; (b) R-3's parity argument has no purchase, because the German and Italian codes are not parallel at the one point that matters; and (c) by the evidentiary standard both sides have used, the German prior differs from the Italian one, in the direction of integration. Condition (o-v) remains the right test of the scholarship. But the burden allocation R-3 sought — "German = Italian until primary sources show otherwise" — is reversed by the standard R-3 itself invoked. The same textual fact also corrects this defense's own earlier characterization at §3.8.O, which asserted the German scholarship's internal engagement with accountability without a source; the code text is now the ground, and the scholarship claim is withdrawn to the status of an open question.

*Falsified if (o-v, this defense's formulation):* primary-source German proceduralist scholarship of 1877–1940 treated § 259/§ 286 S. 2 as a mere form requirement of the judgment (Type-1), organizing the rationality question about *freie Überzeugung* without reference to the stated-grounds requirement; or the 1877 legislative materials show that S. 2 was placed in the free-evaluation norm for reasons unconnected with constraining the conviction. Either would restore the German tradition to the Italian tradition's Possibility A/B underdetermination and revive R-3's parity argument at the scholarship level.

## 4. Anticipated Objections

### 4.1 Directional Redundancy

**Objection:** The Watson transplant hypothesis, properly specified with the civilian judicial authority model (*iura novit curia*) and absent stare decisis enforcement, already predicts the personal-conviction direction of *livre convencimento* distortion. The structural mechanism is sufficient for the directional prediction; importing patrimonialism adds no directional content the mechanism doesn't already supply. The patrimonialism thesis is therefore directionally redundant over a properly specified transplant hypothesis.

**Response:** Section 3.4 above accepts the directional argument and redirects. The directional-redundancy objection succeeds against the directional-specificity claim as originally formulated in this paper. It does not succeed against the thesis's surviving distinctive contribution: that patrimonialism explains the FORM of doctrinalization, not only the direction of drift.

For the directional-redundancy objection to defeat the narrowed thesis, it would need to show that the structural mechanism also predicts DOCTRINALIZATION — that civilian judicial authority + absent stare decisis generates not only informal personal-authority expansion but specifically the conversion of that expansion into a named positive doctrine with a European legitimizing genealogy, absorbed into professional formation as orthodoxy. The adversarial's own account (peripheral-field dynamics, §3.4 of `otherwise/paper1g-transplant-specification.md`) represents a second-generation attempt at this: if peripheral-field legitimation dynamics explains not only the orientation (validation) but also the specific content (validation of the informal personal-authority practice as named doctrine), then the doctrinalization form follows from the combination of structural mechanism + peripheral-field dynamics without requiring patrimonialism.

The generation/reception distinction (adversarial r3) is accepted here as structurally correct and removes Italy as a comparator for peripheral-field reception. The European outcomes that were previously cited as showing that non-patrimonial fields absorb C3 differently (Italy: reform response; France: terse form; Germany: *Begründungspflicht*) are not valid test cases for peripheral-field reception dynamics; they are metropolitan generating-field or internally-developed outcomes. The adversarial r3 correctly identifies this and the response accepts it.

The content-selection argument survives this accepted concession. If Portugal and Argentina — peripheral receiving fields of the Liebman tradition, civilian with weak stare decisis, without the Faoro-Holanda diagnosis — produced equivalent doctrinalization forms to Brazil (a named positive doctrine of personal judicial freedom absorbed into formation orthodoxy), then either peripheral-field dynamics alone suffices for content selection, or those fields share conditions relevant to content selection. If they did not produce equivalent forms, the patrimonialism account retains the content-selection contribution. The structural-factor account of European outcomes (France's cassation mechanism; Germany's Bismarckian requirements) addresses how external benchmarks produce legible deviation from a standard — relevant to enforcement but not to how a receiving field internally orients its doctrinal tradition toward an imported vocabulary when structural constraints are absent. That question, for peripheral receiving fields, remains open pending the Portugal/Argentina comparison.

### 4.2 Parsimony

**Objection:** Paper 1G's patrimonialism thesis requires a historical-sociological claim about the transmission of an Iberian cultural disposition into the Brazilian legal field across centuries — a claim that, even if accepted as generally true for Brazilian state formation, needs a further argument to establish that this disposition specifically shaped the particular reception of *livre convencimento*. If the transplant hypothesis (properly specified with civilian architecture) can explain the distortion without this additional claim, parsimony favors it.

**Response:** The parsimony argument applies when two hypotheses have equivalent explanatory power. They do not. The structural transplant hypothesis explains the general direction of informal personal-authority drift; the peripheral-field dynamics account explains the orientation of the vocabulary's absorption (validation rather than critique); neither alone explains the content of what gets validated — the specific attachment of the vocabulary to the informal practice of personal judicial authority as a named positive doctrine absorbed into formation orthodoxy. The naming, positive framing, genealogical legitimization, and formation-integration of the practice *as that specific practice* are additional phenomena requiring additional explanation. The parsimony argument correctly identifies an additional theoretical commitment (the patrimonial habitus); it does not show the commitment is unwarranted when it explains phenomena the competing hypotheses do not reach at the same level of specificity.

The patrimonialism commitment is not ad hoc. Faoro's and Holanda's accounts of Brazilian patrimonialism were developed to explain features of Brazilian state formation across the political, bureaucratic, and economic domains — not specifically to explain legal doctrine. Their application to the legal field requires showing that the same disposition operates in the judicial context, which Paper 1G's §2.1 provides. Once the application is established, the content-selection prediction follows: a patrimonial field, in which the informal practice of personal judicial authority is exercised under C1-C2 conditions and self-conceptualized as authoritative rather than deviant, will absorb the legitimizing vocabulary specifically as validation of that practice — recruiting it as confirmation rather than as a standard against which the practice should be measured. This is what distinguishes the Brazilian reception from what peripheral non-patrimonial receiving fields (Portugal, Argentina) would be predicted to produce, conditional on those fields not having equivalent C1-C2 and habitual self-conceptualization conditions.

### 4.3 Adversarial R4: Three Independent Content-Selection Failures

**Objection (summarizing adversarial r4, §3.5):** Three independent structural arguments show that Mode III is unnecessary for content selection. (A) The absorbing agents were academic scholars, not judicial practitioners; their absorptive orientation was determined by peripheral-field legitimation dynamics, not by Mode III judicial self-conceptualization. (B) C1+C2 salience and vocabulary specificity jointly over-determine the attachment content without invoking Mode III; the Liebman vocabulary maps with high specificity onto exactly the practice C1+C2 conditions make most durable. (C) P1 generates Mode III as a post-C3 output, so Mode III pre-C3 is an unnecessary assumption: Streck's evidence is consistent with Mode III arising from formation transmission rather than from patrimonial role-constitution.

**Response:** Section 3.5 above accepts that 3.5.A–B establish content over-determination (which practice is targeted) and that 3.5.C correctly identifies the evidential limit of Streck's post-C3 evidence. The response presses the content-form distinction: the three attacks do not establish that the form of the C3 absorption — specifically, retrospective naming of existing informal practice as an accomplished legitimate state (form i) rather than prospective normative alignment (form ii) — was also determined by structural features. Form (i) requires a publicly claimable practice, which only Mode III produces; and Scenario 2's post-C3 Mode III via P1 cannot explain the retrospective form of the original Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra framing at the C3 moment, which is causally prior to P1's operation.

The adversarial r4 attack succeeds in establishing content over-determination. It leaves form over-determination open. Mode III's residual explanatory contribution is at the level of absorptive form, not at the level of attachment content. Adversarial r5's attack on the form-selection level is addressed in §3.6 and §4.4 below.

### 4.4 Adversarial R5: Form-Selection Also Fails

**Objection (summarizing adversarial r5, §§3.6.A–C):** Two independent structural arguments show that the form-selection residual does not require patrimonialism. (A) The Liebman vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy makes Form (ii) specifically deficit-implying in a peripheral receiving field — affirming the reform principle as normative commitment implies the field had been in the inadequate *prova legal* state the reform superseded — so the collective legitimation incentive that was conceded for orientation (validation over critique) operates at this more specific level to select Form (i) over Form (ii), without invoking Mode III. (B) Academic-naming is sufficient for Form (i) production: scholars can observe the informal practice and identify it with the vocabulary's positive designatum without requiring Mode III practitioner endorsement. With (A) and (B) in place, Scenario 2's temporal sequence is structurally complete: (1) structural dynamics → Form (i) framing at C3; (2) P1 generates Mode III post-C3 through formation transmission; no pre-C3 Mode III required.

**Response:** Section 3.6 above develops the full response. Against 3.6.A: the Form (ii) deficit-implication is conditional on the *prova legal*-critique being the operative analytical frame for the Brazilian reception — a premise the adversarial does not establish and that Brazil's 1939 CPC context (already incorporating evidence-evaluation freedom) complicates. Form (ii) framing in a field that does not self-identify as having been in the *prova legal* tradition does not carry the specific deficit implication 3.6.A requires. Against 3.6.B: the academic-naming mechanism produces descriptive Form (i) but not evaluative Form (i). The historical framing — "conquista de racionalidade processual" as an accomplished rationality gain — is evaluative, not merely descriptive. The evaluative step requires that the structural gap between the Brazilian informal practice and the European principle (which carried justificatory requirements the Brazilian practice did not systematically implement) is invisible to the scholar. This gap-invisibility is what the patrimonial constitutive framework provides, by rendering informal personal judicial authority as constitutively legitimate institutional performance — making the justificatory dimension of the European principle imperceptible as a distinct requirement from inside the framework. Neither structural features nor peripheral-field dynamics independently predict gap-invisibility; patrimonialism does.

### 4.5 Adversarial R6: Gap-Visibility Mechanism Does Not Succeed

**Objection (summarizing adversarial r6, §§3.7.A–D):** Four independent arguments show that the gap-visibility mechanism formulated in §3.6 does not establish a patrimonialism-specific contribution to evaluative Form (i) production. (A) The mechanism identifies the wrong gap: the evaluative Form (i) claim targets the vocabulary's primary positive content (liberation from formal hierarchies), not full procedural equivalence; a scholar aware of the European motivation requirement can produce genuine evaluative Form (i) at the primary-content level without gap-invisibility. (B) The evaluative intensity of Form (i) — "conquista" as the achieved rational state — follows from legitimation-incentive maximization within the vocabulary's diagnostic binary under founding-moment field pressure; gap-invisibility is not the generating mechanism for the superlative evaluative form. (C) An internal tension exists between the 3.6.A operative-frame defense (implying comparative awareness) and gap-invisibility (requiring perceptual closure to the same vocabulary's associated features); Liebman's direct presence made the reform framework substantially inescapable, and the Form (ii) deficit implication is relational, not dependent on the absolute *prova legal* starting point. (D) The gap-visibility mechanism is inconsistent with the 3.5.A acceptance: academic scholars' orientation was set by academic field-position dynamics, not the judicial practitioners' constitutive framework; the legitimation project's affirmative epistemic posture generates gap-inattention directly; a selective-blindness problem shows the framework cannot selectively prevent perception of the accountability gap while permitting full engagement with the *prova legal* critique, since both concern the same normative relationship.

**Response:** Section 3.7 above develops the full response through the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence reframing. Against 3.7.A: the primary-content/secondary-safeguard distinction is itself a conceptual resource the C3 field's vocabulary had not developed; scholars engaged with the vocabulary as a unit constituted by the field's theoretical resources, which did not separately constitute the accountability dimension as a distinct positive requirement of the rational standard. Against 3.7.B: legitimation-incentive maximization within the vocabulary's evaluative binary produces "conquista" only when the rational pole is constituted as liberation-specifically; if the rational pole included the accountability dimension, the maximum Form (i) claim would need to address it. The content of the rational pole is what the field's conceptual vocabulary had constituted — which, under patrimonial prior formation, comprised the liberation dimension alone. Against 3.7.C: the two kinds of awareness are at different analytical levels (historical comparative knowledge vs. conceptual vocabulary resources); the tension dissolves when the mechanism is understood as field-level vocabulary absence rather than individual cognitive imperceptibility. Against 3.7.D: the reframed mechanism is consistent with 3.5.A because it locates the mechanism at the field's theoretical resources, within which academic legitimation dynamics operated, rather than at the judicial practitioners' constitutive framework reaching individual scholars; differential conceptual availability (not selective cognitive closure) explains why scholars engaged with the articulated positive content of the vocabulary while not examining an accountability dimension the vocabulary had not constituted as a distinct positive requirement; the patrimonialism contribution grounds the absence in the prior patrimonial formation of the field's conceptual resources.

### 4.6 Adversarial R7: The Italian CPC 1940's Separate Codification Defeats the Vocabulary Absence Claim

**Objection (summarizing adversarial r7, §§3.8.A–C):** Three arguments show that the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence does not hold at the Italian tradition's articulation level. (A) The Italian CPC 1940, enacted during Liebman's USP engagement (1940–1946), separately codified Art. 116 (*libero convincimento del giudice*) and Art. 132, n. 4 (*motivazione*), establishing that the accountability dimension was separately articulable in the tradition Liebman transmitted; the structural probability that Liebman's transmission excluded Art. 132 is very low, and the burden falls on the supportive to provide primary-source evidence of such exclusion. (B) The vocabulary absence has two readings: version (i) — the Italian tradition lacked the concept — is improbable given §3.8.A; version (ii) — the concept was present but not adopted — collapses to a vocabulary selection problem that the structural account (legitimation incentives explaining selective non-adoption) explains as well as patrimonialism. ## 5. Scope of This Support

This formalization strengthens two aspects of Paper 1G's argument:

**The non-accidentality claim.** By identifying the structural conditions C1–C3 and showing that pre-CPC 2015 Brazil met all three, the formalization establishes that doctrinalization was structurally produced — it followed from identifiable features of the field that were simultaneously present. This gives Paper 1G's central claim ("not an accidental anomaly") a precise structural meaning: the claim is not that each historical event in the genealogy was determined, but that the structural conditions for the transition were identifiably met, making the transition neither contingent nor reversible without disrupting those conditions.

**The resistance-to-normative-shock claim.** By identifying P1–P2 and showing that pre-CPC 2015 Brazil met both, the formalization provides the structural basis for Paper 1G's §6 (habitus as resistance to normative change). The resistance is not merely attitudinal; it follows from the path-dependence lock-in produced by formation-integrated transmission and capital-generating function, both of which are structurally identifiable features of how the Liebman-Dinamarco tradition operated in Brazilian legal professional formation.

**Scope revision after adversarial engagement.** Following the bilateral exchange through adversarial r1–r7, the thesis's claims have been progressively narrowed and the explanatory mechanism has been refined through three distinct conceptual moves. The directional-specificity claim has been conceded to the structural civilian-architecture mechanism (r1). The orientation of C3 absorption (validation rather than critique) has been conceded, at the level of peripheral receiving fields, to the peripheral-field dynamics account (r2). Adversarial r4 established that the CONTENT of C3 attachment — which practice becomes the vocabulary's attachment target — is over-determined by C1+C2 salience and vocabulary specificity, without requiring Mode III. Adversarial r5 attacked the form-selection residual through the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy (3.6.A) and the academic-naming mechanism (3.6.B); the response introduced the gap-visibility mechanism, repositioning the patrimonialism contribution from Mode III practitioners' prior public claims to the patrimonial constitutive framework's production of gap-invisibility. Adversarial r6 attacked gap-visibility as individually imperceptible through the judicial constitutive framework, in tension with the accepted 3.5.A concession. Adversarial r7 attacked the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence through the Italian CPC 1940's separate codification (Art. 116 and Art. 132) during Liebman's USP engagement, establishing that the accountability dimension was separately articulable in the Italian tradition as transmitted; the response introduces version (i*) — the two-institution structure of the Italian code is evidence that *libero convincimento* (Art. 116's evidentiary evaluation domain) and *motivazione* (Art. 132's judgment-form domain) were analytically distinct institutions in Italian proceduralism, and that the vocabulary absence operates at the level of the concept's positive designatum specifically, not at the level of the tradition's full institutional vocabulary.

What remains is the FORM contribution, now articulated through **field-level conceptual vocabulary absence**: the Brazilian proceduralist field's theoretical vocabulary at the C3 moment had not developed systematic justificatory accountability as a distinct component of the concept of rational judicial evaluation — because the field's prior patrimonial formation constituted "rational judicial evaluation" as personal authoritative judgment, not as judgment with systematic intersubjective accountability. This conceptual absence explains why academic scholars, maximizing within the legitimation project's vocabulary, produced evaluative Form (i) ("conquista de racionalidade processual" — the achieved rational state) rather than descriptive partial-identification or prospective normative alignment: the rational pole of the vocabulary's binary was constituted by the liberation dimension alone, and existing Brazilian practice fully occupied the rational standard as constituted. The mechanism is at the field's theoretical resources, consistent with 3.5.A (academic legitimation dynamics organized what was maximized, bounded by the field's available conceptual vocabulary), and grounds the vocabulary absence in the prior patrimonial formation of the field.

The Portugal/Argentina comparison remains the primary open empirical test, now framed at three levels: (1) whether those fields produced the same absorptive orientation and content; (2) whether the evaluative intensity of Form (i) framing, where present, matches "conquista"-level superlative or is more hedged; and (3) whether those fields' theoretical vocabularies at the C3 moment had or had not constituted "systematic justificatory accountability" as a distinct positive component of rational judicial evaluation — specifically, whether there is evidence of the accountability dimension being available as a distinct conceptual resource in those fields that the Brazilian field lacked. A secondary open empirical question concerns the Italian tradition's own state of articulation at the time of Liebman's Brazil engagement: specifically, whether the *libero convincimento* scholarship (the evidentiary evaluation debates organized around Art. 116) internally integrated the accountability dimension through *motivazione* (Art. 132) as a constitutively part of the concept's positive designatum, or treated the two as analytically distinct institutions with separate scholarly domains. ## 6. Conditions Under Which This Support Would Fail

**(a) Content-selection equivalence in peripheral non-patrimonial receiving fields.** If comparative evidence established that peripheral non-patrimonial civilian fields that received the Liebman tradition from outside — Portugal and Argentina being the primary comparators — produced the same doctrinalization form as Brazil (a named positive doctrine of personal judicial freedom, absorbed into professional formation as orthodoxy), the patrimonialism thesis's content-selection contribution would be substantially reduced. This would indicate that either (i) C1+C2 conditions alone, without patrimonial habitual self-conceptualization, determine the content of what gets validated under peripheral-field legitimation pressure; or (ii) those fields share conditions relevant to content selection that the comparison inadequately controls. The comparison has not been made at the documentary level either side requires. Note that if Portugal and Argentina produced equivalent forms, this would also challenge the peripheral-field dynamics account's separation from content selection (since it would imply the orientation prediction and the content-selection prediction converge for the structural conditions present in those fields).

**(b) Enforcement deficit revision.** If pre-CPC 2015 Brazilian appellate review was more granular than the enforcement deficit (C2) description allows — if there existed mechanisms that systematically tracked quality-of-engagement violations under art. 131 of the CPC 1973 — then either the disposition could not have been doctrinalized in the first place (C2 prevents doctrinalization from occurring) or the doctrinalization would have required additional mechanisms not identified in Paper 1G. Paper 1G's §2.2 provides substantial evidence for the enforcement deficit, but institutional-level documentation of pre-CPC 2015 appellate quality-tracking practices could revise the assessment.

**(c) Formation transmission failure.** If Brazilian legal education during the CPC 1973 period can be shown to have systematically transmitted the European-origin version of *livre convencimento* — with its rational engagement requirements and its context of procedural guarantees against evidentiary irrationality, not the discretionary-decision extension — then P1 would not hold as described. The doctrine would have been mastered in its legitimate form without its dispositional extension being transmitted as competence content. Paper 1G's §3.3 provides evidence that the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition transmitted the principle without examining the European-Brazilian gap, but direct curriculum analysis of pre-CPC 2015 formation programs could revise this.

**(d) Patrimonialism scope restriction.** If the Faoro-Holanda patrimonialism thesis, as applied to the legal field, is established only for the state-bureaucratic domain and cannot be extended to the judicial field without additional argumentation that Paper 1G does not fully provide, the content-selection argument in §3.4 would require that additional argumentation to stand. The existing literature on Bourdieu's analysis of the Brazilian legal field (Engelmann 2006, cited in Paper 1G) partially addresses this, but a more complete analysis of patrimonialism's legal-field transmission would strengthen the claim.

**(e) Peripheral-field dynamics account proves sufficient for content selection.** If the adversarial can show that the peripheral-field legitimation dynamic — without invoking patrimonialism — specifically predicts that a peripheral receiving field with C1+C2 conditions will attach the validation orientation to the informal personal-authority practice rather than to other available targets (theoretical sophistication, procedural modernity), then the content-selection distinction collapses and the peripheral-field dynamics account is complete. This would require showing either that (i) the legitimation dynamic's direction of validation is always toward the most institutionally exposed informal practice (which would be a general prediction about content selection under legitimation pressure that does not invoke patrimonialism); or (ii) the vocabulary's specific elements (liberation from evidentiary hierarchies; free personal evaluation) map sufficiently specifically onto the personal-authority practice that any field with C1+C2 conditions and legitimation pressure would produce the same attachment, regardless of habitual self-conceptualization. Neither has been established in the current exchange. Adversarial r4 has partially satisfied condition (e) at the content level: 3.5.B establishes that C1+C2 salience and vocabulary specificity jointly over-determine which practice is the attachment target, without requiring Mode III. This narrows the remaining claim: the patrimonialism thesis's contribution has been compressed from content selection to absorptive form selection (see §§3.5–3.6 and 5 above). Condition (e) is now satisfied at the content level but remains open at the form level. For condition (e) to be fully satisfied at the form level, the adversarial must show that the structural dynamics it invokes also specifically predict evaluative Form (i) — the characterization of existing informal practice as an accomplished rationality gain — over descriptive Form (i) or prospective normative alignment, without invoking gap-invisibility. Adversarial r5's attempts to satisfy this through the diagnostic-genealogy argument (3.6.A) and the academic-naming mechanism (3.6.B) are addressed in §3.6 above; the challenge those attempts face is stated in the §3.6 Falsified if condition.

**(f) Conceptual vocabulary absence failure.** If primary-source evidence or structural argument establishes that the Brazilian proceduralist field's theoretical vocabulary at C3 had constituted "systematic justificatory accountability" as a distinct positive component of rational judicial evaluation — distinguishable from freedom from formal proof hierarchies — and scholars still produced evaluative Form (i) ("conquista de racionalidade processual") framing, then the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence mechanism fails. The remaining explanations for evaluative Form (i) would be either the legitimation project's motivated gap-inattention (adversarial 3.7.D's alternative) or a structural account that predicts "conquista"-level framing even when the accountability dimension is conceptually available. This condition supersedes the earlier gap-invisibility condition (condition (f) in prior versions of this paper), which was formulated as individual epistemic imperceptibility through the patrimonial constitutive framework.

**(g) Italian tradition's articulation state at C3 — revised after adversarial r7.** Adversarial r7 has established that the Italian CPC 1940 separately codified Art. 116 (*libero convincimento*) and Art. 132 (*motivazione*) during Liebman's USP engagement (1940–1946), making the original version of this condition — that the Italian tradition had not yet separately articulated the accountability dimension — no longer the live question. The revised condition operates at a more precise level: the support would fail under this condition if the *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically — the Italian proceduralist debates organized around Art. 116 as an evidentiary evaluation principle — internally integrated the accountability dimension through *motivazione* as a constitutively part of the concept's positive designatum, making the two-institution code structure a codification artifact rather than an analytical separation. Under this revised condition, the version (i*) distinction introduced in §3.8 would collapse: if the *libero convincimento* scholarship treated freedom and accountability as internally unified within the concept's positive content (not merely as co-present in the code), then the Italian tradition transmitted a unified concept from which the Brazilian field selectively excluded the accountability dimension — version (ii) rather than version (i*) — and the structural account's legitimation-incentive explanation for selective non-adoption becomes the primary alternative. Primary-source evidence from Italian proceduralist scholarship on *libero convincimento* (Liebman's works, Carnelutti on *libero convincimento* as an evidentiary principle, Italian pre-1940 doctrine on the relationship between judicial evaluation and motivazione) would determine whether the separation in Italian proceduralism was analytically genuine or a codification artifact.

**(h) Operative-frame establishment.** The 3.6.A vector of adversarial r5 would be strengthened considerably if primary sources or structural argument established that the *prova legal*-critique was the operative analytical frame for Brazilian proceduralists' engagement with the Liebman tradition at the C3 moment. If this premise is established, the diagnostic-genealogy's deficit-implication for Form (ii) is more strongly grounded. The counter-argument in §3.6 would then require showing either that the 1939 CPC context produced a sufficiently non-*prova legal* self-identification to neutralize the deficit-implication, or that the operative frame does not generate the specific Form (ii) deficit-implication 3.6.A claims. This condition is also relevant to the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence mechanism in §3.7: if the Liebman tradition's operative engagement frame in Brazil included the accountability dimension as a primary articulated content (not merely the liberation dimension), then the vocabulary's available positive content at C3 would have included the accountability concept, and the conceptual vocabulary absence explanation would require additional argument to explain why scholars nonetheless produced evaluative Form (i) without examining the accountability gap.

**(i) Comparative evaluative intensity.** If comparative evidence from peripheral non-patrimonial civilian fields that received the Liebman tradition at comparable field-founding moments shows that those fields produced evaluative Form (i) framing of comparable intensity — "conquista"-equivalent superlative retrospective validation — despite their field vocabularies including systematic justificatory accountability as a distinct conceptual component, this would indicate that the evaluative maximum was produced by legitimation-incentive maximization under founding-moment field pressure, independent of conceptual vocabulary constitution. This is the most direct empirical test of the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence mechanism and of the §3.7.B adversarial argument: whether "conquista"-level intensity is predicted by structural dynamics alone under equivalent field pressure, or whether it requires the vocabulary's rational pole to be constituted as the liberation dimension specifically.

**(j) Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship's internal integration of accountability.** Added after adversarial r7. If primary-source evidence from Italian proceduralist scholarship on *libero convincimento* as an evidentiary evaluation principle — Liebman's works, or Italian doctrine on Art. 116 specifically — shows that the accountability dimension through *motivazione* was systematically integrated as a constitutively internal positive requirement of what the evidentiary evaluation principle means, not merely as a separate institutional obligation in a distinct code chapter, then version (i*) collapses to version (ii).
