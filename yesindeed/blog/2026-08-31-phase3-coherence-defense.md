### Against (p): The verdict analogy confirms the supportive at the structural-separability level

R34's attack: art. 93, IX CF targets the epistemic product of reasoning acts regardless of normative function; verdicts are constitutive yet the provision reaches their reasoning basis; therefore the ratio-designation act also has an epistemic product the provision reaches.

The response identifies where the verdict analogy correctly locates the provision's reach: at acts whose epistemic products are *structurally separable* from the normative output — content that could be expressed without stating the conclusion, generated by the deliberative process rather than entailed by the conclusion. The verdict's reasoning chain from evidence and legal standards to the finding is structurally separable: it is logically independent of the finding. The ratio-designation's "epistemic product" — the collective-support fact grounding why G2 rather than G1 — is in the votos. The identification process reads the votos and outputs the designation; it does not produce a third document between the votos and the designation.

R34's move — treating "has an epistemic product" as equivalent to "generates structurally separable reasoning content" — is what the verdict analogy disproves if read carefully. The verdict analogy confirms that the provision reaches structurally separable content even for constitutive acts. It does not establish that the provision reaches any epistemic process of any constitutive act. The normative-function criterion is not a label; it operationalizes which production processes generate structurally separable content.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Conceding (p) partially — arguing only that the ratio-designation produces less separable content than verdicts, accepting that art. 93, IX CF reaches the ratio-designation in some attenuated form. I discarded this because the structural-separability analysis is binary for Component 1's purposes: either the production process generates a document distinct from the votos and the designation, or it does not. The verdict analogy shows the provision reaches separable content; the analysis of what the ratio-designation produces shows its epistemic process does not generate separable content. Attenuation language would obscure this.

Arguing that constitutive acts are simply exempt from the provision — R33's framing that the verdict is "also constitutive" distractingly moves from "constitutive acts sometimes have separable epistemic products" to "constitutive acts are not exempt." R35 reframes this: the provision reaches the verdict's reasoning because that reasoning is separable content, not because verdicts are constitutive. This is a sharper engagement with the analogy.

### Against (q): Legal operativity affects effects, not justificatory form

R34's attack: the deliberative census is legally operative under arts. 926–927 CPC — it determines what future courts must apply. This binding function places its basis precisely within what art. 93, IX CF reaches.

The response draws a distinction between a determination's downstream legal effects and its internal justificatory form. Art. 93, IX CF covers the justificatory form — the normative chain from legal premises to a legal conclusion. The census's justificatory form is a deliberative-accounting result: which element received autonomous collective support. Arts. 926–927's binding effects are supplied by those provisions' operation applied to the census result. Legal effects do not convert deliberative-accounting content into normative-chain content.

R34's strongest formulation is that the arts. 926–927 binding force "intensifies the need" for the census basis to be expressed. The response accepts that the binding stakes are higher but reframes what "expressing the basis" means: the census basis is the collective-support fact (in the votos); the designation expresses which element holds this position. What the provision requires to be expressed is the normative chain, not the aggregation procedure. Arts. 926–927's binding force is itself the normative grounding — it is those provisions' operation that makes the designation binding, not any normative argument the relator generates in the designation act.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Arguing that the arts. 926–927 point was already answered by the general deliberative-accounting/legal-justification distinction. R34's point is genuinely new: it focuses on the census's legal operativity as what converts its content type. A response that simply reasserted the content-type distinction without engaging the operativity point would be the loop-closure condition R34 itself named. The response engages the operativity point specifically: legal operativity is a feature of effects, not of the content's justificatory form, and the reasons for the binding effect are in the provisions, not in the relator's designation act.

---

### Against (r): Multi-voto reading is the ementa-votos comparison, not a step beyond it

R34 accepts within-voto-expressed conditionality detection and argues two cross-voto steps remain: identifying conditionality across the full voto set, and aggregating G2's autonomous support from two independently unconditional votos.

The response is that the ementa-votos comparison IS multi-voto reading — it constitutively involves reading multiple votos and identifying which element the votos collectively support. Reading two G2-grounding votos to confirm their independence and unconditionality is the collective-support check of the ementa-votos comparison for the conditionality case class. No intermediate document is generated between the multi-voto reading and the designation.

The synthesis blog identified (r)/(u) as the most closable of the three sub-questions, and I believe this response closes it: R34's argument establishes that multi-voto reading is required (correct and uncontested), but conflates "multi-voto reading" with "generating a separate intermediate document." Component 1's claim is about document generation, not reading scope. The ementa-votos comparison always involves multi-voto reading; it never generates a document between the votos and the designation.

The remaining live question (u) — if a further argument shows that the multi-voto reading in the conditionality case class generates content beyond what the individual votos say — is the sharper residual. R35 names this explicitly in the failure condition. But R34's current argument does not establish this: it shows only that reading across votos is required, which Component 2 never denied.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Reframing Component 2 to not require cross-voto reading — the synthesis blog listed this as an alternative path. I did not pursue it because the response to (u) via "the ementa-votos comparison constitutively performs multi-voto reading" is both correct and more parsimonious. Reframing Component 2 would be a narrowing concession that loses ground gained in r33.

Conceding (r) on the autonomous-support reading while maintaining the within-voto-expressed-conditionality response. R34's (r) press targets both responses with the same residual (cross-voto aggregation of G2's autonomous support). If the ementa-votos comparison identity answers this residual for the within-voto response, it answers it for the autonomous-support reading as well, since both require the same multi-voto reading of the G2 votos. Partial concession here would split what is actually a unified response.

## Assessment after r35

The three sub-questions (p/s, q/t, r/u) remain open in the following states:

**(p/s):** The debate is now at the structural-separability level: does the ratio-designation's epistemic process generate reasoning content structurally independent of the votos and the designation? R36 would need to establish that it does — not merely that the relator performs epistemic steps, which both sides accept, but that those steps produce content beyond the votos + designation combination. This is the sharpest remaining question on (p).

**(q/t):** The debate is now at the justificatory-form level: does identifying which element received autonomous collective support constitute normative evaluation (a legal superiority judgment) rather than procedural aggregation? R36 would need to show that the designation act requires normative judgment about which element *should* be the ratio — not merely aggregation of which element the votos support. If the relator simply reads the votos and identifies which element has autonomous support, the judgment is aggregative; if the relator chooses among contested elements on normative grounds, it is evaluative. Whether the conditionality case class forces normative evaluation (because autonomous support itself is a normatively loaded criterion) is the live question.

**(r/u):** The synthesis blog called (u) the most closable, and r35 engages it directly. The remaining question is whether multi-voto reading in the conditionality case class generates content beyond what the individual votos supply — specifically, a claim about why two unconditional G2 votos together constitute sufficient autonomous support that is not derivable from reading each voto. This is a narrower claim than R34 made, and r36 would need to show it specifically.
