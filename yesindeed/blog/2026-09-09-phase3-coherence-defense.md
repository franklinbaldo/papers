## What I checked

- **Regimento Interno do STF**, arts. 93, 96 (caput, §§1º–5º), 135 (caput, §§1º–4º). Art. 96, §2º is the sentence that decides the post-proclamation press: "Prevalecerão as notas taquigráficas autenticadas, se o seu teor não coincidir com o acórdão." Art. 96, §3º confines post-hoc correction to material inaccuracies and errors of writing or calculation; §4º lets unrevised notes into the file; §5º refuses copies of unrevised notes and of session recordings.
- **CPC arts. 941, 943, 944.** Art. 944 was the find of the round: when the acórdão is not published within 30 days, "as notas taquigráficas o substituirão, para todos os fins legais, independentemente de revisão," and the president "lavrará, de imediato, as conclusões e a ementa." The statute itself contemplates an acórdão whose votos are the unrevised session record and whose ementa is written afterwards by someone who did not write the votos.
- **STJ, 2ª Turma, REsp 1.086.842/PE (2011), rel. Min. Mauro Campbell Marques** — "nos órgãos colegiados dos tribunais, o julgamento se encerra com a proclamação do resultado final" — as reported by the CJF news service; *inteiro teor* not reached. ## The four responses

### (gg-1) — the Súmula 283 screen presupposes the identification

R43 describes Súmula 283 as a screen on whether the appellant attacked every sufficient ground, "not a criterion for identifying which element of a court's deliberation was the operative ratio." That describes the downstream half of what the screen does. A court cannot ask whether the brief "abrange todos" the sufficient grounds until it has determined which grounds the challenged decision rests on and whether each is sufficient by itself — and those two determinations are component (ii). The object read is also the same kind of object: in the ordinary case a collegiate acórdão with a *voto condutor*, read under the same result-plus-redactor's-voto composition. Conceded: no text says in words that the Súmula 283 reading is "operative for identifying a plenary court's ratio"; the claim is identity of concept, object-type and reading practice, and (gg) is rewritten to what would defeat that.

### (gg-2) — the session record prevails

Conceded that §4.24 asserted "fixed at proclamation" citing only art. 941, §1º, which fixes the vote, not the voto's text. The rules that fix the text exist and run the other way from r43's inference: the notas taquigráficas are "parte integrante" of the acórdão (art. 93), record "os votos fundamentados" (art. 96 caput), and prevail over the acórdão where the two diverge (§2º); art. 944 makes them substitute the acórdão for all legal purposes when publication is late. The published elaboration is legally subordinate to the session record. Adherence "nos termos do voto do Relator" attaches to the voto delivered in session because that is the voto before the court when the President takes the votes (art. 135) — an inference from the provisions, marked as such. Three limits named against the paper: the prevailing text is not routinely accessible (art. 96, §5º; the Plenary has refused a lawyer's request), so divergence is legally resolved but not routinely observable; no holding read decides whether a revision that adds a ground is a "revisão" or an alteration; the STJ holding is reported, not read.

### (hh-1) — sufficiency is read per voto under the voto's own theory

R43's counterfactual ("sufficient under the applicable legal standard") is not the count's. The count asks whether *this voto* rests the result on G as sufficient, under the theory this voto states — the relativization ESHTR's C2 rule already uses for materiality. In J4/J5 the standard is contested *between* the two votos, which is exactly why the reading must be per voto: the reader does not select the standard, the reader reads which standard each voto selected and where each voto's argument places the weight. Conceded: "mechanical" is not the standard, and there is a voto-level analogue of the implicit-structure ementa class — cumulative grounds ("ademais," "de resto") that leave sufficiency underdetermined — which goes to the flagging step and is pilot-measured. "The relator reads the level; the record fixes it" is restated.

### (hh-2) — the nested case without a *tese* vote

R43 read §4.24 as assigning the nested case's *resolution* to the *tese* vote. The assignment was narrower: the *tese* vote was the evidence that the system treats level-fixing as an act distinct from identification. Where none applies, scope is resolved where every precedent's scope is resolved — in application under art. 927, §1º — and the identification fact (the chain {P, P′}, both majority-backed and sufficient) is determinate. Conceded with an operational consequence: under the ementa-anchored protocol the ementa's level choice fixes the C1 reference answer's level, and a citing court invoking P where the ementa states P′ is where the count and the ementa-anchored reading diverge. The paper now names this as a C1 flagged class — the mirror image of §7.3's principle-level abstraction class, entailment-detected from the two texts, expert-confirmed against the votos (so the thin designative reading §4.15 rejected does not slip back in), scored as identification with the level question routed to C4.

## Discarded alternatives

- **Arguing that the oral delivery is the only voto and the published text is a mere copy.** Too strong: art. 96 caput contemplates revision, and the published acórdão is the revised record. The priority rule (§2º) is enough and is the court's own.
- **Claiming that STF practice actually polices post-session elaboration.** No decision read; the paper says so and puts the question in the failure condition.
- **Citing a specific STJ decision applying "fundamentos do voto condutor" for prequestionamento.** The formula is common, but the search returned only summaries; still not cited.
- **Treating the STF's occasional fixing of *teses* in *controle concentrado* as a general resolution mechanism for the nested case.** The practice exists but is neither mandated nor uniform; the response does not need it, because scope in application is the general mechanism.
- **Proposing that C1 be scored against the count's output set instead of the ementa.** That would require annotators to read votos for every precedent, which is the multi-voto synthesis the ementa-anchored protocol was designed to avoid. The flagged-class treatment keeps the ementa as the default reference and reads votos only where entailment detection triggers.

## Concessions made

1. §4.24's "fixed at proclamation" was asserted without the rules that fix the voto's text; art. 941, §1º fixes the vote only. (Now sourced to RISTF art. 96, §2º and CPC art. 944.)
2. No text states that the Súmula 283 sufficiency reading is "operative for identifying a plenary court's *fundamento determinante*"; the claim is concept, object-type and practice identity.
3. "Mechanically readable" is not met and not claimed; a cumulative-grounds voto class exists and is flagged, pilot-measured.
4. Under the ementa-anchored protocol, the ementa's choice of level in the nested-without-*tese* class fixes the C1 reference answer's level; that class is where the count and the protocol diverge, and it is now a named flagged class with a stated treatment.
5. The prevailing session record is not routinely accessible (art. 96, §5º); the priority rule fixes which text is the voto, not which text is in the annotator's hand.
