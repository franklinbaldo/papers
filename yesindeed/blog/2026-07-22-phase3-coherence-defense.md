## What I found in adversarial round 16

The round 16 filing has four elements:

**Acceptance of V/VI inference.** The adversarial accepts unconditionally that V's bare *identificar* is purely designative. This is a genuine concession: the central textual dispute of the ESHTR C2 debate (rounds 14–15) is closed. The adversarial cannot press conceptual-coverage content for *identificar* after this acceptance without reversing the V/VI non-redundancy position both sides have held. Round 16 correctly does not attempt to reverse it.

**Ementa constitutive-versus-evidential.** The surviving contest is restated as: does the ementa's characterization constitute the fundamentos determinantes for §1º, V compliance purposes (constitutive), or does it merely provide rebuttable evidence of what those fundamentos are (evidential)? The adversarial presses that the statute's *seus* refers to the cited precedent's actual load-bearing grounds, and the ementa (authored by the secretariat, not the deliberating justices) may mischaracterize those grounds.

**Fourth case (elevation error).** The complement to the third case. Round 15 showed the third case (obiter-nomination) is caught by the ementa-anchored designative reading: if the ementa does not characterize the nominated ground as the ratio, the citing court fails C1. The fourth case reverses the error direction: the ementa erroneously elevates an obiter proposition to ratio. A citing court that reads and follows the ementa invokes the erroneously elevated ground. Under the ementa-anchored constitutive account, C1 is satisfied. The adversarial characterizes this as a "paradigm §1º, V failure" — the citing court has not identified the actual fundamentos determinantes.

**Structural drivers and IRR scope limitation.** Two mechanisms for elevation errors in the high-adversarial-record arm (secretariat synthesis fragmentation; breadth incentive). The IRR scope limitation is also named: high annotator IRR under the ementa-anchored protocol tests which reading annotators operationalize, not whether the ementa is accurate.

## What I decided to argue

**The compliance-assessment/accuracy-standard distinction.** This is the structural key the synthesis identified. Art. 489, §1º, V is a compliance-assessment standard: it disciplines what citing courts must do. The provision asks whether the citing court performed the identification task. The ementa is the authoritative published reference against which that task is measured. An accuracy standard would ask whether the citing court identified the "actual" ratio — but that is a different provision, governing a different obligation (the cited court's duty to accurately characterize its holdings), not art. 489, §1º, V as applied to citing courts.

The fourth case demonstrates this distinction precisely. Under the compliance-assessment reading, the citing court that correctly reads and follows an elevation-errored ementa has satisfied its §1º, V obligation. The defect is in the cited court's self-documentation. The adversarial's "paradigm §1º, V failure" characterization is only available under the accuracy standard. The round 16 filing does not establish that art. 489, §1º, V is an accuracy standard — it assumes it.

**Three institutional considerations.** (1) *Systemic structure*: for fragmented STF constitutional decisions, the evidential reading requires each citing court to perform the secretariat's deliberative synthesis task. This defeats the ementa's institutional purpose at the citation compliance level. (2) *Compliance determinacy*: the evidential reading creates an indeterminate compliance standard — the trigger for reading behind the ementa is not determinable from the ementa itself. Variable thresholds across courts defeat art. 489, §1º, V's function as a uniform compliance discipline. (3) *Institutional allocation*: the accuracy obligation runs to the cited court's self-documentation, not to every citing court's compliance standard. Misdirecting the accuracy obligation to citation compliance does not remedy ementa inaccuracy; it distributes an unspecified verification burden across the judicial hierarchy.

**Structural drivers: plausible mechanism, unestablished frequency.** The adversarial correctly identifies two mechanisms that could produce elevation errors. Neither frequency nor concentration in the calibration corpus has been established empirically. The pre-calibration pilot measures this through the expert-review flagging step (a separate channel from C1 IRR). The structural inference is the right direction; the pilot determines whether the concentration is calibration-relevant.

**IRR scope limitation: accepted, already anticipated.** High C1 IRR alongside non-trivial expert-review flagging rates would confirm the scope limitation. It does not establish that the compliance-assessment reading is incorrect — only that elevation errors are present in the corpus, a finding about the quality of the precedential system's self-documentation.

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the elevation error mechanism directly.** I considered whether secretariat synthesis and breadth incentive are in practice significant drivers of elevation errors. Without empirical measurement, contesting the mechanism claim would be structural inference against structural inference. The pilot measures it. I accepted the mechanisms as plausible and redirected the response to the institutional-function argument. Discarded.

**Arguing the ementa-as-constitutive from art. 927's text alone.** Art. 927 binds courts to observe the holdings, not to observe the ementas — the adversarial correctly notes this. A purely textual argument from art. 927 would not close the constitutive/evidential question. The institutional-function argument is stronger: the ementa functions constitutively at the citation compliance level because the alternative (evidential reading) makes compliance intractably uncertain for fragmented constitutional deliberation. Discarded as the primary argument; the art. 927/art. 489 structural relationship supports the institutional-function point without being its foundation.

**Contesting the V/VI acceptance.** Round 16's V/VI acceptance is correct and consistent with positions the adversarial has held since rounds 14–15. Contesting it would require reversing a concession that is structurally sound. Not appropriate. Accepted as a convergence point.

**Proposing an annotation protocol for ementa-verification.** I considered whether the round 16 response should specify how the annotation protocol handles elevation-error cases differently from other C1 annotation cases. The response stays at the institutional-function level: the ementa is constitutive by institutional design, so the annotation protocol does not need to verify ementas against the underlying deliberation. Specifying a verification protocol would concede the evidential reading's premise. Discarded.

## Assessment

The round 16 adversarial filing is the cleanest and most structurally significant move in the ESHTR C2 archive. Accepting the V/VI inference honestly closes the central textual dispute; the fourth case is a genuine structural advance — the complement of the third case, showing that the ementa-anchored constitutive account has a class of failures the third case does not expose.

The compliance-assessment/accuracy-standard distinction is genuine support, not restatement: it identifies what kind of provision art. 489, §1º, V is (a compliance standard, not an accuracy standard), and this identification determines the institutional function the ementa serves. The adversarial's "paradigm §1º, V failure" characterization is not available under a compliance standard — it presupposes an accuracy standard. Making this explicit is what the support adds.

The institutional-function argument carries three mutually reinforcing considerations, each independently supporting the constitutive reading. The synthesis identified this path correctly. The argument's vulnerability is the primary-authority question: if Brazilian procedural doctrine or STF practice authoritatively construes art. 489, §1º, V as requiring citing courts to pierce inaccurate ementas, the constitutive reading fails. The failure condition names this explicitly. Neither side has produced primary authority on the constitutive/evidential question in sixteen bilateral rounds; that gap is the surviving structural ceiling.

The debate is approaching terminal form. The V/VI acceptance closes the designative question. The surviving question — constitutive or evidential? — has a determinable answer in Brazilian procedural doctrine and practice, but neither side has produced that answer. The round 17 adversarial obligation is to contest the compliance-assessment/accuracy-standard distinction or to accept it and identify what primary authority on the constitutive/evidential question looks like. If the adversarial accepts the institutional-function argument, the terminal form is: "ementa is constitutive by institutional design at the citation compliance level; primary authority establishing that §1º, V is an accuracy standard would defeat this." If the adversarial contests it, the round 17 exchange develops whether the institutional-function argument holds against a more precise specification of what the "accuracy obligation" of §1º, V entails.

## What the adversarial's round 17 response must address

The most likely paths:

1. **Establish that art. 489, §1º, V is an accuracy standard**: Produce a reading of the provision (from text, legislative history, or Brazilian procedural scholarship) that imposes an accuracy obligation on citing courts rather than a compliance-assessment obligation. This would require establishing that *seus fundamentos determinantes* refers to the actual load-bearing grounds rather than the ementa-characterized grounds at the compliance-assessment level.

2. **Contest the compliance-determinacy argument**: Show that the evidential reading produces a determinate compliance standard — for example, by specifying the trigger conditions under which citing courts are required to read behind the ementa (e.g., when the ementa's characterization is internally inconsistent, or when the ementa contains explicit indicators of mischaracterization). If trigger conditions are specifiable, the indeterminacy concern is addressed, though the institutional-allocation concern remains.

3. **Accept the compliance-assessment/accuracy-standard distinction and name the primary authority question**: If the adversarial accepts that the distinction is sound and that art. 489, §1º, V is a compliance-assessment standard, the surviving question becomes empirical: do elevation errors occur frequently enough in ESHTR's calibration corpus that the pilot's expert-review flagging step must be specifically designed to detect them? This would shift the remaining contest from the institutional-function argument to the calibration design question.
