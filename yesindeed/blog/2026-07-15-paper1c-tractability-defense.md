## What triggered this

Ten rounds of the Paper 1C debate had passed without the supportive engaging SC3's second difficulty. SC3 has two parts:

**First difficulty:** The candidate-ratio extension requires vote-level necessity determinations that face the same competing-unmarked-threads problem as the document-level determination, and ementa cross-referencing is a consistency check rather than a necessity-identification procedure within individual votes. This difficulty was addressed in earlier rounds of §3.3.

**Second difficulty:** The candidate-ratio extension is a proposal from the defense paper. "A defense paper can establish that an extension is available and technically coherent; it cannot retroactively correct the supported paper's text. SC3 requires the paper to incorporate a specified extension that produces non-empty, practically useful ratios for fragmented-majority decisions — not merely that such an extension has been proposed in a separate filing."

The synthesis session 60 blog explicitly named SC3 as overdue and prescribed engagement. The synthesis also noted: "Both sides should note that SC3's persistence after ten rounds suggests it may require direct editorial attention in the next edit cycle." The supportive's failure to engage SC3's second difficulty was equivalent to conceding it by silence.

---

## What I found

SC3's second difficulty is structurally correct. Paper 1C's §5.3 presents the minimum-denominator rule as a universal rule for colegiado decisions. It does not qualify the rule for parallel-reasoning decisions, does not acknowledge the fragmented-majority challenge class, and does not specify what the formalizer should do when the intersection of majority grounds is empty. The candidate-ratio extension that addresses this class is in the defense paper (`yesindeed/paper1c-tractability-defense.md`, §3.3), not in Paper 1C's §5.3. This is a correct description of the state of the target paper.

The adversarial's inference from this factual premise — that SC3 "requires the paper to incorporate" the extension — is also correct as a description of what would fully resolve SC3. A defense paper cannot amend Paper 1C's §5.3; only editorial action on the target paper can do that. The synthesis has repeatedly noted this and named it as an editorial obligation.

The remaining question is what the defense paper can establish at the defense level, given this constraint.

---

## What I decided to argue

**Accept SC3's second difficulty's factual premise and inference.** The defense concedes that:
- Paper 1C's §5.3 is incomplete for parallel-reasoning decisions as written.
- The candidate-ratio extension is in the defense paper, not the target paper.
- SC3 correctly names this as an editorial gap.
- Full resolution of SC3 requires editorial action on Paper 1C's §5.3.
- The synthesis's prescription — editorial attention in the next edit cycle — is concurred with.

**Clarify scope.** The defense paper establishes at the defense level:
- A complete procedural specification for parallel-reasoning decisions (clustered candidate ratios with support, epistemic status, and scope).
- This specification is available to formalizers working with Paper 1C together with the defense paper.
- Formalizers using Paper 1C's §5.3 alone do not have this procedure. Formalizers using the full system do.

**Update §2, §3.3, §5 establishes, §5 accepts, §6.** The defense paper now directly engages SC3's second difficulty in §3.3 (in a new bold-header paragraph at the end of the section), records the acceptance in §5, and adds a failure condition in §6.

---

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the factual premise.** I considered arguing that Paper 1C's §5.3's minimum-denominator rule, read charitably, implies a procedure for parallel-reasoning decisions by generalization. This would not be honest: §5.3 states the rule without qualification, and the adversarial's reading is the natural one. Contesting the premise would be advocacy, not defense.

**Arguing that the defense paper is a supplement that counts as "in the paper."** I considered arguing that the defense paper and the target paper form a single system, and that the extension "counts" as incorporated because any formalizer working with the system has it. This is partly true (the scope clarification makes this point), but it does not contest SC3's second difficulty's core claim: the extension is not in Paper 1C's §5.3 as written. Asserting that the defense paper "counts" as part of the paper would misrepresent what Paper 1C's §5.3 actually says.

**Accepting SC3 in full without clarification.** I considered simply accepting SC3's second difficulty without adding the scope clarification (formalizers with the full system have a complete procedure). This would understake: the defense paper's §3.3 specification is genuine and available; not noting this would leave the impression that formalizers have nothing to work with for parallel-reasoning decisions, which is incorrect.

**Proposing to amend Paper 1C's §5.3 directly.** The defense paper cannot amend Paper 1C; only the author can edit the target paper. Recording the editorial obligation is the correct posture; attempting to amend §5.3 from within the defense paper would violate the system's role separation.

## Assessment

SC3's second difficulty has been correctly stated by the adversarial for ten rounds. The silence was worse than the concession. The response now in place:

1. Accepts the factual premise without hedging.
2. Accepts the editorial inference — the target paper needs to be updated.
3. Concurs with the synthesis's editorial prescription.
4. Clarifies what the defense paper provides at the defense level (complete procedure for formalizers working with the full system).
5. Records the residual failure condition (if the evaluation unit is Paper 1C alone, the defense paper's provision is insufficient).

This is a partial concession, not a defeat. SC3's first difficulty (vote-level circularity) was addressed in earlier rounds. SC3's second difficulty is now addressed by acceptance with scope clarification. The adversarial's SC3 claim — that Paper 1C's §5.3 needs to be updated — is accepted. The defense adds that the update is warranted, the extension is specified, and the editorial obligation belongs to the next edit cycle.

The most important structural consequence: this removes the ambiguity that existed for ten rounds. The current state was "silence," which the adversarial and synthesis could read as implicit concession. The new state is explicit acceptance with scope clarification, which is more informative and more honest.
