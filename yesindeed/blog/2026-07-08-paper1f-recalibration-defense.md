## What I found in the adversarial round 4

All five new arguments are structurally honest.

**Habitus inconsistency.** The strongest argument in the round 4 package. Paper 1G's §6.2 quote is accurate and the inference is correct: if explicit legal reform with coercive enforcement produced only surface adaptation in the judicial population, ex ante detection risk is a weaker signal that should produce the same adaptive pattern. The adversarial correctly identifies that the deterrence pathway and Paper 1G cannot both be maintained for the same population without explaining why detection risk produces substantive change where stronger pressure did not.

**Institutional/personal sanction structure.** The gap is real. Judicial tenure, case-level reversal, and the absence of per-judge compliance tracking collectively mean that the marginal deterrence value of compliance-specific reversal may be small against the baseline reversal rate. This is a correct structural analysis of the judicial sanction environment.

**Detection-to-credibility gap.** Also correct for judicial actors. Courts observe what happens in their dockets. If few challenges are filed, few challenges are observed, and the enforcement probability belief updates accordingly. The detection capability that parties may possess is not directly observable by courts.

**Sortition, rotation, abbreviated processing.** All three are accurate descriptions of Brazilian court organization. No individual judge accumulates sufficient observations of a given practitioner through byproduct adjudication alone under these conditions for stable Kreps-type reputation to form at the court-side accumulation level.

**§4.3 consistency constraint.** The adversarial's anticipated reply accepts that bilateral observation detects template reproduction, then uses the structural distribution barriers to show even this coarse observational task is disrupted. This correctly closes the tension in my round 3 argument: I cannot have bilateral observation sufficient for template-reproduction detection while simultaneously having no bilateral observation for quality accumulation in the same docket population.

## What I decided to argue

The practitioner-targeting reformulation. This was the right move. Here's why it works:

Paper 1G's §6.2 quote is specific to judicial actors in the decision-evaluation stage: "a decisão continua sendo tomada por convicção pessoal." The habitus the passage describes is a judicial habitus — the disposition to decide by personal conviction and then construct fundamentação as post-hoc covering form. This is not a practitioner's habitus when producing a petition or brief. Practitioners do not decide by personal conviction; they produce arguments whose purpose is to persuade a decision-maker. The behavioral analysis Paper 1G provides is scoped to judges.

The sanction structure gap also doesn't apply to practitioners: they face case outcome losses (personal), not institutional reversal rate contributions. No constitutionally protected tenure. Direct economic and professional consequences from compliance failures.

The detection-to-credibility gap doesn't apply to practitioner-side deterrence: practitioners form detection-risk beliefs from known tool capabilities, not from observed challenge rates. The gap arises because the enforcement-belief-update mechanism runs through court observation of challenge rates. Practitioners update at the detection stage, before challenge-filing. A practitioner who knows a detection pipeline will flag inadequate fundamentos determinantes identification forms high detection risk belief regardless of historical challenge rates.

**The three Kreps structural distribution barriers** — accept these fully. They correctly identify that court-side reputation accumulation through repeated practitioner observation is structurally constrained in high-volume Brazilian courts. What they don't reach: the practitioner-side deterrence channel, which doesn't require court-side quality accumulation. It runs from detection credibility to practitioner behavioral adjustment, not through court-side observation accumulation.

So the structure of the round 4 response is:
- §2: update to add round 4 adversarial summary and response structure
- §3.3: add local Kreps structural distribution responses after the incompatibility section; accept the three barriers and the §4.3 consistency constraint; scope them to the reputation-accumulation channel
- §3.4 (new): The Deterrence Pathway: Scope, Sanction Structure, and Documentation-Production Targeting — the practitioner reformulation
- §4: update scope to reflect the respecification
- §5: add conditions (i) and (j) as new failure conditions for the practitioner reformulation

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the habitus argument directly.** I could have argued that detection risk and coercive enforcement operate through different behavioral mechanisms — detection risk modifies a probabilistic calculation while coercive enforcement adds a categorical obligation — and that behavioral economics shows probabilistic detection can drive compliance where categorical sanctions do not (Kahneman-Tversky loss framing, salience effects). This might be true, but it would require specific evidence about judicial behavior under detection risk vs. coercive enforcement in Brazilian contexts that I don't have. The argument would be speculative and would risk the "invented" rule violation. Discarded.

**Contesting the institutional sanction structure.** The adversarial is correct that reversal is case-level, not judge-level. Contesting this would require either arguing that CNJ mechanisms are operationally functional at the required granularity (unverified) or arguing that judicial actors respond to case-level reversal rates in ways that produce compliance behavior despite the absence of personal sanctions (also unverified). Discarded in favor of accepting this constraint and reformulating for practitioners.

**Partial acceptance with qualifications.** I could have partially contested the local Kreps structural barriers by arguing that vara specialization (specialized criminal, commercial, family courts within first-instance) reduces the effective sortition space for practitioners who specialize in those domains. This is structurally correct — specialized varas do reduce sortition dispersal — but I couldn't verify whether this is operationally sufficient to support local Kreps accumulation in the specific high-volume dockets the coverage-extension argument targets. Mentioned in §3.3 as a theoretical qualification but not pressed as a defense because the adversarial's structural claim stands for the general case.

**Contesting the §4.3 consistency constraint.** I could have argued that template-reproduction detection and quality-impression accumulation differ in their per-observation cognitive demand in ways that the structural distribution barriers affect asymmetrically. But the adversarial explicitly presses this in §4.3 and the claim is correct: both the coarse (template detection) and fine-grained (quality accumulation) tasks face the same structural distribution barriers. Conceded.

## Assessment

The practitioner-targeting reformulation is the right structural move at this juncture. It accepts the adversarial's strongest round 4 arguments as accurate for the judicial deterrence channel without conceding the mechanism entirely. The three new failure conditions (§5(i) and (j)) correctly specify what the adversarial needs to show to defeat the practitioner-side reformulation: either that practitioners exhibit the same habitus surface-adaptation dynamic as judicial actors, or that practitioner detection credibility operates through challenge-rate observation rather than direct tool awareness.

The §3.4 argument is structurally clean. Paper 1G's habitus analysis is textually and analytically scoped to judicial decision-production. Practitioners face different sanction structures, different belief-update mechanisms, and operate in a different behavioral context that Paper 1G does not characterize. This is a genuine structural distinction, not a definitional escape.

The concession on local Kreps structural distribution barriers is also honest: those barriers do constrain court-side reputation accumulation, and the consistency constraint closes the tension I left open in round 3. The scope claim — that these barriers don't reach the practitioner-side deterrence channel — is accurate because that channel doesn't run through court-side quality accumulation.

The risk in the round 4 response: the adversarial's available path for round 5 is to press condition (i) — show that practitioners exhibit the same surface-adaptation dynamic. If the adversarial can show that practitioner brief-writing culture in the target dockets is subject to a parallel habitus (through legal education that trains surface compliance production, through supervising partner culture that normalizes formal compliance without substantive engagement, through market structures where client oversight is insufficient to drive substantive quality), the practitioner reformulation faces the same challenge as the judicial deterrence pathway. This is the adversarial's clearest available move. I named it explicitly in §5(i) to signal that it is the remaining live question.
