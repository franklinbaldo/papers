## What changed in the position

### Against Attack A — the structural argument does not survive the text

R47 inferred from the "não revistos" flag that revision between lodging and release is the expected course in the virtual plenary. The provisions that create the flag (art. 96, §§1º–3º; art. 95, §2º) are about the Secretaria's transcription of an oral session — votos *lidos*, *apartes*, *texto transcrito* — and "revisão" there is the justice's review of the transcript of what the justice read. A virtual session has no audio, no reading and no transcript; the votos are lodged as text and are public as lodged (art. 1º, §2º), so the hypothesis of art. 96, §3º (a voto read but not released in writing) cannot occur for a lodged voto. R47's second inference — the limiting case shows the ementa depends on the released text — fails for the same reason and because in a virtual session the relator lodges the ementa at the opening.

What fixes the lodged text after closure, provision by provision: no release step exists for a virtual-session voto; the only modification provision is art. 5º, intra-session and by exception; the court classifies a lodged voto as a *voto proferido* under RISTF art. 134, §1º and CPC art. 941, §1º (ADI 5.399-QO), and art. 941, §1º fixes the vote at proclamation, which in the virtual session is the automatic computation at closure. Conceded: art. 941, §1º fixes the *vote*, not in terms the *text*; the vote/text distinction survives at the level of terms. But the text is public during the session and, per the STF's 2022 update, after it — so a divergence between lodged and acórdão texts is a divergence between two public documents, and condition (i) becomes a pilot rate rather than a missing provision. A post-closure edit could not in any case re-attach the adherents' votes to a ground they did not adhere to (art. 135; §4.26's transfer step).

### The correction that strengthens requirement (1)

§4.26's component (3) — voto release sequenced before ementa drafting — is wrong for the virtual arm where the relator prevails: art. 2º, §1º has the relator lodge "ementa, relatório e voto" before anyone else votes. Conceded. The consequence runs the other way from what "the ementa comes first" might suggest: in that arm the ementa is inside the object of adherence — "acompanho o Relator" is cast on a package that contains it; *ressalva* and divergence are declared as text. The designation is not a post-deliberative reading of the votos; it is a proposed designation the majority's votes adopt or not, and an elevation error would require adherents without *ressalva* to adhere to a designation their own voto (the relator's) does not carry, which is impossible by construction. Requirement (1) is restated for every arm: the ground-fact is fixed by votes cast either *on* the designation (virtual, relator prevails) or *before* it (physical; virtual with divergence prevailing); in no arm does the drafting of the ementa fix the majority ground.

### Against Attack B — conceded as stated, corrected in three particulars

The composition is a claim about rules, not a measured corpus property; the three quantities are pilot quantities and were never asserted otherwise. Corrections: the physical-session residual class is checkable against the public session broadcast, not a sealed audio record; the virtual-arm revision rate is checkable against retained lodged texts (or by capture during the session); the calibration arm's virtual share is a mechanical sort on the acórdão's session field, and r47's own premise that high-adversarial-record cases are physical-session cases is the same unmeasured assumption that sort settles.

### Against (hh) — the P4-only class, held open and narrowed

R47's hold is accepted. Added: the norm a P4-only voto fails to restate is the norm of the precedent it applies, whose ementa is the object C1 reads anyway; the flag is mechanical and the fill is a lookup; expert review is needed only where that ementa is itself implicit-structure or principle-level. The (hh) threshold now applies to the lookup-failure subset. In the virtual-relator-prevails arm the class arises only from votos actually written.

## Discarded alternatives

- **Answering Attack A by asserting that lodged votos are never revised.** No provision says so and no practice was checked; the honest position is that no provision contemplates a post-closure text, the only modification provision is intra-session, the court treats the lodged voto as cast, and the rest is measurable. Said so.
- **Treating the ementa-lodged-first finding as a problem to be minimized.** It falsifies §4.26's component (3) for one arm and had to be conceded; but read for what it does to adherence it is the strongest fixing of the ground-fact in the exchange, and the paper says both things.

## Concessions made

- **Vote/text distinction:** CPC art. 941, §1º and ADI 5.399-QO fix the *vote*; no provision in terms forbids editing a lodged voto's *text* after closure. The distinction survives at the level of terms; what it cannot do is move the majority ground, and whether it occurs is a public-record measurement.
- **Component (3) corrected:** the voto-before-ementa sequence holds for the physical session and for the virtual session with a divergence prevailing, not for the virtual session where the relator prevails.
- **Measurement gap:** conceded as stated — the composition is not a measured corpus property and the three quantities are unmeasured.
- **Art. 95, §1º's publication clock** applies to virtual sessions too.
- **(hh) P4-only class:** held open at r47's request; the narrowing to the lookup-failure subset is an addition, not a rebuttal.

Requirement (1) after round 48: the ementa states a proposition that was majority-backed in the votos, fixed by votes cast on or before the designation; narrowed by the physical-session residual class, by the virtual-arm revision rate if the pilot finds one, and by nothing else.
