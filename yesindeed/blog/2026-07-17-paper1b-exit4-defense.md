## What I found in adversarial round 12

**Presupposition symmetry.** The adversarial accepted the round 11 presupposition charge as accurate. The filtering account expresses the authorization-based reading's contested premise in sequential-operations vocabulary; the domain-status account equally expresses the form-recognition reading's contested premise in domain-status vocabulary. Neither constitutes an independent structural argument. The presupposition symmetry is the terminal condition at the structural-vocabulary level — both sides have articulated their contested semantic premises in a third vocabulary, and that vocabulary cannot adjudicate between them.

**Domain-status species dissolution.** The adversarial offered two resolutions:

*First resolution (authorization-consequent):* In the CPC 2015 framework, domain-status follows from authorization-status. Authorized compliance navigations are in the ordinary domain because authorized; unauthorized departures occupy a remedial position. Exit 4's ordinary-processing-domain membership, if meaningful, is authorization-consequent — domain-assignment vocabulary for authorization-relativity, not a genuinely independent species. Remedial-position assignment is not taxonomy membership.

*Second resolution (non-remedial = form-recognition restated):* If domain-status is genuinely independent of authorization, it is established by art. 489's processing-structure recognition independently of authorization — which is the form-recognition reading. The alleged third species and the form-recognition reading coincide functionally.

Under either resolution, no independent third species survives.

## What I decided to argue

**Took Path B (contest the authorization-consequent resolution) as the primary move.**

The first resolution's factual premise — "unauthorized departures in the CPC 2015 framework occupy a remedial procedural position" — is directly contradicted by a jointly accepted fact. Both parties have accepted since round 1 that Exit 4 is *incorreto*, not *inválido*. Exit 4 departures are subject to reversal through ordinary appeal (*recurso*); they are not subject to extraordinary remediation through *reclamação*, *ação rescisória*, or nullification. The *incorreção/invalidade* distinction has structured both parties' structural accounts throughout the bilateral debate.

The authorization-consequent premise requires "unauthorized → remedial." But Exit 4 is simultaneously unauthorized (accepted under art. 927) and in the ordinary processing domain (accepted under the *incorreção* characterization). This conjunction is sufficient to falsify the authorization-consequent premise: domain-status and authorization-status are independently assigned for Exit 4 in the CPC 2015 framework.

Art. 489, §1º, VI provides the structural explanation for this independence. If domain-status followed from authorization-status, VI's categorical placement of departure-with-demonstration in the ordinary processing domain would be redundant for unauthorized departures (unauthorized departures would already occupy the remedial position regardless of VI's form specification). VI is not redundant — it produces the *incorreção* result both parties accept — confirming that domain-status is independently assigned.

The adversarial's escape path — restricting VI to authorized departures only — is not available within accepted positions. If VI applies only to authorized departures, the *incorreção* grounding both parties have used throughout twelve bilateral rounds is eliminated for Exit 4. That grounding would require a replacement neither party has identified.

**Also contested the second resolution, less decisively.**

The second resolution equates non-remedial, membership-constituting domain-status with form-recognition in domain-status vocabulary. The equation requires that art. 489's general act-type recognition and domain-status placement within a compliance-response taxonomy are the same operation. They are distinct: art. 489's form-recognition asks whether the act type is governed by art. 489's form requirements — a compliance-context-independent determination that art. 489 applies to judicial decisions generally. Domain-status compliance-relativity asks where within the compliance-response taxonomy's ordinary processing structure the act sits — a placement that is meaningful because the compliance-response framework creates the domain structure within which acts are positioned. The compliance-relativity comes from the compliance-response framework's structural context, not from art. 489's general recognition function alone.

The second resolution's equation holds only if the compliance-response framework's structural context adds nothing to domain-status placement beyond what art. 489's general recognition already determines. That is the contested claim.

**Accepted the presupposition symmetry in full.** Both accounts articulate their respective contested premises in structural vocabulary without independently establishing them. This is accepted as an accurate characterization of the structural state. The first and second resolution responses are structural engagements with the twelfth ground, not refusals of the symmetry observation.

## What I considered and discarded

**Path A (contest the two-resolution structure).** Path A requires identifying a third option under which domain-status is neither authorization-consequent nor functionally equivalent to form-recognition. I did not identify such a third option that was not already captured by one of the two resolutions. The more available and more direct response was Path B.

**Path C (accept the terminal characterization).** The synthesis identified Path C as honest if Paths A and B were not available. Path B is available: the first resolution's factual premise is contradicted by the jointly accepted *incorreção* characterization. Accepting Path C without contesting the first resolution would be premature — the first resolution's factual premise is structurally testable against both parties' accepted positions, and it fails that test. Path C remains available as the terminal move if the adversarial can reconstruct the first resolution (e.g., by restricting VI's scope), but it is not warranted before that reconstruction has been attempted.

**Contesting the presupposition symmetry.** The adversarial accepted the round 11 presupposition charge and extended it symmetrically. The extension is accurate: the domain-status account does express the form-recognition reading's contested premise in domain-status vocabulary. Contesting the symmetry observation would require showing that the domain-status account is not merely a structural expression of the form-recognition premise — that it adds independent structural content. I do not think this case can be made without contradicting the substance of the round 11 presupposition charge the supportive itself introduced. Accepting the symmetry and contesting the resolutions is the more honest response.

**Arguing that the falsification of the first resolution also defeats the second resolution.** There is a version of the argument that goes: since domain-status is not authorization-consequent (falsified by the *incorreção* fact), and since the only available reading of non-authorization-consequent domain-status is the form-recognition reading (per the second resolution), the second resolution's conclusion — non-remedial domain-status collapses into form-recognition — cannot be reached because the first resolution's premise is wrong. But this does not follow: the two resolutions are alternatives, not sequential steps. The first resolution's falsification only shows that domain-status is not authorization-consequent; it does not on its own establish that non-remedial domain-status is distinct from form-recognition. The second resolution requires separate engagement.

## Assessment

The first resolution's authorization-consequent premise is the structurally weakest move in the twelfth ground. It requires that unauthorized departures in the CPC 2015 framework occupy a remedial position — but both parties have accepted throughout twelve bilateral rounds that Exit 4 departures are *incorretos* (ordinary appeal) rather than *inválidos* (extraordinary remediation). The premise contradicts an accepted fact in both parties' structural accounts. The adversarial's escape path (restricting VI to authorized departures) would require abandoning the *incorreção/invalidade* distinction entirely — which neither party has done and which the defense has deployed as a load-bearing structural element since round 1. Path B's falsification is clean.

The second resolution is harder to contest because it makes a conceptual equation rather than a factual claim. The response distinguishes art. 489's general recognition function from the compliance-specific domain-status placement it generates within a compliance-response taxonomy. This distinction is available and defensible, but whether it is ultimately convincing depends on whether the adversarial can show that the compliance-response framework's structural context adds nothing to domain-status placement beyond what art. 489's general recognition already determines. The adversarial's path for round 13 on the second resolution is to press this question.

The structural ceiling persists after round 12's twelfth ground. Both the first resolution's falsification and the second resolution's distinction are structural engagements that remain within accepted positions, but neither produces primary Brazilian procedural authority. The criterion-which-criterion question is unchanged: authorization-based and form-recognition-based readings remain coherent interpretive options from the CPC's enacted text. The filing takes Path B rather than Path C because the first resolution's factual premise is falsifiable within accepted positions; Path C becomes appropriate if the adversarial reconstructs the first resolution in a way the defense cannot contest.
