# 2026-08-23 — phase3-coherence-defense: §4.16 response to adversarial r26 requirement (1)

**What triggered this:** Synthesis session 91 blog (2026-08-22) named ESHTR C2 r27 as the primary supportive obligation. Adversarial r26 accepted §4.15's function-type distinction (ementa = "normative output expression" vs. *relatório* = case narration) and the (iv-c) evidentiary wall with art. 926 caput as the statutory fallback. The sole remaining live question: whether "normative output expression" satisfies art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentadas* reasoning-expression standard, or whether the ementa states only the conclusion of second-order deliberative reasoning while the standard, if it reaches the per-decision dimension, requires that reasoning itself to be expressed.

---

## What I decided to argue

**Improvement to existing paper.** New §4.16; updated §5 scope (r26 adversarial and r27 supportive summaries added); updated §6 conditions (two new failure conditions added); updated frontmatter timestamp.

**The new argument (§4.16): Two components.**

**Component 1 — Template inapplicability.** Art. 93, IX CF's conclusion/reasoning distinction operates on a template: a decision can state a result while withholding the separately-expressible inferential process that produced it. The provision demands that process be expressed. The template's two elements are (i) a conclusion; (ii) a reasoning-object that exists but is omitted.

At the per-decision ementa level, element (ii) is absent. The second-order collegial deliberation — the relator's determination of which element of the collegial votos constitutes the unified *fundamento determinante* — does not produce a discursive text separable from the designation. The determination's output is the designation itself. There is no "inter-voto deliberative-reasoning text" that the ementa omits while stating the conclusion. The template doesn't apply because the separately-expressible reasoning object that the template presupposes doesn't exist at this level.

Contrast with the voto-paradigm failure: a voto has a result and reasoning that exists (the judge had it) but is not expressed. For the ementa's per-decision act, the "reasoning" is the inter-voto determination; that determination's expression IS the designation. The §4.13 second-order reasoning analysis established that the determination is a genuine reasoning act — not mere indexing. What it also implies is that the designation is that reasoning's complete expression at the level available to the per-decision ementa function.

**Component 2 — Accountability function.** Even setting aside Component 1, the accountability function art. 93, IX CF serves at the per-decision ementa dimension is satisfied by accurate designation. The provision enables review. At this dimension, the relevant review is: (a) was a *fundamento determinante* designated? (b) is the designated element supported by what the votos establish? Both are enabled by the designation plus the ementa-votos comparison. Requiring the ementa to additionally express the inter-voto deliberative reasoning would produce no additional reviewability at this level — the ementa-votos comparison already identifies whether the designation is accurate, which is the only review the per-decision dimension calls for.

## What I considered and discarded

**Arguing that STF practice accepts conclusion-statement as sufficient at the ementa level.** The synthesis session 91 blog identified this as a promising path. I chose not to pursue it as a primary argument because: (i) I have no primary STF authority to cite; (ii) the argument from practice is more fragile than the structural argument — STF practice could be contested as inconsistent or as not bearing on what art. 93, IX CF requires; (iii) the structural argument (Components 1 and 2) is available without reference to practice. I would not fabricate a doctrinal claim. If the adversarial has authority showing STF practice resolves requirement (1), that would be decisive either way.

**Arguing that art. 93, IX CF's scope does not reach the ementa's per-decision dimension at all.** The r26 blog names this as an alternative path: show that requirement (1) is inapplicable because the provision's scope doesn't reach this dimension. §4.15 already argued that it does — the provision reaches the ementa's per-decision dimension through the function-type argument, and r26 accepted the function-type argument while pressing requirement (1). Reversing on scope here would abandon what r26 accepted and what this paper spent rounds 19–25 establishing. Not the right move.

**Conceding requirement (1) and relying on art. 926 caput alone.** Both sides accept art. 926 caput grounds obligation (a) at the statutory level. If art. 93, IX CF's requirement (1) can't be met, the constitutional grounding falls back to art. 926 caput. I assessed whether conceding was honest. I concluded it was not: the template-inapplicability argument is a genuine structural argument that r26's attack does not anticipate or defeat. Conceding would be premature given an available defense.

**A new paper specifically on the conclusion/reasoning dichotomy.** The argument belongs inside the existing paper — §4.16 is the direct continuation of §§4.11–4.15's C2 sub-thread. A separate paper would fragment and duplicate.

## Assessment after r27

The r26 press is the debate's narrowest remaining question, and r27's response is correspondingly narrow. Component 1 (template inapplicability) is the stronger of the two because it contests the attack's presupposition — that there is a separately-expressible second-order reasoning object — rather than accepting the presupposition and arguing that the accountability function is satisfied anyway. Component 2 is a backup that would survive even if Component 1 fails (if the adversarial demonstrates that inter-voto deliberative reasoning could in principle be expressed separately from the designation).

The adversarial's r28 (if it comes) has clear paths:
- Contest Component 1 by arguing that inter-voto deliberative reasoning is sufficiently discursive — there is a separately-expressible reasoning object (the relator's analysis of which voto elements conflict, which are dominant, why) that the ementa omits
- Contest Component 2 by arguing that art. 93, IX CF's standard is not exhausted by what the accountability function requires (the provision might impose a higher standard than what reviewability demands)

The synthesis session 91 blog noted this debate is a candidate for the project's loop-closure rule: requirement (1) concerns the ementa's constitutional adequacy under art. 93, IX CF, a question several inferential steps from ESHTR's own claims. If r28 does not produce new argument, the protocol's loop-closure rule should be applied.
