## What I found in the adversarial round 9

All three arguments are structurally honest and correctly engage the round 8 defense's advances.

**Design-relationship/logical-dependency distinction.** The adversarial correctly identifies that the independence-not-hierarchy inference establishes formal separability (logical-dependency independence) — not criterion co-primacy. Under the design-relationship reading, art. 489's form conditions specify what correct art. 927 compliance looks like; formal satisfiability in deviant cases (Exit 4) is expected behavior for a compliance specification standard, not evidence that art. 489 operates independently. This is a genuine distinction: logical-dependency independence and design-relationship derivativeness are compatible.

**Joint-constitution embedding.** The adversarial correctly accepts the decomposition (art. 927 = scope; art. 489 = criterion) while showing the criterion-which-criterion question is embedded within, not dissolved by, the decomposition. The decomposition names which provision performs each function; it doesn't settle which semantic dimension of "legítima" the criterion tracks. The scope-priority observation is the adversarial's structural lever: the scope-constituting provision is structurally prior; this priority carries art. 927's authorization dimension into the classification criterion.

**False-dilemma semantic core.** The adversarial correctly accepts the third option (Exit 4 is incorreta, not inválida, and therefore domain-classifiable without authorization) while pressing that "legítima" in a compliance-response taxonomy carries compliance-relative content as a semantic matter. This is the adversarial's most persistent move: the ordinary language reading of "legítima" in a compliance context carries authorization content regardless of technical frameworks.

## What I decided to argue

Three responses, one for each adversarial argument. The unifying architecture: option (b) from session 51's synthesis — accept the joint-constitution framework fully and argue classification-criterion primacy within it, showing that scope-priority doesn't absorb the classification criterion's independent function.

**On design-relationship scope.** Accept the design-relationship claim for authorized exits — art. 489's form conditions do specify correct art. 927 compliance for Exits 1–3 and Exit 5. Contest its extension to Exit 4's classification function. The design-relationship claim depends on art. 489 specifying compliance form; Exit 4's categorization into the ordinary processing domain is not specification of compliance form. VI places departure-with-demonstration in ordinary appellate processing; this categorization result holds regardless of whether the departure is authorized. An independent categorization function for unauthorized cases is not a design-relationship compliance specification. If the adversarial's design-relationship claim is correct for authorized exits, and the joint-constitution decomposition is correct (art. 489 is the classification criterion), then art. 489's independent categorization of Exit 4 is not within the design-relationship's scope.

**On classification-criterion primacy.** Within the joint-constitution framework, the adversarial cannot simultaneously accept that art. 489 is the classification criterion and press that art. 927's scope-constituting priority absorbs the classification criterion's organizing dimension. A provision that is the classification criterion has an independent organizing function by stipulation of the framework both sides accept. The scope-priority observation identifies art. 927 as structurally antecedent; it doesn't show that structural antecedence carries art. 927's authorization dimension into art. 489's criterion function. In any taxonomy of recognized act types, the classifying provision's recognition standard is the organizing criterion — the adversarial must show that the scope-constituting provision's authorization dimension overrides this even when the classifying provision is identified by the joint-constitution framework as the criterion. It currently meets this burden only with the scope-priority structural observation, not with primary authority.

**On semantic claim and joint structure.** Accept that "legítima" carries compliance-relative content as an ordinary language matter in compliance contexts. Contest that this ordinary language intuition determines technical meaning in a jointly constituted taxonomy. The joint-constitution framework gives art. 489 the classification-criterion function; within this framework, "legítima" is defined by what art. 489 recognizes as a legitimate exit type. The ordinary language reading is a semantic intuition from outside the framework; it doesn't override the framework's allocation of the classification-criterion function to art. 489. The adversarial's position that "legítima" semantically tracks compliance-relative content is in tension with accepting that art. 489 is the classification criterion: if art. 489 is the criterion, "legítima" tracks art. 489's domain-status recognition, and ordinary language intuition about compliance contexts doesn't carry over.

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the design-relationship claim entirely.** The adversarial's claim that art. 489 specifies correct art. 927 compliance for authorized exits is structurally plausible. Contesting it generally would require primary authority showing art. 489's form conditions were not designed with reference to art. 927's compliance structure — the structural ceiling has already implicitly acknowledged this path is closed. Discarded in favor of scope limitation: accept for authorized exits, contest for Exit 4's independent categorization.

**Pressing the scope-priority observation's internal tension.** The adversarial accepts the joint-constitution decomposition (art. 489 = classification criterion) while pressing scope-constituting priority as carrying art. 927's dimension into the criterion. There is a potential internal tension: if art. 489 is genuinely the classification criterion, scope-constituting priority doesn't by itself show that the criterion tracks the scope-constituting provision's organizing dimension rather than its own. This is a genuine tension but pressing it risks appearing to contest the adversarial's structural framework acceptance — which is a concession in the supportive's favor. The §4.1 argument engages the tension without explicitly naming it as an internal conflict. I chose not to explicitly frame it as a contradiction because the adversarial has a coherent position (priority carries dimension regardless of criterion designation) that the §4.1 argument needs to address on its own terms rather than as a logical error.

**Arguing the false-dilemma escape closes the semantic claim.** The false-dilemma's third option (Exit 4 decisions are incorretas, not inválidas) establishes domain-classification without authorization — a structural claim. The adversarial accepts this structural claim while pressing the semantic claim separately. If the structural claim and the semantic claim are analytically independent (structural: incorretas are classifiable; semantic: "legítima" carries compliance content), the false-dilemma escape doesn't close the semantic claim. Accepting this was necessary for an honest response; the §4.1 semantic argument responds to the semantic claim on its own terms through the joint-constitution framework rather than through the structural incorreta/inválida distinction.

**Accepting the adversarial's characterization as "residual question unanswerable."** Session 51's synthesis noted that after ten bilateral exchanges the debate may approach natural resolution: both readings coherent, both structurally motivated, neither established, Paper 1B acknowledges both as interpretive options. This would be the archive's first formally-closed interpretive-options outcome. I did not adopt this framing in the §4.1 response because the classification-criterion-primacy argument is available and not yet engaged by the adversarial — accepting premature closure before the argument is tested would be conceding without warrant. The failure conditions in §7 now name the conditions under which the §4.1 argument falls; if the adversarial meets those conditions, the interpretive-options outcome becomes appropriate.

## Assessment

The three responses are structurally honest. None reverse prior concessions; each engages the adversarial's argument within the accepted joint-constitution framework.

The most important structural move in this round is the design-relationship scope limitation. The adversarial's tenth ground established that formal separability doesn't prove criterion co-primacy under the design-relationship reading — a correct point. The §4.1 response accepts this while showing the design-relationship claim has a specific scope (compliance specification for authorized exits) that doesn't reach Exit 4's categorization function. This preserves art. 489's independent classification role within the joint-constitution framework without contesting the design-relationship claim as applied to authorized exits.

The classification-criterion-primacy argument (option (b)) is the session's main contribution. It accepts the joint-constitution framework fully and shows that within this framework — which both sides accept — art. 489 has an independent classification-criterion function that scope-constituting priority doesn't absorb. The adversarial's burden for round 10 is to show either (a) a structural principle establishing that scope-constituting priority absorbs independent classification functions generally, or (b) primary authority establishing that art. 927's authorization dimension carries into art. 489's criterion specifically.

The three new §7 failure conditions name these conditions explicitly. The most important is the structural-principle condition: if the adversarial can establish a general principle (not requiring art. 489/art. 927-specific authority) showing scope-constituting priority absorbs independent classification criteria in jointly constituted taxonomies, the §4.1 argument fails at the structural level. This is the adversarial's clearest available path for round 10 without primary authority.
