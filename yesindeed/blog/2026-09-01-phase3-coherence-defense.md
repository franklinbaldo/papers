# 2026-09-01 — phase3-coherence-defense: §4.21 response to adversarial r36 (v/w/x)

**What triggered this:** Adversarial r36 landed on 2026-09-01 with three attacks against r35's §4.20 responses to (s)/(t)/(u). This is the r36 supportive response.

## What I decided to argue

**Improvement to existing paper.** Added §4.21 with three responses against r36's (v)/(w)/(x) attacks; extended the §5 scope paragraph with r36 attack summaries and r36/§4.21 response summaries; added three new failure conditions to §6; updated frontmatter timestamp and description.

**The round 36 shape — three responses.**

---

### Against (v): The generation/derivation distinction preserves the structural-separability analysis

R36's attack: §4.20 conflates where premises are located with whether a reasoning chain is independently expressible; the verdict's chain is also built on premises in prior trial documents; the ratio-designation's chain ("G2 because J4 and J5 independently and unconditionally ground G2") is independently expressible; if structural separability means reasoning-chain expressibility rather than non-document-production, the verdict analogy applies.

The response draws the generation/derivation distinction. The verdict's reasoning chain is content *generated* by the judge through the deliberative act — it does not exist before the act and is produced by the judge's normative deliberation over the evidence and legal standards. Art. 93, IX CF targets this generated content: it requires the court's produced reasoning to appear in the decision. The ratio-designation's "expressible chain" is not generated content; it is *derived* by reading pre-existing voto content and reporting what those votos already express. The content of the chain — that J4 grounds G2 unconditionally, that J5 grounds G2 unconditionally — is in the votos before the relator acts. Constructing a description of what pre-existing materials say is not a reasoning-generation act of the type the provision reaches.

R36's point that the verdict's chain is also built on prior-document premises is correct but irrelevant: the verdict's chain is the judge's own inferential product over those materials, not a description of what those materials say. Art. 93, IX CF requires the judge's generated chain, not every expressible description of the underlying materials' logical implications.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Arguing that post-hoc expressibility is sufficient for some acts but not for others without explaining the differentia. This would have repeated the normative-function vocabulary without engaging R36's expressibility reframe. The generation/derivation distinction is the correct differentia: what the provision reaches is reasoning generated by the deliberating court's act, not reasoning derivable from pre-existing materials by a reader.

Conceding (v) partially — accepting that expressibility is a necessary condition while arguing it is not sufficient (i.e., the designation's chain fails some further test). I discarded this because generation/derivation is a cleaner and more principled account of why the verdict analogy applies to verdicts and not to designations: it tracks what art. 93, IX CF has always been about (the court's own reasoning output), not an ad hoc additional requirement.

---

### Against (w): The fundamento determinante as deliberative fact exists in the votos independently of the designation

R36's attack: §4.20 responded to a strawman — R34's strongest claim was constitutive: the *fundamento determinante* comes into existence through the designation act, not independently of it; the operativity/form distinction §4.20 presses applies equally to the verdict, where binding precedential effects are also applied by later provisions; the dispositive question is whether the designation generates the *fundamento determinante* or identifies a pre-existing deliberative fact.

The response accepts R36's framing as the correct question and answers it directly: the *fundamento determinante* as a deliberative fact — which element received autonomous collective support from the deliberating justices in their votos — exists in the votos before and independently of the designation. J4's voto expresses J4's unconditional grounding of G2. J5's voto expresses J5's unconditional grounding of G2. The collective-support fact is in those votos before the relator acts. The designation identifies that fact and confers legal status on it.

The verdict case is structurally different at this level: before the judge deliberates, the verdict's normative output does not exist. The judge's deliberative act produces the conclusion, and the reasoning chain that produced it is what the provision requires to be expressed. The ratio-designation's conclusion identifies a deliberative fact already present in the votos — the designation adds legal label and arts. 926–927 operative effect, not a new normative judgment over absent materials.

R36 is correct that §4.20 engaged the operativity-converts-form version more directly than the constitutive-existence version. This is the §4.21 direct engagement of the constitutive-existence question.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Arguing that the constitutive-existence question was already answered by the deliberative-accounting/legal-justification distinction from earlier rounds. R36's (w) is genuinely new: it locates the live question at the constitutive-existence level rather than at the justificatory-form level. Treating it as already answered would be the loop-closure condition R36 itself named.

Conceding (w) and reframing Component 2 to not require independence. I discarded this because the deliberative-fact-in-votos analysis is correct: J4's and J5's votos do express their unconditional grounding of G2. The concession would surrender a position that holds.

### Against (x): Threshold-application decomposes without remainder into voto facts and applicable rules

R36's attack: §4.20's ementa-votos comparison identity does not close the residual; individual votos do not assert "J4 and J5 together constitute sufficient autonomous collective support for G2"; applying the threshold criterion to the deliberative record produces content not present in any individual voto; §4.20's own failure condition (u) named this threshold-application step as the potential intermediate document.

The response decomposes the "intermediate content" R36 identifies into three components: (a) J4's voto unconditionally supports G2 [in J4's voto]; (b) J5's voto unconditionally supports G2 [in J5's voto]; (c) two independently unconditional votos satisfy the applicable threshold [in the applicable procedural rules]. Nothing in the conjunction is new content generated by the relator. The relator applies the rule to the facts and reports the result — the same structure as verifying a quorum. This is mechanical rule-following, not normative deliberation.

R36 correctly notes that failure condition (u) named the threshold-application step as the potential intermediate document. The §4.21 response does not deny the step occurs; it denies that the step generates *fundamentação*-bearing content. R36's own condition (x) identifies the structure: if each voto's unconditionality assertion is ipso facto a collective-sufficiency assertion under the threshold rule, no criterion-application step generates intermediate content. §4.21 accepts this: the threshold rule mechanically converts the conjunction of individual-voto facts into the collective-sufficiency fact.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Arguing that the threshold criterion is itself normatively loaded in a way that generates content. I considered whether "autonomous collective support" might be a contested normative concept rather than a fixed rule. But in the conditionality case class as described — where the applicable threshold is specified by the *regimento interno* or majority rule — the criterion is fixed. Whether the criterion is normatively laden in other contexts is a residual question; for this case class the threshold is a specified rule and the application is mechanical. If R37 contests that the threshold is normatively specified rather than fixed, that would be failure condition (x)'s trigger — appropriately named in §6.

Arguing that the threshold-application output is in the designation itself (and therefore not intermediate). The output is in the designation, but R36 is asking whether the threshold-application step produces content — the sufficiency claim — that is neither in any individual voto nor derivable from them alone without the rule. §4.21's response is that the applicable rules supply exactly what is needed to complete the derivation, so the sufficiency claim is derivable from the votos plus the rules, with no additional generation.

## Assessment after r36

The three sub-questions now in the following states:

**(v/p/s):** R36 has sharpened the debate to the generation/derivation level. R37 would need to establish either (a) that post-hoc expressibility is the correct criterion for structural separability (defeating the generation/derivation distinction) or (b) that the ratio-designation act is a generation act rather than a derivation act — specifically that the relator produces content in the designation that did not exist in the votos before the act. The burden is to show something new was made, not merely that a description can be constructed afterward.

**(w/q/t):** R36 correctly identified the constitutive-existence framing as the sharper question. R37 would need to show that the *fundamento determinante* as a deliberative fact is constituted by the designation rather than pre-existing in the votos. This requires establishing either (a) that the votos express only individual-justice conclusions, not a collective-support fact, or (b) that the "collective" dimension of the collective-support fact requires the designation's constitutive act to come into existence. Whether two votos jointly expressing unconditional support for G2 together constitute a "collective-support fact" without the designation is the live question.

**(x/r/u):** The threshold-application residual is now the most specific live question. R37 would need to show either (a) that "autonomous" or "sufficient" in the collective-support threshold is not mechanically determined by applicable rules but involves normative evaluation, or (b) that the threshold rule itself requires normative interpretation to apply, generating content the provision reaches. This is a narrower claim than R36 made, and the condition is correctly named in the updated §6.
