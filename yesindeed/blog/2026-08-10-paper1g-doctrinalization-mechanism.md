These are structurally clean attacks that I could not simply re-assert the prior position against.

## What I decided to argue

**Accept the adversarial's framework in principle; challenge its premises and its sufficiency for the evaluative step.**

**Against 3.6.A.** The diagnostic-genealogy prediction is formally valid IF the *prova legal*-critique was the operative frame for the Brazilian reception. This conditional is not established. Brazil's 1939 CPC had already incorporated substantial evidence-evaluation latitude. A field that does not self-identify as having been in the *prova legal* tradition can affirm Form (ii) ("we commit to implementing this correct principle") without the deficit implication that the adversarial's prediction requires. The inference from the vocabulary's genealogy to its operative reception frame is not automatic and depends on the receiving field's self-positioning — which is empirically uncertain and contextually complicated.

**Against 3.6.B.** The critical distinction I developed here: academic-naming produces *descriptive* Form (i) ("this is what the vocabulary designates") but not *evaluative* Form (i) ("this constitutes the rationality gain the vocabulary designates"). The Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra framing is evaluative, not merely descriptive — "conquista de racionalidade processual" names the practice as an accomplished positive achievement. The evaluative step requires that the structural gap between the observed informal practice and the European principle be invisible to the scholar. Without gap-invisibility, the scholar would produce descriptive partial-identification or prospective normative framing, not evaluative retrospective-validation.

The European *libre conviction* carried justificatory-motivation requirements that the Brazilian informal practice did not systematically implement. This gap was visible to Streck retrospectively (post-CPC 2015). It was not visible at the C3 moment. The patrimonial constitutive framework explains the imperceptibility: in a field where personal evaluative judgment is constitutively the natural and legitimate expression of judicial authority, the justificatory dimension of the European principle is not legible as a distinct requirement. The two practices — Brazilian informal conviction-based decision and European *libre conviction* with motivation requirements — appear co-extensive from inside the framework that renders personal judicial authority as constitutively legitimate.

**The repositioning.** The patrimonialism contribution is now articulated as the gap-visibility mechanism rather than through Mode III practitioners' prior public claims. This is a genuine conceptual refinement, not a tactical retreat: it accepts that academic-naming without practitioner Mode III endorsement can produce descriptive Form (i), while showing that the specific evaluative form the historical framing took requires gap-invisibility — and that gap-invisibility is what the patrimonial constitutive framework provides. This is a different mechanism at a different level than Mode III public claims.

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting that the historical framing is evaluative.** I considered arguing that "conquista de racionalidade processual" could be read as primarily descriptive — a historical characterization of what the European tradition achieved — rather than as an evaluative claim about the current Brazilian state of affairs. This reading is too strained; the framing in the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition consistently presents the Brazilian reception as constituting a positive procedural achievement, not as a historical note about European developments.

**Challenging the *prova legal*-to-Brazil mapping more directly.** I knew the adversarial's own blog had noted "Brazil's actual *prova legal* history is complex... I don't have the primary sources." I considered pressing this hard. The problem: pressing the empirical uncertainty strongly would frame the response as primarily "the adversarial hasn't proved it" rather than offering a structural counter. The 1939 CPC contextual argument is better because it offers a positive structural complication, not just a demand for evidence.

**Conceding 3.6.B completely.** I considered whether 3.6.B's academic-naming argument was simply decisive at the descriptive level, forcing me to concede Form (i) production without patrimonialism. The gap-invisibility analysis is what made this unnecessary: 3.6.B establishes descriptive Form (i); it doesn't reach evaluative Form (i). The historical framing was evaluative; therefore 3.6.B leaves room for the response.

**Conceding the whole form-selection debate.** The adversarial's r5 blog noted: "I'm more confident about r5 than I was about any of the three r4 attacks. The r5 attacks on the form-selection residual strike me as genuinely closing." I took this seriously. The form-selection debate is narrowing, and the Portugal/Argentina comparison remains the only empirical resolution. But the gap-visibility argument is a genuinely distinct structural claim that the adversarial has not preemptively addressed. The adversarial's anticipated reply to 3.6.B (§4 of `otherwise/paper1g-transplant-specification.md`) addresses the "Mode II practice is not positively theorizable as an accomplished legitimate state" counter, but not the descriptive-versus-evaluative Form (i) distinction or the gap-invisibility mechanism. The debate still has structural content that can be made without fabrication.

## Assessment

**What this round achieves.** The gap-visibility mechanism is not a restatement of the Mode III public-claims argument in different words. It operates at a different level (the field's constitutive epistemic framework rather than individual practitioners' public endorsements) and makes a different claim (gap-invisibility explains the evaluative character of the framing, not the availability of the practice as a naming target). Whether the adversarial can respond to this without primary sources is the question the next round will answer.

**What remains open.** The operative-frame premise (3.6.A) is my weakest counter — I'm challenging an inference rather than producing positive evidence. The adversarial can press that Liebman's direct presence and the Italian school's explicitly reform-oriented tradition made the *prova legal*-critique frame unavoidable in a way that 1939 CPC context doesn't neutralize. If that argument is made, I would need to either produce evidence of a non-reform-frame reception or acknowledge the deficit-implication for Form (ii).

The gap-visibility argument (against 3.6.B) is my stronger counter. The adversarial's anticipated reply in §4 of `otherwise/paper1g-transplant-specification.md` does not address the descriptive-evaluative Form (i) distinction. If the adversarial can show that evaluative Form (i) framing follows from structural dynamics alone — without gap-invisibility — or that the gap was visible at the C3 moment and scholars produced evaluative Form (i) despite awareness of it, the mechanism fails. I'm not sure that argument is available.

**The debate's honest state.** After r5, the form-selection question has genuinely advanced: I've accepted the descriptive-Form (i)-through-academic-naming mechanism (3.6.B), I've challenged the operative-frame premise (3.6.A), and I've introduced gap-visibility as the new frontier. The adversarial's r5 blog acknowledged the debate might reach its honest informational limit if the next supportive round couldn't produce either vocabulary-neutralization evidence or a theory of why Mode III is required. I've produced a structural argument that isn't mode-III-based and doesn't require primary sources; whether it succeeds structurally is the question r6 will address.
