## What the reading found

The test as r27 framed it asked whether the fourth condition (motivation as a condition of the conviction, Amaral Santos 1952) was *dropped* from the concept's exposition between 1952 and 1988 (the patrimonial prediction) or *kept and neutralized* by the "conquista" framing (the legitimation prediction). It was neither.

- **1952–1968.** The four-condition exposition is unchanged in the 3rd edition of *Prova judiciária* (1968, vol. I, pp. 330–338, via Pereira 2019). Lopes da Costa (1959, vol. III, p. 297, via Barbosa Moreira's n. 10) gives the same rationale — the reasons precept protects the administration of justice from "o arbítrio e a parcialidade" — as a precept of the judgment; Pontes de Miranda (1958, p. 232) records the freedom's cost rather than its guarantee. The accountability rationale is field-wide at C3; the placement *inside* the conviction is Amaral Santos's alone.
- **1973.** Art. 131 keeps the 1939 design; art. 458, II and art. 165 make the grounds an essential requisite. Barbosa Moreira reads the conjunction as "adoção categórica e irrestrita."
- **1978–1988.** Barbosa Moreira's OAB thesis (UFPR 1979 printing, pp. 281–290, **read in the original**) relocates motivation from the technique of evaluation to "o plano dos princípios fundamentais, de ordem política," as a guarantee inherent in the Estado de Direito; its sentence on discretion ("a motivação é tanto mais necessária quanto mais forte o teor de discricionariedade da decisão ... sobretudo se não terá ultrapassado os limites da discrição para cair no arbítrio," p. 286) is Type-2 vocabulary in the exchange's sense; and it states that the Constitution then in force had no express motivation clause. Liebman published "Do arbítrio à razão" in *Revista de Processo* in 1983; Cruz e Tucci's USP *livre-docência* on motivation came in 1987; art. 93, IX CF 1988 constitutionalized the duty with nullity. Meanwhile the tradition's textbook expounds *persuasão racional* without motivation inside the definition ("o juiz só decide com base nos elementos existentes no processo, mas os avalia segundo critérios críticos e racionais," 22nd ed., p. 74) and handles motivation as a separate constitutional principle — a *contrapartida* to the freedom, in the teaching summaries' word.
- **Enforcement.** STF: "a decisão com motivação sucinta é decisão motivada" (AI 816.457 AgR, 2010); STJ: "nulidade só alcança decisões ausentes de motivação, não aquelas com fundamentação sucinta" (REsp 437.180/SP, 2002); Greco: the STJ is "muito tolerante com motivações insuficientes"; Badaró: the courts maximize the succinct class and minimize the absent class. All post-1988; all readings of the constitutional clause.

**Reading:** promoted and decoupled in doctrine; neutralized in enforcement. The *casca* is the enforcement threshold — absence is nullity, insufficiency is not — and it survived the 1988 constitutionalization of the duty.

## What changed in the paper, and what was conceded

1. **R27's *casca/núcleo* pattern as located — taxonomic treatise vs. dominant treatise — is withdrawn.** The dominant tradition did not reduce the accountability dimension; it elevated and relocated it. Z-1's target does not exist, for a reason Z-1 did not give.
2. **Z-1 conceded on doctrine, and more.** The decoupling was itself received: Barbosa Moreira sources the guarantee framing to Taruffo (1975), Brüggemann (1971), Trocker (1974) and art. 111 of the Italian Constitution. On doctrinal form selection the legitimation-and-reception account is now **better** supported than patrimonialism, and the paper says so. Condition (p-i)(a) is not met and not pursued.
3. **Z-2: Reading α adopted, and harmless.** The reasons duty is in the *Ordenações Filipinas* (liv. III, tít. LXVI, §7), and "uma portaria de 31.03.1824 vinha lembrar aos juízes recalcitrantes o dever a que estavam sujeitos" (Barbosa Moreira 1979, p. 283). Paper 1G never claimed the duty was transplanted; the dilemma has no purchase. The datum cuts both ways and both are stated: reception-selectivity cannot originate a pattern a century older than the reception; and the doctrinalization of *livre convencimento* did not produce the pattern either — it named a practice whose threshold was already where it is, which is what Paper 1G's thesis says.
4. **Z-3 conceded for doctrine; relocated to enforcement.** A within-field doctrinal trajectory cannot discriminate P1 lock-in from patrimonial selection, and did not need to. The explanandum that survives is the enforcement threshold under normative shock. Comparative pair offered, attribution left open: Portugal at the same presence threshold (Alberto dos Reis, *CPC anotado* vol. V, p. 140: "a insuficiência ou mediocridade da motivação ... não produz nulidade"; Portuguese STJ formula "só a falta absoluta de fundamentação"); Argentina with an apex-court channel keyed to the quality of grounding since *Rey c. Rocha* (1909, Fallos 112:384), applied explicitly from *Storani de Boidanich* (1939) — a channel that is structurally grounded (federal extraordinary appeal; "sentencia fundada en ley"), which the paper states in the adversarial's favour. Portugal is recorded as consistent with both readings of "ibérico" and discriminating under neither.
5. **Z-4: "better supported" conceded on doctrine.** One explanandum named on which the structural account has not yet stated a prediction: why Brazilian review held the threshold at presence through 1988, like Portugal and unlike Argentina. New condition (p) states what would end this support altogether — if the comparative enforcement test comes back structural, the paper concedes form and enforcement alike and keeps the C1–C3/P1–P2 formalization as description only.
