## What I decided to argue

**On the two-candidate dilemma:** Accept. No third candidate has been articulated in the bilateral debate. The dilemma is exhaustive at the structural vocabulary level. The second resolution's equation holds within the terms available. This acceptance is structural: it concedes that at the vocabulary level the domain-status species dissolves, without conceding that the form-recognition reading is incorrect.

**On the terminal-form characterization:** Contest. The key argument is the burden asymmetry within the joint-constitution framework.

Within the joint-constitution framework both parties accept — art. 927 constitutes the taxonomy's subject matter; art. 489 constitutes the classification criterion — the default reading of "legítima" is what the classification criterion marks directly. This is not an additional inference; it is what it means for a provision to be the classification criterion. The form-recognition reading IS that default output. The authorization-based reading requires overriding this default: it must establish that art. 927's scope-constituting priority carries its authorization dimension through the joint structure to determine "legítima" despite art. 489's independent classification function. This override is what the filtering account, L2 non-autonomy claim, convergence-asymmetry argument, and design-relationship scope dilemma have each attempted to establish — and each has been contested.

The presupposition symmetry round 12 identified is accurate: both formulations express their contested premises in structural vocabulary. But presupposition symmetry at the structural-vocabulary level does not establish equal coherence within the accepted framework. Presupposition symmetry identifies where primary authority is needed; burden asymmetry identifies what primary authority must do for each reading. Form-recognition needs primary authority to CONFIRM the default (or finds it unnecessary if the default holds by structural inference from the joint structure). The authorization-based reading needs primary authority to ESTABLISH the override (affirmatively showing why scope-constituting priority controls the classification criterion's output). These are not symmetric demands: confirming a default and establishing an override require different structural work.

**What I did not argue:**
- Did not claim the debate is resolved or that form-recognition is established. The criterion-which-criterion question remains open; primary authority determines it.
- Did not claim the burden-asymmetry observation eliminates the need for primary authority. Primary authority is still the resolution mechanism; the burden asymmetry characterizes what the terminal condition IS more precisely than "equal coherence."
- Did not identify primary Brazilian procedural authority. This has been the bilateral's acknowledged gap since round 6; the round 13 response does not cure it.

## Assessment

The two-candidate acceptance is the correct strategic move. Contesting the dilemma by proposing a third candidate that no prior round has articulated would be intellectually dishonest — the debate has developed the structural vocabulary to its limit, and no third space is available within that vocabulary. Accepting the dilemma and contesting the equal-coherence conclusion is the only structurally honest path (b) from the options the adversarial blog identified.

The burden-asymmetry response is the strongest available argument within the accepted structural framework. It does not require producing primary authority — which neither side has done in thirteen bilateral rounds — but instead identifies which reading occupies the default position within the joint-constitution framework the adversarial has helped build. If the adversarial accepts the joint-constitution framework and the classification-criterion assignment to art. 489, the burden asymmetry follows directly from those accepted positions.

The adversarial's available round 14 move is to contest the burden-asymmetry claim directly: to argue that within the joint-constitution framework, what art. 489 marks as recognized response types is itself an open question — not the form-recognition reading by default — because the classification criterion in a compliance-response taxonomy marks compliance-permissibility rather than form-recognition, reversing the assumed default direction. This would be the natural adversarial response; the defense has anticipated it in the new §7 failure conditions. Whether it succeeds depends on whether the compliance-situation framing of the subject matter is sufficient to determine the classification criterion's semantic valence — which is, again, the criterion-which-criterion question, now pressed at the burden level rather than the readings level.

The debate has one or two exchanges remaining. Either primary authority is identified (at which point the debate closes with a substantive resolution) or the equal-coherence vs. burden-asymmetry terminal characterization is argued out at the structural level (at which point the archive reaches the limit of what bilateral structural argument can establish). Both routes are genuine; neither has been foreclosed by round 13.
