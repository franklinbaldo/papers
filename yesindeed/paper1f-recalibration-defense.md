# Defending the Proxy-Devaluation Mechanism: Statutory Compliance Standards and Commitment Displacement in Legal Reputation

---

## 1. Thesis Supported

Target paper: `paper1F_reputacao_sistema_juridico.md` — "Reputação como Mecanismo de Coordenação no Sistema Jurídico" (Baldo, 2025).

Thesis:

> "A redução tecnológica do custo de verificação da qualidade argumentativa recalibra esse sistema em dois sentidos: desvaloriza proxies institucionais quando qualidade é verificável diretamente, e abre novo canal de construção de reputação para agentes antes excluídos do sistema."

Adversarial paper engaged: `otherwise/reputation-constitutive-field.md` — "The Constitutive Field Problem: Why Cheaper Verification May Not Devalue Legal Reputational Capital."

## 2. What This Support Adds

**Vector:** Direct defense. This paper responds to three adversarial vectors — constitutive capital (§3.1), signaling persistence (§3.2), and network diffusion (§3.3) — on their own terms. The defense shows: (a) the constitutive capital critique is attenuated where the verification standard is statutory compliance for the structural compliance core, and that the mechanism's value in this domain extends to the high-volume, low-review population of proceedings where professional review does not systematically operate, independent of whether it adds detection-value over professional review in cases that review already reaches; (b) the signaling persistence argument is asymmetric across incumbents and new entrants, and that art. 489, §1º, V–VI's requirement of analytical engagement — identification of fundamentos determinantes, specification of distinguishing facts — raises mimicry costs above formal structural-marker optimization; (c) the network diffusion problem, while genuine for wide propagation, does not eliminate a first-stage local reputation-building channel operating through the Kreps repeated-game mechanism within specific bilateral court-practitioner relationships.

The adversarial's round 3 presses four constraints on the coverage-extension argument and the mimicry-resistance position: (i) the utilization constraint — detection and challenge-filing are separate steps, and the resource constraints preventing systematic review equally prevent systematic challenge-filing; (ii) the conditional prevalence gap — high-volume standardized dockets may have low rates of structural compliance violations because their institutional design is calibrated to produce compliant output; (iii) informed mimicry — the mechanism's success generates an official compliance-decision corpus that enables pattern reproduction below the statutory ceiling; (iv) the local Kreps incompatibility — the coverage-extension judicial-attention premise (courts can't catch violations in high-volume dockets) conflicts with the local Kreps bilateral-observation premise (courts track practitioner compliance through repeated interaction). This paper responds as follows: (1) the utilization constraint applies to the documentation/diffusion pathway of the mechanism; a distinct deterrence pathway operates through credible detection with non-zero challenge probability, creating ex ante compliance incentives independent of challenge-filing scale; automated detection additionally breaks the linear scaling between detection cost and caseload size, enabling effective triage; (2) compliance standardization in high-volume dockets addresses formal structural violations, not substantive engagement violations — the class the mechanism most distinctively targets — which arise from non-standard case configurations that templates cannot adequately address; (3) configuration-variance limits template reproduction to standard-pattern cases; in the non-standard configurations where both violations are more likely and the mechanism's value is highest, prior compliant decisions provide limited guidance for case-specific fundamentos determinantes identification; (4) the local Kreps incompatibility conflates parties' systematic per-filing review capacity with courts' byproduct observation from ordinary adjudication — different actors performing different cognitive operations; the adversarial's own §4.3 accepts that bilateral repeated observation is the mechanism by which template reproduction becomes detectable, conceding the same observational capacity that local Kreps requires.

The adversarial's round 4 advances three specific obstacles to the deterrence pathway in the Brazilian institutional context and three structural distribution barriers to local Kreps reputation accumulation. On deterrence: (i) the series' own Paper 1G documents that even explicit CPC 2015 sanctions with coercive enforcement produced only surface adaptation in the judicial habitus — the deterrence pathway must work in the same population where stronger normative pressure failed; (ii) judicial actors face institutional rather than personal sanctions — case-level reversal under constitutionally protected tenure, with appellate reversal already a normalized event; (iii) courts update enforcement probability beliefs from observable challenge-filing rates, not from theoretical detection capability. On local Kreps: sortition-based case distribution disperses court-side practitioner observations across many judges; judge turnover and vara rotation interrupt accumulation before threshold; abbreviated adjudicative processing through clerks attenuates quality-impression byproduct at the judge level. This paper responds: (1) the three judicial deterrence obstacles are accepted as correctly scoped to judicial actors in the decision-evaluation stage; Paper 1G's §6.2 analysis concerns judicial decision-production, not practitioner brief-production; (2) the primary deterrence target is reformulated as practitioners in the documentation-production stage, who face personal rather than institutional consequences from inadequate compliance, direct detection credibility from tool awareness rather than challenge-rate-mediated credibility, and incentive structures not governed by the judicial habitus Paper 1G documents; (3) the three local Kreps structural distribution barriers are accepted as constraining the reputation-accumulation channel in high-volume courts; they do not reach the practitioner-side deterrence channel, which operates from practitioner beliefs about detection probability rather than from court-side accumulation of quality observations.

The adversarial's round 5 presses three obstacles to the practitioner-side deterrence channel: (i) information penetration barrier — none of the three candidate channels for submission-specific detection-risk signals (opposing counsel deployment, court administrative screening, institutional client auditing) reaches the high-volume, low-review population, and population-wide tool-existence awareness does not substitute for submission-specific detection risk; (ii) enforcement credibility through repeated observation — practitioners in established practice patterns update enforcement-probability beliefs from observable challenge rates in their specific dockets, so the detection-stage/enforcement-stage distinction holds only for docket entrants without prior practice history; (iii) competitive market scope and campo jurídico-level habitus — public defenders and court-appointed counsel lack competitive market discipline from clients unable to evaluate compliance quality, and Paper 1G's habitus analysis operates at the campo jurídico level through shared legal education rather than specifically at the judicial decision-evaluation role. This paper responds: (1) the information penetration barrier correctly identifies that submission-specific channels are blocked, but the deterrence mechanism operates at the practice-context level rather than the submission level — practice-context detection credibility (from docket-type signals in judicial decisions, professional network communication, CNJ guidance) generates non-trivial conditional detection-risk priors for practitioners entering new dockets; for established practitioners, the two obstacles connect and jointly require that triage-enabled challenge filing produce observable post-deployment rate increases in the specific target dockets; (2) on competitive market scope, the personal-consequence mechanism is accepted as absent for institutional practitioners in civil-service and standardized-fee structures; CNJ institutional accountability mechanisms for public defenders' coordinating bodies represent a structural alternative not yet established at the required specificity; (3) the campo jurídico-level habitus transmission channel through shared legal education is accepted; what the adversarial has not established is that the specific surface-adaptation pattern Paper 1G documents in judicial decision-making — deciding by conviction and constructing fundamentação as post-hoc covering form — transfers structurally to practitioner brief-production, where the production task is argumentative rather than conviction-followed-by-rationale construction.

The adversarial's round 6 accepts the connection the defense drew between the information penetration and enforcement credibility obstacles: for established practitioners, both jointly reduce to the triage-outcome condition — whether triage-enabled challenge filing produces an observable post-deployment challenge rate increase in the specific institutional dockets where those practitioners form enforcement beliefs. Three structural features of institutional dockets are pressed as preventing this condition from being met: the institutional motivation gap (detection reduces costs but challenge filing requires organizational policy infrastructure that has not been developed in public defenders' offices and legal aid organizations); the threshold observability constraint (the absolute challenge rate increase triage enables in high-volume dockets may be statistically indistinguishable from background procedural challenge noise); and the case-separation routing effect (CPC arts. 1036–1041 route the highest-violation-density cases out of the main high-volume docket through separation procedures before the automated detection system's scale coverage applies, leaving the settled-ratio cases where templates are calibrated to compliance requirements). The adversarial also accepts that the detection-stage / enforcement-stage distinction holds for new-docket entrants but presses that new-docket entrants are the most mobile segment — not the primary constituency of the coverage-extension argument, which is institutionally embedded practitioners. This paper responds: (1) the institutional motivation gap applies to systematic full-caseload challenge filing but not to selective-escalation policies that triage makes feasible; the organizational calculus for developing a compliance-challenge policy changes when identification cost approaches zero, and the absence of current policies does not establish structural foreclosure since Brazilian public defenders' coordinating bodies have demonstrated coordination capacity in analogous litigation domains; (2) the threshold observability argument presupposes that compliance challenges are embedded in undifferentiated background procedural noise, but art. 489 compliance challenges are distinguishable by citation form — practitioners can track this specific category separately, and Bayesian updating on rare-event distinguishable signal types has different dynamics than updating on background noise rates; (3) the case-separation routing effect incorrectly presupposes docket-bounded enforcement belief updating — practitioners working in repetitive-resource dockets appear in both the main proceedings and the separated proceedings that arise from their own caseloads, so compliance outcomes in separated proceedings are observed by the same practitioners whose enforcement beliefs the mechanism targets; additionally, the routing effect removes the extreme tail of configuration-varied cases but does not eliminate the intermediate tail where automated triage may identify tractable compliance challenges.

## 3. The Argument

### 3.1 Constitutive Capital and the Exogenous Standard

The adversarial's §3.1 argues that verification technology trained on existing legal production would encode existing capital relations into the quality standard it verifies. The constitutive capital critique holds because what counts as sound legal argument is partly determined by agents recognized as competent judges — and that recognition is capital-structured.

The adversarial anticipates a response and narrows its claim accordingly: the proxy-devaluation mechanism requires verification technology to operate against an exogenous quality standard. It then identifies the criterion: "Field-transforming episodes in legal history have typically involved an external normative shock — legislation, constitutional amendment, international treaty — that imposed standards that existing capital holders could not unilaterally define" (§4).

The paper's verification program meets this criterion. CPC 2015 arts. 489, §1º, V–VI and 927 established obligatory compliance requirements for fundamentação that pre-exist and constrain current field practice. These provisions were themselves the normative shock the adversarial identifies as the exception to constitutive capital stickiness — the paper series (`paper1A` through `paper1E`) is precisely an analysis of the rationality requirements those provisions introduced. `pipeline_lean_argdown.md` operationalizes formal verification against these statutory provisions using a Lean 4 pipeline. This is not technology calibrating against field-relative quality norms; it is technology operationalizing a statutory standard that field practice must comply with.

The adversarial acknowledges this within its own text: "A procedural-validity standard has more independence from capital relations than a peer-assessment standard — CPC 2015 is law, not field consensus" (§3.1). It then responds that "even procedural validity is interpreted through field practice." This is correct for contested margins — what constitutes "adequate engagement" with a precedent's *fundamentos determinantes* involves interpretive judgment at the edges. But the paradigm cases Paper 1A and the series defend are not marginal: omission of a required precedent engagement, absence of a stated statutory provision, internal inconsistency within the fundamentação — these are identifiable by structural textual comparison, not interpretive weighting. For the core class, the exogenous standard is not significantly constituted by field capital.

A further objection presses that where the exogenous standard is clearest, verification technology adds least value: structural compliance violations in the core class are already identifiable through ordinary professional review, making the mechanism redundant in the domain where it operates without constitutive capital interference. This objection correctly identifies a limitation on the mechanism's marginal detection value over professional review in cases where professional review already operates. It does not address the complementary population.

The Brazilian legal docket is not uniformly high-stakes. The principal sources of binding precedent under art. 927 coexist with an enormous volume of individual proceedings at each level where one or both parties lack resources for systematic professional quality review — criminal appellants represented by public defenders carrying hundreds of simultaneous matters, civil defendants without specialized counsel in high-volume collection dockets, administrative proceedings where one party is an underresourced municipal or state body. In these proceedings, structural compliance failures in the core class go undetected not because the exogenous standard is unclear, but because the review cost is prohibitive for the party that would benefit from detection. Systematic automated verification extends detection and deterrence to this population — not because it outperforms professional review in high-stakes proceedings that review already reaches, but because it reaches proceedings where the cost structure currently forecloses review entirely. The inverse relationship between exogenous-standard clarity and mechanism added-value holds within the subset of cases professional review already reaches; it does not characterize the mechanism's value in the larger population where professional review is absent.

This does not fully resolve the constitutive capital problem. It bounds it. Where the verification standard is formal compliance with unambiguous statutory requirements, the constitutive capital critique loses most of its force. Where the standard shades into interpretive quality assessment, the critique regains force. The paper's thesis holds precisely where the standard is statutory — which is where the series locates its central cases — and the mechanism's coverage value in the high-volume, low-review population reinforces the claim that this domain matters at scale.

### 3.2 Signaling Persistence and Commitment Displacement

The adversarial's §3.2 distinguishes two components of institutional proxies under the Spence-Kreps framework: (a) the informational component — the proxy's correlation with quality — and (b) the commitment component — the observable investment record that creates predictable future performance. It argues cheaper output verification disrupts component (a) while leaving component (b) intact.

Two structural points resist this division.

**First, the Kreps framework does not fix the commitment signal to institutional investment.** Kreps's analysis requires only that commitment history be observable by counter-parties. An agent who accumulates verified high-quality outputs across multiple interactions generates an observable commitment history. The observation is not "this agent invested in institutional credentials" but "this agent has consistently produced outputs meeting the formal compliance standard." For repeated-game purposes, this record functions identically to an institutional investment record: it creates predictable future performance and carries reputational accountability. The adversarial's §3.2 assumes that only institutional investment constitutes observable commitment; but Kreps's framework imposes no such restriction. The paper's §4.3 — "mudar o que conta para construir essa reputação: de proxies institucionais para qualidade argumentativa verificável" — is precisely a claim about which commitment vehicle the recalibration transitions between, not a claim that commitment mechanisms disappear.

**Second, the commitment-displacement argument is asymmetric across new entrants and incumbents.** For incumbent practitioners, the adversarial is correct: cheaper output verification diminishes the informational component of proxies while leaving the commitment component (accumulated investment record) intact. The net effect on incumbents is partial, not total. For new entrants, however, the situation is different. New entrants by definition lack the institutional investment record that constitutes the commitment component. What cheaper verification provides is an alternative path to building a commitment record — through accumulated verified outputs rather than through institutional investment. The adversarial's §3.2 analysis addresses the incumbent's position well; it does not address the new entrant's position, which is the democratization half of the paper's claim.

A further objection presses whether formal compliance records constitute mimicry-resistant commitment signals. Kreps's separating equilibrium requires that commitment signals be costly to mimic — specifically, that accumulating the signal requires the same analytical investment that distinguishes high-quality from low-quality agents, rather than optimization for measurable formal markers. Whether this holds depends on what the compliance standard actually tests.

Art. 489, §1º, V–VI specifies obligations not reducible to structural formal completeness. V requires that a decision invoking precedent *identify the fundamentos determinantes* of that precedent and either apply them, *distinguish the case by specifying the distinguishing fact*, or justify overruling. VI requires that a decision invoking a súmula or court holding identify the specific applicable enunciado and explain why it applies. These requirements cannot be satisfied by citation inclusion alone — the decision must locate and engage with what the precedent decided and on what grounds, then establish a specific doctrinal relationship between that holding and the case at hand. Optimizing for these requirements at minimal analytical cost requires producing text that appears to identify fundamentos determinantes and specify distinguishing facts without genuinely doing so — which is harder to sustain across repeated interactions with the same court than across a single isolated filing, because the court accumulates observations about whether the purported distinctions track the cases they claim to distinguish. Mimicry-resistance under these provisions is not guaranteed; it is not reducible to presence-of-reference checking. Whether the pipeline's implementation (`pipeline_lean_argdown.md`) tests for engagement rather than presence determines where on this spectrum the standard lands. The statutory text establishes an upper bound on how gameable the standard can be; the implementation determines how close the actual threshold is to that upper bound.

The adversarial's round 3 accepts the statutory upper bound and introduces an informed mimicry argument: the same official publication channels the mechanism relies on for diffusion produce a growing corpus of prior compliant compliance determinations that identify the relevant fundamentos determinantes for controlling precedents. An agent with access to this corpus can reproduce the pattern of fundamentos determinantes identification from prior decisions without performing the independent doctrinal analysis that distinguishes genuine quality engagement from template reproduction.

Two points qualify the informed mimicry argument.

First, the argument's strength depends directly on case-configuration regularity. Art. 489, §1º, V–VI requires case-specific engagement — not generic compliance with the formal requirement, but identification of which fundamentos determinantes of the specific cited precedents bear on the specific factual configuration of the case at hand, and establishment of the doctrinal relationship between those grounds and the present configuration. A prior compliant decision correctly identifies the fundamentos determinantes of precedent P for the facts of case A. A new case B invoking the same precedent P with a materially different factual configuration requires fresh identification at the configuration-specific level — reading which grounds of P are determinative given B's facts, and characterizing the relationship between those grounds and B's configuration, in ways that are not derivable from case A's analysis. Template reproduction works for configurations that closely match prior compliant decisions; it fails for configurations that differ on the legally relevant dimensions. The mechanism's coverage-extension value is highest precisely in the tail of high-volume dockets where non-standard configurations appear — the same configurations where template reproduction from prior decisions is least feasible. Configuration-variance thus creates an alignment between where the mechanism is most valuable and where the mimicry cost is most demanding.

Second, the adversarial's §4.3 accepts that template reproduction "is detectable in repeated interactions, because case-specific factual configurations vary across cases and template-based fundamentos determinantes identification fails when applied to facts that differ from the template cases." This acceptance concedes the bilateral observation mechanism: courts receiving repeated filings from the same practitioner accumulate observations across factually varied cases, and practitioners who apply the same fundamentos determinantes pattern across varying configurations generate a recognizable signal. If bilateral repeated observation is sufficient to detect template reproduction — as the adversarial's own anticipated reply establishes — then it is sufficient to accumulate genuine quality impressions across the same repeated interactions. The adversarial cannot simultaneously accept bilateral observation as the mechanism by which courts detect mimicry while denying it as the mechanism through which courts accumulate quality assessments for local Kreps reputation. The cognitive capacity either operates or it does not in a given docket; the adversarial's §4.3 asserts that it does.

The paper's differential claim survives: cheaper verification changes the competitive position of new entrants relative to incumbents more than it changes the absolute position of either. Proxies lose relative weight for new entrants specifically because an alternative record-building path opens. This is the paper's §4.1 mechanism — "abre novo canal de construção de reputação" — stated as a differential change, not a total replacement.

### 3.3 Network Diffusion and Decision-Text Records

The adversarial's §3.3 identifies three conditions for the democratization prediction: legibility/access of verified quality scores to decision-makers, cross-interaction propagation through the reputation network, and absence of counter-signal investment by existing capital holders. It correctly notes the paper does not establish these conditions.

The response has two parts.

**First, the adversarial conflates non-establishment with non-existence.** The paper's §5.1 claims that cheaper verification "abre canal de construção de reputação para agentes antes excluídos" — it opens a channel, not that the channel operates without friction or at full effectiveness. The paper does not assert that the three diffusion conditions are fully satisfied; it argues that the channel becomes available where it was previously closed. The adversarial's objection reduces the strength of the democratization claim, which is appropriate, but does not eliminate it.

**Second, for formal compliance verification specifically, the diffusion channel has structural features the adversarial does not engage.** When an argument's compliance with art. 489, §1º is formally demonstrable, and a court acts on that compliance (granting or denying a procedural remedy), the quality record is produced in the decision text itself. Judicial decisions are published through official channels (Diário da Justiça, court websites) that are independent of practitioner networks. This is a diffusion path that does not require traversal through the professional relationship channels that existing capital holders dominate.

The counter-signal investment problem (condition c) — high-capital agents investing to shape what counts as verified quality — applies most forcefully to peer-review and reputational-assessment systems. It applies less forcefully to formal statutory compliance records produced in the course of judicial proceedings. An argument's compliance or non-compliance with art. 489, §1º in a given decision is determined by the court, not by peer assessment, and the record is produced in an authoritative document that high-capital practitioners cannot revise.

The adversarial's round 2 response presses the attribution gap: court decisions record compliance findings attributed to case outcomes, not to practitioner identity. A compliance victory enters the official archive as a case outcome — "case X: fundamentação inadequacy found; remand ordered" — not as a practitioner-attributed quality record. Wide network propagation from case outcomes to practitioner reputation requires intermediaries — institutional victory profiles, academic publications, co-counsel networks, professional directories — that are capital-holder-structured.

The attribution gap correctly identifies the binding constraint on wide network propagation. It does not bind the first-stage mechanism.

The Kreps repeated-game framework implies that reputation accumulates through repeated interactions where the same counter-parties observe the same agent's behavior over time. A practitioner who files formally compliant challenges before the same tribunal repeatedly generates an observable compliance track record for the judges and clerks who receive those filings directly — without requiring intermediaries to attribute case outcomes to the practitioner's identity. The court observes the practitioner's arguments across cases; the practitioner's compliance pattern is visible to that court through direct repeated observation. This is not wide reputation propagation; it is local repeated-game reputation accumulation within the bilateral court-practitioner relationship — the same mechanism Kreps identified, operating at smaller geographic and institutional scale.

The paper's democratization claim has two stages: (a) opening a local reputation-building channel for practitioners previously lacking institutional proxies; (b) propagating that reputation through the broader network. The attribution gap constrains stage (b): without practitioner-attributed outcome records reaching reputational network nodes beyond the specific court, compliance records do not accumulate into the networked reputation the paper's §5.1 ultimate prediction envisions. Stage (a) operates through the Kreps mechanism in repeated bilateral court-practitioner interactions, and the attribution gap does not block it.

The mechanism's democratizing effect at stage (a) is localized. For practitioners concentrated in specific courts and dockets — public defenders before their regular tribunals, regional specialists appearing repeatedly before specific administrative bodies, state attorneys before the same appellate chambers — local reputation accumulation through compliance track records provides a new commitment signal channel, independent of institutional affiliation proxies, within those specific relationships. Whether this local reputation propagates into the broader network remains constrained by the attribution gap for stage (b).

**On the utilization constraint.** The adversarial's round 3 correctly identifies that detection and challenge-filing are separate steps: the resource constraints preventing systematic professional review in high-volume dockets also prevent systematic challenge-filing, so that automated detection does not automatically produce the decision-text compliance records the diffusion channel requires.

The utilization constraint applies to the documentation/diffusion pathway, which operates from challenge-filing through judicial adjudication to decision-text record. The constraint does not address a distinct deterrence pathway through which verification technology operates. When parties and practitioners know that compliance failures are reliably detectable — even if the conversion from detection to filed challenge is low — this changes compliance incentives ex ante. A court whose decisions are subject to automated compliance screening faces a positive probability of challenge for each violation, regardless of whether that probability reaches systemic scale. Ex ante deterrence operates through the credible threat of detection with some non-zero challenge probability, not through high-volume documentation of violations in decision texts. The deterrence effect on compliance behavior — the court's incentive to produce adequately reasoned decisions — is independent of the number of challenges actually filed. Even imperfect coverage at the documentation pathway level produces this ex ante deterrence, which is a direct mechanism for the compliance improvements the paper's thesis predicts.

A second constraint on the utilization argument concerns the cost structure automated detection introduces. The adversarial treats detection cost and challenge-filing cost as equivalent burdens on the same resource-constrained parties: "the same resource constraints that forecloses detailed review forecloses the systematic challenge filing." This equivalence overstates the structural similarity between the two costs. Systematic professional review requires variable-cost attention per filing — each additional filing adds proportional review cost. Automated detection, once the infrastructure is in place, approaches near-zero marginal cost per additional filing: a caseload of five hundred cases can be scanned for compliance violations at near-zero incremental cost per case. This creates a cost structure asymmetry: detection cost approaches near-fixed (infrastructure setup cost amortized over large caseloads) while challenge-filing cost remains roughly variable (each challenge requires per-case preparation and litigation). A resource-constrained practitioner with automated detection can scan an entire caseload to identify candidate violations at near-zero total identification cost, then selectively litigate the violations most worth pursuing. The utilization constraint correctly identifies that low-conversion triage produces few decision-text records; it does not engage the deterrence pathway through which even low-conversion triage creates genuine compliance incentives at the ex ante stage.

**On the conditional prevalence gap.** The adversarial predicts that high-volume standardized dockets have low rates of structural compliance violations because their institutional design is calibrated to produce compliant standardized output. High-volume repetitive-resource proceedings under CPC arts. 1036–1041 and high-volume criminal appellate dockets develop standardized decision formats calibrated to the compliance requirements those proceedings formalize.

This prediction addresses formal structural compliance — the presence of statutory citations, the mention of relevant precedents, the formal completeness of dispositif statements. It does not address substantive engagement compliance — the requirement that the specific fundamentos determinantes of each invoked precedent be identified and engaged at the level of the specific case configuration. Art. 489, §1º, V–VI sets requirements that extend beyond structural formal completeness: V requires that the decision either apply the precedent's fundamentos determinantes to the present facts, distinguish the case by specifying the factual difference, or justify overruling; VI requires explanation of why the identified precedent applies to the present case. Standardized templates calibrate to formal structural compliance — ensuring the relevant precedent is cited, the applicable statutory provision is referenced, the dispositif format is correct. They do not, as a structural matter, ensure that each case's specific factual configuration is engaged with at the doctrinal level those provisions require.

In high-volume dockets, individual case fact configurations vary even when the governing legal framework is stable. A repetitive-resource proceeding on a settled legal question still presents individual cases where the party argues that the concrete facts distinguish the case from the leading precedent on grounds specific to this configuration. A template that correctly addresses the standard configuration may fail to engage the configuration-specific distinguishing argument that art. 489, §1º, V requires be addressed or rebutted. This is precisely the class of substantive engagement violation that formal structural compliance review misses and that the mechanism's case-specific analysis most distinctively targets. The adversarial has not established that such violations are rare in high-volume dockets — it has established that formal structural violations (citation omission, statutory provision absence) are likely rare due to template standardization. The prevalence of substantive engagement violations in the configuration-specific tail of standardized dockets is an empirical question the adversarial's structural prediction does not resolve.

**On the local Kreps incompatibility.** The adversarial identifies what it characterizes as an incompatibility between two premises the defense runs simultaneously: the coverage-extension premise (courts in high-volume dockets cannot systematically review each filing for compliance quality) and the local Kreps premise (courts in those same dockets observe and accumulate practitioner compliance patterns through repeated bilateral interaction). The adversarial argues both cannot be true in the same docket population.

The incompatibility dissolves under a distinction that the adversarial's framing does not draw: between the actors bearing the resource constraint in each premise and the cognitive operations each premise requires.

The coverage-extension premise concerns *parties* — specifically, parties in the resource-constrained position who would benefit from identifying compliance violations in opposing filings or in judicial decisions issued against them. The claim is that the party seeking to challenge a non-compliant decision cannot afford systematic per-filing compliance review across the party's caseload. The cognitive operation this describes is systematic evaluation of each filing with dedicated compliance-auditing attention directed to the compliance question — an operation whose cost scales with the number of filings reviewed.

The local Kreps premise concerns *courts* — specifically, courts receiving repeated filings from the same practitioners across multiple cases. The observation the Kreps mechanism requires is not systematic per-filing compliance auditing by the court, but the accumulation of quality impressions as a byproduct of ordinary adjudication. A judge who reads a practitioner's brief as part of deciding a case acquires information about that brief's quality as a natural byproduct of the adjudication process — not through dedicated compliance auditing, but through the reading that deciding the case requires. Over many cases involving the same practitioner, quality impressions accumulate through repeated adjudication byproduct without requiring the court to conduct a dedicated compliance audit of each filing it receives. These are different cognitive operations with different resource requirements: systematic compliance auditing (the operation coverage-extension says is absent) requires attention devoted specifically to the compliance question for each filing; byproduct observation (the operation local Kreps requires) is a consequence of ordinary adjudication that does not add a distinct compliance-review burden.

The incompatibility the adversarial identifies — that both premises cannot be true "in the same docket population" — treats these as equivalent resource demands on the same actor. They are not: coverage extension concerns the party's review capacity; local Kreps concerns the court's observational byproduct from its adjudicative function. The court that lacks the capacity to undertake systematic compliance auditing of every filing it receives retains the capacity to accumulate quality impressions through the reading that deciding cases requires. High-volume dockets do not eliminate courts' ordinary adjudicative processing of the filings they receive; they constrain dedicated compliance-review attention beyond that adjudicative processing.

**On the local Kreps structural distribution barriers.** The adversarial's round 4 presses three structural features of high-volume Brazilian courts that disrupt the reputation-accumulation mechanism independently of the incompatibility argument.

*Sortition.* Brazilian courts distribute cases through random sortition (distribuição aleatória por sorteio) at both first-instance and appellate levels. A practitioner filing before any given tribunal does not reliably appear before the same desembargador, panel, or judge across a series of filings. Byproduct quality observation accumulates in the specific judge or panel that adjudicates a series of cases; random distribution disperses observations of the same practitioner across many judges within the same court. The adversarial's claim is accepted: no individual judge accumulates sufficient observations of a given practitioner through byproduct adjudication alone, in the general case, to form a stable quality assessment through the local Kreps mechanism as described.

*Judicial mobility and vara rotation.* In high-volume first-instance courts, judges rotate through different vara assignments and are promoted to appellate positions. A judge who begins accumulating byproduct quality observations about a specific practitioner may be reassigned or promoted before accumulation reaches the threshold for stable reputational assessment. This structural instability compounds the sortition constraint: even in specialized varas where sortition produces repeated practitioner-judge pairings more often, judge rotation disrupts the accumulated observation sequence.

*Abbreviated adjudicative processing.* In high-volume dockets, assessores or clerks prepare relatorios summarizing filings for judicial approval. The quality-impression byproduct from engaging with individual briefs accumulates in the clerk who prepared the relatorio, not in the judge who approved it. For this byproduct to serve the local Kreps function, the same clerk must accumulate observations of the same practitioner across cases and transfer those observations to relevant judges over time — requiring clerk assignment continuity and institutionalized information transfer that standard Brazilian court organization does not structurally guarantee.

These three structural barriers are accepted as correctly identifying constraints on the court-side reputation-accumulation channel in high-volume Brazilian courts. They address a specific mechanism — local Kreps reputation accumulation through repeated court-side practitioner observation — and are accurate for that channel.

They do not reach the practitioner-side deterrence channel, for reasons developed in §3.4.

The adversarial's §4.3 consistency constraint presses a further point: it accepts that template reproduction is detectable in repeated interactions through bilateral observation, and then argues that the same observational capacity that detects template reproduction should serve quality accumulation for local Kreps — but that the adversarial's §3.6 structural distribution barriers show this capacity is disrupted in any case. This is correct for the court-side reputation-accumulation channel: both template-reproduction detection and quality-impression accumulation through repeated observation face the same sortition, rotation, and abbreviated-processing constraints. The consistency constraint and the structural distribution barriers operate together — the former shows the two tasks share the same observational infrastructure, the latter shows that infrastructure is constrained in the target docket population. The conclusion affects reputation accumulation as a channel.

The deterrence channel's independence from this conclusion is addressed in §3.4.

### 3.4 The Deterrence Pathway: Scope, Sanction Structure, and Documentation-Production Targeting

The adversarial's round 4 advances three specific obstacles to the deterrence pathway in the Brazilian institutional context: (i) the series' own Paper 1G demonstrates that the judicial habitus produces surface adaptation to normative compliance pressure rather than substantive behavioral change; (ii) the sanction structure for judicial actors is institutional rather than personal; (iii) courts update enforcement probability beliefs from observable challenge-filing rates, not from theoretical detection capability that parties may possess. Each obstacle is accepted as correctly characterizing the judicial deterrence channel. Together they define its scope.

**Paper 1G's habitus analysis is scoped to judicial actors in the decision-evaluation stage.** Paper 1G's §6.2 states: "o operador pode adaptar o comportamento superficial — usar a linguagem do art. 489, §1º, incluir menção aos fundamentos determinantes — sem que a disposição profunda mude: a decisão continua sendo tomada por convicção pessoal, e a fundamentação continua sendo casca que oculta, não núcleo que expõe." The passage describes a judicial actor in the decision-evaluation stage: the decision is the product, the conviction is the cause, and the fundamentação is the post-hoc covering form. Paper 1G's genealogical analysis of the patrimonial habitus concerns the transmission and reproduction of judicial decision-making culture — through legal education systems calibrated to conviction-based reasoning, through judicial career structures that historically rewarded doctrinal facility over analytical rigor, through the supervisory culture that normalized the gap between stated rationale and operative reasoning. This is a specific institutional habitus specific to judicial decision-producers. The adversarial correctly identifies that the deterrence pathway must work in the same population where Paper 1G shows normative pressure has failed to produce substantive change. For the judicial deterrence channel, this is a strong constraint.

**The sanction structure gap applies to judicial actors specifically.** Brazilian judges hold constitutionally protected tenure (art. 95, CF/88: vitaliciedade after two years). The sanction from a successful compliance challenge is decision reversal — a case-level institutional outcome, not a personal sanction. Appellate reversal in the relevant dockets is already a statistically normalized event; the marginal deterrence from compliance-specific reversal, relative to the baseline reversal probability from substantive and procedural appeals, may be small. For institutional deterrence to translate into per-judge compliance incentives, governance mechanisms — CNJ performance reviews tracking fundamentação reversal rates specifically — would be required to make individual judges personally responsive to compliance-specific reversal. The adversarial's analysis of this gap is accepted.

**The detection-to-credibility gap applies to court-side beliefs.** Courts observe challenge-filing rates in their dockets, not detection rates in parties' automated systems. If the utilization constraint holds the conversion rate from detection to filed challenge near-zero in the high-volume, low-review population, courts in that population receive near-zero compliance challenges and form near-zero expected challenge probability from observable base rates. Courts update enforcement probability beliefs from observed enforcement rates, not from theoretical detection capability they cannot directly observe. The adversarial's argument is accepted for this channel.

**The primary deterrence pathway targets practitioners in the documentation-production stage.** The three obstacles above accurately describe constraints on a deterrence pathway that runs: detection capability → court's enforcement probability belief → court's compliance incentive at decision-evaluation stage → substantive quality improvement in judicial decisions. This chain has institutional vulnerabilities the adversarial correctly identifies at the second and third links (sanction structure and belief update mechanism) and a habitus vulnerability the adversarial correctly identifies as limiting the fourth link's scope.

A distinct deterrence pathway operates through practitioners at the documentation-production stage: detection capability → practitioner's detection-risk belief → practitioner's compliance incentive at brief-writing stage → substantive quality improvement in arguments submitted to courts. This chain does not pass through the institutional vulnerabilities the adversarial identifies, because practitioners are not the actors whose habitus Paper 1G analyzes, whose tenure structure insulates them from personal consequences, or whose enforcement beliefs are mediated by observed challenge-filing rates.

The documentation-production stage concerns practitioners producing the arguments that appear in petitions, briefs, razões recursais, memoriais — the practitioner-authored side of the legal proceeding. Art. 489, §1º, V–VI governs the quality of judicial decisions, but the quality of those decisions depends in part on the quality of the arguments submitted to the court: a practitioner who identifies and articulates the correct fundamentos determinantes of the controlling precedent, specifies the relevant distinguishing facts, and structures the argument to satisfy the compliance requirements at the documentation level creates the conditions for the court to decide correctly. Compliance failures in judicial decisions are not always judicial errors; they sometimes reflect the absence of adequate practitioner argumentation to which the court could respond. The mechanism's deterrence value operates on practitioners because practitioners produce the documentation whose compliance quality is subject to automated auditing.

**Practitioners face personal rather than institutional consequences.** The sanction structure for practitioners is not analogous to the sanction structure for judges. A practitioner whose inadequate fundamentos determinantes argumentation results in a failed compliance challenge suffers a case outcome loss — the client's case fails, the professional track record registers the outcome, the fee recovery (where contingent) is affected, and the client relationship is strained. These are direct personal consequences, not institutional reversal rates on a court. CPC art. 77 establishes good faith obligations for litigants' representatives, with sanctions under art. 81 for serial non-compliance. Most directly, the quality of a practitioner's argumentative compliance with art. 489, §1º determines whether the argument prevails — inadequate compliance is not a statistical contribution to an institutional reversal rate but an argument failure in the specific case, with immediate personal stakes for the practitioner who produced it.

**Practitioners' detection credibility is not mediated by challenge-filing rates.** The adversarial's detection-to-credibility gap argument concerns how courts form enforcement probability beliefs: courts observe challenge rates in their dockets, not parties' detection capabilities, so near-zero challenge rates generate near-zero expected enforcement probability regardless of theoretical detection precision. This argument depends on the enforcement-belief-update mechanism that applies to courts. Practitioners update compliance-risk beliefs through a different mechanism. A practitioner who knows that automated compliance auditing is applied to submissions — by opposing counsel, by the court's administrative screening, or by the practitioner's own institutional clients — forms a detection-risk belief based on the known capability of the detection system, not on the historical rate at which detected violations have been challenged. The practitioner's compliance-risk belief is formed at the detection stage, not at the enforcement stage. If the practitioner knows a detection pipeline will flag inadequate fundamentos determinantes identification with high precision, the expected detection risk is high regardless of whether historical challenge rates are near-zero — because the detection occurs before the challenge-filing decision, and the practitioner cannot observe which detected violations will be challenged while producing a given brief.

This distinction dissolves the detection-to-credibility gap for the practitioner-side deterrence channel. The gap arises from the inference: detection-capable party → few challenges filed → court observes few challenges → court forms low enforcement-probability belief → court has low compliance incentive. This chain presupposes that the ultimate behavioral target (the court) observes enforcement rates as the credibility signal. The practitioner-side chain is: detection-capable party → practitioner knows detection is applied to her work → practitioner forms direct detection-probability belief → practitioner has compliance incentive at documentation stage. The practitioner does not observe enforcement rates as the credibility signal; the practitioner's compliance incentive is formed at the detection stage before the enforcement-rate update mechanism operates.

**Paper 1G's habitus analysis does not directly characterize the practitioner population in the documentation-production stage.** Paper 1G's analysis concerns judicial actors — specifically, the judge who makes a decision by personal conviction while constructing fundamentação as post-hoc covering form. Practitioners in the documentation-production stage do not produce decisions from personal conviction; they produce arguments whose purpose is to persuade a decision-maker. The disposition Paper 1G identifies as resistant to normative pressure — the disposition to decide by conviction and then construct covering fundamentação — is a judicial disposition. Its transmission and reproduction (through legal education calibrated to judicial careers, through judicial supervisory culture, through the institutional environment that produces judges) does not necessarily characterize the practitioner who produces the petition or brief that precedes the judicial decision.

Practitioners in competitive markets face quality incentives from which judges are partially insulated. A practitioner who consistently produces inadequate fundamentos determinantes identification loses client outcomes at the case level, without the institutional tenure protection that insulates judges from the personal consequences of inadequate decision quality. The behavioral baseline for practitioners in competitive markets is not the habitus equilibrium Paper 1G identifies for judges; it is shaped by client accountability mechanisms, market competition, professional reputation among clients and referral networks, and direct exposure to case outcome consequences. Whether automated compliance detection produces *substantive* rather than *surface* behavioral adjustment in the practitioner population at the documentation-production stage is a distinct empirical question from whether it produces substantive adjustment in the judicial population at the decision-evaluation stage. Paper 1G answers the latter; it does not answer the former.

The scope restriction this implies is accepted: the practitioner-side deterrence pathway is respecified, not simply asserted as unaffected by the adversarial's round 4 arguments. The pathway operates on a different population, through a different sanction structure, with a different credibility mechanism, in a population Paper 1G's habitus analysis does not directly characterize. These are structural differences that make the practitioner-side deterrence pathway independent of the three judicial deterrence obstacles the adversarial correctly identifies. Whether the practitioner population in the high-volume, low-review dockets the coverage-extension argument targets exhibits sufficient quality incentive structure for the deterrence pathway to produce substantive behavioral adjustment is a further empirical question addressed in §5(i) as a new failure condition.

**On the information penetration barrier (round 5).** The adversarial's §3.5 correctly identifies that the three named channels for submission-specific detection-risk signals are each structurally blocked in the target population. Opposing counsel in the target population is resource-constrained at the same level as the practitioner under evaluation, making detection deployment invisible to the practitioner whose behavior must change. Court administrative screening is inconsistent with accepting that high-volume dockets lack systematic review capacity. Institutional client auditing applies only to large-organization practitioners outside the coverage-extension population. Each channel analysis is accepted for submission-specific signals.

The deterrence mechanism does not require submission-specific signals. A practitioner's compliance-risk belief is formed at the practice-context level — the tribunal type and proceeding category where they regularly file — not at the submission level. The conditional belief that governs deterrence is: "if I submit a non-compliant brief in proceedings of this type, what is the detection probability?" Practice-context detection credibility is generated through signals at this level: judicial decisions in the practitioner's tribunal type that reference compliance analysis findings in their fundamentação-adequacy determinations; CNJ regulatory guidance about compliance evaluation methodology for specific proceeding categories; professional network communication about tool deployment in specific docket types. These signals are intermediate between submission-specific notification and general market awareness — they are docket-type-specific and tell the practitioner that compliance analysis is operative in their proceeding category without identifying their specific submission as detected.

For practitioners entering new docket types without established practice patterns, these practice-context signals generate the non-trivial conditional detection-risk prior the deterrence mechanism requires. Without prior docket history establishing a near-zero challenge-rate observation in the new practice context, the practitioner's enforcement belief is formed from available practice-context signals. If tools are deployed in the new docket type, those signals create a non-negligible detection risk prior before practice-history observations have anchored enforcement beliefs to near-zero. The adversarial's information penetration argument correctly presses that submission-specific signals are blocked; it does not close the practice-context channel for practitioners without established base rates in the target docket.

For established practitioners with extended practice histories in the target dockets, practice-context signals face competition from the accumulated near-zero challenge-rate prior. This connects to the second round-5 obstacle.

**On enforcement credibility through repeated observation (round 5).** The adversarial correctly identifies that practitioners in established practice patterns update enforcement-probability beliefs from observable challenge rates in their specific dockets, and that the detection-stage/enforcement-stage distinction the §3.4 deterrence pathway draws holds specifically for docket entrants without established base-rate histories. For established practitioners in the target dockets, the adversarial presses that the utilization constraint that holds challenge rates near-zero in aggregate also holds them near-zero in the specific practice contexts those practitioners observe.

The deterrence mechanism's enforcement credibility claim is about post-deployment equilibrium. The near-zero challenge rates that established practitioners observe are pre-deployment observations — formed in conditions where systematic automated detection was not operationally deployed in the target dockets. If automated detection, once deployed, enables triage-based challenge filing that produces any observable challenge rate increase in specific docket types, practitioners monitoring those dockets update from the new observable rate. The rate they observe post-deployment is not the same as the rate they observed pre-deployment; the deterrence mechanism's claim is about what happens to enforcement beliefs after the post-deployment rate becomes observable.

The two round-5 obstacles are structurally connected. The information penetration argument and the enforcement credibility argument jointly require, for established practitioners, that triage-enabled challenge filing produce an observable challenge rate increase in the specific target docket populations — more specifically than near-zero, enough to shift enforcement beliefs in practice contexts where near-zero has been the prior. This is the same empirical condition the utilization argument addresses: whether triage conversion is achievable at rates observable in the specific institutional dockets where established practitioners form enforcement beliefs. If the utilization constraint prevents triage from producing any observable rate increase in those specific dockets, both round-5 obstacles persist simultaneously for the established-practitioner segment. If triage produces even marginal observable rate increases, both are addressed. The two obstacles do not add independent empirical constraints for established practitioners; they jointly require the same triage-outcome condition the utilization argument already identifies.

The detection-stage/enforcement-stage distinction therefore has differentiated scope: it holds for new-docket entrants who have not accumulated a near-zero base rate in their specific practice context; for established practitioners in established practice patterns, the distinction is valid in theory but operative only if the post-deployment challenge rate in their specific dockets becomes observably non-zero. This differentiation is accepted.

**On competitive market scope and campo jurídico-level habitus (round 5).** The adversarial's market scope argument is accepted in full for institutional practitioners: public defenders are civil-service employees with civil-service accountability structures; court-appointed counsel in criminal proceedings receive standardized fees; clients in the target population cannot attribute case-outcome variation to practitioner compliance quality because the credence-good opacity that generates the market failure the mechanism addresses applies equally to the client's ability to evaluate compliance performance. The personal-consequence mechanism identified in §3.4 as distinguishing practitioner deterrence from judicial deterrence rests on competitive market accountability, and that mechanism is structurally absent for institutional practitioners in civil-service and standardized-fee structures.

CNJ institutional accountability for public defenders' coordinating bodies represents a structural alternative. The Conselho Nacional de Justiça's oversight mandate covers the Defensoria Pública and extends to systematic quality monitoring and performance evaluation. If CNJ frameworks track compliance-specific quality metrics at the coordinating body level — which is consistent with CNJ's monitoring function over judicial service quality — institutional accountability for compliance quality is available without market discipline. The adversarial correctly identifies this as a natural response path and correctly presses that whether such mechanisms are operationally calibrated to compliance-specific quality in the specific docket populations the coverage-extension argument targets remains unestablished. The availability of the CNJ mechanism is structural; whether it is operationally specific to these docket populations is an empirical question not yet resolved.

The campo jurídico-level habitus argument requires separate assessment. The adversarial correctly identifies that Brazilian legal education is shared socialization infrastructure for both judicial and practitioner roles, and that Paper 1G's genealogical analysis traces the patrimonial habitus through this shared formation system. A Brazilian legal professional trained in the system Paper 1G characterizes does not exit that habitus upon taking a practitioner role rather than a judicial role. The shared educational channel is real, and the adversarial correctly presses that the round 4 response overstated the behavioral separation between practitioner and judicial roles if both are formed by the same socialization.

What the adversarial has not established is that the specific surface-adaptation pattern Paper 1G documents transfers structurally across production contexts. Paper 1G's §6.2 describes a sequential structure: (1) the decision is reached by personal conviction; (2) the fundamentação is constructed afterward as covering form. This is a pattern where the conclusion precedes the rationale, and the rationale's function is to cover rather than to expose the operative reasoning. This structure presupposes a decision — a conclusion the actor has reached — around which the rationale is then built. A practitioner producing a petition or brief does not operate this sequential structure: there is no prior conviction to cover. The practitioner's task is to produce argumentation aimed at persuading a decision-maker; the argumentation is the terminal output, not a post-hoc covering form for a conclusion already reached by other means.

Even if the same educational habitus governs both roles, the surface-adaptation pattern it generates in each context is structurally different because the production tasks are structurally different. In judicial decision-making, the habitus generates post-hoc fundamentação that covers a prior conviction. In practitioner brief-writing, a structurally analogous surface-adaptation pattern would generate compliance-vocabulary text that formally satisfies art. 489, §1º requirements without genuine case-specific engagement — but this is not the same behavioral pattern, and the mechanisms that generate it (if it exists in this population) are not the same as those Paper 1G documents. Whether the patrimonial habitus as constituted through shared legal education produces this specific pattern in practitioner brief-production is a question about how the habitus operates in a different task environment, which Paper 1G does not address. The adversarial has identified the transmission channel; it has not established the outcome in the practitioner brief-production context.

**Scope restriction after round 5.** The practitioner-side deterrence pathway operates most clearly for competitive-market practitioners in the coverage-extension population — private counsel and specialized regional practitioners in high-volume proceedings who face quality-sensitive clients and whose case-outcome exposure creates direct personal accountability. For institutional practitioners (public defenders, court-appointed counsel in the high-volume, low-review population), the deterrence pathway requires either (i) CNJ institutional accountability mechanisms calibrated to compliance-specific quality at the coordinating body level, or (ii) observable post-deployment triage-based challenge rate increases in the specific target dockets that shift established practitioners' enforcement beliefs. Neither has been established at the required specificity. The adversarial's round 5 successfully establishes that the practitioner-side deterrence pathway is not uniformly operative across the full target population, and that the round 4 reformulation's behavioral distinction between practitioner and judicial roles is less structurally clean in the institutional practitioner segment than for competitive-market practitioners. The mechanism's deterrence value in the institutional practitioner segment remains conditional on institutional and empirical specifics not yet provided.

**On the triage-outcome condition in institutional dockets (round 6).** The adversarial's round 6 accepts the connection and concentrates its challenge on the triage-outcome condition: whether triage-enabled challenge filing can produce observable post-deployment challenge rate increases in the specific institutional dockets where established practitioners form enforcement beliefs. Three structural features are pressed as preventing this.

*Institutional motivation gap — response.* The adversarial correctly identifies that triage reduces detection costs but that challenge-filing costs remain variable and require organizational policy infrastructure. The argument, however, addresses a maximally demanding interpretation of the condition: systematic conversion of all detected violations into filed challenges across a caseload of hundreds of matters. Triage's distinctive contribution in the institutional setting is selective escalation — identifying the five or ten highest-value violations from a detection pool of fifty at near-zero identification cost. The policy required for this is categorically different from a policy requiring systematic challenge-filing across the full caseload. Resource-constrained institutional practitioners already exercise selective escalation routinely: public defenders litigate constitutional arguments, procedural defenses, and evidentiary challenges selectively across high-volume caseloads, without organizational policies governing every instance of each type. Compliance-challenge selective escalation based on triage ranking does not require new structural infrastructure different in kind from what institutional practitioners already practice. The adversarial's institutional motivation gap argument establishes that no systematic compliance-challenge policies currently exist; it does not establish that such policies cannot be developed at the selective-escalation level the triage mechanism actually requires. Brazilian public defenders' coordinating bodies have developed specialized litigation units for constitutional reform, criminal justice, and consumer protection matters — selective litigation coordination is within demonstrated institutional capacity. Whether compliance-challenge coordination is similarly tractable is an open empirical question; the adversarial has established a current-policy absence, not a structural impossibility.

*Threshold observability — response.* The adversarial's threshold observability argument presupposes that compliance challenges are embedded in an undifferentiated distribution of background procedural challenges and that practitioners update enforcement beliefs from the aggregate procedural challenge rate. This presupposition does not hold for art. 489, §1º compliance challenges. Such challenges are distinguishable procedural actions: they invoke specific statutory provisions, take specific procedural forms (embargos de declaração characterizing fundamentação inadequacy under art. 489, §1º; agravo regimental on fundamentação grounds; constitutional complaints citing inadequate engagement with fundamentos determinantes in binding-precedent cases), and are visible to practitioners as a distinct challenge category. A practitioner monitoring their docket can track the compliance-specific challenge rate independently of the aggregate procedural challenge rate. If a docket that previously had zero compliance challenges begins generating several per month, that constitutes a categorical emergence rather than an incremental increase in a noisy signal — a near-infinite percentage increase in a previously empty category, observable regardless of its small absolute weight in the total docket volume. Bayesian updating on rare-event categories with distinguishable signal types is more sensitive than updating on background noise rates: a prior near-zero is substantially moved by very few observations of a clearly distinguishable signal. The adversarial's threshold argument addresses a signal-in-noise scenario; compliance challenges are distinguishable from procedural noise by their specific citation and doctrinal form. The relevant threshold for belief updating is the per-category threshold, not the aggregate-docket threshold.

*Case-separation routing effect — response.* The adversarial's routing effect argument correctly establishes that CPC arts. 1036, §1º and 1037, §9º route configuration-varied cases — those with the highest violation density — out of the main high-volume docket into separate proceedings. The inference from this structural fact — that practitioners in the main docket do not observe the compliance outcomes that concentrate in the separated proceedings — requires an enforcement belief premise that does not hold: that practitioners form enforcement beliefs from one specific docket category in isolation from the separated proceedings that emerge from their own caseloads.

A practitioner embedded in a repetitive-resource docket does not operate exclusively in the main proceedings of that docket category. When a case the practitioner is handling presents a factual configuration the presiding judge determines warrants separate processing under art. 1037, §9º, the case moves into a separated proceeding — but it remains the same practitioner's case, before the same system, with the same compliance obligations. A compliance challenge that succeeds in the separated proceeding is observed by the practitioner who litigated it. Established practitioners who regularly file in repetitive-resource dockets accumulate enforcement beliefs across the full scope of their practice in that system — main proceedings and separated proceedings combined — not only in the specific docket category that the routing effect selects for lower violation density. The routing effect correctly identifies where compliance challenge outcomes concentrate; it does not create an observational barrier between those outcomes and the practitioners whose enforcement beliefs the mechanism targets.

A further limitation on the routing effect: CPC arts. 1036, §1º and 1037, §9º select the most clearly meritorious distinguishing arguments for separate processing — cases where the configuration-specific variation is substantively significant enough to warrant suspension of the main proceeding pending separate analysis. This selection removes the extreme tail of the configuration-variance distribution from the main docket. It does not eliminate the intermediate tail: cases where the factual configuration presents a potentially distinguishing argument that does not clearly meet the separation threshold. Automated triage applied to the intermediate tail identifies compliance challenges with non-trivial expected value that systematic professional review would deprioritize as insufficiently clear-cut for selective escalation. The routing effect constrains the mechanism's detection value within the main docket but does not reduce it to zero.

**Position after round 6.** The three round-6 challenges are engaged, not dissolved. The institutional motivation gap correctly identifies that converting triage outputs to filed challenges requires organizational policy; the response contests the level at which the policy must operate (selective escalation vs. systematic filing) and the inference from current-policy absence to structural foreclosure. The threshold observability constraint correctly identifies that absolute rate increases matter for belief updating; the response contests the presupposition of signal undifferentiation, showing compliance challenges form an observable category distinct from background procedural noise. The case-separation routing effect correctly identifies that high-violation-density cases concentrate in separated proceedings; the response contests the inference of docket-bounded enforcement belief updating for practitioners whose practice spans both main and separated proceedings. Each response challenges a structural premise of the adversarial's argument rather than denying the adversarial's empirical observations. None of the round-6 challenges establishes that the triage-outcome condition is structurally impossible to meet in institutional dockets. Each specifies conditions under which meeting the condition is more or less demanding. The scope restriction maintained after round 5 continues to hold: the mechanism's deterrence value in the institutional practitioner segment is conditional on selective-escalation policy development and on compliance-challenge rates reaching the observable-categorical-emergence level in specific target dockets.

## 4. Scope of the Support

This defense strengthens the paper's partial, differential claim for the domain where formal statutory compliance standards are operative. It establishes: (a) the constitutive capital critique is bounded by the exogenous standard for the structural compliance core, and the mechanism's coverage value extends to the high-volume, low-review population where professional review is absent — not just to marginal detection over professional review in high-stakes cases; (b) the commitment-displacement argument is asymmetric, operating for new entrants through an alternative record-building path, and the analytical engagement requirement of art. 489, §1º, V–VI constrains how gameable the compliance record is, with configuration-variance in non-standard cases further limiting template reproduction precisely where substantive engagement violations are most likely; (c) local repeated-game reputation accumulation operates through direct bilateral court-practitioner observation as a first-stage mechanism, independent of the attribution intermediaries that block wide network propagation; (d) the deterrence pathway operates through practitioners in the documentation-production stage; the practitioner-side reformulation is structurally distinct from the judicial deterrence channel on habitus, sanction structure, and credibility mechanism; within the practitioner segment, the pathway operates most clearly for competitive-market practitioners facing quality-sensitive clients; for institutional practitioners (public defenders, court-appointed counsel), the pathway requires either CNJ institutional accountability mechanisms calibrated to compliance-specific quality at the coordinating body level or observable post-deployment triage-based challenge rate increases in the specific target dockets sufficient to shift established practitioners' enforcement beliefs; the round-6 response establishes that the institutional motivation gap addresses systematic challenge-filing policies rather than selective-escalation policies that triage makes feasible, that compliance challenges form an observable categorical signal distinct from background procedural noise, and that case-separation routing concentrates compliance outcomes in separated proceedings while leaving the same practitioners' enforcement belief updating spanning both main and separated docket contexts; none of these responses dissolves the underlying empirical contingency, but each shows the round-6 challenges do not establish structural impossibility.

The defense does not establish the full democratization prediction. Cross-interaction propagation beyond specific court-practitioner relationships, and practitioner attribution through the reputational network to its authoritative nodes, remain constrained by the attribution gap. Court-side reputation accumulation through repeated practitioner observation faces structural distribution barriers — sortition, judicial mobility, and abbreviated adjudicative processing — that the defense accepts as genuine constraints in high-volume Brazilian courts. The judicial deterrence channel is constrained by institutional sanction structure, the detection-to-credibility gap as applied to judicial belief formation, and the surface-adaptation pattern Paper 1G documents for the judicial population. For the institutional practitioner segment in the target population, the deterrence pathway faces the three round-5 obstacles the defense engages and does not fully resolve: competitive market accountability is absent, CNJ mechanisms are structurally available but operationally unspecified, and the campo jurídico-level habitus transmission argument identifies a plausible surface-adaptation channel in the practitioner context that Paper 1G does not directly address. The defense establishes that the deterrence pathway operates for competitive-market practitioners in the coverage-extension population, that the mechanism in this segment is not zero, and that the first-stage channels are not entirely closed. It does not establish uniform deterrence across the full institutional practitioner population, and it does not establish that the mechanism operates at the systemic, network-wide scale the paper's strongest prediction requires.

The defense does not address Paper 1F's broader reputational analysis (§2–§3) of network proxies, win-history tracking, and credential-based signaling. The adversarial did not attack that descriptive infrastructure; neither does this support extend it.

## 5. Conditions Under Which This Support Would Fail

**(a) The formal compliance standard is shown to be interpretively constituted in the core cases.** If the paradigm categories (precedent omission, statutory provision omission, internal inconsistency) require contested interpretive judgment rather than structural textual comparison in most cases, the exogenous-standard argument loses its grip and the constitutive capital critique regains force across the operative domain.

**(b) Kreps's commitment framework is shown to require institutional investment specifically.** If commitment records require institutional investment (not just observable output history) to function as separating signals — because institutional investment is the only costly signal that cannot be cheaply mimicked across a single interaction — then the commitment-displacement argument fails. This would require showing that accumulating verified quality records across many arguments is cheaply mimicable in a way that institutional credentials are not.

**(c) The decision-text diffusion path is shown to be practically inaccessible to the reputational network.** If courts' formal compliance records do not propagate through the professional network in ways that influence reputational assessments — because practitioners and judges do not track outcomes of formal compliance challenges — then the diffusion path identified here is nominal rather than effective, and the network diffusion problem is not narrowed.

**(d) Structural compliance core violations are shown to be concentrated in proceedings where professional review already operates, and substantive engagement violations are shown to be rare in high-volume dockets.** If empirical analysis establishes that the population of proceedings where structural compliance failures go undetected is small — because high-volume repetitive resource dockets are both formally and substantively compliant, and because non-standard case configurations generating substantive engagement violations are infrequent even in those dockets — the coverage-extension argument loses both its formal and its substantive scale claim.

**(e) The Lean 4 pipeline tests formal completeness rather than analytical engagement.** If the pipeline's implementation (`pipeline_lean_argdown.md`) checks citation presence, statutory reference inclusion, and dispositif non-contradiction without testing whether fundamentos determinantes were identified and whether distinguishing facts were specified, the mimicry-resistance argument fails. Agents optimizing for measurable formal markers would satisfy the pipeline's tests without providing the analytical engagement art. 489, §1º, V–VI requires, and the compliance record would become a gameable proxy of the same type the paper's mechanism is designed to replace.

**(f) Courts do not maintain repeated-game observation of individual practitioner compliance records.** If the courts that receive the highest volume of compliance challenges operate without systematic observation of which practitioners consistently produce correct compliance arguments — because volume, judge turnover, or case-management systems do not permit tracking past interactions with specific practitioners — the local Kreps reputation-accumulation channel does not operate. The first-stage democratization requires that courts observe practitioner behavior repeatedly; if the observing party does not retain and use past interaction records, stage (a) fails along with stage (b). The structural distribution barriers identified in §3.3 (sortition, rotation, abbreviated processing) constitute the primary threat to this condition in high-volume Brazilian courts.

**(g) Deterrence effects are shown to require documentation scale to operate.** If ex ante compliance incentives in the Brazilian judicial context require demonstrated enforcement at systemic scale — not merely credible detection with non-zero challenge probability — and if the utilization constraint reduces the challenge conversion rate below the threshold needed to create credible deterrence, then the deterrence pathway does not operate independently of the documentation pathway. This failure condition now applies primarily to the judicial deterrence channel; the practitioner-side deterrence channel specified in §3.4 operates from detection-stage credibility rather than enforcement-rate credibility.

**(h) Configuration-variance in high-volume dockets is shown to be low, or courts in those dockets cannot detect template reproduction.** If the non-standard case configurations where template reproduction fails are rare within high-volume standardized dockets — because the governing legal question is sufficiently settled that nearly all individual cases fall within the template's coverage — the configuration-variance limit on mimicry does not apply at meaningful scale. Additionally, if courts in high-volume dockets cannot accumulate bilateral observations sufficient to detect template reproduction across repeated cases (contradicting the adversarial's own §4.3 assertion), then the same limitation applies to quality accumulation for local Kreps purposes. Either condition would require resolving the adversarial's internal tension between its §4.3 accepted reply (courts detect template reproduction through bilateral observation) and its local Kreps incompatibility argument (courts cannot systematically track practitioner compliance quality in high-volume dockets).

**(i) The campo jurídico-level habitus is shown to generate surface-adaptation in practitioner brief-production analogous to what Paper 1G documents in judicial decision-making.** The adversarial's round 5 §3.5 identifies shared legal education as the transmission channel for the patrimonial habitus across judicial and practitioner roles, and argues that the same socialization that produces conviction-based reasoning with covering fundamentação in judges may produce compliance-vocabulary text without genuine analytical engagement in brief-writers. If this transfer is established — by showing that the practitioner brief-writing population in the target dockets responds to detection risk through surface compliance language addition (deploying fundamentos determinantes vocabulary without genuine case-specific doctrinal analysis) rather than through substantive argumentative improvement — then the §3.4 deterrence pathway's distinction from the judicial deterrence channel collapses for the institutional practitioner segment. Establishing the transfer requires evidence about what the habitus produces specifically in the brief-production context, not merely that the educational transmission channel is shared; Paper 1G documents the judicial context and does not address the practitioner brief-production context directly.

**(j) Triage-enabled challenge filing is shown to produce no observable post-deployment challenge rate increase in the specific institutional dockets where established practitioners form enforcement beliefs.** The §3.4 deterrence pathway for established practitioners in established practice patterns depends on the post-deployment challenge rate in their specific docket populations being observably above the pre-deployment near-zero level. If the utilization constraint prevents triage conversion from producing any observable rate increase in the specific institutional dockets the coverage-extension argument targets — because even marginal challenge filing exceeds the resource capacity of the parties operating in those specific populations — then both the information penetration argument and the enforcement credibility argument from adversarial round 5 persist simultaneously for established practitioners: they do not observe practice-context deployment signals through challenge outcomes, and their enforcement beliefs remain anchored to the pre-deployment near-zero rate. The detection-stage/enforcement-stage distinction survives for new-docket entrants without established base-rate histories, but not for established practitioners if the post-deployment challenge rate does not shift observably in their practice contexts.
