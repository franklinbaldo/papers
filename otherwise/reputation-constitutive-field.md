# The Constitutive Field Problem: Why Cheaper Verification May Not Devalue Legal Reputational Capital

---

## 1. Thesis Attacked

"Reputação como Mecanismo de Coordenação no Sistema Jurídico" (Baldo, 2025; `paper1F_reputacao_sistema_juridico.md`) defends a two-part prediction about the Brazilian legal system:

> "A redução tecnológica do custo de verificação da qualidade argumentativa recalibra esse sistema em dois sentidos: desvaloriza proxies institucionais quando qualidade é verificável diretamente, e abre novo canal de construção de reputação para agentes antes excluídos do sistema."

The prediction has two components. First, when argumentative quality becomes directly verifiable at lower cost, institutional proxies (affiliation, win history, credentials, network) lose relative value as reputation-formation inputs. Second, agents previously excluded from the reputation network can build reputation through verified quality alone, democratizing access to the system. This attack targets the causal mechanism linking cheaper verification to proxy devaluation to democratization.

---

## 2. Faithful Reconstruction

The paper's argument is strongest when understood through its three-stage structure.

**Stage 1 (Informational diagnosis):** Argumentative quality in law is a credence good — expensive or impossible to verify even after consumption. A judge receiving a petition cannot cheaply verify whether it correctly identifies the *ratio decidendi* of the relevant precedent, whether a proposed distinction is genuine, or whether an omission weakens the fundamentação. This creates information asymmetry of the type Akerlof analyzed: buyers (decision-makers) who cannot distinguish quality from non-quality pay for expected quality; sellers (practitioners) of genuine quality cannot extract a premium; quality production incentives collapse.

**Stage 2 (Proxy mechanism):** The legal system avoids quality collapse by operating a distributed reputation network. Observable proxies — institutional affiliation, win history, credentials, professional network — function as screening devices: they correlate with quality without directly measuring it. Reputation, in the paper's framework, is "the decentralized price of argumentative quality — the mechanism through which information about quality propagates through the network without direct verification." The analysis correctly notes that proxies are imperfect and that agents with accumulated reputational capital have a structural interest in maintaining the informational opacity that makes proxies necessary.

**Stage 3 (Technology prediction):** When verification technology reduces the cost of direct quality assessment, the screening function of proxies diminishes. The argument follows the structure of information disclosure: when quality becomes visible, signals that substitute for quality visibility lose informational value. The paper integrates Kreps's repeated-game analysis by noting that reputation serves as a credible commitment device — but the paper's prediction is that cheaper verification reduces the need for the commitment device by making direct assessment possible, lowering the value of the proxy through which the commitment was built.

The paper's strongest version is its own: it does not claim proxies disappear but that their "peso relativo na formação de reputação diminui quando verificação direta se torna mais barata" — a partial, differential claim that is more defensible than a strong devaluation claim.

## 3. The Attack

### 3.1 The Constitutive Capital Problem

The paper draws on Bourdieu's concepts of symbolic capital and the legal field. In Bourdieu's framework, the legal field is not simply a market where quality is produced exogenously and then signaled. The field constitutes the standards through which quality is recognized: what counts as a sound argument, as genuine precedent distinction, as adequate *ratio* engagement, is determined in part by agents recognized as competent judges of these things — and recognition of competence is itself a function of capital distribution within the field.

Verification technology trained on legal production would encode existing quality standards. The systems the paper's program proposes — formal verification pipelines, structured argument auditing — are calibrated against legal argumentation as it is produced and recognized in the current field. "Verified quality" would be "quality as recognized by the current field," which is quality structured by existing capital relations. Under this reading, proxies would not devalue upon the introduction of verification technology; they would be embedded in the quality standard the technology verifies.

The paper's exogenous-standard response has force for a defined core class. CPC 2015 arts. 489, §1º, V–VI and 927 establish obligatory compliance requirements for fundamentação that pre-exist and constrain current field practice. These provisions represent the kind of external normative shock that can create standards existing capital holders cannot unilaterally define. For paradigm cases — omission of required precedent engagement, absence of stated statutory provisions, internal inconsistency — the compliance standard is identifiable through structural textual comparison without interpretive weighting. For this core class, the constitutive capital critique loses most of its force.

The inverse relationship between exogenous-standard clarity and mechanism value is the residual objection. Cases in the structural compliance core are cases that competent readers identify through ordinary professional review without verification technology: a senior practitioner reviewing an adversary's brief already identifies the missing precedent citation; a court reviewing a challenged decision already sees the statutory provision gap. Verification technology on these cases is a cost-reduction tool — it makes raising compliance objections cheaper and more systematic — but it does not displace the reputational evaluation of argument analytical quality, because the cases it catches are already visible to professional review without it.

Where verification technology would add value beyond ordinary professional review — identifying inadequate ratio decidendi engagement at the doctrinal-specific level, detecting substitution of principle-level citation for required doctrinal analysis, assessing whether the argument addresses the fundamentos determinantes the case demanded — the question of what counts as adequate engagement is determined through interpretive judgment that existing field practice structures. The constitutive capital critique retains force precisely where the mechanism's added value is highest. The core-class defense succeeds where the mechanism is weakest and fails where the mechanism needs to be strongest for systemic recalibration.

### 3.2 The Signaling Persistence Problem

The paper cites Spence (1973) on job market signaling and Kreps on repeated-game reputation. Both frameworks present structural difficulties the paper does not fully engage.

In Spence's separating equilibrium, high-quality agents invest in costly signals to distinguish themselves from low-quality agents. The signal's value comes from two sources: (a) its informational component — the proxy's correlation with quality; and (b) its commitment component — the cost, which screens out low-quality mimics because the signal is cheaper for high-quality agents to produce than for low-quality agents to replicate. Cheaper output verification disrupts component (a) while leaving component (b) intact.

The Kreps framework does not require that commitment history take the form of institutional investment; observable verified output history can function as a commitment record in principle. This is accepted. The commitment-displacement argument is also accepted as asymmetric across new entrants and incumbents: for incumbents, cheaper output verification diminishes the informational component of proxies while leaving the commitment component intact; for new entrants, who lack the institutional investment record constituting the commitment component, the alternative path through verified output accumulation is the mechanism's democratizing contribution. The democratization claim is most defensible in this differential form.

The residual objection concerns mimicry-resistance for informed optimization. Kreps's separating equilibrium requires that commitment signals be costly to mimic at scale. Whether formal compliance records share this property depends on whether the compliance standard captures quality distinctions requiring genuine analytical depth.

The statutory upper bound is accepted: art. 489, §1º, V–VI requires identification of fundamentos determinantes and specification of distinguishing facts — not citation inclusion alone — raising mimicry costs above structural formal-marker optimization.

The bound does not apply with equal force to informed mimicry. The same diffusion channel the paper relies on for reputation propagation makes a growing corpus of prior compliant compliance determinations available: decision texts published through official channels document what fundamentos determinantes identification looks like for specific precedents in specific case constellations. An agent with access to this corpus can reproduce the pattern of fundamentos determinantes identification for controlling precedents from prior compliant decisions without performing the independent doctrinal analysis that distinguishes genuine quality engagement from template reproduction. The mechanism's success conditions generate the conditions for its own circumvention over time.

### 3.3 The Network Diffusion and Attribution Gap

The paper's democratization prediction requires that verified quality signals propagate through the reputation network to agents with reputational authority without being filtered or amplified by existing capital holders. Three conditions are required: legibility and access of verified quality scores to decision-makers in formats they consult and trust; cross-interaction propagation through the reputation network; and absence of counter-signal investment by existing capital holders.

The decision-text channel is accepted as a real propagation path. When an argument's compliance with art. 489, §1º is formally demonstrable and a court acts on it, the quality record is produced in the decision text, published through official channels (Diário da Justiça, court websites) independent of practitioner networks. Counter-signal investment by high-capital agents applies most forcefully to peer-review systems; it applies less forcefully to formal statutory compliance records produced in the course of judicial proceedings and determined by the court itself.

The attribution gap constrains the decision-text channel's contribution to practitioner reputation. Court decisions record compliance findings attributed to case outcomes — "case X: fundamentação inadequacy found; remand ordered" — not to practitioner identity. A compliance victory enters the official archive as a case outcome. For this to become a practitioner reputation input, the network must attribute the outcome to practitioner Y — requiring intermediaries: institutional profiles listing significant victories, academic publications analyzing counsel strategy, co-counsel networks tracking who argued what, professional directories recording significant outcomes. These intermediaries are capital-holder-structured.

A practitioner's compliance victory in Tribunal X propagates to counter-parties in Tribunal Y only through these same intermediaries. The decision-text channel supports local reputation accumulation at the specific court where the compliance argument was made; the networked propagation the paper's democratization prediction requires cannot be established from this channel alone, and cross-court propagation remains bound by the attribution gap.

### 3.4 Coverage Extension: Utilization Constraint and Conditional Prevalence Gap

The paper's most promising terrain for the mechanism operates in the high-volume, low-review docket population — proceedings where professional review is systematically absent due to resource constraints (public defenders carrying hundreds of simultaneous matters, civil defendants without specialized counsel in high-volume collection dockets). In this population, the constitutive capital critique is weakest and the mechanism's coverage value is most distinct from what professional review already provides.

Two constraints bound this claim.

*Utilization constraint.* Coverage extension requires not only detection of compliance failures but that detected failures result in effective compliance challenges producing decision-text records. In the high-volume, low-review population, the resource constraints preventing systematic professional review apply equally to challenge-filing capacity: a public defender carrying hundreds of simultaneous matters who detects compliance failures through automated tools cannot systematically litigate compliance objections across that caseload. Automated detection does introduce a cost-structure asymmetry: review cost scales linearly per filing while automated detection approaches near-zero marginal cost per additional filing once infrastructure is established. This asymmetry allows effective triage — identifying the five strongest violations among fifty detected. But triage producing a small number of compliance records contributes marginally to the systemic recalibration the paper predicts; scale requires that violations systematically enter the record.

*Conditional prevalence gap.* The claim that meaningful violation rates exist in the high-volume, low-review population faces structural complications. Repetitive-resource proceedings under CPC arts. 1036–1041 are institutionally characterized by settled legal questions and decision templates calibrated to the compliance requirements those proceedings formalize.

The response that formal structural compliance ≠ substantive engagement compliance is accepted: standardized templates address citation presence and statutory references without ensuring case-specific engagement with the specific fundamentos determinantes of each invoked precedent. However, in repetitive-resource proceedings, the ratio of the threshold precedent is fixed and publicly known — that is the mechanism's definitional condition. Substantive engagement under art. 489, §1º, V in this context requires applying a fixed and known ratio to the present facts — which is the templated operation. The non-standard case configurations requiring fresh case-specific doctrinal analysis are those that fall outside the repetitive-resource designation: CPC arts. 1036, §1º and 1037, §9º provide mechanisms for separating cases that present genuine distinguishing factual or legal arguments from the main repetitive-resource proceeding. The configuration-specific tail the supportive identifies as the mechanism's highest-value domain is, by institutional design, routed out of the repetitive-resource docket through these separation procedures before the automated detection system's coverage applies at scale.

### 3.5 The Deterrence Pathway's Credibility Problem

A deterrence pathway targeting practitioners in the documentation-production stage is structurally distinct from a deterrence pathway targeting judicial actors in the decision-evaluation stage. The judicial deterrence channel faces three obstacles in the Brazilian institutional context: the series' own Paper 1G documents that the patrimonial habitus produces surface adaptation rather than substantive compliance change in response to normative pressure; the sanction structure for judicial actors is institutional rather than personal under art. 95, CF/88, with appellate reversal already a normalized event in the relevant dockets; and courts update enforcement-probability beliefs from observable challenge-filing rates, which the utilization constraint holds near-zero in the target population. These obstacles correctly describe the constraints on the judicial channel. The practitioner reformulation is structurally independent: practitioners face personal case-outcome consequences as the enforcement mechanism, are not the actors whose habitus Paper 1G most directly analyzes, and are argued to form compliance-risk beliefs from direct awareness of detection tool capabilities rather than from observed challenge rates. Three structural problems constrain this reformulation in the target population.

*Information penetration barrier.* The practitioner-side deterrence requires that practitioners in the documentation-production stage know that automated compliance auditing is applied to their submissions. In the high-volume, low-review population, this knowledge depends on one of three channels: opposing counsel who deploys automated detection; courts that conduct administrative compliance screening; or the practitioner's own institutional client. Each channel is structurally constrained in the target population. The opposing party is resource-constrained — the same resource constraint that forecloses systematic professional review also forecloses systematic deployment and signaling of automated detection to opposing counsel; detection occurring in the opposing party's workflow is not observable by the practitioner whose behavior must change. The coverage-extension premise accepts that courts in high-volume dockets cannot conduct systematic compliance review; court administrative screening as the detection channel is structurally inconsistent with this acceptance. Institutional client auditing applies to practitioners employed by large organizations with internal compliance monitoring functions — the sophisticated-practitioner segment where the inverse-relationship objection applies, not the high-volume, low-review population the coverage-extension argument targets. Individual resource-constrained clients in the target population do not maintain compliance auditing of the practitioners representing them.

The most natural response is that tool awareness diffuses through professional networks, academic publications, and general market channels, creating a population-wide detection-risk prior independent of submission-specific knowledge of opposing-party deployment. This response relocates the mechanism from the target population to the general market. The behavioral adjustment the deterrence pathway requires is specific: practitioners must believe that their current filings carry a non-negligible detection risk. A general market prior about tool availability — formed from professional network sources, academic channels, and technology publications — generates awareness at the level of tool existence without generating the submission-specific detection risk belief the deterrence chain requires. The practitioner who knows that automated compliance tools exist in the abstract but has never observed a compliance challenge in their specific docket does not face the same compliance incentive as the practitioner who knows that detection is applied to their specific submissions.

*Enforcement credibility through repeated observation.* The supportive distinguishes the practitioner's credibility mechanism from the court's: practitioners form detection-risk beliefs from known tool capabilities at the detection stage, not from observable challenge-filing rates at the enforcement stage. This distinction holds for one-shot interactions where the practitioner has no prior docket history to observe. It does not hold for practitioners in established practice patterns in the target dockets.

Practitioners working repeatedly in the same court and docket type observe enforcement rates across interactions. A practitioner who files before Tribunal X in docket type Y over many cases and observes near-zero compliance challenges in that context forms a near-zero expected enforcement probability for subsequent filings — not because they discount the detection tool's theoretical capability, but because observable base rates in their practice context update enforcement beliefs through the same mechanism the adversarial identified for courts. The detection-stage vs. enforcement-rate distinction the supportive draws correctly characterizes practitioners entering new dockets without prior enforcement history; it does not describe practitioners in established practice patterns in the target population, where the utilization constraint that holds challenge rates near-zero in the aggregate also holds challenge rates near-zero in the specific practice contexts those practitioners observe over time.

*Competitive market scope and habitus in the campo jurídico.* The practitioner reformulation requires that practitioners respond to detection risk through case-outcome accountability, competitive market discipline, and incentive structures not governed by the judicial habitus Paper 1G documents. Two limitations apply in the target population.

The competitive market scope argument has an asymmetric relationship with the coverage-extension population. Public defenders are institutional employees whose compensation and career trajectories are governed by civil service structures, not case outcome rates. Court-appointed counsel in criminal proceedings receive standardized fees; inadequate fundamentação does not affect fee recovery. In high-volume dockets with high baseline defeat rates, outcome variation attributable to compliance quality is not distinguishable from variance attributable to case merit, judicial assessment, or procedural factors. The client in the target population — the criminal appellant represented by a public defender, the civil defendant without specialized counsel — cannot attribute case-outcome variation to practitioner compliance quality. The credence-good opacity that creates the market failure the mechanism addresses applies equally to clients' ability to hold practitioners accountable for compliance quality. The competitive market discipline the supportive invokes as the distinguishing feature is weakest precisely where the mechanism's claimed value is greatest.

Paper 1G's habitus analysis operates at the level of the campo jurídico, not the individual judicial role. The genealogical analysis traces the patrimonial habitus through Brazilian legal education as the socialization mechanism — the same educational system that constitutes the shared habitus of the campo jurídico before the differentiation of judicial from practitioner roles. Practitioners trained in the system Paper 1G identifies as calibrated to conviction-based reasoning internalize the same field-level disposition that produces surface adaptation in response to compliance pressure. The surface-adaptation pattern Paper 1G identifies — using the vocabulary and formal markers of compliance without substantive behavioral change — is available to practitioners who have learned the compliance vocabulary from published decisions. Applying this vocabulary does not require genuine fundamentos determinantes analysis; it requires familiarity with what compliance text looks like, which practitioners acquire from the same field socialization the paper's democratization effect is meant to alter. The claim that practitioners face different incentive structures than judges correctly characterizes market-competitive practitioners with quality-sensitive clients; it does not describe institutional practitioners and low-stakes specialists in the target population, for whom the credence-good problem applies to their clients' ability to evaluate analytical compliance quality precisely as it applies to courts' ability to evaluate judicial decision quality.

### 3.6 Local Kreps: Structural Distribution Barriers

The coverage-extension and local Kreps premises concern different actors performing different cognitive operations. Coverage extension concerns the party's systematic per-filing compliance auditing — dedicated attention to the compliance question per filing, scaling with filing volume. Local Kreps concerns the court's byproduct quality observation from ordinary adjudication — quality impressions accumulating as a natural consequence of deciding cases, without adding a distinct compliance-review burden per filing. These are different actors (party vs. court) with different cognitive operations and resource conditions. The logical incompatibility between the two premises is dissolved under this distinction.

Dissolving the logical incompatibility does not establish that local Kreps accumulation operates effectively in high-volume Brazilian court populations. Three structural features disrupt the accumulation independently.

*Case distribution by sortition.* Brazilian courts distribute cases through random sortition (distribuição aleatória por sorteio), both at first-instance and appellate levels. A practitioner filing before any given tribunal does not reliably appear before the same desembargador, panel, or judge across a series of filings. Byproduct quality observation accumulates in the specific judge or panel that adjudicates a series of cases; random distribution disperses observations of the same practitioner across many judges within the same court. No individual judge accumulates sufficient observations of that practitioner to form a stable quality assessment through byproduct adjudication alone.

*Judge turnover and vara rotation.* In high-volume first-instance courts, where the coverage-extension claim most directly applies, judges rotate through different vara assignments and are promoted to appellate positions. A judge who begins accumulating byproduct quality observations about a specific practitioner may be reassigned or promoted before accumulation reaches the threshold for stable reputational assessment.

*Abbreviated adjudicative processing.* In high-volume dockets, courts operate through clerk-mediated processing: assessores or clerks prepare relatorios summarizing filings for judicial approval. Under this model, the quality-impression byproduct from engaging with individual briefs accumulates in the clerk who prepared the relatorio, not in the judge who approved it. For this byproduct to serve the local Kreps function, the same clerk must accumulate observations of the same practitioner across cases (requiring clerk assignment continuity), and the judge must receive and integrate those clerk-level assessments across cases over time (requiring judge-assignment stability and institutionalized information transfer). In high-volume dockets with rotation-based clerk assignment, volume-based case distribution, and elevated judge turnover, these conditions may not be satisfied.

Additionally, cross-case consistency detection — which bilateral observation enables — is a coarse statistical task (recognizing repeated patterns across filings) while per-filing quality assessment is a fine-grained analytical task (evaluating the correctness of analytical conclusions for a specific configuration). The bilateral observation capacity conceded for detecting template reproduction does not extend to accumulating quality impressions for reputational formation, which requires the finer-grained per-filing assessment.

## 4. Anticipated Reply

### 4.1 The Constitutive Capital and Inverse-Relationship Anticipated Replies

The most natural response to the constitutive capital argument is that it proves too much: if quality standards were entirely constituted by field capital, legal fields could never change, which contradicts historical evidence of legal field transformation (including CPC 2015 itself). This response has force, and the constitutive capital objection should not be read as claiming quality is entirely field-constituted.

The narrower claim stands: the paper's specific mechanism (cheaper verification → proxy devaluation) requires verification technology to operate against an exogenous quality standard that provides independent traction against existing capital distributions. Technology that audits compliance with existing legal norms does not, by itself, constitute such a shock; it verifies quality-as-currently-understood, which is quality-as-currently-structured. The CPC 2015 normative shock is accepted as genuine. The inverse-relationship residual applies to what verification adds beyond what professional review already captures.

On the coverage-extension response to the inverse relationship: this correctly identifies a population where the core-class exogenous standard applies and the inverse relationship does not. The utilization constraint in §3.4 addresses whether the mechanism actually operates in that population at the scale the coverage-extension argument claims.

### 4.2 Coverage Extension, Utilization, and Deterrence Anticipated Replies

The most natural response to the utilization constraint is that detection capability enables effective triage — and that even imperfect utilization creates deterrence independent of documentation scale.

On triage: triage producing a small number of compliance records contributes marginally to systemic recalibration.

On the information penetration barrier: the most natural response is that tool awareness diffuses through professional networks, academic publications, and general market channels, creating a population-wide detection-risk prior independent of submission-specific observation. The adversarial's §3.5 presses that population-wide tool-existence awareness does not substitute for submission-specific detection risk: practitioners in established practice patterns observe near-zero challenge rates in their specific dockets and update enforcement beliefs from those observable base rates, regardless of abstract awareness that tools exist in the broader market. The two obstacles — information penetration and enforcement credibility through repeated observation — are connected: they jointly require not only that practitioners know tools exist, but that practitioners in the target dockets believe detection is applied to their specific submissions at rates that sustain non-negligible compliance risk in their practice context.

On the competitive market scope and habitus arguments: the most natural response is that practitioners in all segments, including public defenders and court-appointed counsel, face quality incentives through institutional evaluation mechanisms, peer professional culture, and internalized standards from legal education. The adversarial's §3.5 does not claim that practitioners face zero quality incentives, but that the specific accountability mechanism the practitioner-side deterrence pathway requires — case-outcome consequences that practitioners can attribute to compliance quality and that clients can observe and act on — is structurally attenuated in the target population. Institutional accountability mechanisms that translate compliance-specific reversal into per-practitioner-institution metrics (CNJ mechanisms applicable to public defenders' coordinating bodies, court appointment systems) would close this gap independently of market discipline. Whether such mechanisms are operational in the specific docket populations the coverage-extension argument identifies has not been established.

On conditional prevalence: the most natural response is that the configuration-specific tail exists even in repetitive-resource dockets. The adversarial's §3.4 presses that the repetitive-resource institutional designation selects settled-ratio cases into the high-volume docket and routes the configuration-varied tail out through case separation procedures (CPC arts. 1036, §1º; 1037, §9º) before the automated detection system's volume coverage applies.

### 4.3 Local Kreps and Mimicry Anticipated Replies

On local Kreps: the most natural response to the sortition and turnover arguments is that reputation accumulates at the tribunal level through horizontal quality-information sharing, or through clerk-mediated processing with clerk-level continuity. The adversarial's §3.6 identifies the institutional conditions for both mechanisms as the critical unestablished requirements: for tribunal-level accumulation, what infrastructure aggregates practitioner-level quality observations across randomly distributed cases? For clerk-mediated accumulation, what conditions ensure clerk assignment continuity, judge-assignment stability, and institutionalized information transfer across the same practitioner's cases over time? Standard Brazilian court organization provides clerk networks tracking case management data, not practitioner quality profiles; case conferencing addresses doctrinal questions, not counsel assessment. Whether the specific docket populations the coverage-extension argument identifies maintain these conditions is an empirical question the supportive has not resolved.

On the §4.3 consistency constraint (bilateral observation detecting template reproduction conceding the same capacity local Kreps requires): template reproduction detection is a coarse cross-case consistency observation (statistical pattern across filings); quality accumulation for local Kreps is per-filing analytical evaluation (correctness of analytical conclusions for the specific configuration). These are different cognitive tasks. The concession on detection does not extend to the quality-accumulation capacity the local Kreps mechanism requires.

On mimicry-resistance: the most natural response is that template reproduction is detectable in repeated interactions. This detection requires the same bilateral accumulation that sortition-based distribution disrupts; the mimicry-detection capacity and the quality-accumulation capacity face the same structural constraints in the target population.

## 5. Scope of the Attack

This attack does not claim that technology has no effect on reputational capital in legal systems, that excluded agents cannot benefit from quality verification tools, or that the paper's general direction is wrong. The claim is bounded and specific to the populations the paper's central thesis most directly concerns: the high-volume, low-review, resource-constrained dockets where the mechanism's systemic and democratizing effects would be most significant.

The constitutive capital problem identifies the inverse relationship between exogenous-standard clarity and mechanism value. The signaling persistence problem identifies that commitment-value proxies survive cheaper output verification, and that mimicry-resistance for informed optimization is not established. The network diffusion problem identifies that the attribution gap prevents the decision-text channel from translating compliance victories into networked practitioner reputation. The coverage-extension argument identifies the high-volume, low-review population as the mechanism's most promising terrain; the utilization constraint and conditional prevalence gap show that scale in that population is bounded by challenge-filing capacity and by the institutional design of repetitive-resource proceedings that routes the configuration-varied cases most likely to generate substantive engagement violations out of the high-volume docket through separation procedures. The deterrence pathway faces three obstacles in the target population: the series' own habitus analysis (Paper 1G) documents that stronger normative pressure produced only surface compliance adaptation in the same institutional context, making ex ante detection risk's capacity for substantive behavioral change structurally doubtful; the sanction structure is institutional rather than personal; and the information channel from detection capability to court belief runs through observable challenge rates, which the utilization constraint holds near-zero in the target population. The local Kreps mechanism is real but disrupted in Brazilian high-volume courts by sortition-based case distribution, judicial mobility and vara rotation, and abbreviated adjudicative processing that attenuates the quality-impression byproduct the mechanism requires.

The attack accepts: the core-class exogenous standard defense (CPC 2015 arts. 489 as a genuine normative shock); the new-entrant asymmetry (the differential competitive effect for new entrants is genuine); the decision-text channel (real but attribution-constrained); the actor/cognitive-operation distinction for local Kreps (party auditing ≠ court adjudicative byproduct); the three judicial deterrence obstacles — habitus resistance, institutional sanction structure, and challenge-rate-mediated credibility — as correctly characterizing the constraints on a deterrence pathway targeting judicial actors; and the structural distinction between the practitioner documentation-production role and the judicial decision-evaluation role as a principled reformulation of the deterrence pathway. What the attack contests is whether the practitioner-side reformulation resolves the mechanism's problems in the high-volume, low-review docket population the coverage-extension argument makes central — specifically: whether practitioners in the target population receive the submission-specific detection-risk signal the channel requires, whether enforcement credibility through repeated observation re-enters through the utilization constraint, and whether the competitive market scope and campo jurídico-level habitus arguments apply to the institutional practitioners and low-stakes specialists who constitute the primary target population.

## 6. Surrender Conditions

The paper's thesis would survive this attack under the following conditions.

**(a) Exogenous quality standard with demonstrated scale in the high-volume population.** The paper identifies a quality standard not constituted by existing field capital and shows the mechanism reaches populations where the inverse-relationship objection does not apply. Partial satisfaction: the structural compliance core's exogenous standard is accepted. For full satisfaction: evidence that parties in the high-volume, low-review population have institutional capacity to convert automated detections into effective compliance challenges at rates sufficient to produce compliance records in decision text at meaningful scale; and evidence that detectable substantive engagement violations — distinct from formal structural violations that templates address and configuration-varied cases that repetitive-resource separation procedures route out — are prevalent at meaningful rates in the high-volume population the mechanism targets.

**(b) Mimicry-resistance for informed optimization.** The paper shows that the formal compliance record is resistant not only to uninformed structural formal-marker optimization but to informed optimization by agents with access to the published corpus of compliant compliance determinations. Meeting this condition requires showing either that the compliance standard requires fresh case-specific doctrinal analysis that cannot be reproduced from prior compliant decision patterns in the standard case, or that courts in the relevant dockets systematically distinguish template reproduction from genuine analysis — subject to the structural distribution constraints identified in §3.6.

**(c) Independent diffusion mechanism reaching new entrants without existing court relationships.** The paper identifies a network diffusion mechanism through which verified quality signals reach reputational authority without being filtered through capital-holder-structured intermediaries. The decision-text channel and local Kreps first-stage channel are accepted as existing. For full satisfaction: the attribution gap must be closed through a mechanism that attributes compliance victories to practitioner identity in a format accessible to the reputational network's authoritative nodes; and the local Kreps mechanism must be shown to operate for practitioners without pre-existing repeated-interaction dyads in Brazilian courts — subject to the sortition-distribution and judicial mobility constraints identified in §3.6.

**(d) Deterrence pathway produces substantive (not surface) behavioral adjustment in the target population.** The deterrence pathway is correctly reformulated as targeting practitioners in the documentation-production stage; the judicial deterrence obstacles (habitus resistance, institutional sanction structure, challenge-rate-mediated credibility) are accepted as correctly characterizing the constraints on the judicial channel. For the practitioner-side deterrence channel to satisfy this condition, three requirements must be met: (i) evidence demonstrates that practitioners in the high-volume, low-review docket population know that automated compliance auditing is applied to their specific submissions — through a channel (opposing counsel, court screening, institutional client) that actually reaches the target population, not merely through general market awareness of tool existence; (ii) evidence demonstrates that enforcement rates in the specific dockets the target population serves are sufficient to sustain non-zero compliance-incentive beliefs through repeated-game observation, or that practitioners in established practice patterns in those dockets update from tool capability rather than from observable challenge rates; (iii) evidence demonstrates that practitioners in the target population face competitive market accountability structures that translate case-outcome consequences into compliance-quality incentives — through either client-facing quality-evaluation mechanisms or institutional accountability systems (CNJ metrics for public defenders' coordinating bodies, court appointment systems) tracking compliance-specific outcomes. Any path requires institutional specificity for the docket populations the coverage-extension argument identifies; the practitioner-targeting reformulation has not yet provided it.

**(e) Local Kreps operates despite structural distribution constraints.** The first-stage reputation accumulation mechanism requires either that the same cognitive agent accumulates sufficient observations of the same practitioner over time, or that an institutional aggregation mechanism performs this function. For this to operate effectively in Brazilian high-volume courts: either sortition-based distribution must be shown to produce systematic repetitions of judge-practitioner or clerk-practitioner dyads sufficient for Kreps accumulation (perhaps through vara specialization that concentrates certain practitioner populations before the same adjudicators), or a clerk-continuity mechanism must be identified through which the same clerks accumulate practitioner quality observations across cases and transfer them to relevant judges over time with sufficient institutional persistence. The specific institutional conditions for these mechanisms in the high-volume docket populations the coverage-extension argument identifies have not been established.

As of round 5, each condition remains open: (a) core-class exogenous standard accepted; utilization evidence and prevalence evidence in the low-review, high-volume population pending; (b) new-entrant asymmetry established; mimicry-resistance against informed optimization unestablished; (c) decision-text and local-Kreps channels established for practitioners already in repeated-interaction positions; new-entrant channel and judicial-attention conditions unresolved; (d) deterrence pathway reformulated as targeting practitioners in the documentation-production stage; judicial deterrence obstacles accepted as correctly characterizing the judicial channel; practitioner-side channel contested on three grounds — information penetration, enforcement credibility through repeated observation, and competitive market scope and campo jurídico-level habitus — none resolved; (e) local Kreps mechanism accepted as structurally available; institutional conditions supporting accumulation in high-volume target dockets — through sortition-resistant practitioner concentration or clerk-level continuity mechanisms — unestablished. The mechanism as articulated may improve the quality and efficiency of legal argument production in the high-engagement, high-resource segment of the Brazilian docket without producing the systemic recalibration and democratization effects the paper's central thesis predicts for the high-volume, low-review population.
