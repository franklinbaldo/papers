**The round 34 shape — three attacks, one per failure condition.**

### Against (p) — Normative-function criterion assumes the conclusion

R33's normative-function criterion: act-type identity is individuated by institutional role — the relator's designation act is constitutive (it creates the authoritative ratio) while the evaluator's reconstruction act is epistemic (it assesses whether the created ratio tracks the votos). Their outputs differ in normative kind even when they share input materials and output format. R31's evaluator-output concession is evidence about the epistemic act-type, not the constitutive one R32 needed it to cover.

My attack: R33's criterion correctly identifies that constitutive acts and epistemic acts have normative products that differ in kind — the designation constitutes the ratio; the accuracy claim does not. But art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentadas* requirement is not about the normative product of an act; it targets the epistemic product of reasoning acts — the basis-reasoning grounding why this determination rather than another was reached — regardless of what normative function the act serves. Both act-types have both a normative product and an epistemic product. The constitutive act's normative product is the designation (which constitutes the ratio); its epistemic product is the basis-reasoning grounding why G1 rather than G2 was designated.

R33 imports the conclusion by defining the constitutive act as one whose output IS only the normative product (the designation), which is precisely Component 1's claim that is at issue — it cannot serve as the criterion that resolves whether Component 1 is correct. The normative-function criterion assumes the answer rather than providing evidence for it.

The verdict-analogy R33 offered confirms the adversarial's position, not the supportive's. Judges' verdicts are paradigmatically constitutive acts (they create the legal finding), yet art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentadas* requirement demonstrably reaches the reasoning basis behind them. The constitutive character of the verdict's normative product does not exempt its epistemic product from the *fundamentadas* standard. R33's own analogy, correctly analyzed, shows that constitutive act-type status is compatible with — not exempting from — the requirement that basis-reasoning be expressed.

New failure condition: (s) A structural argument establishing that the normative-function criterion correctly individuates act-type identity for art. 93, IX CF's analysis AND that the criterion reaches epistemic products of reasoning acts regardless of whether those acts are constitutive or epistemic in their normative role.

### Against (q) — Deliberative census is legally operative precedential content

R33's deliberative-census response: identification-basis content has a determinate structure — it records which justices invoked which ground and reports a plurality result. Its justificatory form is a plurality-count claim (element E was invoked by more justices than F), not a normative chain from legal premises to conclusions. Art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentadas* requirement covers the second type, not the first. The provision ensures legal grounds are articulable and reviewable through legal reasoning; a deliberative census describes the procedural record rather than articulating a legal argument.

My attack: a deliberative census identifying the *fundamento determinante* is not merely a sociological observation — it is a legally operative act whose product has binding precedential force under arts. 926–927 CPC. What the census identifies determines which proposition future courts are obligated to apply as the *fundamento determinante* in citation practice. This binding function means the census basis — the plurality pattern grounding the operative identification — is precisely the type of content art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentadas* requirement reaches: it is the basis for a legally operative determination that structures subsequent legal decision-making.

R33's distinction between "deliberative-accounting content" and "legal-justification content" collapses at the point where the census is the act that determines what courts are legally bound to apply. The provision's accountability function is not satisfied by showing that individual justices' normative chains are in their votos — if the act that converts those chains into a binding *fundamento determinante* identification is itself unaccountable, the institutional accountability gap the provision is designed to close remains. Arts. 926–927 CPC's binding effect does not eliminate art. 93, IX CF's expression requirement; it intensifies the need for it, because the plurality determination has higher normative stakes than an individual justice's opinion.

New failure condition: (t) A structural argument establishing that deliberative-accounting content (who said what; plurality result) does not constitute legal-justification content of the type the provision covers EVEN WHEN the deliberative census is the basis for a legally operative determination with binding precedential force under arts. 926–927 CPC.

### Against (r) — Within-voto-expressed conditionality still requires cross-voto synthesis

R33's two independent responses to (m): (first) the autonomous-support reading — conditional G1 invocations do not establish G1 as an autonomous *fundamento determinante*; G2 with two-justice autonomous support is the correct designation; (second) within-voto-expressed-conditionality — each G1-invoking justice explicitly states the conditionality within their own voto, making the conditional structure detectable from within-voto reading without inter-voto synthesis; the Marks-rule and *Anschlusskontrolle* analogies apply to implicit-conditionality settings, not to Brazilian STF practice.

My attack on both responses: each requires cross-voto synthesis at a step that the ementa-votos comparison alone does not supply.

**Against the autonomous-support reading:** R33's enrichment of Component 2 — tracking not merely which element appears in the most votos but which element has AUTONOMOUS (not conditional) collective support — is a substantive improvement to the claim. But it defeats itself: to apply the autonomous-support check, the relator or evaluator must identify (a) which votos invoke each element, (b) whether each invocation is conditional, and (c) which elements receive autonomous (unconditional) support from sufficient justices. Steps (b) and (c) require reading across multiple justices' votos, not merely reading each voto in isolation. The autonomous-support reading precisely requires the inter-voto synthesis that Component 2's original formulation was supposed to avoid. R33 has not eliminated the inter-voto synthesis requirement — it has restated it more precisely.

**Against the within-voto-expressed-conditionality response:** R33 correctly notes that detecting whether G1 is conditionally invoked is within-voto — each G1 justice says "given G2, proportionality requires X" within their own text. This point is accepted: conditionality is within-voto-expressed and detectable without cross-voto reasoning synthesis. But this addresses only half of the problem. After detecting that all three G1 votos are conditional on G2, the relator or evaluator must determine that G2 constitutes the autonomous *fundamento determinante* — which requires establishing that two other justices independently ground G2 without conditionality. Establishing G2's autonomous collective support requires reading across the two G2-grounding votos to confirm they are independent and unconditional — a cross-voto synthesis step that is not completed within any single voto. The within-voto-expressed-conditionality argument eliminates the need for inter-voto reasoning about conditionality itself; it does not eliminate the cross-voto step of aggregating the autonomous support for the underlying element.

The two responses thus each require at least one cross-voto synthesis step: the autonomous-support reading requires it for autonomy assessment across all votos; the within-voto-expressed-conditionality response requires it for aggregating autonomous G2 support. The ementa-votos comparison the supportive relies on is itself a cross-voto operation. Neither response eliminates the inter-voto synthesis that the adversarial argues should be expressed.

New failure condition: (u) A structural argument establishing that the autonomous *fundamento determinante* in the conditionality case class can be identified without cross-voto synthesis — either by showing the autonomous-support check is completable within individual votos alone, or by showing the cross-voto aggregation step the within-voto-expressed-conditionality argument does not eliminate is already captured by what the ementa-votos comparison constitutively performs.

## What I Considered and Discarded

**Conceding (p) and focusing on (q) and (r).** I examined whether R33's normative-function criterion is a genuine improvement over R32's act-type equivalence challenge. It is more precise: R33 correctly distinguishes normative products from epistemic products and argues that institutional role determines act-type identity. The question is whether "institutional role determines act-type identity for Component 1's expressive-separability claim" amounts to an independent argument or to a restatement of Component 1 itself. I believe the verdict-analogy confirms it is the latter: verdicts are constitutive and yet art. 93, IX CF reaches their reasoning basis. This is not a concession to make.

**Conceding (q) on the ground that R33's census/normative-chain distinction is formally valid.** R33's distinction between deliberative-accounting content and legal-justification content is formally coherent. The question is whether it holds when the census is legally operative. I think the arts. 926–927 CPC binding-force argument shows it does not — the census's legal operativity means its basis is legal-justification content by any functional definition of that phrase. This is worth pressing.

**Conceding both parts of (r) and focusing only on (p) and (q).** R33's two-part response to (m) is the most structurally developed. The autonomous-support reading is a genuine improvement to Component 2, not a mere restatement. The within-voto-expressed-conditionality response correctly identifies a structural difference between Brazilian STF practice and the implicit-conditionality settings the Marks/Anschlusskontrolle analogies describe. I considered whether these two responses together might be sufficient to defeat (m). They are not, because each requires at least one cross-voto synthesis step — autonomy-check for the first response; G2-support aggregation for the second. These cross-voto steps are the residual adversarial claim. But the case is now narrower: it is not that the Marks analogy applies (R33 correctly rebuts that), nor that conditionality is implicit (it is within-voto-expressed). The claim is specifically that G2's autonomous collective support requires cross-voto aggregation not completable within any individual voto.

**Not fabricating STF case evidence.** As in r32, I considered whether I could identify specific STF decisions with conditional-majority reasoning patterns. I cannot do this without primary source access. The structural argument — that cross-voto synthesis is required to aggregate G2's autonomous support after within-voto conditionality is detected — does the same work without requiring case-specific evidence. The synthesis blog's warning that (m/r) is at risk of a loop-closure verdict specifically because neither side has offered institutional evidence is acknowledged: my response engages the within-voto-expressed-conditionality argument on its own terms (accepting that conditionality is within-voto-expressed) rather than restating the Marks/Anschlusskontrolle structural analogies.

## Assessment After R34

Three failure conditions now pressed from the adversarial side on the r33 responses:

**(p)**: Normative-function criterion assumes conclusion. R35 (supportive) must show that the normative-function criterion identifies act-type identity for Component 1's expressive-separability analysis without assuming Component 1's conclusion — AND that the verdict-analogy confirms the supportive's position rather than the adversarial's.

**(q)**: Deliberative census as legally operative content. R35 must show that deliberative-accounting content does not constitute legal-justification content of the type the provision covers EVEN WHEN the census grounds a binding precedential determination under arts. 926–927 CPC. The arts. 926–927 binding-force point requires an affirmative response, not only a restatement of the census/normative-chain distinction.

**(r)**: Cross-voto synthesis residual. R35 must show that the autonomous *fundamento determinante* in the conditionality case class can be identified without cross-voto synthesis — specifically, without the cross-voto aggregation step of establishing G2's independent unconditional support across two justices' votos. The within-voto-expressed-conditionality response eliminated one inter-voto synthesis step (conditionality detection); R35 must address the remaining one (autonomous-support aggregation).

The loop-closure condition for r35: restating the normative-function criterion without engaging the verdict-analogy counterexample; restating the census/normative-chain distinction without engaging the arts. 926–927 binding-force argument; or restating the within-voto-expressed-conditionality response without engaging the G2 autonomous-support aggregation step.
