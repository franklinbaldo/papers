## What I decided to argue

**Coverage extension — two constraints.** The synthesis correctly identified that the round 2 inverse-relationship objection was scoped to the professional-review-reaches population. The supportive round 2 correctly pressed that the mechanism's claimed value in the low-review, high-volume population is a distinct claim that the adversarial had not addressed. Round 3 engages it directly.

*Utilization constraint.* The coverage-extension argument requires not only automated detection but effective challenge filing. Detection and challenge-filing capacity are separate steps. The same resource constraints that prevent systematic professional review in the high-volume, low-review population — public defenders with hundreds of simultaneous matters, civil defendants without specialized counsel in mass collection dockets — equally prevent systematic compliance challenge filing. Detection locates violations but does not supply the institutional capacity to litigate them. The mechanism requires compliance records to enter decision text at meaningful scale; the utilization constraint bounds the rate at which detections convert to such records.

*Conditional prevalence gap.* High-volume dockets are also the dockets with the most advanced compliance standardization. Repetitive-resource proceedings under CPC arts. 1036–1041 are institutionally defined by settled legal questions and standardized decision formats. Administrative dockets in social security, labor, and tax operate under templates refined over years. The structural compliance failures the mechanism targets are least prevalent in dockets whose institutional design is calibrated precisely to produce compliant standardized output. The supportive asserts violations are prevalent; the adversarial contests this is established.

**Mimicry-resistance — informed optimization.** The supportive's mimicry-bound argument correctly establishes the statutory upper bound: art. 489, §1º, V–VI requires analytical engagement, not mere citation inclusion. The adversarial accepts this bound. The adversarial's new argument: the bound applies to uninformed mimicry. The same official publication channels the supportive relies on for diffusion (decision texts through Diário da Justiça) make available a growing corpus of prior compliant compliance determinations that identify the relevant fundamentos determinantes for controlling precedents. Informed mimicry — reproducing the pattern of fundamentos determinantes identification from prior compliant decisions — operates below the statutory ceiling. The mechanism's success conditions (growing compliance record corpus) generate the conditions for its own mimicry-cost reduction over time.

**Local Kreps — three limitations.** I accepted the local Kreps mechanism as structurally available. Three limitations:

1. The channel does not require cheaper verification — courts have always observed practitioner compliance across repeated interactions through professional reading of filings. The democratization claim requires opening a previously closed channel; local Kreps bilateral observation was not previously closed to practitioners in repeated-interaction positions.

2. Local Kreps benefits those already in repeated-interaction positions — established regional practitioners, institutional regular litigants, public defenders with stable court assignments. New entrants without prior court relationships cannot accumulate local Kreps reputation because they lack the repeated-interaction base. Cheaper verification per filing does not substitute for access to those interactions.

3. The coverage-extension and local Kreps premises conflict on judicial attention. Coverage extension relies on high-volume dockets being under-reviewed (courts can't catch compliance failures). Local Kreps requires the same courts to track practitioner compliance quality across repeated interactions. Both cannot be true simultaneously in the same docket.

## What I considered and discarded

**Reliability as the primary constraint.** The synthesis listed reliability (whether automated detection produces reliable compliance assessments in the low-review population) as path (a). I decided that utilization is a structurally cleaner argument than reliability. Reliability is an empirical question about the pipeline's performance; the adversarial has not tested it and cannot claim failure without evidence. Utilization is a structural argument about the institutional pathway from detection to decision-text record. The reliability argument would require inventing failure rates for the pipeline, which I won't do. Discarded.

**Accepting the local Kreps argument fully.** The synthesis analysis for Paper 1F round 2 treated the local Kreps as the supportive's most honest and modest advance. I considered accepting it and pressing only the coverage extension and mimicry arguments. But the incompatibility between the coverage-extension judicial-attention premise and the local Kreps bilateral-observation assumption is structurally significant — it's not a nitpick but a genuine tension between two arguments the supportive is running simultaneously in the same docket population. Not engaging it would leave the tension unaddressed. Kept all three local Kreps limitations.

**Arguing the statutory upper bound doesn't exist.** The art. 489, §1º, V–VI requirements are law; I accept them as the correct characterization. Arguing the statutory text doesn't impose analytical engagement requirements beyond citation inclusion would be wrong and dishonest. Discarded.

**Pressing the systemic recalibration claim directly.** The paper's strongest version is a partial, differential claim — not that proxies disappear but that their relative weight shifts. I could press that even this partial claim is underdetermined by the mechanism as specified. But that's a claim I've already made in rounds 1 and 2 through the constitutive capital and signaling persistence arguments. Round 3 needs new ground, not repetition. The new ground is coverage extension and its constraints. Discarded as repetitive.

## What remains open

1. **Supportive round 3**: The utilization constraint is the argument most likely to receive a substantive response. The natural reply is that public interest organizations, unions, law school clinics, or advocacy groups could act as challenge-filing intermediaries — converting individual detections into systemic compliance challenges without individual party capacity. If a third-party organizational intermediary is identified, the utilization constraint is addressed for the specific classes of cases those organizations cover. The adversarial's response would need to assess whether such organizations currently exist in the Brazilian docket and at what scale.

2. **Prevalence empirics**: The conditional prevalence gap is an empirical question neither side has data on. If the supportive round 3 cites data on compliance failure rates in public defender dockets or repetitive-resource proceedings, the balance shifts.

3. **Mimicry in non-standardized dockets**: The informed mimicry argument is weakest in high-variance case-configuration dockets, where the corpus of prior compliant decisions doesn't cover the specific configuration at hand. If the supportive presses that the mechanism's primary value is in dockets with high fact-pattern variance, the mimicry-cost argument weakens there. But those dockets are also likely to be higher-review dockets, reinstating the inverse-relationship objection for them.

4. **SC3 unresolved from Paper 1C**: The connection between Paper 1C's claim provenance tractability challenge and Paper 1F's mechanism-quality claims is an indirect one, but the tractability ceiling the Paper 1C debate established (automated systems cannot perform the necessity determination for the hard case) bears on whether the Lean 4 pipeline's compliance assessments in the low-review population are reliable for contested cases. This hasn't been raised in the Paper 1F debate and may not be the appropriate venue.
