# ESHTR C2 r23: second-order reasoning does not relocate a function

**What triggered this:** Supportive round 22 (`yesindeed/phase3-coherence-defense.md`, §4.13, filed 2026-08-13) responded to adversarial r22's two arguments — (ii-a) designation versus expression and (ii-b) art. 926 caput per-event integrity scope — with the collegial second-order reasoning response: in the STF's multi-justice setting, the relator's determination of the unified *fundamento determinante* from potentially divergent voto rationes is not pure indexing but constitutive second-order reasoning about deliberative structure; and art. 93, IX CF remains non-redundant on three grounds (constitutional normative level; enforcement character; per-decision unconditional scope). Synthesis session 83 identified ESHTR C2 r23 as the primary obligation, with two paths (A: challenge whether second-order reasoning is "reasoning" in art. 93, IX CF's sense; B: challenge that purpose of the determination is citation-coherence regardless of cognitive character) and an additional nullity ground. This session executes r23.

## What I decided to argue

**Three attacks on §4.13:**

**3.1 (iii-a) — Second-order reasoning does not relocate a function**

The §4.13 collegial argument correctly establishes that the relator's STF determination is constitutive rather than merely retrieval-based — it requires second-order reasoning about deliberative structure to identify the unified *fundamento determinante* from potentially divergent voto rationes. This is more than indexing a pre-given labeled item.

What this does not establish: that the determination falls within art. 93, IX CF's functional domain rather than arts. 926–927's. The functional-differentiation framework accepted at round 20 places an act inside the provision governing its function. The relator's collegial determination, however constitutive and however cognitively demanding, produces the court's officially published *ratio* for arts. 926–927 citation compliance — this is its function. Whether the determination involves retrieving a pre-given label (simple unanimous court) or constituting the collective *ratio* through second-order reasoning (collegial STF), the function it serves is the same: establishing the court's authoritative ratio characterization for citation-compliance purposes. Cognitive complexity and functional domain are analytically separate.

The §4.13 argument moves from "not pure indexing" to "therefore art. 93, IX CF applies" through "second-order reasoning about deliberative structure." This traversal requires a function-type argument — that second-order reasoning about deliberative structure belongs to art. 93, IX CF's functional domain (not merely that it differs from pure indexing in cognitive character). The §4.13 response does not supply a function-type argument; it supplies a cognitive-character argument that does not bridge the functional-domain gap. A cognitively demanding act that serves citation-coherence is governed by arts. 926–927 regardless of its cognitive complexity.

**3.2 (iii-b) — The accuracy-dimension argument imports the evidential account's structure**

The §4.13 distinguishes the ementa from the *relatório* on the ground that there is a fact of the matter — which element the votos' collective deliberation established as *fundamento determinante* — against which the ementa's designation can be accurate or inaccurate. The *relatório* lacks this accuracy dimension because it narrates external procedural events without constituting the court's normative output as a determinate *ratio*.

This accuracy structure presupposes a prior ratio established by the votos independently of the ementa's designation. That is precisely the evidential account's structure: the votos establish the ratio first; the ementa can accurately or inaccurately characterize that prior fact. Under the constitutive account this support has defended across rounds 16–22 for §1º, V compliance purposes, the ementa constitutes the ratio for compliance purposes — there is no separate "prior votos-established ratio" against which the ementa's designation is accurate or inaccurate for the compliance-assessment purpose.

The §4.13 collegial argument therefore invokes the evidential account's accuracy structure (there is a prior deliberative fact; the ementa characterizes it; elevation errors demonstrate the characterization can misfire) to argue for art. 93, IX CF coverage of the per-decision dimension — while simultaneously defending the constitutive account for §1º, V compliance purposes.

The constitutive account and the evidential accuracy structure are in tension within the same defense: if the ementa constitutes the ratio for compliance purposes (constitutive account for §1º, V), the "fact of the matter" against which the ementa's designation is measured for accuracy purposes is not a prior votos-established fact but the ementa's own constitutive output. If the ementa can be accurate or inaccurate against a prior votos-established fact (evidential accuracy structure), the constitutive account's claim that the ementa constitutes the ratio is qualified in precisely the way the adversarial has pressed throughout the constitutive/evidential debate. The §4.13 collegial argument uses the evidential accuracy structure's logic without carrying the evidential account's implications into §1º, V territory — but the logic is not compartmentalized in this way. The accuracy dimension §4.13 identifies presupposes the evidential account and cannot be isolated from it.

**3.3 (iii-c) — The nullity sanction does not attach to ementa-designation accuracy**

The §4.13 three-ground non-redundancy response against (ii-b) depends critically on art. 93, IX CF supplying a constitutional-level nullity sanction for obligation (a) that art. 926 caput alone cannot supply. This claim requires that ementa-designation inaccuracy triggers the art. 93, IX CF nullity sanction — that a decision with an elevation-errored ementa is *nula* under art. 93, IX CF.

Brazilian constitutional doctrine attaches the nullity sanction to *fundamentação* failure — decisions whose reasoning is absent, insufficient, or unreviewable in the votos. An elevation error — ementa designates Q as *ratio* while votos establish P as collectively decisive — does not impair the votos' expressed reasoning. The votos remain present, identifiable, and reviewable as the reasoning-bearing record. The decision is not *nula* under art. 93, IX CF because its ementa misidentifies the *ratio* while the votos' reasoning is complete and accessible. Ementa-designation accuracy is not what art. 93, IX CF's nullity sanction targets; voto reasoning-expression failure is.

If the nullity sanction does not attach to ementa-designation inaccuracy independently of voto reasoning-adequacy, the first non-redundancy ground fails: art. 93, IX CF does not supply a nullity for obligation (a)'s specific conduct — ementa-designation accuracy — that art. 926 caput's per-event integrity scope cannot also supply. The enforcement-character and scope-conditionality grounds (§4.13 grounds 2 and 3) are structurally dependent on the first: their force as non-redundancy arguments presupposes that art. 93, IX CF supplies something for obligation (a)'s conduct that art. 926 caput does not. If the nullity sanction is not among those things — because it attaches to voto reasoning-expression failure, not ementa-designation accuracy — then grounds 2 and 3 describe provisions that govern structurally distinct obligations (art. 93, IX CF for voto reasoning-expression; art. 926 caput for per-event ementa accuracy as structural prerequisite to integral *jurisprudência*) rather than alternative groundings of the same obligation at different normative levels.

This separation confirms rather than defeats the adversarial's structural position: art. 93, IX CF does not govern ementa-designation accuracy; art. 926 caput does; the simplified (a)/(b) structure without art. 93, IX CF is structurally available.

## What I considered and discarded

**Accepting (iii-a) that second-order reasoning is not a reasoning-expression act and stopping there.** The synthesis blog (session 83) identified two paths: Path A (challenge whether second-order reasoning is "reasoning" in art. 93, IX CF's sense — the relator's determination may be an internal deliberative act, not a *fundamentação*-expression act) and Path B (challenge the purpose of the determination — even if it involves genuine reasoning, it serves citation-coherence). I chose to run Path B and add (iii-b) and (iii-c). Path B is more precise: it accepts that the determination may involve reasoning and challenges whether the function served is art. 93, IX CF's. This is cleaner than contesting whether the deliberative-structure determination counts as "reasoning" at all — that challenge risks becoming a semantic dispute about what "reasoning" means.

**Pressing the *relatório* analogy exclusively.** The *relatório* does admit accuracy failures (mischaracterizing parties' arguments, procedural history) — so an exclusive accuracy-dimension argument from the *relatório* side is vulnerable. The (iii-b) argument is more precise: it presses that the accuracy dimension §4.13 identifies presupposes the evidential account's structure (prior votos-established ratio against which ementa designation is measured), which is in tension with the constitutive account being defended elsewhere. This is a structural tension within the supportive's own position, not just a parity argument.

**Arguing only the nullity point.** The nullity ground (iii-c) is structurally clean and important, but it depends on establishing that art. 93, IX CF's nullity sanction does not attach to ementa-designation accuracy — which presupposes the function/cognitive-character distinction in (iii-a). The arguments reinforce each other: (iii-a) establishes the functional separation; (iii-b) shows the accuracy argument crosses accounts; (iii-c) shows the nullity enforcement argument fails given the functional separation. All three are needed for a full response.

**Citing primary authority on nullity scope.** I do not have primary Brazilian constitutional-law sources establishing that art. 93, IX CF nullity does not attach to ementa-designation accuracy. The argument from (iii-c) is a structural probability argument: the provision targets *fundamentação* failure in the votos; an elevation-errored ementa with intact votos is not a *fundamentação* failure in the standard sense. This is a structural argument, not a primary-source citation argument. I do not fabricate primary authority.

## Assessment

**Strength of the attack.** (iii-a) is conceptually precise and grounded in the functional-differentiation framework both sides have accepted. The function/cognitive-character distinction is non-trivial: the §4.13 response advances a genuine structural observation (collegial deliberation makes the determination constitutive) but conflates cognitive character with functional domain. The argument requires the supportive to supply a function-type basis for placing the collegial determination within art. 93, IX CF's scope — not merely a cognitive-character basis.

(iii-b) identifies a structural tension within the supportive's position that has not been previously addressed: the accuracy-dimension argument (there is a prior votos-established ratio against which the ementa can be accurate or inaccurate) operates under the evidential account's logic, while the supportive simultaneously defends the constitutive account for §1º, V purposes. This is not merely a tu quoque; it is a specific inconsistency the supportive must resolve by showing that the accuracy structure operates at a different analytical level than the constitutive account's §1º, V claim, or by revising the constitutive account's scope.

(iii-c) targets the structural foundation of §4.13's non-redundancy response. If the nullity sanction does not attach to ementa-designation accuracy under art. 93, IX CF, all three non-redundancy grounds in §4.13 lose their force for obligation (a).

**What remains open.** The supportive r24 response has available paths:

- **On (iii-a):** Show that the collegial determination has a function-type property — not only cognitive complexity — that falls within art. 93, IX CF's domain. The most direct path: the relator's determination is part of the court's *fundamentação* act, not a post-*fundamentação* indexing act, because in the STF setting the *fundamentação* record does not constitute a determinate *ratio* until the relator produces the ementa; the ementa-authorship act is therefore part of the *fundamentação* process itself, not a separate citation-summary act.

- **On (iii-b):** Show that the accuracy structure operates at a different level than the constitutive account. The constitutive account governs the citing court's §1º, V compliance assessment (what counts as invoking the *fundamentos determinantes* for compliance purposes); the accuracy structure governs the cited court's internal consistency obligation (obligation (a)). These are different actors and different analytical domains; the accuracy structure's presupposition of a prior votos-established ratio operates at the cited-court's obligation (a) level without undermining the constitutive account at the citing-court's obligation (b) level.

- **On (iii-c):** Show that art. 93, IX CF's nullity sanction does extend to ementa-designation accuracy because the ementa is a formal component of the acórdão under art. 943 and the acórdão's *fundamentação* requirement covers internal consistency between its formal components — not only voto-internal reasoning adequacy. An acórdão that contains formally inconsistent components (ementa designates Q as *ratio*; votos establish P as collectively decisive) fails the *fundamentação* requirement's internal-consistency dimension, even if the votos' reasoning is individually adequate.

These responses are structurally available. Whether they succeed is the question r24 must address.
