### O-1: Against N-1 — Criterion/Mechanism Separation Requires Independent Behavioral Proxies

N-1's criterion/mechanism distinction holds at the *conceptual* level: it is true that legal rationality criteria can specify internal states without the verification mechanism being *definitionally* internal to the criterion's content. The good-faith analogy establishes this. But the adversarial's M-1 attack was not about definitional content — it was about *legal-system actionability* as a correction standard. At that level, the analogy fails.

Good faith has independent behavioral proxies: commercial custom, course of dealing, industry practice standards, reliance patterns — observable behavioral features that track whether a party acted in good faith without requiring direct access to the party's internal cognitive state. These proxies allow appellate review and doctrinal assessment to operate against the criterion *without* the party's own disclosure of their mental state as the information source. Good faith's criterion/mechanism separation is operationally viable because the behavioral proxies supply independent evidence.

Epistemic sensitivity lacks this proxy structure. The adversarial analysis identifies three evidentiary domains where the criterion's satisfaction condition does not map onto content-verifiable logical structure: mixed evidence cases (no objective fact-finding algorithm determines which assessment is more epistemically sensitive), weight-assignment decisions (assessment of competing witness credibility turns on the judge's orientation toward evidence), narrative reconstruction from conflicting testimonies (the judge's integrative approach to contradictory accounts). In all three domains, the criterion's satisfaction condition is internally opaque without the judge's disclosure of their evaluative process. There is no behavioral indicator — analogous to commercial custom for good faith — that tracks criterion satisfaction across this range without relying on the *motivazione* mechanism as the information source.

N-1's proves-too-much argument: the adversarial's M-1 claim was not that *all* internal-state criteria require constitutive dependence on their verification mechanisms, but that epistemic sensitivity *specifically* lacks behavioral proxies in the relevant domain. The proves-too-much argument has limited reach against this targeted claim. It shows that the *general* principle (internal-state criteria are not constitutively dependent on verification mechanisms) holds, but it does not establish that epistemic sensitivity *specifically* has the behavioral proxy structure that makes criterion/mechanism separation operationally viable as a correction standard.

The code structure argument for O-1: N-1 reads the Italian CPC 1940's two-institution design (Art. 116 + Art. 132 as separate provisions) as institutional evidence that the reform tradition recognized the criterion as analytically distinct from the verification mechanism. The adversarial concedes separate violability — the obligations *can* be separately violated (a judge can attend epistemically to evidence while failing to express the evaluation in *motivazione*, and vice versa). But separate violability does not establish *independent operationalizability*. Art. 116's criterion satisfaction could be separately violable from Art. 132's disclosure requirement while still being independently evaluable *only through* Art. 132's disclosure — because the only evidence available to appellate review about whether Art. 116's criterion was satisfied is the Art. 132 exposition. The regulatory-technique separation of the provisions (different procedural moments, different enforcement mechanisms, different default exceptions) is consistent with this picture, and the code structure's institutional evidence does not distinguish separate violability from independent operationalizability.

### O-2: Against N-2 — General Type-2 Vocabulary Does Not Supply Proceduralist-Domain Constituting Vocabulary

N-2's Type-1/Type-2 distinction is accepted for what it establishes: non-patrimonial rational-legal fields' general institutional formation produces Type-2 accountability concepts (accountability as a positive dimension of what judicial rationality consists in), while patrimonial fields produce only Type-1 accountability concepts (accountability as a formal compliance obligation). The adversarial concedes this distinction and the asymmetry N-2 identifies at the general adjacent-domain level.

What O-2 contests is whether general Type-2 vocabulary, present in non-patrimonial fields through general institutional formation, constitutes the specific *proceduralist-domain* constituting vocabulary that condition (a) requires. The condition (a) operation is not merely: "can scholars in this field articulate that accountability is a positive dimension of judicial rationality?" It is: "can scholars in this field constitute 'should the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive designatum incorporate the accountability dimension?' as a tractable scholarly question within the proceduralist theoretical tradition?"

The gap N-2's response does not close: the proceduralist integration question requires treating the evidentiary evaluation concept as a theoretical object with specifiable internal positive structure, and treating the accountability dimension as a potential *component of that concept's specific rational content* — not merely as a general property of judicial rationality that belongs in a separate procedural provision. Scholars who have Type-2 vocabulary in the general sense (accountability is internal to judicial rationality as such) are still one conceptual step away from the proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary for the evidentiary evaluation concept integration question: they need the additional theoretical framework for treating the evidentiary evaluation concept as the site where the internal/external accountability distinction must be resolved.

The temporal-origin problem applies at this constituting level: N-2 concedes that vocabulary for *answering* the integration question (resolving it through the Liebman proceduralist framework) develops through C3 engagement. O-2's claim is that vocabulary for *constituting* the integration question as tractable within the proceduralist tradition also develops through C3 proceduralist engagement — specifically, the Liebman-period scholarly engagement with the Italian reform tradition that supplied the theoretical framework for treating evidentiary evaluation concepts as objects with internally specifiable rational content, and for positioning the accountability dimension as a question about that concept's internal structure rather than a separate institutional obligation. General Type-2 formation provides the orientation; the proceduralist-domain vocabulary that makes the integration question tractable as a scholarly question within the field's theoretical tradition requires field-specific conceptual formation that Brazil's pre-C3 formation did not supply.

The Brazilian post-1988 diagnostic is accepted: Streck's compliance-failure framing is consistent with Type-1 structure in the relevant domain. But this diagnostic is also consistent with O-2's claim: the absence of Type-2 vocabulary at the proceduralist-domain constituting level means Brazilian proceduralists could not constitute the integration question as tractable within their field's theoretical tradition, and that the post-1988 Brazilian doctrinal record takes the compliance-failure form precisely because proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary for the integration question was never developed through the C3 engagement it required.

## What I Considered and Discarded

**Conceding N-1 and focusing only on O-2.** N-1 has genuine content — the criterion/mechanism distinction is real, the good-faith analogy is not incoherent, and the proves-too-much argument correctly identifies a limitation of M-1's general verificationist structure. But the attack I filed on M-1 was targeted at the *legal-system actionability* level, not the conceptual level. N-1's response holds at the conceptual level without fully answering the actionability challenge: showing that epistemic sensitivity is conceptually distinct from *motivazione*'s accountability function does not show that epistemic sensitivity generates an independently operable correction standard. The behavioral-proxy formulation (O-1) presses this actionability gap specifically rather than retargeting at the conceptual level. I did not concede N-1.

**Pressing that good faith also lacks independent behavioral proxies.** One response to O-1 would be: good faith is itself an internal-state criterion that requires judicial inference about the party's mental state from observable behavior, and that inference is not categorically different from judicial inference about the judge's own epistemic sensitivity from their outputs. This is an available move for r17. But it does not affect O-1's structural claim about the *type* of behavioral indicator available: commercial custom and course of dealing are behavioral proxies for good faith that function without requiring the party's self-report; there is no analogous behavioral proxy for epistemic sensitivity in mixed evidence cases and narrative reconstruction. If r17 argues that good faith also lacks such proxies, that would narrow the analogy dispute to whether behavioral inference from outputs (judicial decisions, evidence holdings) constitutes the relevant proxy — a more technically constrained debate that O-1 is positioned to engage.

**Arguing that the Brazilian post-1988 record shows Type-2 *proceduralist-domain* vocabulary.** The Streck-framing diagnostic cuts against this interpretation: constitutional proceduralism framed as compliance correction is the Type-1 structure N-2 identifies and the adversarial's O-2 accepts. If r17 identifies Brazilian proceduralist scholarship that constitutes the evidentiary evaluation concept's internal structure as the theoretical site where the integration question is tractable — scholarly work from within the proceduralist tradition treating *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum as an internally specifiable concept requiring accountability integration — that would be the specific evidence O-2 requires for condition (o). No such evidence has been identified; the discarded move was to assert its absence without the structural argument O-2 provides.

**Treating O-2 as requiring a new paper.** The Type-1/Type-2 constituting/answering distinction developed by N-2 and O-2 is operationally tight enough to be carried within §3.8.O. The distinction between general Type-2 orientation and proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary is the specific gap O-2 identifies; it does not require a general theory of institutional vocabulary formation beyond what is specified in §3.8.O. A new paper would fragment the exchange from the specific M-2/N-2/O-2 argument chain that gives the distinction its force.

Without one of these, the criterion/mechanism distinction holds conceptually but the actionability gap remains open.

**(O-2/N-2 at r17):** The proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary gap is condition (o). R17 would need to identify the specific pre-C3 proceduralist vocabulary resource that makes the integration question tractable within the field's theoretical tradition — present in non-patrimonial fields' general institutional formation but absent from Brazil's. Without that identification, the Type-2 asymmetry holds at the general level but fails at the proceduralist-domain constituting level where condition (a) operates.

**Loop-closure condition for r17:** restating the criterion/mechanism distinction without engaging the behavioral-proxy gap at the actionability level; or restating the Type-1/Type-2 asymmetry at the general adjacent-domain level without identifying the proceduralist-domain constituting vocabulary resource.
