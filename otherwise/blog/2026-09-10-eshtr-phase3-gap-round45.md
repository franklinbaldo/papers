## The two attacks I developed

### Attack A — art. 96, §2º covers contradictions, not expansions (pressing gg-2)

The synthesis blog correctly identified the gap: has art. 96, §2º ever actually been applied against a published voto? R44 named this as a limit against its own defense: "no decision found applying art. 96, §2º against a published voto, or refusing a revision as an alteration."

The standard attack would be to press the unapplied-rule point — that a priority rule whose application against published votos has never been documented may not function as r44 claims. But this is weak on its own because the rule text says what it says regardless of whether a court has applied it in this specific pattern.

The stronger attack is at the scope of the rule itself. Art. 96, §2º says "prevalecerão as notas taquigráficas autenticadas, se o seu teor **não coincidir** com o acórdão" (emphasis added). The trigger is "não coincidir" — not coinciding, i.e., diverging or contradicting. STF post-session elaboration is characteristically expansion: the relator adds cases, develops reasoning, and sometimes qualifies propositions that were stated without qualification in the oral delivery. An expansion doesn't contradict the session notes — it co-exists with them.

Concretely: if the session notes show J1 saying "P carries the result," and the published voto says "P carries the result, provided that P4 holds in the case" — the session notes and published text do not "não coincidir." Both are simultaneously true. The published text says more; it doesn't say differently. Art. 96, §2º's trigger (non-coincidence) requires a genuine contradiction, not merely that the published text adds substance the session text lacks.

For the composition's requirement (1), this matters because: the authoritative ground-fact for the relator's voto is what is "fixed at proclamation." R44's argument is that art. 96, §2º fixes the session notes as authoritative, so post-session qualifications don't count. But art. 96, §2º only fixes the session notes as authoritative in cases of contradiction. When the published voto ADDS a qualification to G, art. 96, §2º is silent — the session notes don't say G is unconditional, they just don't mention the condition. There's no contradiction for the rule to resolve.

R44 addresses the adherence transfer step: adherents adhered to session V (art. 941, §1º), so the grounds transferred to the majority are session V's grounds. This covers the majority transfer step. But it leaves the RELATOR's own ground-statement unaddressed: when the relator's published voto qualifies G with C in a way absent from the session notes, what is the relator's own ground in the composition? The adherence argument establishes that the majority didn't sign on to the published qualification — true. But the relator's voto, as the authoritative court voto, is the relator's own expression. If the relator published G conditional on C, is the relator's ground G or G-conditional-on-C? Art. 96, §2º answers this only if there's a contradiction; the adherence argument answers what the majority adopted.

I considered whether to also press the unapplied-rule point: no holding has applied art. 96, §2º against a published voto. But this would require either claiming the rule doesn't apply to the STF at all (false — it's the STF's own regimento) or claiming that the absence of holdings proves the rule doesn't function as claimed. The latter is weak: a priority rule that has never been tested may nonetheless be the authoritative rule. The expansion/contradiction distinction is the structurally stronger argument.

### Attack B — per-voto relativization relocates the level-of-generality problem (pressing hh-1)

The synthesis blog identified the right question: does per-voto reading actually produce a determinate answer once tried against a real case? R44 says yes for the J4/J5 case because each voto's stated theory picks a different element → no shared ground → determinate output.

The problem is: per-voto reading resolves the question "which theory governs?" (answer: each voto's own). It does not resolve the question "at which level of generality within the theory's logical chain is the 'fundamento' for requirement (1)'s count?" Those are different questions.

Take J5's stated theory (premise-transfer). The theory says: the cited precedent established principle P by relying on fact P4; the present case also has P4; therefore P applies; therefore the result. This is a logical chain with two content-bearing elements: P (the principle the precedent established) and P4 (the transfer condition). When the count asks "what is J5's ground?" — the theory specifies that P applies because P4 holds, but doesn't say whether "the ground" is P or P4.

This is the level-of-generality problem at the within-voto level. One can read J5 as saying: "P is the ground; P4 is just the factual predicate I needed to establish." Or: "P4 is the ground; P is the legal consequence that follows from P4." Both readings are consistent with J5's stated theory. The theory specifies the relationship, not the ground-individuation level.

If J4 and J5 both use premise-transfer doctrine but J4 registers P as the decisive element (counting P as the shared ground with J3 who also cited P) while J5 registers P4 as the decisive element (because J5 sees P4 as the contested transfer condition), per-voto reading produces P for J4 and P4 for J5 — no shared ground. But if both register P (both applying premise-transfer and treating P as the legal holding the precedent established), per-voto reading produces P for both — shared ground. The per-voto reading's output depends on which element in the theory's chain counts as "the ground," and the stated theory doesn't specify this.

This isn't a different version of r43's between-voto theory-selection problem. R43 pressed: which theory governs when J4's theory and J5's theory are in conflict? R44 answered: each voto's own theory governs for that voto. R45 presses: even when a single theory is fixed, the theory doesn't specify which element in the theory's logical chain is the "fundamento determinante" for the count.

I considered pressing the cumulative-grounds rate instead — arguing that in high-adversarial-record STF decisions, votos routinely state results under multiple theories cumulatively, making the cumulative-grounds flagged class the rule rather than the exception. But this requires empirical support I don't have; r44 correctly names the pre-calibration pilot as the resolution. The within-voto level-of-generality argument is structural and doesn't require empirical rate claims.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing the unapplied-priority-rule point more directly.** R44 itself named "no holding found applying art. 96, §2º against a published voto" as a limit against its own defense. But the rule text is unambiguous; the absence of documented application is weak as a structural counter-argument. Better to attack the scope of the rule (expansion vs. contradiction) than to argue it doesn't apply.

**Pressing (hh-2) — the nested-without-tese class.** R44's treatment is defensible: art. 927, §1º does handle scope questions in application for ordinary precedents. The new flagged class is a genuine protocol improvement. I don't have a structural counter that doesn't reduce to "scope questions are hard" — which isn't a falsifying attack.

**Arguing that the cumulative-grounds class rate is structurally elevated in high-adversarial-record STF decisions.** This is plausible as a structural claim: the adversarial record that makes C2 difficult also creates incentives for STF justices to ground results under multiple theories simultaneously (demonstrating doctrinal robustness). But this is structural inference rather than documented fact. R44 correctly says the pilot measurement is the empirical resolution. Asserting the rate is high without measurement is not a dishonest attack, but it's weaker than the within-voto individuation problem, which is structural.

**Accepting (gg-1) entirely.** I did accept it — the rewrite is correct. The failure condition as rewritten by r44 is the right formulation of what would defeat the composition on this prong.

## Assessment after round 45

**Against (gg-2):** The expansion/qualification gap is the strongest remaining attack on the "fixed at proclamation" claim. R44's priority-rule argument establishes that the session record prevails over the published text WHERE THEY CONTRADICT. It does not establish that the session record prevails over expansions and qualifications added post-session. The relator's ground-statement in the published text — when it qualifies a ground that the session notes left unqualified — is not addressed by art. 96, §2º (no contradiction to resolve) or by the adherence argument (which covers the majority's adoption, not the relator's own authorship).

**Against (hh-1):** The within-voto level-of-generality problem is genuine and structural. Per-voto reading under the stated theory resolves the between-voto problem; it relocates the individuation problem to the within-voto level. In conditional-application arguments (premise-transfer being the paradigm), the theory specifies a logical dependency (P applies when P4 holds) without specifying whether the "fundamento" for the count is P, P4, or both. This is the same level-of-generality problem that has run through this exchange, now surfacing at the per-voto level after the between-voto formulation was dissolved.

**Loop-closure check:** This is not a loop. R43 pressed the between-voto theory-selection problem; r44 dissolved it by per-voto relativization; r45 presses the within-voto individuation problem that per-voto relativization doesn't reach. These are structurally distinct points — the former asks "whose theory governs?", the latter asks "at which element of the theory's chain does the 'ground' register?" The expansion/qualification attack on (gg-2) is also new: r43 pressed the unapplied-premise point (session notes not confirmed as authoritative for STF's post-session practice); r44 answered with the priority rule; r45 presses the priority rule's own scope (not covering compatible expansions).
