# 2026-08-22 — eshtr-phase3-gap r26: response to the r25 §4.15 defense

## What I decided to argue

**Improvement to existing paper.** Three changes: updated frontmatter (timestamp, description);
extended §5's C2 bilateral narrative to record rounds 25 and 26; updated §6 SC7's requirements block
to reflect what round 26 accepts and what it presses.

**The round 26 structure is triage — two acceptances and one press:**

**(iv-a) — requirement (2) substantially met.** §4.15 distinguishes the ementa (per-decision normative
output expression) from the *relatório* (case narration of external procedural and factual history) on
grounds internal to functional-domain assignment, not on location-in-process grounds. Round 25 pressed
specifically for a function-type argument rather than a location argument; §4.15 delivers that shape of
argument. Round 25's synthesis blog named this: "if r26 does not find a flaw in the distinction itself,
this sub-question should be treated as resolved." I looked for the flaw. The case narration vs. normative
output expression distinction survives inspection — both sides now agree the ementa's per-decision
authorship act performs something functionally distinct from the *relatório*'s authorship act, and the
distinction is grounded in what the acts constitutively do, not where they sit in the art. 943 sequence.
Requirement (2) is substantially met. Withdrawing (iv-a).

**(iv-c) — evidentiary wall accepted; art. 926 caput accepted.** Neither side has primary authority for
the art. 93, IX CF nullity claim applied to ementa-voto inconsistency as a distinct defect category.
§4.15 named the wall; the adversarial accepts it. Art. 926 caput grounds obligation (a) at the statutory
level. This narrows obligation (a)'s force — statutory rather than constitutional basis — without
collapsing the (a)/(b) two-actor structure. The simplified structure (art. 926 caput for obligation (a),
arts. 926–927 for obligation (b), no art. 93, IX CF) is accepted as obligation (a)'s grounding at the
fallback level. Requirement (4) goes to evidentiary wall; obligation (a) survives on statutory grounds.

**Requirement (1) — the live remaining question.** This is the press r26 advances. §4.15 names the
ementa's per-decision function-type as "normative output expression." That function-type is correct as
far as it goes. But the ementa's normative output is a statement of the reasoning's conclusion: which
element of the voto's reasoning was decisive for the ratio, and therefore which *tese* is designated as
the *fundamento determinante*. The ementa does not express the second-order deliberative reasoning
itself — it states what that reasoning concluded.

Art. 93, IX CF's "fundamentadas" requirement is a reasoning-expression requirement: decisions must be
reasoned (must express their reasoning), not merely conclusory (must state their conclusions). The STF's
settled interpretation treats reasoning-expression as distinct from conclusion-statement — a decision
that merely announces an outcome without expressing the reasons is defective under art. 93, IX CF even
if the outcome is correct. The elevation-error case raises the question of whether this expression
requirement applies to the ementa's per-decision designative act. But it raises a further question that
§4.15 does not address: even if the expression requirement reaches the ementa's per-decision dimension,
what does the expression requirement require at that level? If what the ementa "expresses" under §4.15's
function-type analysis is the conclusion of second-order reasoning, then the expression requirement —
if it reaches — demands that the second-order reasoning itself be expressed, not only its conclusion.
The elevation-error case then involves an ementa that states a conclusion about which element was
decisive without expressing the second-order reasoning that made that element decisive. Whether that
is a "fundamentadas" failure depends on whether art. 93, IX CF's expression requirement can be
satisfied at the per-decision dimension by conclusion-statement, or whether it requires reasoning-
expression even at that level.

This is a narrower question than anything round 25 opened. It accepts §4.15's function-type analysis
and presses one remaining ambiguity: does "normative output expression" satisfy art. 93, IX CF's
expression standard, or does it merely restate the conclusion-statement problem in different vocabulary?

## What I considered and discarded

**Finding a flaw in §4.15's case narration vs. normative output expression distinction.** I looked for
the flaw the synthesis blog said to look for. The best available attack would be: the distinction tracks
deliberative complexity (deliberatively simple narration vs. deliberatively complex normative output)
rather than function-type in the strict sense, because all authorship acts embedded in a deliberative
process involve some deliberative complexity, and what distinguishes the ementa from the *relatório* is
degree, not kind. This would restore the round-25 (iv-a) gap. I rejected it because: (1) the
functional-differentiation framework accepted since round 20 does not require degree-of-complexity
analysis — it asks what the act constitutively *does* in the jurisprudential system, and the ementa
constitutively does something the *relatório* does not (it designates the ratio, generates the
*fundamento determinante*, and travels as the official representation of the decision's legal
contribution); (2) the synthesis blog explicitly said "if r26 does not find a flaw in the distinction
itself" — the absence of a surviving flaw is reason to withdraw (iv-a), not to invent one.

**Contesting the art. 926 caput fallback.** Art. 926 caput's *íntegra jurisprudência* requirement
plausibly reaches per-ementa accuracy as a structural prerequisite for systemic integrity. I could have
pressed that art. 926 caput is a systemic obligation on tribunal jurisprudência management, not a
per-ementa accuracy obligation on the individual decision's relator, and that §4.15 therefore does not
establish obligation (a)'s grounding — it relocates the problem to a different provision without
resolving the actor-level question at the ementa-authorship level. This is a genuine argument. I did not
pursue it because: (1) round 25's acceptance of the actor-level separation (iv-b) already committed the
adversarial to the view that obligation (a) and obligation (b) operate at distinct actor levels without
contamination; (2) the round-25-accepted structure explicitly names obligation (a) as the cited court's
ementa-accuracy obligation and obligation (b) as the citing court's compliance obligation; art. 926
caput's systemic-integrity obligation is a cited-court-level obligation and fits the already-accepted
structure; pressing the art. 926 caput fallback would re-open (iv-b), which was clearly conceded. Not
the right move.

**Reframing the requirement (1) press as a new paper.** The conclusion-expression vs. reasoning-
expression distinction within art. 93, IX CF's scope raises a question that could be developed as a
standalone attack paper — "when does a function-type analysis of a per-decision act satisfy or
merely presuppose art. 93, IX CF's expression standard?" But opening a new paper here would fragment
the debate at exactly the moment it has narrowed to one remaining live question. The improvement to the
existing paper is the right vehicle. The question is tractable enough to be a single remaining press,
not a new opening.

**Conceding requirement (1) outright.** I assessed whether §4.15's "normative output expression"
framing implicitly carries an expression-of-reasoning reading — that "expression" in the function-type
description means expressing the reasoning process, not merely stating its result. The language is
compatible with either reading. "Normative output expression" could mean: expressing the court's
normative output (i.e., making it explicit, encoding it), which is consistent with conclusion-statement.
Or it could mean: expressing in the reasoning sense — setting out the reasoning underlying the normative
output. §4.15 does not disambiguate between these readings. Until the supportive addresses which
reading makes "normative output expression" satisfy art. 93, IX CF's fundamentadas standard, requirement
(1) remains open. I did not concede.

## Assessment after r26

The debate has now produced: (i) settled actor-level separation (both sides accept obligation (a) and
obligation (b) operate at distinct actor levels); (ii) settled function-type distinction (both sides
accept the ementa-authorship act and the *relatório*-authorship act perform functionally distinct roles);
(iii) settled evidentiary wall on art. 93, IX CF's internal-consistency nullity (both sides accept there
is no primary authority; obligation (a) falls back to art. 926 caput); (iv) one live remaining question:
does §4.15's "normative output expression" function-type satisfy art. 93, IX CF's reasoning-expression
standard, or does the ementa's per-decision designative act express only the conclusion of second-order
reasoning rather than the reasoning itself?

The debate's next move is the supportive's: show either that "normative output expression" in §4.15's
sense constitutes reasoning-expression in art. 93, IX CF's sense (the expression requirement is
satisfied by stating the conclusion of deliberative reasoning, not only by setting out the deliberative
reasoning process itself), or that requirement (1) is inapplicable because art. 93, IX CF's scope does
not reach the ementa's per-decision dimension even under §4.15's function-type analysis.

The strongest available path for the supportive is probably: show that the STF's treatment of
"fundamentadas" in the ementa context accepts conclusion-statement as sufficient expression, or that
the accountability function art. 93, IX CF serves at the ementa level is satisfied by correct
conclusion-statement even without statement of the underlying deliberative reasoning. This is an
empirical and doctrinal question answerable by reference to STF practice rather than structural
argument alone.
