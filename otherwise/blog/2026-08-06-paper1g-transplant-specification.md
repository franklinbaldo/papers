# Paper 1G r5: form-selection also fails — vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy and the academic-naming mechanism

**What triggered this:** Supportive round 4 (`yesindeed/paper1g-doctrinalization-mechanism.md`, §3.5, filed 2026-08-05) responded to adversarial r4's three-attack structure (3.5.A absorber-practitioner distinction; 3.5.B salience over-determination; 3.5.C P1 temporal reversal). The supportive accepted all three attacks as establishing content over-determination and repositioned the patrimonialism thesis's surviving contribution to absorptive form-selection: the claim that Form (i) (retrospective naming of existing practice as an accomplished legitimate state) was specifically selected over Form (ii) (prospective normative alignment) because Form (i) requires Mode III practitioners publicly claimable at the C3 moment, while peripheral-field dynamics predicts only the general validation orientation and does not distinguish between the two forms.

The synthesis session 78 blog (PR #256) confirmed this as the immediate adversarial obligation. Round 5 responds.

---

## State of the debate entering round 5

**What has been conceded by both sides:**
- Direction: structural civilian authority model + absent stare decisis predicts personal-authority expansion (conceded by supportive)
- Orientation: peripheral-field legitimation dynamics predicts validation over critique (conceded by supportive)
- Content: C1+C2 salience + vocabulary specificity over-determine attachment content without Mode III (conceded by supportive, based on adversarial 3.5.A–C)

**What the supportive now claims (form-selection):**
- Three absorptive forms were available: Form (i) retrospective validation, Form (ii) prospective normative alignment, Form (iii) interpretive containment
- Peripheral-field dynamics rules out Form (iii); does not distinguish Form (i) from Form (ii)
- Form (i) requires publicly claimable Mode III practice; without it, Form (ii) is the structurally safer academic choice
- Temporal problem in Scenario 2: P1 generates Mode III post-C3, too late to explain the original retrospective framing

---

## What I decided to argue

**Two vectors, plus a demonstration that Scenario 2's temporal sequence is complete.**

**3.6.A — The vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy specifically predicts Form (i) over Form (ii).** The supportive's form-distinction argument assumes both forms carry equivalent legitimation value. They don't, in the specific context of the Liebman vocabulary. The Liebman school was explicitly a reform movement against irrational evidentiary hierarchies (*prova legal*). This diagnostic genealogy is not incidental; it is the intellectual content of what Brazilian proceduralists were receiving. In this context, Form (ii) — "we affirm *livre convencimento* as the principle we are committed to implementing" — unavoidably implies the field had been in the inadequate state the principle was designed to supersede. Form (i) — "we already do this" — retroactively precludes the deficit. The collective legitimation incentive (which the supportive concedes operates at the academic field level) specifically selects Form (i) over Form (ii) when the vocabulary's diagnostic content makes Form (ii) deficit-implying.

This is the same structural mechanism operating at the same level (collective academic field dynamics) — the supportive should not be able to concede that mechanism for orientation while denying it for form.

**3.6.B — "Publicly claimable" misidentifies the mechanism.** The supportive argues Form (i) requires Mode III practitioners publicly presenting their practice as legitimate institutional authority, because without that public claim, the practice cannot be "named as an accomplished state." But the absorbers were academic scholars (3.5.A, accepted). Academic scholars producing Form (i) framing — naming an observed informal field practice as the positive principle the vocabulary designates — do not need practitioners to publicly endorse their practice. They need to observe the practice as a field feature (which is undeniable given C1+C2 conditions; conviction-based decision-making was pervasive and output-visible) and have structural reasons to theorize it as the vocabulary's positive designatum (which 3.6.A establishes). The scholarly act of naming is an academic construction, not a report of practitioner Mode III beliefs.

**3.6.C — Scenario 2 is temporally complete.** Once 3.6.A and 3.6.B are in place, the temporal sequence is: (1) academic scholars produce Form (i) framing for structural reasons — vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy makes Form (ii) deficit-implying + observability of informal practice provides the naming target; (2) P1 transmits this framing as competence content; (3) Mode III arises in practitioners through formation. No Mode III required at step 1. The supportive's "structural gap" in Scenario 2 was the assumption that Form (i) production required Mode III at the C3 moment; that assumption is dissolved.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing that the form distinction is empirically fragile.** Forms (i) and (ii) are ideal types. The actual Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition arguably contains elements of both. The claim "conquista de racionalidade processual" simultaneously names existing practice as achieved (Form i) and positions the field within the legitimizing normative trajectory (Form ii-adjacent). I considered arguing that the distinction is too fine to bear the weight the supportive puts on it. I didn't pursue this: the adversarial's stronger argument is structural, not hermeneutic. If the form-selection residual fails on structural grounds (3.6.A, 3.6.B), the empirical-fragility argument is unnecessary and less clean.

**Arguing that the Liebman vocabulary was so general as to apply to any form.** The vocabulary's diagnostic content is what makes 3.6.A work; arguing it's general would undercut the salience-gradient argument (3.5.B) that the adversarial already used successfully. Inconsistency risk.

**Conceding the temporal problem in Scenario 2 and arguing it's acceptable.** The supportive's temporal argument is genuinely interesting — it correctly identifies that if P1 generates Mode III, Mode III can't have been the causal input to the framing that enabled P1. But once 3.6.A establishes that form (i) was predictable from structural dynamics alone, the temporal issue dissolves. There's nothing to concede.

**Arguing that Brazil's prova legal history makes Form (ii) implausible for a different reason.** Brazil's actual *prova legal* history is complex — it wasn't simply a system the Liebman tradition was reforming against; it was partially already in transition before Liebman arrived. I don't have the primary sources to make this argument without fabrication. Discarded.

## What I was confident about

The core of 3.6.A is tight. If the supportive accepts (as it does) that the collective legitimation incentive operates at the academic field level and explains orientation, it must explain why the same mechanism doesn't apply to the specific form-selection question when the vocabulary's diagnostic content makes one form more deficit-implying than the other. The supportive's §3.5 relies on abstracting the vocabulary from its genealogy; that abstraction is not available in the specific historical context.

The core of 3.6.B is also tight, though it has a potential weak point: the supportive could distinguish "form (i) production by observation" from "form (i) production as an accomplished state claim" — arguing that naming a practice as "achieved rationality gain" (as the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra framing does) requires more than mere observation; it requires positive endorsement from practitioners who ARE claiming it as their achieved authority. This is the strongest available supportive counter, and I anticipated it in §4's final reply. The response: the "achieved" framing is a scholarly theoretical characterization, not a transcript of practitioner endorsements. The scholar says "this is *livre convencimento*," placing the observed practice within the vocabulary's positive designatum. The "achievement" is the scholar's theoretical attribution to the field, not a report of what practitioners claim for themselves.

## What remains open

- Portugal/Argentina comparison: the only empirical test that could definitively close the debate. Neither side has it. Remains the primary open question.
- Whether the supportive can find a way to neutralize the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy — show that Brazilian proceduralists engaged with Liebman in a context-stripped way that made Form (ii) available without deficit implication. I don't think this is available, but I haven't seen primary sources.
- The adversarial r4 blog note ("if the next supportive round doesn't produce pre-C3 Mode III documentation or peripheral-peripheral comparative evidence, I will consider acknowledging the debate has reached its honest informational limit") was relevant at the content-selection level. That level is now closed (adversarial succeeds). At the form-selection level, the question is whether the supportive can supply either (e) vocabulary content neutralization evidence or (f) a theory of academic-naming that requires Mode III practitioner endorsement as a necessary input. Neither looks available from the current record. If the supportive's r5 response cannot supply these, I will need to assess honestly whether the debate has reached its informational limit at the form-selection level as well.

## Assessment of where this stands

After five rounds:
- Direction: settled (structural mechanism suffices)
- Orientation: settled (peripheral-field dynamics suffices)
- Content selection: settled (C1+C2 salience + vocabulary specificity suffice)
- Form selection: live — adversarial r5 attacks on two independent structural grounds; supportive has not yet responded

The debate has been progressively compressing around an increasingly narrow question. The current front — why Form (i) specifically, not just validation in general — is real and non-trivial. The adversarial's answer is that the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy does the work Mode III was invoked to do. The supportive's answer is that abstracting the vocabulary from its genealogy makes Form (ii) equivalently available, and only Mode III explains the specific form.

The debate's honest informational limit is approaching at the form-selection level, but has not been reached yet — unlike the content-selection level (where neither side had the Portugal/Argentina evidence to decide between Mode III pre-C3 and P1 post-C3), the form-selection level has structural arguments available that can in principle close without primary sources. Whether 3.6.A is correct turns on whether the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy was operative in the specific reception context. This is a structural-interpretive question, not an empirical one requiring primary sources unavailable to either side.

I'm more confident about r5 than I was about any of the three r4 attacks. The r4 attacks were correct but the supportive had a genuine structural move available (content-form distinction). The r5 attacks on the form-selection residual strike me as genuinely closing, unless the supportive finds a way to neutralize the diagnostic genealogy argument — which would require either (a) showing Liebman was received as a neutral methodological principle in the Brazilian context, or (b) showing the "publicly claimable" requirement is more demanding than academic-naming. Neither is apparent from the exchange record.
