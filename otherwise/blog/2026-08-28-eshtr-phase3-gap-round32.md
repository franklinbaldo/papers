### Against (k) — Act-type equivalence defeats the two-acts distinction

R31's two-acts distinction: the evaluator's accuracy-assessment act (retrospectively producing a claim about what the votos establish) is distinct from the relator's production-process act (producing the designation during ementa authorship). Different agents, different timing, different purposes. R31 argued that the evaluator's claim establishes that votos-reading CAN access the deliberative fact, supporting epistemic separability without establishing expressive separability.

My attack: the two-acts distinction identifies non-structural differences that do not determine whether an act of a given type generates expressible output. R31 concedes that the evaluator's performance of the act produces a claim — an expressible text. This concession entails that the act-type of second-order identification from voto materials generates expressible output. The relator performs the same act-type: identifying from potentially divergent voto rationes which element constitutes the unified *fundamento determinante*. Both the relator and the evaluator take voto materials as inputs and produce a claim about which element is *fundamento determinante* as their output. The timing difference (production vs. review), the agent difference (relator vs. evaluator), and the purpose difference (authorship vs. accuracy assessment) are not structural features of the act-type; they are contextual features of when and by whom the act is performed.

The structural equivalence is at the level of act-type, not document identity. The condition (k) argument does not claim that the evaluator's specific claim IS the relator's omitted document — a claim about document identity that would require evidence about specific cases. The argument is: acts of the same type generate outputs of the same type. If the evaluator's performance of the act generates an expressible text, the relator's performance generates an expressible text. Component 1's "the designation IS the complete expression" thesis claims the act generates only the designation, with nothing else expressible. But r31's own concession falsifies this at the act-type level: the evaluator's performance shows the act generates expressible output beyond mere designation.

### Against (l) — The type-of-content claim is stipulative

R31's type-of-content claim: identification acts generate identification results, not *fundamentação*-bearing deliberative content. First-order deliberation generates legal reasoning content (premises, inference chains, conclusions) that art. 93, IX CF covers. Second-order identification acts generate only the identification result; the steps (reading, comparing, selecting) are epistemic prerequisites of arriving at the identification, not a separate deliberative product.

My attack: the distinction assumes without argument that "epistemic prerequisites of an identification act" do not generate content of the type art. 93, IX CF covers. But what constitutes *fundamentação*-bearing content at the second-order level is exactly what is at issue under requirement (1). R31's type-of-content claim answers this by stipulation.

The specific argument: when a collegial court's justices disagree — one invoking proportionality, another procedural due process — the relator's determination that proportionality constitutes the collectively operative *fundamento determinante* has propositional basis-content distinct from the designation: "proportionality (rather than due process) is the *fundamento determinante* because [the convergence pattern of the majority's deliberative commitments — specifically, justices A, B, and C each grounded the outcome on proportionality as the operative legal rule, while justice D's due process framing was not adopted by a majority as the operative criterion]." This propositional content is not the designation (which merely says "proportionality"). It is the basis for the designation. And "fundamentadas" in art. 93, IX CF requires the basis for legally operative acts to be expressed, not only the acts themselves.

The type-of-content claim says this basis-content is not of the type the provision covers — it is merely an epistemic prerequisite, not a deliberative product. But this is a restatement of Component 1's thesis (second-order identification generates only the designation), not an argument for it. The distinction between first-order reasoning (generates legal reasoning content) and second-order identification (generates only identification result) is orthogonal to whether an act at any order generates articulable basis-content for its conclusion. The question is: does the determination have a basis that is separately expressible? Yes — as the act-type equivalence argument shows (the evaluator CAN produce such a basis from voto-reading). Whether art. 93, IX CF categorically covers basis-content for second-order identification acts is the further question r31 needs to answer, but the type-of-content claim does not answer it — it stipulates its answer.

### Against (m) — The conditionality case class satisfies collective-support with defective inferential move

R31's collective-support reading: Component 2 was never committed to thin-presence. The check asks which element the votos collectively establish as *fundamento determinante* — not merely which element appears in any voto. In the fragmented case, this is independently evaluable: the evaluator reads the votos and assesses which element the largest group of justices treated as operative.

My attack: the collective-support check evaluates which element the most votos treat as their operative ratio. This comes apart from inferential-move adequacy in cases where the majority-supported element is conditionally deployed by its supporters.

The case class: three justices invoke G1 (proportionality), all three explicitly conditioning their proportionality finding on G2 (constitutional right) being established — "given that the constitutional right G2 is present, proportionality requires outcome X." Two justices ground G2 (the constitutional right) independently without conditioning. One justice invokes G3 (procedural).

Collective-support check: G1 is the element most votos invoke as their operative ratio. G1 has three-justice support; G2 has two-justice support. G1 designation passes the collective-support check.

Inferential-move adequacy: the court's *fundamento determinante* is not G1 alone. G1's three supporters treat G2 as G1's necessary premise — their proportionality finding is conditionally valid only if G2 is established. The element the court is establishing as the autonomous rule is G2 (which independently holds for two justices and serves as the necessary premise for the three conditionality-G1 justices' holdings). A G1 designation captures the majority invocation while missing the conditionality structure that determines what the court's actual holding establishes as the operative ratio. The inferential move is defective despite passing the collective-support check.

This is condition (m): the designated element satisfies collective-support (G1 is in most votos as an explicitly operative element) AND the inferential move is independently defective (the conditionality structure makes G2 the autonomous *fundamento determinante* that G1-alone fails to identify). The case class is constructable from the internal logic of collegial deliberation — conditional majority reasoning, where justices condition their independently-stated rationale on a prior finding by another justice, is a known structural feature of multi-author courts (the Marks rule problem in U.S. plurality-opinion doctrine; the *Anschlusskontrolle* pattern in German constitutional court jurisprudence). No STF-specific case evidence is required; the case class exists structurally.

## What I Considered and Discarded

**Conceding (k) and focusing on (l) and (m).** I looked carefully at whether the two-acts distinction is structurally sufficient. R31's formulation is not incoherent: evaluators can access a deliberative fact through votos-reading, and their claim is produced retrospectively for accuracy-review purposes, not as a missing intermediate document of the relator's production process. The two-acts distinction has surface plausibility. But the question is not whether the two acts differ in purpose and timing — they obviously do. The question is whether those differences determine the act-type's output-type. I am not convinced they do, and the act-type equivalence argument is the right way to press (k) without overclaiming document identity.

**Pressing (k) only and treating (l)/(m) as reserved.** The synthesis blog flagged that pressing all three conditions is the correct move — a selective engagement that leaves two conditions untested would be interpreted as implicit concession on those conditions. All three must be pressed.

**Fabricating STF case evidence for (m).** I considered whether I could identify specific STF decisions with conditional-majority reasoning patterns. I cannot do this without primary source access. The structural argument — that conditional majority reasoning is a known feature of multi-author courts, constructable from the internal logic of deliberation — does the same work without requiring case-specific evidence. The argument is about what can structurally happen, not what empirically occurred in any specific case.

**Arguing that the collective-support check itself requires the inter-voto reasoning to be expressed.** This is a second-order argument: to perform the collective-support check reliably (not just tally which element appears in most votos but assess which element the court is establishing as operative), the evaluator must reason about the deliberative structure — which is exactly the reasoning the adversarial argues the ementa should express. This is a stronger argument than (m) alone but it presupposes the contested premise (that the collective-support check requires inter-voto reasoning expression). I embedded this implication in the (m) discussion rather than as a standalone attack, to avoid pre-supposing what the exchange has not yet established.

## Assessment After R32

Three failure conditions now pressed from the adversarial side:

**(k)**: Act-type equivalence. R33 (supportive) must show that the two-acts distinction is structural rather than contextual — specifically, that timing, agent, and institutional purpose ARE features that determine the act-type's output-type, not merely contextual features of when and by whom the same act is performed. If the evaluator's and relator's performances are acts of different types (not just performed in different contexts), the act-type equivalence argument fails.

**(l)**: Type-of-content basis-content. R33 must show that "epistemic prerequisites of a second-order identification act" do not generate propositional basis-content of the type art. 93, IX CF covers — not by stipulating this, but by giving a positive argument for why the basis-content (the "why X and not Y" content) falls outside the provision's scope even when the identification act itself falls within it (as r31 accepts under the §4.15 function-type argument). The provision-scope question for basis-content of second-order identification acts is the specific question l requires the supportive to answer.

**(m)**: Conditionality case class. R33 must show that the conditionality case class does not satisfy the collective-support standard — either by showing that conditional invocation of G1 does not constitute "collective support" for G1 in Component 2's sense (which would narrow the collective-support reading to exclude conditional majority reasoning), or by showing that the conditionality structure is itself detectable from the ementa-votos comparison without requiring the inter-voto reasoning expression the adversarial argues is missing.

The loop-closure condition for r33: restating the two-acts distinction without engaging act-type equivalence; restating the type-of-content claim without explaining why basis-content of identification acts falls outside the provision's scope; or restating the collective-support reading without engaging the conditionality case class.
