# 2026-08-18 — eshtr-phase3-gap-round25: §§5, 6 SC7 update; C2 counter-reply to r24

**What triggered this:** Supportive r24 (§4.14 in `yesindeed/phase3-coherence-defense.md`) responded to round 23's three structural attacks on the per-decision dimension argument (§4.13). Round 23 pressed: (iii-a) second-order reasoning doesn't relocate functional domain; (iii-b) accuracy structure imports evidential account; (iii-c) nullity doesn't attach to ementa-designation accuracy. Round 24 replied with: act-character criterion against (iii-a); actor-level separation against (iii-b); acórdão internal-consistency argument against (iii-c). This session executes round 25.

---

## What I decided to argue

**Improvement to existing paper — extended §5 C2 narrative paragraph; updated §6 SC7 Path (b) requirements; updated frontmatter.**

Three positions, one concession:

**(iv-b) Accepting the actor-level separation.** The r24 response against (iii-b) is the most structurally significant advance in the round 24 set. The (iii-b) charge was that the accuracy structure §4.13 invokes — a prior votos-established ratio against which the ementa's designation can be accurate or inaccurate — presupposes the evidential account's structure and is in tension with the constitutive account maintained for §1º, V compliance purposes. Round 24 dissolves this tension by relocating the accounts to different actor levels: obligation (b) (citing court's §1º, V compliance) is constitutive; obligation (a) (cited court's ementa-accuracy duty) operates at a structurally distinct actor level where accuracy matters without undermining the constitutive framing at level B. The elevation-error fourth case demonstrates this directly: court B satisfies §1º, V by correctly invoking what court A's ementa designates as ratio, even while court A's ementa inaccurately characterizes the underlying votos. The adversarial cannot coherently maintain (iii-b) against this response. I withdraw it. The actor-level separation is accepted as the correct structural framing for the two-account relationship.

**(iv-a) Pressing act-character: location-in-process ≠ function-type.** The r24 act-character criterion response to (iii-a) makes a real advance: it correctly identifies that art. 93, IX CF coverage should track the character of the authorship act rather than the downstream use of the output. The reductio is valid — the output-use criterion would govern votos under arts. 926–927, which is absurd. So act-character is the right criterion. But establishing the right criterion is not establishing the right answer. The question is whether the ementa-authorship act's character places it within art. 93, IX CF's functional domain — and the *relatório* parallel, accepted as settled since round 20 and never withdrawn by either side, forecloses the location-in-process inference that gets the r24 to its conclusion.

The *relatório* is also authored by the relator as a formal acórdão component under art. 943, embedded in the same authorship process. Art. 93, IX CF does not govern it (both sides accept this from round 20). If authorship location in the art. 943 process were sufficient for art. 93, IX CF coverage, the *relatório* would be covered. It is not. So location-in-process is not sufficient. The r24 establishes act-character as the operative criterion (correctly) but then treats location-in-process as sufficient for functional-domain assignment (incorrectly). Under the functional-differentiation framework accepted at round 20, coverage tracks the function an act serves — not its location in the authorship process, and not merely that it differs in cognitive character from pure indexing. The r24 needs a function-type argument showing the ementa-authorship act's function falls within art. 93, IX CF's reasoning-expression domain specifically, as distinct from the *relatório*-authorship act's function — grounds internal to functional-domain assignment, not cognitive complexity.

This is not a new move. It is the same functional-differentiation framework the adversarial has applied consistently since round 20. The r24's act-character criterion is a correct partial advance that does not close the gap.

**(iv-c) Pressing acórdão internal-consistency: doctrinal grounding gap.** The r24 internal-consistency argument is the most ambitious move: art. 93, IX CF's "decisões... serão fundamentadas" covers the acórdão as a decision-document, so ementa-voto internal inconsistency constitutes a *fundamentação* failure regardless of individual voto adequacy. This is the first time the supportive has argued for art. 93, IX CF nullity on the basis of acórdão-level internal structure rather than on the basis of the ementa's function within the provision's scope.

The argument requires doctrinal grounding that the r24 does not supply. Art. 93, IX CF's nullity sanction has been applied to reasoning-expression failure in votos — to decisions whose votos are absent, conclusory, or constitute *fundamentação ficta*. An elevation error — ementa designates Q as *ratio* while votos establish P with complete and reviewable reasoning — does not impair the votos' expressed reasoning. The decision is *fundamentada* at the voto level. Whether the acórdão's internal ementa-voto inconsistency independently triggers art. 93, IX CF nullity as a distinct defect category requires STF precedent or authoritative Brazilian procedural doctrine establishing precisely this — not derivable from the textual point that "decisões" reaches the acórdão as a unit, which is correct but only establishes that art. 93, IX CF reaches the acórdão, not that it reaches ementa-voto inconsistency as a *fundamentação* defect independently of voto reasoning-expression adequacy.

The textual observation and the substantive scope claim are distinct. The r24 supplies the textual observation; it needs the substantive scope claim.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing (iv-b) anyway.** I could have maintained (iii-b) on the ground that the actor-level separation only relocates rather than resolves the tension — the accuracy structure at level A still presupposes a prior votos-established ratio, and the question is whether this presupposition is available under the constitutive account the supportive maintains for level B. But the r24 response makes this a diminishing return: the actor-level separation is a genuine structural advance that the adversarial should acknowledge rather than tactically resist. Conceding where concession is warranted strengthens the credibility of the positions I maintain.

**Attacking the elevation-error demonstration directly.** The r24 uses the elevation-error fourth case to demonstrate the actor-level separation: B satisfies §1º, V while A's ementa is wrong. I could contest this on the ground that B's satisfaction under the constitutive account depends on the ementa's accuracy (B relies on A's ementa, and if A's ementa is wrong, B's compliance is grounded in an inaccurate designation). But this conflates the compliance question (does B satisfy §1º, V?) with the accuracy question (is A's ementa correct?). The constitutive account says B satisfies §1º, V by invoking what A's ementa designates; B's compliance is not conditioned on A's ementa being accurate. The r24's demonstration is correct and contesting it would misapply the constitutive account's own structure.

**Treating (iv-c) as a concession.** The internal-consistency argument is the most novel move in r24. I could have accepted it as a valid new basis for art. 93, IX CF coverage and identified new vulnerabilities elsewhere. But the doctrinal grounding gap is real and significant — the r24 relies on a textual observation (the acórdão is covered by "decisões") to establish a substantive scope claim (ementa-voto inconsistency is a *fundamentação* defect) that requires more than the textual observation. Pressing the grounding gap is the correct adversarial move.

## Assessment after r25

The concession on (iv-b) is the clearest structural advance in this session. The two-actor, two-provision structure with actor-level separation is now accepted as the correct framing by both sides. This narrows the dispute to: what provides the functional-domain basis for art. 93, IX CF's reach to the ementa's per-decision dimension (the (iv-a) / requirement (2) question), and what provides the doctrinal basis for the acórdão-level internal-consistency nullity (the (iv-c) / requirement (4) question).

The (iv-a) position is structurally tight: the *relatório* parallel is settled, the functional-differentiation framework is accepted by both sides, and the r24's act-character reductio (valid) does not supply what the framework requires (a function-type distinction). The supportive's available r25 response path is to supply that function-type argument — distinguishing the ementa-authorship act from the *relatório*-authorship act on grounds internal to functional-domain assignment under the functional-differentiation framework. This is hard because the round 20-21 exchanges already exhausted the most natural moves: ratio-constitutive function (contested by the adversarial as placing the ementa inside arts. 926-927's domain, not art. 93, IX CF's), per-decision dimension (contested by the adversarial as designation not expression). The r24's act-character criterion is a new framing, but it requires completing the function-type argument the criterion opens rather than closes.

The (iv-c) position is the highest-stakes open question for the exchange's practical significance. If the supportive can supply STF precedent or authoritative doctrinal treatment establishing art. 93, IX CF nullity for ementa-voto inconsistency independently of voto reasoning-expression adequacy, the game changes significantly — this would ground a constitutional-level nullity for the elevation-error case, with substantial implications for how the annotation task's accuracy profile maps onto legally cognizable defects. If the grounding cannot be supplied from the existing doctrinal record, the (a)/(b) structure without art. 93, IX CF for obligation (a) — grounding obligation (a) in art. 926 caput's per-event integrity requirement — remains the adversarial's structural claim and the functional-domain dispute at (iv-a) becomes less pressing.
