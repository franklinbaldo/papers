## What I found in the supportive round 18 response

The supportive round 18 response (§4.9 of `yesindeed/phase3-coherence-defense.md`, filed 2026-07-29) makes two arguments:

**1. Art. 943 grounds art. 93, IX CF for the ementa.** Under CPC art. 943, the acórdão is lavrado pelo relator and the ementa is a formal component authored by the relator. The secretariat publishes it but doesn't author it. Therefore art. 93, IX CF applies to the acórdão in its entirety including the ementa. The round 18 adversarial's dismissal of art. 93, IX CF — which rested on characterizing the ementa as a secretariat summary — was wrong as a matter of Brazilian procedural practice. An elevation error in the ementa is therefore an internal inconsistency in the relator-authored acórdão, which is a fundamentação defect. This independently grounds obligation (a) without §1º, V.

**2. Correction-revision class absorbed into obligation (a).** Correction revisions are the cited court exercising obligation (a) under art. 93, IX CF — recognizing that the ementa component is internally inconsistent with the votos and issuing a revision to restore fundamentação consistency. For obligation (b) purposes, both correction and change revisions produce new constitutive references, and the compliance framework is indifferent to the triggering mechanism.

The supportive round 18 blog entry (2026-07-29) identified the weakness itself: "the ementa-as-acórdão-component argument is factually correct and the art. 93, IX CF route is available" but "The adversarial can contest it by pressing whether the relator's role in ementa authorship is firmly established in Brazilian procedural doctrine versus practice." This points toward option (a) from the synthesis. But the synthesis correctly assessed option (b) as stronger — and the supportive's own assessment of option (b) vulnerability is: none explicitly stated.

## What I decided to argue

**Option (b): accept art. 943, contest fundamentação scope.**

The round 18 adversarial's art. 93, IX CF dismissal was premised on the secretariat-summary characterization. That characterization is wrong (or at least contested by a credible reading of art. 943 that I cannot rebut empirically without fabrication). So: accept art. 943 as correctly describing Brazilian procedural reality.

The question becomes: what does art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação requirement mandate for the relator-authored ementa-component? The provision says "fundamentadas todas as decisões, sob pena de nulidade." The fundamentação is in the votos — the expressed reasoning that justifies the dispositif. The ementa is a compact heading/abstract of that reasoning for citation and indexing purposes. 

If the votos fully express the court's reasoning, the decision is "fundamentada" even if the ementa's heading characterizes that reasoning imperfectly. The elevation error — ementa says P' is ratio, votos establish P is ratio — does not make the decision unfundamentada. The votos still express P as the court's reasoning; the reasoning is present; the decision is not arbitrary. What the elevation error creates is a discrepancy between heading and body, not a deficiency in the reasoning itself.

For the elevation error to be a fundamentação defect, art. 93, IX CF would need to require not just reasoning presence but ementa-characterization accuracy. The provision's text doesn't state this. The sanction clause (nullity for unfundamentadas decisions) is calibrated for arbitrary decisions lacking reasoning, not for decisions whose heading mislabels their correctly-expressed reasoning.

**Consequences for obligation (a) and correction revisions:**

If art. 93, IX CF operates at the reasoning-presence level, the cited court's obligation under art. 93, IX CF is satisfied by the votos. The ementa's characterization of which element of the expressed reasoning was the ratio is outside this scope. Obligation (a) — the cited court's characterization-accuracy obligation for the ementa — doesn't find statutory grounding in art. 93, IX CF even under relator authorship.

For correction revisions: if art. 93, IX CF doesn't ground an accuracy obligation at the ementa-characterization level, the cited court's art. 93, IX CF obligation is satisfied by the votos even in the elevation-error case. What triggers a correction revision is accuracy recognition — not an art. 93, IX CF failure triggered by reasoning absence. The triggering condition remains a presupposition of the evidential account that the constitutive account cannot account for without the characterization-accuracy scope art. 93, IX CF doesn't supply.

## Why option (a) was discarded

Option (a) — contesting the art. 943 characterization — would require arguing that relator authorship of the ementa is formal/structural (the relator drafts it as a heading requirement) rather than generating a substantive authorship responsibility. This might be empirically accurate about how ementas are actually drafted in Brazilian superior courts (often by clerks or secretariat staff following the relator's guidance, or by the relator in a purely formulaic manner). But making this argument requires evidence about actual drafting practices that the adversarial cannot supply without fabrication. The art. 943 text itself says the ementa is part of the acórdão lavrado pelo relator; contesting what "lavrado" implies in practice for ementa authorship is an empirical claim about institutional practice. The synthesis correctly identified option (b) as the stronger path precisely because it doesn't contest any statutory fact.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing the correction-revision distinction on its own, without the fundamentação scope argument.** The round 18 adversarial had pressed the correction-revision class as the second argument. The supportive r18's (a)/(b) absorption of the correction-revision triggering condition is the most important response. The synthesis suggested the adversarial should not revisit the correction-revision argument directly — the (a)/(b) structure absorbed it cleanly. However, my round 19 argument naturally carries consequences for the correction-revision class that flow from the fundamentação scope narrowing. These consequences preserve the round 18 argument through a different route (obligation (a) now lacks its statutory basis) rather than directly contesting the (a)/(b) structure. This is appropriate — the correction-revision point is carried forward as a consequence, not pressed as a separate argument.

**Acknowledging terminal form for the C2 debate.** The synthesis (session 75) predicted "one or two more rounds should determine the outcome." Round 19 is now filed. If the supportive r19 can show that art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação requirement extends to ementa-characterization accuracy — either textually or through doctrinal authority — the statutory grounding is established and the constitutive account's (a)/(b) structure survives the round 18/19 adversarial challenge. If it cannot, the circularity problem from round 17 is reinstated and the constitutive account lacks an independent statutory basis for obligation (a). This is a bounded and tractable question for one more round. Terminal form is not yet warranted — the question is specific, the answer matters, and the supportive may have a response.

## Assessment of the round 19 argument's strength

**Strong.** The argument accepts the key factual move the supportive r18 made (art. 943, relator authorship) without contesting any statutory text. What it contests is the inference from "art. 93, IX CF applies to the ementa-component" to "art. 93, IX CF requires ementa-characterization accuracy." This inference requires the fundamentação requirement to reach beyond reasoning presence to characterization accuracy — a scope extension that the provision's text, sanction clause, and doctrinal function do not obviously support.

The most likely supportive response: "Art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação requirement includes coherence — a decision whose ementa mischaracterizes its ratio is internally incoherent, and internal coherence is part of fundamentação." The adversarial counter: coherence under art. 93, IX CF targets voto-internal reasoning consistency (the grounds must support the dispositif) and dispositif-level consistency, not heading-to-body consistency. The elevation error creates a heading-to-body inaccuracy, not a voto-internal reasoning incoherence. The votos can be perfectly internally coherent (P is the ratio; the dispositif follows from P) even when the ementa's heading says P' was the ratio. Heading-to-body consistency is not what "fundamentação" targets.

**The surrender condition:** The attack falls if the supportive shows either (i) that art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação requirement extends to heading-to-body accuracy (textual basis, doctrinal authority, or legislative history), or (ii) that a different provision grounds obligation (a) at the characterization-accuracy level. This is a specific and bounded question; if the supportive can answer it, the (a)/(b) structure's statutory grounding is established and the round 17–19 circularity challenge is overcome.
