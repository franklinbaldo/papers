# Paper 1G r8: version (i*) is caught in a structural dilemma

**What triggered this:** Supportive round 7 (`yesindeed/paper1g-doctrinalization-mechanism.md`, §3.8, filed 2026-08-15) responded to adversarial r7's three attacks on the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence with version (i*): the two-institution structure of the Italian CPC 1940 — separately codifying Art. 116 (*libero convincimento*) and Art. 132 (*motivazione*) — is itself evidence that the Italian proceduralist tradition treated the two obligations as analytically distinct at the scholarly level. The claim: accountability was available in the Italian vocabulary as a separate procedural institution (Art. 132's domain), but was not constituted as an internal positive component of *libero convincimento*'s positive designatum in the scholarship organized around Art. 116's evidentiary evaluation domain. This is version (i*): not version (i) (the concept was unavailable — improbable given Art. 132's existence, as r7 established) and not version (ii) (the concept was in the unified vocabulary but selectively not adopted — where the structural account already competes), but a third position that locates the analytical separation within the Italian tradition's own organizational structure. Synthesis session 84 had identified adversarial r7's central structural claim as hard to avoid and had previewed the supportive r7 path (version (i*)) as the most productive move available after r7. This session executes r8.

## What I decided to argue

**Two attacks on version (i*):**

**§3.8.D — The version (i*) dilemma**

Version (i*) requires the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship to have been organized around Art. 116's evidentiary evaluation domain while keeping accountability analytically separate. This generates an exhaustive two-possibility structure:

*Possibility A:* The Italian scholarship maintained this analytical separation. Then any receiving field inherits it from the Italian tradition without patrimonialism — the tradition as transmitted organized the scholarship in the way that predicted non-integration. Portugal and Argentina, receiving the same tradition organized the same way, would show the same non-integration. The patrimonialism contribution is eliminated.

*Possibility B:* The Italian scholarship did not maintain this analytical separation — it engaged with accountability as part of the rational predicate that organized the *libero convincimento* debates (what makes free evaluation rational rather than arbitrary). Then the code's two-institution structure does not establish the scholarship's analytical separation; it reflects codification technique, not the organization of scholarly discourse. Version (i*)'s structural inference is invalid.

The reform tradition's rational predicate establishes a prior in favor of Possibility B. The *libero convincimento* debates were organized around a normative question: what distinguishes rational from arbitrary free evaluation? The accountability dimension — that free evaluation must be capable of reasoned exposition, intersubjectively reviewable — is the answer. A scholarship organized around this question would engage with accountability as an internal feature of the concept's scholarly positive designatum, not as an external Art. 132 obligation. Possibility A requires the scholarship to have engaged with the rationality distinction while excluding the accountability answer — leaving the central normative predicate empirically empty at the scholarly deployment level.

Possibility A eliminates patrimonialism's contribution; Possibility B defeats version (i*)'s structural inference. Neither is available to the supportive without cost.

**§3.8.E — The selection problem relocation**

Under version (i*), *motivação* was available as a separate judgment-form obligation but not integrated into *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum as an internal component. The selection question shifts from "within a unified concept" to "across the institution-to-concept integration boundary." The adversarial's structural account applies at this level with equal force: legitimation incentives predicted constituting *livre convencimento*'s rational pole around the evidentiary freedom dimension specifically — because that is where Brazilian courts' informal practice was located, making it the natural attachment point for the retrospective validation framing. Integrating *motivação* as an internal positive component would require acknowledging that the freedom principle, to be fully instantiated, requires an accountability practice Brazilian informal courts didn't exhibit. The same legitimation incentives that predicted Form (i) over Form (ii) and validation over critique predict non-integration of the separate accountability institution into the evidentiary concept's rational pole. Version (i*) relocates the selection problem without resolving the explanatory competition.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing only the Possibility A horn without the Possibility B horn.** The Possibility A argument alone is strong: if the Italian scholarship maintained analytical separation, any receiving field inherits it. But pressing only Possibility A would leave the supportive an escape route: say the Italian scholarship didn't maintain strict separation, but Liebman's specific USP transmission did (due to the reform project's focus on the *prova legal* problem rather than the *motivazione* structure). The two-horn dilemma is cleaner: the supportive has to defend either that the Italian scholarship maintained separation (Possibility A → patrimonialism eliminated) or that it didn't (Possibility B → code structure doesn't establish version i*). Both horns need to be in place simultaneously.

**Arguing that codification structure always tracks scholarly discourse.** This would be historically inaccurate and easily rebutted. The Pandectist tradition's relationship between code structure and scholarly discourse is the obvious counterexample. The correct argument is more precise: the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship was organized around the rationality question, and the accountability dimension is the answer to that question within the tradition's own terms. The code's two-institution structure doesn't distinguish between Possibilities A and B because code structure and scholarly discourse organization are independent dimensions. The structural prior comes from the reform tradition's normative logic, not from a general claim about code-discourse tracking.

**Providing primary-source evidence from Liebman's own works.** I do not have access to Liebman's USP-period works and do not fabricate citations. The argument is a structural probability argument from the reform tradition's rational predicate, not a primary-source citation argument. The falsification condition I've added (§6 condition k) correctly places the burden on the supportive: to show from the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically that the evidentiary evaluation debates engaged with the rationality question while excluding accountability from the answer. This is the right empirical path, and it's their burden, not mine.

**Conceding version (i*) and arguing only at the §3.8.E level.** §3.8.E alone (the selection problem relocation) is sound but allows the supportive to claim that version (i*) has isolated a level at which the structural account applies less directly — the decision to integrate a separate institution into a concept's positive designatum is arguably more dependent on conceptual framework than the decision to adopt vocabulary from within a unified concept. This is worth examining but I'm not convinced it's a strong escape. Still, the version (i*) dilemma (§3.8.D) is the more decisive structural challenge, and both are needed.

## Assessment

**Strength of the attack.** §3.8.D is the most structurally decisive attack in the paper1G debate since §3.7.D (the accepted concession). It forces the supportive into a dilemma neither horn of which is available without defeating version (i*)'s key purpose. Possibility A defeats the patrimonialism contribution by showing any receiving field would show the same non-integration. Possibility B defeats the structural inference from code structure to scholarly discourse organization. The only escape is to establish, from primary sources in the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically, that the evidentiary evaluation debates engaged with the rationality question while keeping accountability analytically external — §6 condition (k) specifies this precisely.

§3.8.E is structurally sound but not as novel: it extends the §3.8.B competition argument to the version (i*) level. The structural account applies at every level of the selection problem the supportive introduces. This is a pattern the supportive must address if it wants to claim that patrimonialism generates an independently distinguishable prediction at the concept-formation level.

**What the supportive r8 response needs to do:**

The most demanding requirement is to establish Possibility A without triggering its implication: that any receiving field would show the same non-integration. This requires showing that the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship was analytically organized around Art. 116's domain while keeping accountability separate — AND that the analytical separation is not simply inherited by any receiving field (requiring some field-specific mechanism for non-integration that patrimonialism supplies while non-patrimonial fields would overcome). This is a difficult showing: Possibility A's analytical-inheritance implication is direct, and blocking it would require either (a) claiming that non-patrimonial peripheral fields under equivalent legitimation pressure would integrate the accountability dimension through some mechanism that patrimonial fields lack, or (b) claiming that Portugal and Argentina (which received the same analytically-separated tradition) did in fact integrate the accountability dimension into their evidentiary concept.

If the supportive goes to (a), it needs to specify what mechanism non-patrimonial peripheral fields have for integrating the accountability dimension into their evidentiary concept that patrimonial fields lack — and this is precisely where the Portugal/Argentina comparison operates. If the supportive goes to (b), it needs comparative evidence from Portuguese and Argentine proceduralist vocabularies at the concept-formation level. Either path leads to the empirical comparison that both sides have acknowledged as the debate's primary open empirical test.

**Open after r8:** Whether the supportive can establish from Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship (as specified in condition k) that Possibility A is the actual organization of the scholarship — and, if so, what mechanism explains why non-patrimonial receiving fields would integrate the accountability dimension while patrimonial fields would not. The version (i*) argument would survive the §3.8.D dilemma only if Possibility A is established and this mechanism specified.
