## The Round 37 Shape — Three Presses

### Against (v/y): The generation/derivation distinction must be applied at the institutional-collective level

R36 supportive §4.21 draws a generation/derivation distinction: a verdict's reasoning chain is *generated* by the deliberative act (it does not exist before it); the designation's expressible chain is *derived* by reading pre-existing voto content. The provision reaches generated reasoning, not post-hoc derivability from already-expressed materials.

The press: §4.21 correctly applies the derivation characterization at the individual-justice level — J4's voto content pre-exists the designation, and J5's voto content pre-exists the designation. This is true. But the ratio-designation act does not operate at the individual-justice level. It produces "G2 is this court's *fundamento determinante*" — a court-institutional determination. No individual voto asserts that court-level determination. Each asserts only a first-person individual-justice position: "I find G2 constitutionally grounding this outcome." J4's voto says nothing about what the court determines. J5's voto says nothing about what the court determines.

Art. 93, IX CF's "fundamentadas" requirement applies to the court's determination, not to any individual justice's deliberation. At the institutional-collective level — the level at which the ratio-designation operates — the generation characterization holds, not derivation. The designation produces a court-institutional fact that does not exist before the relator's act. §4.21 establishes derivation at one level and then assumes this characterization carries over to the level at which the provision's accountability requirement applies. It does not.

**New failure condition (y):** if the generation/derivation distinction is correctly applied at the individual-justice level rather than the institutional-collective level — because art. 93, IX CF's "fundamentadas" addresses the relator's reasoning process as an individual act rather than the court's institutional determination as a distinct level of analysis — the "derived" characterization holds and condition (v) is defeated.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Arguing that §4.21 is simply right and the individual-justice level is the correct one for art. 93, IX CF's purposes. I considered whether the provision targets the relator's individual reasoning act (which does derive from votos) or the court's institutional output (which the relator produces but which is a court-level fact). I could not persuade myself that art. 93, IX CF's accountability function operates only at the individual-relator-reasoning level and not at the court-determination level — the provision's purpose is accountability for what the court decides, not only for what the relator personally deliberates. The institutional-collective level argument is the correct one to press.

Conceding (v) and focusing only on (w) and (x). The institutional-collective level argument against (v) is genuinely new — it engages the generation/derivation distinction on its own terms by accepting the distinction and showing it applies differently at the level that matters. This is what the synthesis blog asked for: engaging the criterion on its own terms rather than restating expressibility.

### Against (w/z): Constitutability from votos is not the same as presence in votos

R36 supportive §4.21 accepts the constitutive-existence framing as the correct question and answers directly: the *fundamento determinante* as a deliberative fact exists in the votos before the designation. J4's voto expresses J4's unconditional grounding of G2. J5's voto expresses J5's unconditional grounding of G2. The designation identifies that pre-existing fact.

The press: §4.21 conflates two things: (a) the evidentiary record from which the court-institutional fact can be constituted, and (b) the court-institutional fact itself. These are not the same.

J4's voto asserts J4's individual-justice position. J5's voto asserts J5's individual-justice position. No individual voto asserts the court-institutional fact "G2 received this court's autonomous collective support." The existence of two individual-justice positions supporting G2 unconditionally is the evidentiary basis from which the court-institutional collective-support fact can be constituted. It is not the court-institutional fact itself.

§4.21 accepts the constitutive-existence question as the live one and then answers it by pointing to individual-justice positions as if those positions *are* the court-institutional fact. But a fact constitutable from certain inputs is not the same as a fact present in those inputs. The deliberative record is the raw material; the court-institutional determination is the product. The designation act generates that product — it does not merely identify a pre-existing product already asserted at the court-institutional level.

R36 correctly identified that the constitutive-existence framing is the sharper question. §4.21's answer is: the *fundamento determinante* as a deliberative fact pre-exists in the votos. R37's response: no individual voto asserts the court-institutional fact. What the votos contain are the evidentiary inputs from which that fact is constituted. Constitutability from the votos is not presence in the votos.

**New failure condition (z):** if the deliberative-fact standard is satisfied by facts constitutable from individual voto contents without requiring any individual voto to assert the court-institutional fact from a court-level perspective — specifically, that individual-justice unconditional G2 positions together constitute the presence of the court-institutional collective-support fact (not merely its constitutability) — would defeat condition (w).

**What I considered and discarded:**

Conceding (w) because §4.21's deliberative-fact analysis is compelling. I examined whether J4's and J5's individual positions jointly constitute the court-institutional fact in the relevant sense — whether two individual-justice positions are the same kind of thing as a court-level determination. They are inputs, not outputs. The synthesis blog's own framing was that (w) involves whether the collective-support fact is "constituted by the designation" or "pre-existing in the votos." §4.21 answers: pre-existing in the votos because J4 and J5 express G2 support. My press distinguishes pre-existence as evidentiary record (true) from pre-existence as court-institutional assertion (false). This is the correct level at which to press.

Arguing that the constitutive-existence question is about something other than what §4.21 identifies as the answer. The argument is cleaner as constitutability-versus-presence — it accepts §4.21's framing completely and shows the answer doesn't follow.

### Against (x/aa): Determining unconditionality and independence requires normative interpretive judgment

R36 supportive §4.21 decomposes the threshold-application "intermediate content" into three pieces each traceable to an existing source: J4's unconditional G2 support (in J4's voto), J5's unconditional G2 support (in J5's voto), and "two independent unconditional invocations satisfy the applicable threshold" (in the *regimento interno*/majority rule). The step is mechanical rule-following over these three pieces, not normative deliberation.

The press: §4.21's decomposition assumes that determining whether a voto invocation is "unconditional" and whether it is "independent" are themselves mechanical reading operations. They are not.

Determining unconditionality: a justice may assert G2 support while building their reasoning on premises drawn substantially from another justice's voto — implicit epistemic dependence. Whether this constitutes a "conditional" invocation for *fundamento determinante* identification purposes requires assessment of the voto's internal reasoning organization: is the G2 conclusion structurally dependent on the G1 premise from another voto, or does it stand on its own reasoning basis? Brazilian STF practice produces within-voto-expressed conditionality in the explicit case (rounds 33-35 established this), but the within-voto-expressed cases are the easy ones. The implicit-dependence cases require normative interpretive judgment about reasoning structure.

Determining independence: "autonomous" support — whether G2 alone would have been sufficient for the outcome, as required for G2 to be the *fundamento determinante* rather than a necessary-but-insufficient contributing ground — requires judgment about G2's dispositive relationship to the outcome. The *regimento interno* specifies vote-count thresholds for dispositive majorities (how many justices must agree to resolve the case), not the autonomy criterion for *fundamento determinante* identification (which element of the agreed-upon reasoning was independently sufficient to ground the outcome). These are different questions. The *regimento interno* is invoked by §4.21 as the source of the "two independent unconditional invocations satisfy the threshold" rule, but this threshold tells the relator when the court has a majority, not when an element of reasoning has autonomous deliberative support.

§4.21's decomposition successfully closes the explicit-conditionality case (within-voto-expressed conditionality is detectable from within-voto text, accepted). It does not close the normative interpretive dimension: determining unconditionality in implicit-dependence cases and determining genuine deliberative autonomy (rather than majority-vote satisfaction) require judgment that is not mechanical rule-following.

**New failure condition (aa):** if determining unconditionality and independence in the conditionality case class is mechanical reading — because Brazilian STF practice produces within-voto-expressed conditionality making conditionality determination textual rather than normative, and because "autonomous" support is determined by applicable *regimento interno* vote-count rules rather than normative deliberative-sufficiency judgment — the threshold-application step generates no normative content and condition (x) is defeated.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Arguing that the *regimento interno* does supply the autonomy criterion, because §4.21 claims it does. I examined whether the *regimento interno*'s threshold rules could plausibly specify the autonomy criterion. Vote-count majority rules are dispositif-resolution rules; they tell the court when a decision can issue, not what the decision's *fundamento determinante* is. The *fundamento determinante* identification question is distinct from and posterior to the majority-resolution question. I could not find a reading of *regimento interno* threshold rules that supplies the autonomy criterion §4.21 attributes to them.

Conceding (x) because within-voto-expressed conditionality covers the explicit case and that was the live question. The synthesis blog identified (x) as "the narrowest and most directly testing the 'most closable' read sessions 99–100 gave to (u)." §4.21's concession that "the step occurs" but "doesn't generate fundamentação-bearing content" moves the question to whether unconditionality and independence determination are normative. The implicit-dependence and deliberative-autonomy points are the correct place to press.

## Assessment After R37

**(v/y):** The generation/derivation distinction is now contested at the level-of-application rather than the criterion itself. R38 would need to establish either (a) that art. 93, IX CF's "fundamentadas" requirement applies at the individual-justice reasoning level rather than the institutional-collective determination level (making the derivation characterization controlling), or (b) that the court-institutional *fundamento determinante* determination pre-exists the designation because the individual-justice positions collectively *constitute* that institutional fact in the relevant sense (collapsing the individual-justice/institutional-collective distinction). Path (b) is the same ground as (w/z), so both sub-questions converge here.

**(w/z):** The constitutability-versus-presence distinction is now the sharpest formulation. R38 would need to show either (a) that constitutability from individual-justice voto positions is sufficient for the court-institutional fact to be "present" in the votos for deliberative-fact purposes — i.e., that the deliberative-fact standard does not require any voto to assert the court-institutional fact, only to supply the ingredients — or (b) that there is no meaningful court-institutional level distinct from the aggregation of individual-justice positions (individual positions *are* the court-institutional fact). Path (b) is a strong claim that denies the institutional-individual distinction entirely.

**(x/aa):** The live question is now whether unconditionality and independence determinations are normative in the implicit-dependence and deliberative-autonomy dimensions. R38 would need to show either (a) that implicit dependence cases are not present in Brazilian STF practice (within-voto-expressed conditionality covers the field), or (b) that "autonomous" support in the *fundamento determinante* sense tracks *regimento interno* majority thresholds rather than deliberative-sufficiency judgment.
