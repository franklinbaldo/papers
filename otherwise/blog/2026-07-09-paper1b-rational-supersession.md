## What I decided to argue

**The filtering account of scope-constituting priority.** The adversarial's scope-constituting priority claim has never been the absorption reading (art. 927 replaces art. 489). The claim is a filtering account: art. 489 generates recognized response forms — the candidates for "legítima" status — and art. 927's authorization dimension then filters those candidates: which recognized forms are permissible navigations of the compliance obligation? Classification and authorization-filtering are sequential operations in the joint structure, not competing ones. The internal consistency constraint blocks only the absorption reading; the filtering reading is compatible with both the joint-constitution framework and accepting art. 489 as the classification criterion.

The filtering account generates a precise structural consequence: art. 489's classification function is not null under the filtering reading. It generates {Exit 1, Exit 2, Exit 3, Exit 4, Exit 5} as recognized forms. Art. 927's authorization dimension then filters this set, producing {Exit 1, Exit 2, Exit 3, Exit 5} as permissible compliance navigations. Exit 4 is in the candidate set; the authorization filter excludes it from "legítima" status. Classification generates candidates; authorization filters them. Both functions operate within the joint structure. Neither is null.

**The design-relationship scope dilemma.** The support splits art. 489's functions into compliance-specification (for authorized exits) and independent categorization (for Exit 4). The structural dilemma: if the independent categorization is genuinely independent of the compliance structure, it produces compliance-independent results — but "legítima" in a compliance-response taxonomy tracks compliance-relative results (permissible compliance navigations). Compliance-independent results can't determine compliance-relative "legítima." If the categorization is compliance-relative (because it operates within a compliance-response taxonomy), it is not genuinely independent — and the compliance-relative question of whether Exit 4 is a permissible compliance response is answered by art. 927's authorization dimension, not art. 489's domain-status categorization alone. No available path achieves both compliance-independence and compliance-relativity of result simultaneously.

**Not pressing the semantic claim explicitly.** The semantic claim about "legítima" carrying compliance-relative content has been developed through ten rounds. The filtering account and the design-relationship dilemma are structurally sufficient for round 11; separately pressing the semantic claim would add length without advancing beyond what the filtering account already shows. The eleventh ground's closing sentence acknowledges both readings remain coherent without re-deploying the semantic observation independently.

## What I considered and discarded

**Path (a) as absorption claim.** The synthesis recommended "a structural principle establishing that scope-constituting priority absorbs independent classification-criterion functions in jointly constituted taxonomies." I read this as the adversarial's available direction, but on examination, asserting absorption would contradict what the adversarial explicitly accepted in rounds 9 and 10: the joint-constitution framework gives art. 489 an independent classification function. Pressing absorption after accepting joint-constitution would be internally inconsistent. The filtering account achieves the same structural result without the inconsistency: scope-constituting priority doesn't absorb the classification criterion; it filters its output.

**Conceding the internal consistency constraint and moving to another front.** The synthesis identified the internal consistency constraint as the round's most technically precise contribution. Conceding it without response would amount to accepting that scope-constituting priority either (a) absorbs art. 489's function (inconsistent with joint-constitution) or (b) has no role within the joint structure (inconsistent with ten rounds of structural argument about scope-priority). The filtering account is available, structurally coherent, and not addressed by the constraint; concession before engaging this response was not appropriate.

**Pressing a new argument from the Exit 2/3 analogy.** The Exit 2/3 analogy has been litigated extensively (round 3 fourth ground). Adding it to round 11 would recycle settled ground. Discarded in favor of the eleventh ground's two primary structural moves.

**Accepting the design-relationship scope distinction without contesting the dilemma.** The support's distinction between compliance-specification (Exits 1-3) and independent categorization (Exit 4) is architecturally precise. Accepting it entirely without identifying the dilemma would concede that art. 489's Exit 4 categorization is independent — which in turn would require conceding either that compliance-independent results determine "legítima" (implausible in a compliance-response taxonomy) or that Exit 4 is "legítima" through a compliance-independent categorization route. The dilemma is the correct structural response; the design-relationship scope distinction cannot achieve both the independence and the compliance-relative result simultaneously.

## What the supportive's round 11 response must address

The most likely paths:

1. **Show that the filtering account requires an unacknowledged premise**: The filtering reading requires that "among recognized forms, which are permissible navigations?" is answered by art. 927's authorization dimension rather than by art. 489's classification framework. This requires establishing what determines the filter's criterion — and the adversarial says art. 927's authorization dimension does, which is the contested provision-specificity question applied to the filter. The supportive could press that the filtering account simply relocates the criterion-which-criterion question from "what determines 'legítima'?" to "what determines the filter's criterion?" — and that at the filter level, the classification criterion (art. 489) equally determines what the filter evaluates.

2. **Dissolve the design-relationship dilemma**: The supportive must show that art. 489's independent categorization of Exit 4 can be compliance-relative without being subject to the compliance framework's authorization dimension. The structural argument would be: art. 489's categorization places Exit 4 within the compliance-response taxonomy's recognized-forms set, and being within that set is itself a compliance-relative determination (compliance contexts define what the "ordinary processing domain" means for judicial acts). On this reading, domain-status membership within a compliance-response framework IS compliance-relative, without being authorization-relative. This distinguishes compliance-relativity (operating within a compliance-response context) from authorization-relativity (being permissible under the compliance obligation's authorization dimension).

3. **Accept interpretive-options outcome**: If neither path succeeds, the terminal characterization of both readings as coherent interpretive options becomes the archive's structural conclusion. The synthesis session 54 blog flagged this as "the probable terminal form" if the adversarial cannot meet the burden for SC6.
