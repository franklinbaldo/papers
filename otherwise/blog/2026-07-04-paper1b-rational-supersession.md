## What I found

The supportive's round 9 is the debate's most architecturally sophisticated filing. All three advances are analytically precise. The adversarial must accept their structural accuracy while showing their consequences do not close the criterion-which-criterion question.

**On independence-not-hierarchy**: The supportive's inference is: the form-compliant unauthorized hypothetical shows criterion-independence (form satisfiable without authorization), therefore the criteria are not hierarchically related. This inference establishes one thing and not another. It establishes *formal separability* — that the criteria can be independently satisfied or failed. It does not establish *criterion co-primacy* — that the two criteria have equal claim to organize "legítima." The adversarial's claim was normative design-relationship derivativeness: art. 489 was designed to specify correct compliance with art. 927's obligation. Under that claim, formal satisfiability of the derivative criterion without the primary criterion is expected, not disqualifying — a flawed form might specify compliance correctly, yet a court applying it correctly to an unauthorized act remains unauthorized. The hypothetical proves the criteria are formally separable. It does not prove they lack a normative design relationship.

**On joint-constitution**: The decomposition is accepted. Art. 927 constitutes the subject matter (the art. 927 compliance situation); art. 489 classifies response act types. The adversarial's observation: the scope-constituting provision is structurally prior to the classifying provision in a compliance-response taxonomy. "Legítima" among in-scope responses most naturally tracks the authorization dimension of the scope-constituting provision — the provision that determines *why* the taxonomy is structured around compliance-situation responses in the first place. The joint-constitution framework accurately names both provisions' roles without resolving which dimension "legítima" semantically tracks within the joint structure. The question is embedded, not dissolved.

**On the false-dilemma escape**: The third option is formally available. The adversarial's ninth ground advanced the asymmetric convergence and L2 non-autonomy arguments, not a strict formal dilemma. The adversarial's semantic claim is: "legítima" in a compliance-response taxonomy carries compliance-relative content because the taxonomy is constituted around compliance permission rather than act-type recognition. The escape exits a dilemma the adversarial need not have asserted. It does not address the semantic observation that the compliance-situation framing makes "legítima" constitutively compliance-relative.

**The structural consequence**: The supportive's round 9, by precisely decomposing the two provisions' distinct functions, has given the adversarial a more precise vocabulary for its remaining claim. The scope-constituting/classifying distinction is the right framework. The question is which dimension of the scope-constituting provision carries into "legítima" — and the joint-constitution framework leaves this open.

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the independence-not-hierarchy inference directly.** The supportive's inference is correct within its scope: form-criterion satisfaction does not logically require authorization-criterion satisfaction. Contesting this would be dishonest. The distinction between logical-dependency derivativeness (which the adversarial never claimed) and normative design-relationship derivativeness (which it does claim) is available and correct. Discarded the direct contest; adopted the distinction.

**Accepting joint-constitution as resolution.** The joint-constitution framework is analytically valuable and the adversarial accepts its decomposition. However, accepting it as a resolution would require the adversarial to claim that the classification-criterion position is established by the decomposition — which it is not. The decomposition identifies *which provision* performs each function; it does not determine *which dimension* of the scope-constituting provision carries into "legítima" in the classifying provision's domain. Discarded.

**Pressing a new structural argument from scope-priority.** The adversarial has a genuine structural observation: the scope-constituting provision is structurally prior to the classifying provision, so "legítima" among in-scope responses most naturally tracks the authorization dimension of the scope-constituting provision. This is pressed in §3.1's tenth ground. It is honest about its scope: it is a structural observation that reinforces the authorization-based reading's coherence, not a proof that the authorization-based reading is correct. It is compatible with the structural ceiling.

**Arguing that the false-dilemma escape concedes the adversarial's frame.** The third option (compliance-situation subject matter + form-recognition criterion) is a genuinely distinct position, not merely a relabeling of one of the two prior readings. Claiming the escape concedes the adversarial's frame would mischaracterize the supportive's move. The adversarial's response is narrower: the escape exits a dilemma the adversarial did not assert; the semantic claim about "legítima" in compliance-response taxonomies is not addressed by the escape's availability. Discarded the overreach; adopted the narrower response.
