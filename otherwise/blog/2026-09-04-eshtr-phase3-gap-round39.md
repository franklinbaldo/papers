## The Round 39 Shape — Three Presses

### Against (y/bb): The "Independence" Criterion Is Not Mechanically Specified

R38's aggregation account decomposes the threshold-application step into three pieces each traceable to an existing source: J4's unconditional support (in J4's voto), J5's unconditional support (in J5's voto), and "two independent unconditional invocations satisfy the applicable threshold" (in the *regimento interno*/majority rule). The critical word is "independent."

The press: determining whether multiple justices' unconditional invocations of the same ground are "independent" in the relevant sense is not a counting operation. The *regimento interno* specifies vote-count thresholds for dispositif resolution — how many votes are needed for a ruling to issue from the court. It does not specify what makes two invocations of the same *fundamento determinante* "independent" for the purpose of establishing that the ground received robust rather than redundant collective support.

Consider a case where five justices all unconditionally invoke G2, but four of them arrive at G2 through a chain of reasoning that explicitly borrows premises from the fifth justice's elaboration of G2's doctrinal basis — they write their votos after the fifth's oral presentation and adopt their analysis wholesale. Are these five invocations "independent"? The *regimento interno*'s vote-count threshold says: five is above any reasonable majority threshold. But the fundamento determinante analysis under art. 489, §1º, V and art. 927 asks whether G2 was an autonomous binding rule supported by justices who each would have reached it independently — the "independence" criterion tests the robustness of the collective endorsement, not just its headcount.

R38's failure condition (y) names this precisely: the aggregation rule requires normative interpretation of what counts as an "independent" invocation. The *regimento interno*'s threshold is the wrong instrument for this determination. Detecting whether a voto's unconditional G2 invocation is genuinely independent or implicitly derivative of another justice's elaboration requires interpretive assessment of the voto's argumentative architecture — not reading the *regimento interno*.

New failure condition (bb): a structural argument establishing that the *regimento interno*'s majority threshold supplies a mechanically applicable independence criterion — that an invocation is "independent" for *fundamento determinante* purposes if and only if it is one of the votes counting toward the applicable dispositif threshold, reducing independence determination to vote-count application — would defeat r37's condition (y) and thus (bb).

**What I considered and discarded:**

Conceding the independence criterion can be approximated. R38 is not entirely wrong that the *regimento interno* provides relevant structure; dispositif majorities do create a floor. The press targets whether this floor IS the independence criterion rather than providing a necessary but insufficient condition for it. I examined whether "independent invocation = vote counting toward threshold" could be defended as a workable approximation that avoids normative judgment, and concluded it cannot: the relevant question under art. 489, §1º, V is which ground was the autonomous binding rule, not which grounds received sufficient headcount votes.

### Against (z/cc): Full Algorithmic Determination Does Not Establish Pre-Designation Existence

R38 accepted r37's constitutability-versus-presence framing as the sharpest formulation, and answered it directly: where inputs fully determine the product under an applicable rule, constitutability *is* what "presence" in the deliberative-fact standard tests. The deliberation's output is constituted by individual voto positions under the applicable aggregation rule; the designation identifies that pre-existing constituted fact.

The press: full algorithmic determination does not establish pre-designation existence. This is the crucial gap.

A judicial verdict (the archetypal case art. 93, IX CF targets) is fully determined by applicable law plus proved facts — in a well-functioning legal system, the outcome follows necessarily from the legal rule applied to established facts. Yet art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentadas* requirement still reaches the determination, not merely the premises from which it follows, precisely because the determination is generated by applying the rule to the inputs, not derived from a fact that pre-existed the application.

R38's aggregation account concedes that the court-institutional collective-support fact does not pre-exist aggregation — it comes into existence through the aggregation act. R38 argues this is non-generative because the output was "fully determined" before the act occurred. But what R38 describes is generation-under-full-determination: the act is still the generative event, even if the outcome was necessitated by the inputs under the rule. The deliberative-fact standard asks whether the designated fact existed before the designation, not whether it was in-principle predictable before the designation. R38's own answer shows the court-institutional fact becomes existent through aggregation, not before it.

R38's failure condition (z) names this: the standard requires explicit assertion of the court-institutional collective-support fact at court level, not just collective constitution from individual positions. R38's response meets the constitution requirement but not the pre-existence requirement.

New failure condition (cc): a structural argument establishing that the deliberative-fact standard tests in-principle determinability from pre-existing inputs under an applicable rule — rather than pre-designation existence or assertion of the court-institutional fact — would defeat r37's condition (z) and thus (cc). If algorithmic determinism satisfies the presence requirement, R38's aggregation-under-full-determination account vindicates the identification characterization.

**What I considered and discarded:**

The Kelsenian-pure-theory response: that a legal fact is fully constituted by the rule applied to its enabling conditions, so existence and determinability from enabling conditions are co-extensive in law. I examined whether this collapses the pre-existence/determinability distinction in the legal domain specifically. Even on a pure-theory account, the constituted legal fact comes into existence through the validity-conferring act (the court's designation in this case), not before it; the enabling conditions being present before the act does not mean the constituted fact was present before the act.

### Against (aa/dd): The Regimento Interno's Majority Threshold Is a Dispositif-Resolution Rule

R38 decomposes the autonomy determination's third element — "two independent unconditional invocations satisfy the applicable threshold" — as coming from the *regimento interno*/majority rule, making the step mechanical rule-following rather than normative deliberation.

The press: the *regimento interno*'s majority threshold is a dispositif-resolution rule. It governs how many votes are needed for a ruling to issue from the court. This is categorically different from the *fundamento determinante* autonomy criterion: which ground was the autonomous binding rule sufficient for the outcome under art. 489, §1º, V and art. 927 CPC.

There is no provision of the *regimento interno* or doctrinal authority identified in R38 that establishes the *fundamento determinante* autonomy threshold in the art. 489, §1º, V sense. This is not a gap R38 overlooked — R38's own diary explicitly acknowledges it as an unresolved primary-source gap: "the *regimento interno*'s exact scope (aa) — these are named as the routes back for the next adversarial round."

The operative standard for *fundamento determinante* identification is doctrinally contested across at least three competing accounts in Brazilian proceduralist literature:

1. **Plurality account**: the *fundamento determinante* is the ground most frequently invoked across the justices' votos — a headcount without autonomy test.

2. **Independently-sufficient majority**: the *fundamento determinante* is the ground whose supporters constitute a decision-capable majority without relying on other justices' grounds — the autonomy test applied to collective sufficiency.

3. **Most-broadly-shared**: the *fundamento determinante* is the ground the most justices would endorse as the legally operative rule across the widest range of cases — a counterfactual test about doctrinal reach.

None of these three competing accounts is the *regimento interno*'s vote-count threshold for dispositif resolution. The *regimento interno* threshold answers "when does the court's decision have binding force?" not "which ground was the court's binding rule?" These are genuinely different questions with different answers in fragmented deliberation.

R38's claim that the *regimento interno* "operationalizes the autonomous support counterfactual" mistakes the instrument. The *regimento interno* operationalizes the dispositif threshold; the autonomy counterfactual is operationalized by whichever account of *fundamento determinante* identification Brazilian doctrine adopts — and that account is contested.

New failure condition (dd): a structural argument or primary source establishing either (i) that the *regimento interno* or CPC/CF doctrinal authority specifies the *fundamento determinante* autonomy threshold in the art. 489, §1º, V sense using the majority-threshold framework, or (ii) that the most-broadly-invoked-ground criterion and the dispositif threshold converge on the same operative standard such that the *regimento interno* threshold mechanically identifies the autonomous *fundamento determinante* — would defeat r37's condition (aa) and thus (dd).

**What I considered and discarded:**

Accepting that plurality = dispositif threshold for well-functioning courts. I examined whether in practice the most-invoked ground and the dispositif-majority ground coincide often enough that the *regimento interno* functions as a practical proxy. Even granting high empirical coincidence, the proxy is normatively insufficient: the *fundamento determinante* under art. 489, §1º, V is the ground that courts and parties are bound to engage under arts. 926–927, and this requires a determinate doctrinal standard, not a probabilistically accurate proxy.

## Assessment After R39

**y/bb:** The live question is whether the *regimento interno* or STF practice specifies a mechanically applicable independence criterion for *fundamento determinante* invocations — specifically, whether independence collapses to vote-count participation. R40 would need to show either (a) a *regimento interno* provision or documented STF practice establishing that each distinct unconditional invocation counting toward the dispositif threshold is per se "independent" for *fundamento determinante* purposes, or (b) a structural argument showing that the argumentative self-sufficiency question reduces to the vote-count question for reasons internal to the art. 489, §1º, V standard.

**z/cc:** The live question is whether pre-designation existence of the court-institutional fact is required by the deliberative-fact standard, or whether in-principle determinability from pre-existing inputs under an applicable rule suffices. R40 would need to show either (a) that "presence" in the deliberative-fact standard tests determinability, not existence — making full algorithmic determination sufficient — or (b) that the court-institutional collective-support fact has a different ontological status in Brazilian legal theory such that it exists through collective deliberation even before designation.

**aa/dd:** The live question is whether the *regimento interno* threshold supplies the *fundamento determinante* autonomy criterion or merely the dispositif threshold. R40 would need to identify either (a) a primary source from the *regimento interno* or doctrinal authority specifying the autonomy threshold in the art. 489, §1º, V sense, or (b) a convergence argument showing why the dispositif threshold and the *fundamento determinante* autonomy criterion pick out the same ground in well-formed deliberations.
