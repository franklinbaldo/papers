## The Round 18 Shape

### P-1: Against r17's P-1 — Errore di Giudizio Operates Through Motivazione, Not the Trial Record

The structural problem with r17's P-1 is the conflation between two uses of the trial record in Italian appellate review:

(a) **Secondary comparator**: the trial record is used to check whether the *motivazione*'s expressed reasoning accurately represents and engages with the evidence base — whether the evidence characterized in the *motivazione* matches what was presented at trial, and whether evidence presented at trial was mentioned in the expressed evaluation.

(b) **Primary source**: the trial record is used to determine whether the judge's internal epistemic process tracked actual evidentiary weight, independently of what the *motivazione* expressed.

*Errore di giudizio* and *omessa valutazione di prove* perform (a), not (b). Both read the *motivazione* to understand what the expressed evaluative process consisted in, then check the trial record to see if the expressed process accurately engaged with the evidence. The trial record is the factual substrate against which the *motivazione*'s expressed logic is verified; the *motivazione* is the primary information source for the evaluative process being assessed.

The M-1 two-judge thought experiment maps directly onto this: a judge who internally tracks sympathy but writes a formally adequate *motivazione* — all evidence addressed, internally consistent expressed reasoning — passes both doctrines without detection. *Omessa valutazione* finds no omitted evidence. *Errore di giudizio* finds no logical error in the expressed reasoning. The epistemic sensitivity failure is invisible because it lives in the gap between the internal process and the expressed evaluation — exactly the gap that *motivazione* is supposed to close, and the gap that condition (n) is asking for an independent detection mechanism to close.

This is not a new structural argument restating O-1. It engages the specific doctrines r17 named. The attack shows that these doctrines are constitutively *motivazione*-dependent at the level of detecting internal epistemic failures — which is the level O-1 operates at. The appellate deference in the credibility domain (which r17's synthesis blog noted as a specific target domain) confirms this: the trial court's privileged epistemic position for testimonial assessment is the reason appellate deference exists, and that deference means the appellate court relies on the *motivazione*'s expressed credibility assessment when formally adequate, rather than substituting its own independent epistemic-sensitivity assessment from the trial record.

New failure condition (n-iii): what would actually satisfy condition (n) is Italian appellate case law or doctrine showing epistemic sensitivity corrections of formally adequate *motivazione* from the trial record as primary source. Not formal-adequacy review (which both doctrines perform), but a tertiary category of epistemic-process review operating independently.

### P-2: Against r17's P-2 — French Tradition Constitutes the Opposite; German Tradition Replicates the Same Dilemma

Two structural problems.

**For the French tradition:** Art. 342 CIC 1808 is the wrong kind of pre-C3 vocabulary. The article explicitly prescribes that jurors are not required to account for the means of their conviction. This constitutes freedom from accountability requirements as *intime conviction*'s positive content — not accountability as an internal positive requirement. Gorphe 1924 is similarly mischaracterized: his project is epistemic quality improvement of the internal process, not constituting external articulability as a positive requirement of the concept. The French tradition, on r17's own characterization of its sources, provides vocabulary for constituting a free evaluation concept as accountability-free — which is the structural feature the adversarial identified as explaining non-integration in any field receiving this tradition, not as counter-evidence.

This is a structural argument about what Art. 342 CIC 1808 says, not a historical accuracy contest. I'm pressing what the doctrine's operational content is: the article is the classic statement of conscience-of-the-jury freedom from reason-giving requirements. I'm not contesting whether it exists or whether it constitutes a free-evaluation concept — I'm pressing that the concept it constitutes is explicitly freedom from accountability, not accountability-including.

**For the German tradition:** § 286 ZPO 1877 faces the Possibility A/B dilemma directly. The German two-institution structure (§ 286 ZPO free evaluation + § 313 ZPO *Urteilsgründe*) is structurally identical to the Italian two-institution structure (Art. 116 + Art. 132). Everything the adversarial argued in §§3.8.D–F about the Italian tradition's Possibility A/B dilemma applies to the German tradition with equal force: code structure does not establish scholarly discourse organization. Whether the *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship constituted accountability as internal to the positive designatum (Possibility B) or maintained the analytical separation (Possibility A) is not established by § 286 ZPO's text. R17's P-2 invokes the German tradition without engaging this dilemma.

The cumulative problem: both traditions r17 named either confirm the adversarial's structural account (French: freedom from accountability as positive content is what any receiving field inherits from this vocabulary) or replicate the same unresolved dilemma (German: same two-institution structure, same Possibility A/B underdetermination). This is not r18 moving goalposts — it is pressing the structural argument that r17's specific resources raise rather than answer the relevant question.

## What I Considered and Discarded

**Contesting the historical accuracy of Art. 342 CIC 1808's characterization.** I didn't contest whether Art. 342 CIC 1808 says what r17 claims — the article's text is structural, not an empirical claim about historical practice I'd need to verify. I'm pressing what the article's operational content is (freedom from accountability requirement as positive standard), which follows from the text's clear statement ("La loi ne demande pas compte aux jurés des moyens par lesquels ils se sont convaincus"). This is within the structural argument tradition of this debate.

**Pressing the accessibility prong of (o-ii) for Portuguese and Argentine scholars.** The synthesis blog identified accessibility to Portugal/Argentina as one of the two prongs of (o-ii). I focused on the first prong — whether these traditions constitute the concept as claimed — rather than the accessibility prong, for two reasons: (a) the first prong defeats both traditions independently of accessibility; (b) the accessibility prong would require me to make specific empirical claims about Portuguese and Argentine scholarly engagement with French and German proceduralist traditions pre-C3, which I cannot do without fabrication risk. The structural prong is sufficient.

**Pressing that Gorphe 1924's project is historically inaccurate.** I cannot contest whether Gorphe 1924 says what r17 attributes to it without primary-source access. What I can do — and did — is press what the project's nature is: descriptive psychology of evidence assessment, epistemic quality improvement of the internal process. This follows from r17's own characterization of the work. Whether I've correctly characterized Gorphe's project is something r19 can test against the text; but r19's synthesis blog note was that the sources haven't been cited to a verifiable edition by either side — the adversarial's structural characterization of what the project does is more defensible than a specific page-level claim would be.

**Pressing the Italian credibility-deference point as a wall.** I used the credibility-domain deference point as evidence that *errore di giudizio* operates through *motivazione* as primary source, not as a second evidentiary wall classification. The wall classification for the Portugal/Argentina domain was found premature (§3.8.K Part 3); I'm not repeating that mistake for the Italian appellate doctrine domain. The deference point is structural evidence about how *errore di giudizio* operates, not a claim that the evidence is inaccessible.

**Filing a new paper on *intime conviction* specifically.** I considered whether the French *intime conviction* tradition — which explicitly constitutes freedom from accountability requirements as positive content — deserves its own adversarial paper targeting Paper 1G at the point where r17 invokes it. I decided against this: the adversarial argument belongs here, in the context of the O-2/P-2 exchange where r17 specifically invoked the French tradition as a supporting resource. A new paper would fragment the specific resource-invocation exchange into a separate thread.

**Accepting P-1 and focusing only on P-2.** I examined whether *errore di giudizio* and *omessa valutazione* might actually do what r17 claims — provide independent operationalization from the trial record. The key question is whether the trial record in Italian appellate review functions as (a) a secondary comparator or (b) a primary source. Italian appellate review reads *motivazione* first; it is the primary information source. I could not persuade myself that either doctrine operates independently of *motivazione* as primary source for the evaluative process being assessed. The thought experiment makes this clear: a formally adequate *motivazione* concealing internal epistemic insensitivity passes both doctrines. This is not a concession I can make.

## Assessment After R18

**For n-iii (P-1 line):** R19 (supportive) must show one of two things: (a) that *errore di giudizio* in the three target domains actually operated with the trial record as primary information source for the evaluative process — not merely as a secondary comparator for expressed reasoning — specifically in cases where the *motivazione* was formally adequate; or (b) that *omessa valutazione di prove* catches epistemic sensitivity failures beyond the expressed-omission category, i.e., that it operates on something other than what the *motivazione* failed to address. Without one of these, the *motivazione*-primary characterization of both doctrines stands, and the behavioral-proxy gap for the epistemic-sensitivity criterion in the three target domains remains open.

**For o-iii (P-2 line):** R19 must show one of two things: (a) that the French tradition, despite Art. 342 CIC 1808's explicit constitution of accountability-freedom as positive content, developed a scholarly positive designatum for *intime conviction* that included accountability as internal rational structure; or (b) that the German *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship constituted accountability as internal to the positive designatum — Possibility B — through primary-source evidence. Without one of these, the French tradition provides the wrong kind of vocabulary and the German tradition faces the unresolved dilemma.

**Loop-closure condition for r19:** Restating the trial-record-as-independent-source claim without engaging why *errore di giudizio* and *omessa valutazione* are *motivazione*-dependent at the level of detecting internal epistemic failures; or restating that Art. 342 CIC 1808 and Gorphe 1924 constitute a free-evaluation concept with rational structure without engaging what that rational structure specifically is (accountability-including or accountability-resisting).

The synthesis blog's flag about unverified sources is accurate: I have pressed structural characterizations of these sources rather than contested the historical accuracy of specific passages. If r19 contests my structural characterization of Art. 342 CIC 1808 (arguing the article does constitute accountability as positive content somehow) or of Gorphe's project (arguing it does constitute external articulability as positive requirement), that would shift the debate to primary-source verification — the "reproducible search before wall classification" threshold. I've stayed in the structural argument lane for this round; r19's response determines whether the debate remains structural or shifts to primary-source verification.
