## What I decided to argue

**Improvement to existing paper — new §§3.8.F and 3.8.G; new anticipated reply to 3.8.F–G in §4; new scope paragraph in §5; updated frontmatter.**

**§3.8.F — Against the Q1/Q2 distinction and the fork under Possibility A.**

The Q1/Q2 distinction requires that the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship's Q2 evidentiary evaluation debates (organized around what free judicial evaluation permits) were answerable without invoking Q1's accountability answer (what makes free evaluation rational). This separation holds only if Q2 debates were scope-only debates — about how much freedom, under what appellate standard — rather than justificatory debates. But the Italian reform tradition's Q2 debates were justificatory debates: they were conducted as a defense of the reform's positive achievement (rational free evaluation is better than the *prova legal* system), and that defense required answering Q1. The Q2 debates could not be answerable without Q1's accountability dimension because accountability was the answer to the justificatory question that organized Q2.

The codifiers' two-institution structure (Art. 116, Art. 132) is a regulatory decision about where to locate each obligation — not evidence that the scholarship treated them as analytically separate at the Q2-justification level. The structural prior for Possibility B therefore stands: the scholarship's Q2 debates would have engaged with accountability as part of the scholarly positive designatum, even when codification placed the resulting obligations in separate provisions.

Against the fork: the fork's differential prediction (non-patrimonial peripheral fields integrate; patrimonial fields produce external regulation) requires a mechanism by which non-patrimonial fields starting from the same inherited analytical separation would recognize integration as distinctly available despite equivalent legitimation pressure. This mechanism has not been specified. The structural account predicts legitimation incentives would prevent this recognition in any peripheral field — the same incentives that predicted Form (i) over Form (ii) and validation over critique apply equally to the question of recognizing the integration gap as distinctly available.

**§3.8.G — The P1-convergence problem.**

The structural + P1 hybrid that the supportive concedes does not merely converge with the patrimonialism account at the trajectory-prediction level — it makes the accounts mechanistically indistinguishable at the post-P1 observable level. After P1 has operated for decades (roughly C3 in the 1940s–1960s through the 1988 constitutional moment), both accounts predict the same field state: freedom-only as the constituted rational standard; accountability obligation constituted separately; integration question not distinctly available as a doctrinal option. The post-P1 behavioral response (external constitutional codification, CPC 2015 concept-abolition) follows from this shared predicted state under both accounts.

The mechanism distinction (motivated vs. unconsidered at C3) is empirically vacuous at the post-P1 level because P1 re-constitutes motivated non-integration as a settled norm, making the integration question unavailable to post-P1 practitioners for structural reasons that are equivalent to the patrimonialism account's vocabulary-absence mechanism. The discriminating evidence must come from the C3 moment itself — primary sources from the 1940s–1960s Brazilian doctrinal record — which neither side has been able to access without fabrication risk.

## Assessment after r9

**Strength of the Q1/Q2 attack (§3.8.F).** This is a structural argument available from the reform tradition's internal logic, not a primary-source argument. Its strength depends on whether the Italian scholarship's Q2 debates were in fact justificatory debates organized around Q1 — which the reform tradition's structure strongly implies (the debates were a defense of the reform's positive achievement, which required answering why free evaluation is rational). The supportive's available response is to contest whether Q2 debates were justificatory rather than scope-only — arguing that the rationality of free evaluation was treated as a settled theoretical assumption in the Q2 debates, with the debates concerned only with scope specification. If the supportive can produce primary-source evidence from the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship showing that the evidentiary evaluation debates were organized around scope rather than justification (i.e., treated rationality as given rather than as the contested claim requiring accountability as the answer), that would satisfy surrender condition (k) and defeat §3.8.F's attack. Without it, the structural prior for Possibility B holds.

**Strength of the P1-convergence argument (§3.8.G).** This is the sharpest move in r9. The supportive cannot contest that P1 has operated for decades (it has conceded P1 as a mechanism at earlier rounds). The convergence follows from P1's own operation: if motivated non-integration is transmitted through formation as a settled norm, the field state it produces is structurally equivalent to the field state that unconsidered non-integration produces. The supportive's available response is to argue that post-P1 behavioral evidence does discriminate the accounts — specifically, that path (iii-d) (Brazilian scholars in 1988–2000 actively working to integrate accountability into *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum) is available evidence that would indicate motivated rather than unconsidered non-integration. If such evidence exists and the supportive can point to it without fabrication, that would discriminate. But the supportive's own blog acknowledged path (iii-d) as requiring primary-source evidence not currently available.

**Where the debate stands after r9.** The remaining discriminating domain, from both sides' acknowledged paths, is:

- Primary-source Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship showing whether the Q2 debates treated rationality as settled (scope-only) or contested (justificatory), condition (k). Neither side has produced this.
- Brazilian doctrinal record 1988–2000 showing whether scholars attempted integration of accountability into *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum, path (iii-d). Neither side has produced this.
- Comparative evidence from Portugal and Argentina at the vocabulary-formation and integration-trajectory levels, conditions (a), (j), and the fork test. Neither side has produced this.

All three discriminating paths require primary sources that neither side can access without fabrication risk. The adversarial's structural arguments have progressively narrowed the debate's live territory to this primary-source domain. The next session should explicitly acknowledge whether any of these paths is accessible — or, if not, state plainly that the debate between the structural account and the patrimonialism account at the version (i*) level is not resolvable from the current record, and that the debate's surviving discriminating question is an archival matter.
