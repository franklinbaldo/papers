## What I decided to argue

**Three attacks on the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence (§3.8):**

**3.8.A — Italian CPC 1940's separate codification.** The Italian CPC of 1940 (effective 28 October 1940 — concurrent with Liebman's USP engagement from 1940–1946) separately codified the freedom and accountability dimensions:
- Art. 116 (*libero convincimento del giudice*): the judge evaluates evidence through free personal assessment, not formal hierarchical rules
- Art. 132 (*contenuto della sentenza*, n. 4): judgments must contain the "concisa esposizione dei motivi in fatto e in diritto"

These are distinct provisions governing distinct obligations. The Italian reform tradition's positive output separately codified what the supportive claims the Brazilian field's vocabulary had not constituted as distinct. A scholar whose scholarly identity was the tradition that produced this code — Liebman, transmitting the reform at USP during exactly the period the code entered force — was transmitting a tradition that separately articulated accountability through *motivazione*. The probability that Liebman excluded Art. 132 from his intellectual program at USP is very low. The burden falls on the supportive to provide primary-source evidence from Liebman's Brazilian-period writings establishing that exclusion; the current record does not include it.

**3.8.B — Vocabulary absence vs. vocabulary non-adoption.** The field-level vocabulary absence has two structurally distinct readings:
- Version (i): the Italian tradition had not articulated accountability at the Liebman period — concept unavailable
- Version (ii): the Italian tradition had articulated it, but the Brazilian field did not constitute it as a distinct component — concept available, not adopted

Version (i) is the claim the mechanism needs to explain (rather than redescribe) the explanandum. Version (i) is improbable given §3.8.A.

Version (ii) shifts from absence to non-adoption. Under version (ii), the vocabulary absence label does no explanatory work — it redescribes the observation (the field's vocabulary didn't constitute accountability as distinct) as an explanans without supplying a mechanism not already available to the adversarial's structural account. The legitimation incentives account explains selective non-adoption: constituting accountability as a distinct positive requirement of the vocabulary's rational pole would require acknowledging that Brazilian informal practice doesn't occupy the full rational state, undermining the legitimation project. The patrimonialism account and the structural account compete at the mechanism level under version (ii). Without comparative evidence (Portugal/Argentina vocabulary-selection level), the patrimonialism contribution isn't independently established.

**3.8.C — CF art. 93, IX CF as evidence.** The 1988 Federal Constitution's art. 93, IX CF constitutionalizes the accountability dimension as a distinct requirement for Brazilian judicial decisions. This is the provision at the center of the ESHTR C2 debate — which makes its history directly relevant. The constitutional adoption is more parsimonious under the adversarial's reception-selectivity account (accountability concept present in the Italian tradition as transmitted; not constituted in *livre convencimento*'s positive pole at C3 for legitimation reasons; eventually constituted at the constitutional level in the 1988 moment for accountability-to-history reasons) than under a genuine vocabulary absence account (concept unavailable at C3, requiring an external or de novo conceptual source for the 1988 adoption). The framers of the 1988 constitutional provisions on judicial motivation were substantially formed in the proceduralist tradition Liebman influenced — making the reception-selectivity account more parsimonious.

## What I considered and discarded

**Arguing only from 3.8.A without the version (i)/(ii) distinction.** The Italian CPC 1940 argument is the synthesis-specified path and stands as a strong structural challenge to version (i). But without the version (i)/(ii) distinction, the supportive can simply say: "Of course Liebman's tradition included motivation requirements — that's why it's the Italian tradition's articulation state that matters, and the Brazilian field's *prior formation* is what prevented it from being constituted." The version (i)/(ii) distinction is what closes this escape: if the concept was transmitted (version ii), the mechanism is selective non-adoption, not absence, and the adversarial's structural account is already available. Both arguments are needed.

**Conceding condition (ii) and arguing only that the structural account explains the selective non-adoption.** The version (ii) argument alone is strong but allows the supportive to claim: "I always meant the vocabulary absence at the constituted-concept level, not at the available-vocabulary level." This retreat preserves the form of the mechanism while evacuating its content (it becomes a redescription). By pressing 3.8.A first, the adversarial shows condition (i) is improbable — which forces the supportive either into primary-source evidence for Liebman's exclusion of Art. 132 (high burden) or into version (ii) (which collapses to a structural account competition). Both options are less favorable to the supportive than the current vocabulary-absence framing.

**Fabricating Liebman primary sources.** Considered and rejected categorically. The argument from the Italian CPC 1940 is a structural probability argument, not a primary-source citation argument. I do not claim to have read Liebman's USP course notes. I claim that the tradition he embodied had separately articulated the two dimensions in codified form contemporaneously with his engagement, making it structurally improbable that his transmission excluded Art. 132. The burden of establishing that exclusion is on the supportive.

**Pressing 3.7.A–D again without adding new content.** The synthesis correctly identified that the adversarial cannot just repeat the r6 attacks, which the supportive accepted at the individual imperceptibility level. The new mechanism is at a different analytical level (field vocabulary, not individual access) and requires attacks at that level. §3.8 is targeted at field-level vocabulary absence; §3.7 attacks the individual imperceptibility mechanism. The two sections are complementary, not redundant.

## Assessment

**Strength of the attack.** §3.8.A is structurally clean and grounded in verifiable facts: the Italian CPC 1940 is a documented historical artifact; its Arts. 116 and 132 are known provisions; Liebman's USP engagement 1940–1946 is documented. The argument from these facts to the improbability of version (i) is a structural probability argument, not a primary-source fabrication. It establishes a high prior that the burden must overcome.

§3.8.B is the most analytically precise attack: it shows that the vocabulary absence label, unless it means version (i) (which is improbable), collapses into a vocabulary non-adoption claim that doesn't distinguish the patrimonialism account from the structural account. This is the clearest demonstration that the field-level vocabulary absence mechanism, as the r7 debate now stands, is not independently establishing the patrimonialism contribution over the structural alternative.

§3.8.C is supporting evidence rather than a standalone attack. It shows that the eventual constitutional adoption (art. 93, IX CF) is more consistent with the reception-selectivity account than with the vocabulary absence account. It is not decisive alone but reinforces the reading that the concept was available and selectively not adopted.

**What remains open.** The supportive r7 response has two available paths:

First: establish primary-source evidence from Liebman's Brazilian-period work (lectures, publications, academic exchanges 1940–1946) showing that he specifically presented *libero convincimento* as freedom from formal proof hierarchies without separately elaborating the accountability dimension through the *motivazione* counterpart. If such evidence exists, version (i) survives — the transmitted vocabulary genuinely lacked the accountability dimension as a distinct articulated concept. This is an empirical path with bounded source material.

Second: contest the version (i)/(ii) distinction by arguing that the field-level vocabulary absence is specifically about what the Brazilian field's prior formation had constituted as the *positive content* of rational judicial evaluation within the *livre convencimento* concept — that Art. 132 *motivazione* was available as a general procedural form requirement but was not part of the vocabulary's positive designatum of free rational evaluation, and that the patrimonial prior formation explains why the field distinguished the concept's positive content (freedom) from the procedural form requirement (motivation). This would require arguing that the positive-content/form-requirement distinction is not the adversarial's version (i)/(ii) distinction — that the vocabulary absence operates within the concept's positive designatum specifically, not at the level of all available Italian proceduralist concepts. The adversarial r7 reply anticipates this response in §4 (Reply to 3.8) and shows why it either fails to rescue version (i) or falls back to version (ii) under a different label.

The debate after seven rounds has the following structure: five settled adversarial concessions (direction, orientation, content-selection, Mode III form-selection, individual cognitive imperceptibility); one active adversarial challenge on the field-level vocabulary absence, now contesting condition (ii). The edit cycle is one session away; whether the supportive r7 arrives in time to produce a bilateral exchange before session 84 depends on the routines' cadence.
