## The two attacks I developed

### Attack A — virtual-plenary revision gap (pressing component (4))

R46's component (4) — "in the virtual plenary, the session text IS the lodged voto text" — closes the priority-rule gap for the virtual-plenary arm by collapsing the distinction between "session" and "published" versions: if the session text and the lodged text are identical, no gap can arise between them.

R46 explicitly concedes it is not established whether the lodged voto can be revised before its release enters the acórdão. This concession is the attack's foundation.

**The "não revistos" mechanism shows revision is expected.** ER 54/2020's art. 95, §§2º–3º creates a specific procedure for the event that a justice does not release a voto within sixty days: the gabinete may file a notation "de que não foram revistos," and in that case the ementa is drawn from the winning voto's dispositivo. This mechanism has two structural implications. First, "não revistos" means "not reviewed/revised" — the modifier presupposes that revision between lodging and release is the ordinary expectation; votos that are released without revision have been reviewed; votos flagged as "não revistos" have not. If revision were impermissible after lodging, the "não revistos" flag would apply to every lodged voto and the procedure would have no discriminating function. Second, the limiting case (ementa drawn from the winning voto's dispositivo when the voto is unreleased) confirms that the ementa's content depends on the released written voto, not the lodged text — which is exactly what component (4) would need to deny.

**New art. 96 does not rank the lodged text above the released text.** Post-ER 26, art. 96, §2º requires gabinetes to release the written voto within twenty days; §3º requires the Secretaria to transcribe the audio of votos not so released, with the notation that they were not revised; §4º routes the file to the relator for the acórdão and ementa after §§2º–3º are completed. The regime makes the released written voto the ordinary text of the acórdão. No provision in any text r46 read ranks the lodged text above the released written text; the released written text supersedes the lodged text as the ordinary acórdão input.

**The gap.** Where a justice revises a lodged voto before releasing it under art. 96, §2º — and the "não revistos" mechanism shows this is the expected course — the released text may qualify G with C in a way absent from the lodged text. For the purposes of requirement (1), the "session text" equivalent in the virtual plenary is the lodged text (what was registered before the session closed); the "published text" equivalent is the released written voto. If the released text adds C to G, the same conditionality gap r45 identified at the physical-session level reopens at the virtual-plenary level. No provision fills it; component (4) holds only for the subclass of decisions where lodged and released texts are identical.

**I considered and discarded:** pressing the argument that the virtual plenary has no "session" in the relevant sense (votes are registered asynchronously, not simultaneously proclaimed). This would undercut component (1)'s proclamation foundation, which is the accepted part of r46's replacement. The better attack accepts component (1) as settled and presses component (4) separately, for the conditionality-gap point, without requiring the between-voto transfer question to be reopened.

### Attack B — measurement gap (the residual class and the virtual rate)

R46 names the physical-session residual class as unmeasured and observable only against session audio. The synthesis blog at session 109 named this "its size is a pilot measurement." Sessions 110 and 111 have passed without that measurement being performed. A composition asserted as a property of the calibration corpus cannot rest on an unmeasured residual class at the arm that would most frequently produce high-adversarial-record decisions — physical-session plenary.

This is not a structural argument but a calibration argument: until the pilot is run, the composition has an acknowledged hole in the arm most likely to generate the cases C2 is designed to handle.

**The virtual rate is also unknown.** R46 reports the docket-wide virtual share (95.5% in 2020, 98.4% in H1 2021) but explicitly concedes the arm-specific share is unknown — the *destaque* mechanism selects contested cases into physical sessions, so the calibration arm's virtual share may be substantially lower than the docket-wide figure. Both unknowns compound: the arm most likely to generate high-adversarial-record cases is least likely to be fully covered by the virtual-plenary defense.

**I considered:** arguing that the residual class is likely large, based on the structural incentive for relators to elaborate their reasoning after seeing the session's outcome. But this is conjecture; R46 is right that it requires empirical measurement. The correct press is to hold the measurement gap open — not to assert the class is large before it is measured.

### Against (hh) — P4-only votos

R46 concedes the within-voto individuation problem for P4-only votos and names them a flagged class requiring pilot measurement. The adversarial's best move is to accept the resolution for the paradigm case and press the measurement gap for the flagged class — the same calibration structure as attack B.

**I considered:** arguing that P4-only votos are structurally incentivized in high-adversarial-record STF cases, because a justice who is uncertain whether a precedent's norm applies but is certain that the transfer condition holds in the facts will state the fact-intensive conclusion without restating the general norm. This is plausible but structural inference without empirical support. R46 correctly says the pilot resolves it; the adversarial should hold the gap rather than claim a rate.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing the lack of any STF holding that has applied (or tested) the post-2008 regime against a divergence.** R46 names this as a remaining uncertainty. But absence of a holding does not establish that the regime fails to work as stated — the new art. 96 is unambiguous about sequencing; whether it has been tested is an empirical gap, not a structural gap. The "não revistos" mechanism is the structural argument; the absence of a holding is supporting color but not the load-bearing press.

**Pressing the STJ's RISTJ art. 103, §1º's text being taken from secondary quotations.** R46 acknowledges this limitation honestly. But the attack is weaker than the structural gaps in the virtual-plenary arm; the STJ's share of the calibration corpus (TJRO decisions citing STF and STJ precedents in unknown proportion) means the STJ rules matter but are not the primary battleground.

**Accepting r46's (gg-1) rewrite (that the failure condition from r44 was correct).** This was already accepted in r45 and does not need to be re-accepted here. The condition (gg-1) front is closed.

**Pressing the adherence-transfer step.** R46's component (2) — that the majority's grounds are fixed by session adherence — was accepted in r45 and remains accepted. The transfer step is not the target; the relator's ground-statement (physical session) and the lodged-voto revision gap (virtual plenary) are.

## Assessment after round 47

**Against (gg):** Component (4)'s virtual-plenary defense is structurally undermined by the "não revistos" mechanism: the procedure for when votos are not revised before release presupposes revision is the expected norm; where revision happens, the released text is the acórdão text and no provision ranks the lodged text above it; the conditionality gap reopens. The measurement gaps (physical residual class; virtual revision rate; arm-specific virtual share) prevent the composition from being asserted as a general corpus property. The STJ arm is answered by r46 for the determinate-divergence class.

**Against (hh):** R46 answers the paradigm case. The P4-only class is conceded as a measurement obligation. R47 holds the measurement gap open; r46 is entitled to say the class is small once it measures it.

**Loop-closure check:** R45 pressed the expansion/qualification gap in the old art. 96, §2º; r46 showed the rule is gone and supplied four replacement components; r47 presses the gap that the "não revistos" mechanism reveals in component (4). The argument moves at each round — from the scope of the old rule, to its abolition and replacement, to the structural implications of the replacement's own limiting-case procedure. Not a loop.

**What the adversarial should do next (if r48 is needed):** r46 names four open uncertainties that are primary-source gaps, not structural gaps. If r48 comes, the productive route is either: finding a primary-source answer to the lodged-voto revision question (the consolidated RISTF's art. 21-B, or ER 53/2020, or the STF's virtual-plenary procedural documentation), or pressing the arm-specific virtual share against the corpus description in ESHTR §5.1 (TJRO; cited precedents are STF and STJ in unknown proportion). The structural argument at (gg) is now as developed as it can be without primary-source resolution.
