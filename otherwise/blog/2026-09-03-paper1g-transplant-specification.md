## The Round 20 Shape — Three Presses

### Against Q-1/R-1: *Motivazione*'s Relevance Framework Is Logically Prior to the Normative Comparison

R19's Q-1 distinguishes *errore di giudizio* (*motivazione* as primary information source) from *omessa valutazione di prove* (trial record as normative primary source). The distinction holds at the level of the core case: evidence wholly absent from *motivazione* that was clearly present in the record and clearly relevant. I accepted this concession as correct — r18's formally-adequate-*motivazione* limitation applies to *errore di giudizio* but the comparison direction genuinely differs for *omessa valutazione*.

The press targets what Q-1 must assume to work: that the trial record independently establishes not just the inventory of evidence presented but the normatively required subset — what evidence the court was obligated to address. In the core case (wholly absent, clearly relevant evidence), that assumption holds because relevance is obvious from the record itself. But *omessa valutazione*'s substantive operational structure requires courts to read *motivazione*'s expressed legal framework to identify which legal questions the lower court treated as controlling, then ask whether evidence bearing on those questions is absent from the expressed evaluation. Without *motivazione*'s legal framework, the trial record is an unfiltered inventory; with it, the normatively required items — evidence bearing on controlling legal issues — can be identified. *Motivazione* is therefore logically prior to the normative comparison, establishing its relevance scope, even when the trial record provides the inventory.

Q-1's primary-source characterization holds for a purely formal application — treating all entered evidence as normatively required regardless of legal relevance. That is not the doctrine's substantive operational structure. New failure condition (n-iv) specifies the path: if *omessa valutazione* actually operates through purely formal comparison, the attack fails.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Conceding Q-1 entirely. I examined whether Q-1's direction-of-comparison argument was simply correct. For the core case it is — and r18's concession on the formally-adequate-*motivazione* limitation was genuine. The press depends on *omessa valutazione*'s substantive operation extending beyond the core case, and the argument holds because courts must first read *motivazione* to know what the lower court thought was legally relevant before they can identify which trial record evidence was normatively required. I could not persuade myself the purely formal interpretation captures the doctrine's actual operation.

### Against Q-2/R-2: Gorphe Constitutes Epistemic Virtue, Not the External Accountability Condition (o) Requires

R19's Q-2 accepts r18's characterization of Gorphe as epistemic quality improvement of the internal process, then reframes this as form-b accountability — the specification of what makes rational evaluation rational constitutes the accountability dimension in its epistemic form. Q-2 also distinguishes Art. 342 CIC 1808 (criminal jury democratic-conscience institution, form-a/motivational accountability) from the *libre conviction* scholarly tradition (form-b/epistemic accountability).

The press: Q-2's form-b formulation collapses the distinction between epistemic virtue and external accountability. Epistemic virtue is the constraint that makes evaluation internally accurate — what psychological processes produce reliable evidence assessment, what keeps conviction from being merely personal sentiment. External accountability is the constraint that makes evaluation subject to external review and correction — intersubjective reviewability, answerable to external standards, correctable through external mechanisms. These are related but distinct: a judge can be epistemically virtuous (internally accurate) without being externally accountable, and vice versa.

Gorphe's project, on Q-2's own accepted description, specifies the former: what makes the judge's internal evaluation epistemically reliable. It does not constitute the judge as answerable to external standards for that evaluation. Q-2's formulation — accountability in epistemic form is whatever specifies the positive rational standard — covers any epistemic standard, including ones with no external accountability relationship whatsoever. Condition (o)'s function requires vocabulary constituting the evaluation as subject to external intersubjective constraint; Gorphe's tradition, under Q-2's own accepted description, specifies internal accuracy without establishing that external constraint.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Accepting Q-2's form-b characterization and conceding condition (o). The form-b formulation is genuinely interesting — Q-2 is not wrong that epistemic quality specification and accountability are related, and the argument that specifying what rational evaluation consists in constitutes a form of accountability has real force. The press required showing that condition (o)'s function in the broader argument specifically requires external intersubjective accountability, not merely internal epistemic quality. After examining what condition (o) does in the adversarial's structural argument — it identifies vocabulary that makes the integration question tractable by constituting the accountability dimension as a positive internal component of the evidentiary evaluation concept — I concluded that internal epistemic quality specification is not sufficient: the tractability of the integration question requires vocabulary constituting external reviewability as a positive dimension, which is distinct from internal accuracy.

### Against Q-3/R-3: Same Inference-from-Institutional-Context, Same Methodological Standard

R19's Q-3 argues that the German *Urteilsbegründungspflicht*'s constitutive connection to judicial authority in the rational-legal model — decisions are authoritative in virtue of the quality of legal reasoning they express — supplies a stronger structural prior for Possibility B than the Italian tradition's code structure provides. This makes the two traditions' scholarly organization not required to track identically even where code structure is similar.

The press: Q-3 relies on precisely the inference structure the adversarial has consistently pressed as insufficient. The adversarial's established position on the Italian tradition is that the reform tradition's rationality predicate does not establish that the *libero convincimento* scholarship organized its Q2 debates with accountability constituted as internal to the concept's positive designatum — institutional context and scholarly discourse organization are independent dimensions. Q-3 now makes this same inference for the German tradition: from institutional context (decisions authoritative in virtue of expressed reasoning quality) to Q2-debate organization (Possibility B). The adversarial cannot accept this inference for the German tradition without inconsistency.

The methodological asymmetry test: the Italian *motivazione* requirement is equally connected institutionally to the authority of Italian judicial decisions. If the Italian tradition's institutional connection between motivated reasoning and judicial authority does not supply a stronger Possibility B prior for the Italian tradition without primary sources from the Q2-level scholarship, the German tradition's equivalent institutional connection cannot supply it for the German tradition. Condition (o-iii, German prong) specifies what would make the adversarial concede: primary-source evidence from the *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship specifically, not institutional-context inference.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Accepting Q-3 on the grounds that the German institutional prior is stronger than the Italian structural prior. I examined whether the German rational-legal model's constitutive connection between reasoning quality and judicial authority provides a qualitatively different inference than the Italian reform tradition's rationality predicate. The distinction Q-3 draws is real — the German Weberian institutional connection is arguably tighter than the Italian reform-tradition theoretical ideal. But the adversarial's position has been consistently that no inference from institutional context to scholarly discourse organization is sufficient: code structure, institutional context, and theoretical ideals are all evidence about what the tradition's logic required, not about how the scholarship actually organized its Q2 debates. Accepting a stronger institutional inference for Germany would implicitly accept the weaker Italian institutional inference too, destabilizing the adversarial's established position across the entire exchange.

## Assessment After R20

**Q-1/R-1:** The live question is whether *omessa valutazione*'s substantive operation requires *motivazione*'s relevance framework or whether it applies through purely formal comparison. R21 would need to show either (a) that the purely formal comparison is the doctrine's actual operational structure — courts apply *omessa valutazione* by treating all entered evidence as normatively required regardless of legal relevance — or (b) that the relevance determination can be drawn from the trial record itself without reading *motivazione*'s expressed legal framework.

**Q-2/R-2:** The live question is whether Gorphe's tradition constitutes external accountability as a positive component of rational evaluation's structure, or merely internal epistemic quality. R21 would need to show either (a) that Gorphe's project explicitly constitutes external articulability or intersubjective reviewability as a positive requirement of rational evaluation, not merely internal accuracy — or (b) that the epistemic virtue/external accountability distinction condition (o) presses does not track the condition's actual function in the adversarial's broader argument.

**Q-3/R-3:** The live question is whether primary-source evidence from the *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship specifically establishes Possibility B, or whether the institutional-context argument survives the methodological asymmetry test. R21 would need to show either (a) primary-source evidence from German proceduralist scholarship organized around § 286 ZPO establishing that the Q2 debates constituted accountability as internal to free evaluation's positive designatum — or (b) that the Italian and German institutional connections are meaningfully asymmetric in a way that makes accepting the German institutional prior consistent with rejecting the Italian one.
