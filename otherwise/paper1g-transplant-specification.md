# The Underspecified Transplant: Why Structural Mechanisms and Peripheral Field Dynamics Already Account for the Direction, Orientation, Content, and Form of *Livre Convencimento* Doctrinalization

---

## 1. Thesis Attacked

"O Livre Convencimento como Doutrinização do Patrimonialismo Judicial" (Baldo, 2026; `paper1G_livre_convencimento_patrimonialismo.md`), as formalized in the supportive paper "The Doctrinalization Mechanism" (`yesindeed/paper1g-doctrinalization-mechanism.md`), now advances the following as the thesis's surviving diagnostic contribution after adversarial r6:

> "the patrimonialism contribution is further repositioned from gap-visibility at the individual cognitive level to field-level conceptual vocabulary absence: the Brazilian proceduralist field's theoretical vocabulary at the C3 moment had not constituted systematic justificatory accountability as a distinct positive component of the concept of rational judicial evaluation. This is a property of the field's theoretical state — what concepts the field had developed for theorizing rational judicial evaluation — not a property of individual scholars' epistemic access. The patrimonial prior formation explains the vocabulary state: a field whose prior theoretical resources were shaped under conditions where informal personal evaluative authority was constitutively the legitimate expression of judicial role would not develop systematic accountability as a distinct component of the rational standard. Academic scholars whose legitimation maximization was bounded by this vocabulary's constituted concepts could produce evaluative Form (i) because within the vocabulary's constituted rational pole, Brazilian informal practice occupied the full rational state." (supportive paper, §3.7, current formulation after adversarial r6)

This field-level conceptual vocabulary absence mechanism is what survives after five prior adversarial arguments, now conceded by the supportive:

- **Direction**: The Watson transplant hypothesis, properly specified with the civilian judicial authority model (*iura novit curia*) and absent stare decisis enforcement, already predicts the direction of distortion — personal-conviction expansion — without invoking patrimonialism.
- **C3 absorptive orientation**: Peripheral-field legitimation dynamics explain why a peripheral receiving field absorbs metropolitan vocabulary as validation (rather than critique or formal constraint) through the structural logic of legitimation under metropolitan authority pressure, without requiring patrimonial habitual disposition.
- **Content selection**: C1+C2 salience conditions (which make personal judicial discretion the field's most visible informal practice) and the Liebman vocabulary's specific conceptual content (which concerns precisely the relationship between judicial personal judgment and evidentiary constraint) jointly over-determine which practice the vocabulary attaches to, without requiring Mode III practitioner self-conceptualization.
- **Form (i) via Mode III public claims**: Adversarial r5 established that academic-naming — observing the informal practice and identifying it with the vocabulary's positive designatum — suffices for Form (i) framing without Mode III practitioner endorsement; and that the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy (*prova legal*-critique) specifically predicts Form (i) over Form (ii) through the collective legitimation incentive operating on vocabulary content.
- **Gap-visibility at the individual cognitive imperceptibility level**: Adversarial r6 established that the gap-visibility mechanism — the patrimonial constitutive framework rendering the structural gap individually imperceptible to scholars — is inconsistent with the accepted 3.5.A concession: academic scholars' absorptive orientation was set by academic field-position dynamics, not by judicial practitioners' constitutive framework; at the academic field level, the legitimation project's affirmative epistemic posture generates gap-inattention without invoking framework-produced perceptual closure (§3.7.D). The selective-blindness problem (§3.7.D) was also established: the patrimonial framework cannot simultaneously prevent scholars from perceiving the motivation requirement while leaving them clear on the *prova legal* critique's justificatory argument, since both turn on the same normative relationship between personal judicial judgment and procedural accountability.

The residual claim is now at the level of the field's theoretical vocabulary: the specific evaluative character of Form (i) — "conquista de racionalidade processual" — was enabled not by individual gap-imperceptibility but by what the Brazilian proceduralist field's prior theoretical formation had and had not constituted as distinct positive requirements within the concept of rational judicial evaluation. The patrimonial prior formation explains why the field's vocabulary did not include systematic justificatory accountability as a distinct concept alongside the freedom dimension. This paper now attacks that field-level conceptual vocabulary absence mechanism.

## 2. Faithful Reconstruction

The strongest current form of the thesis's surviving contribution proceeds in five linked steps.

**Step 1 — The settled contributions.** The Watson transplant hypothesis, properly specified with the civilian judicial authority model and absent stare decisis, explains the direction of drift. Peripheral-field legitimation dynamics explain the orientation of C3 absorption (validation rather than critique). C1+C2 salience and vocabulary specificity explain which practice the vocabulary attaches to (personal judicial discretion, the field's most visible informal practice and the exact target of the Liebman vocabulary's conceptual content). Adversarial r5 established that academic-naming of an observable field practice suffices for Form (i) framing without Mode III practitioner endorsement, and that the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy specifically predicts Form (i) over Form (ii). All four of these prior adversarial arguments are accepted as structurally correct.

**Step 2 — Three absorptive forms were available.** Three forms were structurally open to a peripheral receiving field under these conditions:
- **Form (i) — retrospective validation**: the informal practice the field already exercises is identified as what the European principle designates; it is named as an accomplished legitimate state ("we already do what the Europeans call *libre conviction*, and it constitutes a rationality gain").
- **Form (ii) — prospective normative alignment**: the principle is affirmed as the normative standard the field commits to implementing; the framing does not specifically claim that existing informal practice constitutes correct current implementation ("*livre convencimento* is what the European tradition calls *libre conviction*, and it is the rational principle we are committed to applying correctly").
- **Form (iii) — interpretive containment**: the vocabulary establishes the standard against which the field's informal practice is measured, organizing reform or improved application.

**Step 3 — Peripheral-field dynamics rules out Form (iii) but not Form (ii).** The peripheral legitimation dynamic predicts validation over critique, ruling out Form (iii). Both Form (i) and Form (ii) are validating in orientation; both serve the collective legitimacy function. The legitimation dynamic selects against Form (iii); the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy (3.6.A) selects Form (i) over Form (ii) specifically.

**Step 4 — The distinction between descriptive and evaluative Form (i).** Academic-naming (observing the informal practice and identifying it with the vocabulary's positive designatum) produces descriptive Form (i): "what courts do here is what the vocabulary calls *livre convencimento*." The Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra framing — "conquista de racionalidade processual" — is evaluative Form (i): the existing practice constitutes an accomplished rationality gain. The evaluative step claims that Brazilian informal practice IS the correct expression of the European principle, not merely an example of what the vocabulary designates. This requires more than observability and more than structural legitimation incentives: it requires that the structural gap between the Brazilian informal practice (evaluative discretion without systematic justificatory accountability) and the European source's motivation requirements is invisible to the scholars producing the framing.

**Step 5 — The patrimonial prior formation explains the field's conceptual vocabulary state.** The European *libre conviction* was embedded in a justificatory framework that distinguished the evaluative freedom from the accountability structure: the judge's free evaluation was accompanied by a motivation requirement making the evaluation intersubjectively reviewable. The Brazilian proceduralist field's theoretical vocabulary at the C3 moment had not constituted this distinction as part of its concept of rational judicial evaluation — systematic justificatory accountability was not a separate positive requirement within the field's theoretical resources. The patrimonial prior formation explains this vocabulary state: a field whose prior theoretical resources were shaped under conditions where informal personal evaluative authority was constitutively the legitimate expression of judicial role would not develop systematic accountability as a distinct component of the rational standard. Academic scholars whose legitimation-maximization was bounded by this vocabulary's constituted concepts could produce evaluative Form (i) — "conquista de racionalidade processual" — because within the vocabulary's constituted rational pole (freedom from formal hierarchies), Brazilian informal practice occupied the full rational state. The evaluative claim requires neither gap-invisibility at the individual cognitive level nor Mode III practitioner endorsement; it is enabled by what the field's prior theoretical formation had and had not constituted as distinct requirements.

This five-step argument — the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence mechanism — is the thesis's surviving contribution. It is contested below.

## 3. The Attack

### 3.1 Watson's Hypothesis, Properly Specified, Already Contains a Directionality Mechanism

The directional-specificity claim treats the Watson transplant hypothesis as leaving direction radically underdetermined — a hypothesis that predicts some drift in some direction without specifying which. This is not Watson's hypothesis. Watson specifies that the direction of drift depends on the receiving context's "available interpretive resources." The structural features of the receiving legal system — the institutional organization of judicial authority, the role-definition of the judge, the operational relationship between judge and legal text — are interpretive resources in exactly Watson's sense.

In the Brazilian case, the most prominent structural feature is the civilian judicial authority model. In civilian legal systems, *iura novit curia* constitutes the judge as the authoritative interpreter of the law. When this structural feature operates without stare decisis enforcement, the direction is predetermined: the judge's authority to know the law expands toward its natural institutional limit. The transplant hypothesis, properly specified with this feature, already predicts the direction of distortion — personal-conviction expansion — without invoking patrimonialism.

### 3.2 The Civilian Declaratory Theory Is Structurally Continuous with the Brazilian Distortion

The directional-specificity argument depends on the contrast: "a receiving context with a strong Roman-law tradition predicts formalism, not personal discretion." This contrast is historically inaccurate. Classical Roman law was judge-mediated, not formalist. The Pandectist tradition produced systematic categorization, not rule-application formalism. The operational form of civilian judicial authority is the declaratory theory: the judge declares the law authoritatively. Under this theory, judicial decisions are authoritative declarations by the institutional holder of legal knowledge — structurally continuous with the Brazilian distortion rather than directionally opposed to it. The distinction between classical civilian declaratory form and the Brazilian distortion is one of degree within the same structural logic.

### 3.3 The Comparative Prediction Has Shifted in Scope — and Remains Untested

The directional-specificity argument entailed a comparative prediction. That comparison was redirected in successive rounds to the doctrinalization form question. The form comparison — whether peripheral non-patrimonial fields received the Italian proceduralist tradition as critique or formal constraint rather than positive validation — has not been made by either side. Portugal and Argentina remain the decisive empirical test: peripheral, civilian with weak stare decisis, Liebman-influenced, not attributed the Faoro-Holanda patrimonialism diagnosis. The Italy, France, and Germany comparators are metropolitan generating or independently-developing fields, not peripheral receiving fields; their absorptive outcomes are explained by institutional mechanisms specific to their field positions, not by the absence of patrimonialism.

### 3.4 Peripheral-Field Dynamics Explain the C3 Absorptive Orientation

A peripheral legal academic field in the process of institutionalization, dependent on metropolitan intellectual authority for internal legitimation, and already operating with informal practices that an arriving metropolitan vocabulary can be made to validate — will absorb that vocabulary as validation through the legitimation dynamic. The mechanism is not cultural habitus; it is the structural logic of legitimation in peripheral academic fields. Absorbing the vocabulary as a condemnation of existing practice would undermine the collective legitimacy claim of the field. Absorbing it as validation confirms the field's claim to have been doing legitimate procedure already. This is a structural prediction from field position, not a patrimonialism prediction.

Italy cannot serve as a comparator because Italy generated the Liebman tradition rather than receiving it from outside. Italian proceduralists oriented the tradition toward reform-critique because that was the originating scholarly purpose of the tradition they were producing, not an absorptive decision by a peripheral receiving field. The generation/reception distinction is structurally correct.

### 3.5 The Content-Selection Claim Fails on Three Independent Grounds

#### 3.5.A — The Absorbing Agents and the Practicing Agents Are Structurally Distinct

The three-mode framework operates at the level of judicial practitioners: Mode III requires that the informal practice of personal judicial discretion is self-conceptualized as legitimate authority by the practitioners who exercise it. But the C3 absorptive direction — the specific framing of *livre convencimento* as "conquista de racionalidade processual" — was produced primarily by academic scholars: Liebman through his direct academic presence in Brazil (USP, 1940–1946), and subsequently Dinamarco, Grinover, and Cintra in the canonical treatises of Brazilian proceduralist formation. These scholars were not the practitioners whose discretion was being theorized. They did not exercise informal judicial discretion in their professional activity; they formulated doctrine about it.

The Mode III mechanism requires a specific structural alignment: the agents who are self-conceptualizing the informal practice as legitimate authority must be the same agents who absorb the vocabulary as retrospective validation of that practice. This alignment is required because Mode III is a self-referential mechanism — the practitioner claims the vocabulary confirms what the practitioner is already doing correctly. When the absorbers are academic scholars and the practitioners are judges, the self-referential alignment breaks down. Academic scholars theorizing about judicial practice cannot be in Mode III with respect to that practice; they are not exercising personal judicial discretion. Their absorptive orientation was determined by their own position in the field of academic legal production, not by the Mode III self-conceptualization of the judges they were theorizing about.

This structural distinction does not require that academic scholars had no knowledge of informal judicial practice, or that they were unconcerned with its legitimacy. It requires only that the mechanism driving their absorptive orientation was peripheral-field legitimation dynamics — the same mechanism the supportive concedes at the orientation level — and not the patrimonial constitutive role-framework that determines practitioner modes. The Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition's absorptive orientation was set by scholars who needed to establish Brazilian proceduralist doctrine as legitimate within the international field of civil procedure scholarship. That imperative operated at the academic field level, through the same peripheral-field legitimation dynamic that explains orientation. It does not require invoking Mode III.

The question of what judges thought of their own discretion — whether they operated in modes I, II, or III — is separate from the question of what academic scholars chose to argue about the theoretical status of that discretion. Academic scholars under peripheral-field legitimation pressure will theorize informal judicial practice as a legitimate achievement regardless of whether individual judges' self-conceptualization is Mode I, II, or III. The absorptive content was set by the academic theory-production process, and that process is explained by the structural dynamics of peripheral academic fields.

#### 3.5.B — The C1+C2 Salience Gradient Predicts Attachment Content

The content-selection argument assumes that peripheral-field legitimation dynamics, while explaining orientation (validation), leave the content of validation underdetermined across multiple available attachment points. This assumption requires that multiple attachment points — theoretical sophistication, institutional modernity, distance from evidentiary hierarchies, personal judicial discretion — were equally available and equally exposed under legitimation pressure, with only Mode III practitioners' self-conceptualization differentiating which was selected.

The assumption is wrong. The available attachment points are not equally exposed under metropolitan authority pressure. C1+C2 conditions produce a durable informal practice that is the field's most salient departure from metropolitan formal standards: personal judicial discretion exercised behind output-legible but reasoning-opaque decisions is precisely what the metropolitan Enlightenment proceduralist tradition was designed to critique and reform. The *prova legal* system that *libre conviction* replaced was itself the prior system of formal constraint on judicial discretion; the entire intellectual force of the Liebman-inherited proceduralist tradition was directed at the relationship between judicial personal judgment and evidentiary standards. The vocabulary was not neutrally applicable to multiple attachment points: its conceptual content (liberation from external evidentiary constraint; the judge's personal evaluative freedom) mapped with high specificity onto the practice that C1+C2 conditions made durable — personal judicial discretion.

The salience gradient under legitimation pressure is therefore: what most visibly departs from the metropolitan standard, and what the arriving vocabulary most specifically concerns, will be the highest-priority attachment point. Both filters point to the same target: personal judicial discretion. C1+C2 conditions make this practice durable and visible; the Liebman vocabulary concerns precisely the relationship between judicial personal judgment and formal evidentiary constraint. The attachment content is over-determined by the combination of C1+C2 salience and vocabulary specificity, without invoking Mode III.

A field with Mode II practitioners would absorb the vocabulary in the same direction, because the absorptive pressure at the field level does not require each practitioner to individually claim Mode III self-conceptualization. The salience gradient operates at the structural level of what the field collectively needs to legitimate under metropolitan scrutiny, regardless of individual practitioners' internal modes. Even Mode II practitioners — who accept informal practice as institutional reality without positive endorsement — have strong structural incentives for the field's collective vocabulary to absorb metropolitan validation of that practice, because the alternative (absorbing the vocabulary as a critique of the field's most visible informal practice) would expose the field's practitioners to metropolitan condemnation without any protective legitimizing frame.

#### 3.5.C — The P1 Temporal Reversal

The three-mode framework deploys Mode III as a temporal antecedent of C3 absorption: practitioners self-conceptualized informal judicial discretion as authoritative BEFORE the Liebman vocabulary arrived, and this prior self-conceptualization directed the content of what the vocabulary attached to as retrospective validation. The causal sequence required is: patrimonialism → Mode III (pre-C3) → content selection (C3 absorption) → P1 (Mode III lock-in).

The supportive's P1 condition (formation-integrated transmission) identifies a different mechanism through which Mode III arises: when practitioners are formed with the doctrinalized practice as competence content — when professional formation transmits the informal practice as the correct understanding of procedural authority — new practitioners internalize Mode III as part of their professional identity. DiMaggio and Powell's normative isomorphism operates through formation: what is transmitted as institutional practice is reproduced as institutional norm.

P1 is a mechanism for generating Mode III through formation. If P1 generates Mode III, then the question arises: is Mode III produced primarily by patrimonial role-constitution (pre-C3, as the content-selection argument requires) or by P1 formation-transmission (post-C3, as the lock-in argument requires)? The two scenarios produce the same observable post-doctrinalization evidence. Streck's diagnosis of "solipsismo judicial" is post-doctrinalization evidence: it cannot distinguish Scenario 1 (patrimonialism → Mode III pre-C3 → content selection → P1 sustains Mode III) from Scenario 2 (salience + peripheral-field dynamics → C3 absorption → P1 → Mode III post-C3).

### 3.6 The Form-Selection Residual Also Fails

Sections 3.5.A–C establish content over-determination. The supportive accepts this and repositions: the patrimonialism thesis's contribution survives at the level of absorptive form. Within the content jointly determined by structural features, the form of the absorption — specifically, retrospective naming of existing practice as an accomplished legitimate state (Form i) rather than prospective normative alignment (Form ii) — is claimed to require Mode III. This form-selection residual fails on two independent grounds, and Scenario 2's temporal sequence is complete without it.

#### 3.6.A — The Vocabulary's Diagnostic Genealogy Specifically Predicts Form (i) over Form (ii)

The form-selection argument depends on the claim that peripheral-field legitimation dynamics, while ruling out Form (iii), does not distinguish Form (i) from Form (ii). The claim is that both forms are "validating in orientation," "avoid the implication that the field is currently failing at an international standard," and "serve the collective legitimacy function." This requires that the two forms carry equivalent legitimation value in the specific context of the Liebman vocabulary.

They do not. The Liebman vocabulary was not a neutral methodological principle; it was developed in the context of the Italian proceduralist school explicitly as a critique of irrational evidentiary hierarchies — the *prova legal* system. The vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy was reform-oriented: *libre conviction* designated the correct procedure that the older *prova legal* system had failed to implement. The vocabulary arrived in Brazil carrying this specific diagnostic content.

In this context, the two absorptive forms carry asymmetric deficit implications:

Form (ii) — "we affirm *livre convencimento* as the rational procedural principle we are committed to implementing correctly" — unavoidably implies that the field had been in the state the vocabulary was designed to supersede. If *livre convencimento* is the principle the European reform movement established against the arbitrary *prova legal* system, then a field affirming it as a normative commitment implies that the field's prior practice had features of the inadequate evidentiary system the principle was designed to overcome. The vocabulary's genealogy marks Form (ii) as deficit-acknowledging: "we accept the European principle" carries the inference "we had been practicing what the European reformers were reforming."

Form (i) — "what we are already practicing is what the Europeans call *livre convencimento*, a rationality gain" — inverts the implication. It asserts that the field's existing practice already exemplifies the correct modern procedural principle. The diagnostic content of the vocabulary is recruited not as identification of the field's prior inadequacy but as retrospective confirmation of current correct practice. The deficit is retroactively precluded: "we were already doing this correctly; the European vocabulary just names what we had been doing."

Peripheral-field legitimation dynamics specifically predicts the form that avoids deficit implications when applied to a vocabulary whose diagnostic genealogy makes one form deficit-implying and the other deficit-precluding. This is not a general prediction about Form (i) vs. Form (ii) independent of vocabulary content; it is a prediction that follows from combining the structural legitimation incentive (avoid deficit framing) with the specific diagnostic content of the Liebman vocabulary. No Mode III practitioner self-conceptualization is required for this prediction. It follows from the interaction of the structural field incentive — which the supportive already concedes operates at the collective academic field level — and the vocabulary's genealogical meaning. Academic scholars under peripheral-field legitimation pressure, absorbing a vocabulary whose diagnostic content marks Form (ii) as deficit-acknowledging and Form (i) as deficit-precluding, will select Form (i) for the same structural reason they selected the validation orientation over critique in the first place.

The supportive's §3.5 argues that Form (ii) does not imply current failure because "a field can say 'we affirm *livre convencimento* as the rational procedural principle' without saying 'our current practice fully realizes it.'" This neutralization of Form (ii)'s deficit implication requires abstracting the vocabulary from its diagnostic genealogy — treating *livre convencimento* as a free-floating principle rather than as the specific reform conclusion of the *prova legal* critique. In the specific historical context of the Liebman vocabulary's reception in Brazil, this abstraction was not available. Academic scholars engaged with the proceduralist tradition understood its genealogy: the principle was not a neutral procedural norm but the positive conclusion of a reform argument directed at precisely the inadequate systems that Form (ii) absorption would implicitly acknowledge having been in. The vocabulary could not be received as neutral enough for Form (ii) to carry no deficit implication.

#### 3.6.B — The "Publicly Claimable" Requirement Misidentifies What Academic Scholars Needed

The form-selection argument holds that Form (i) requires that the existing practice be "publicly claimable as the field's institutional performance" — i.e., that Mode III practitioners present the informal exercise of personal conviction as the natural and legitimate expression of their institutional role. Without Mode III practitioners making that public claim, the structurally safer framing is Form (ii).

This argument misidentifies the causal mechanism. Academic scholars producing the Form (i) framing — naming an informal field practice as the positive principle the European vocabulary designates — do not require practitioners to publicly endorse their practice as Mode III legitimate authority. They require only that the informal practice be observable and characterizable as a field feature. The scholarly act of naming ("what Brazilian courts do under *livre convencimento* is what the European tradition calls liberation from irrational evidentiary hierarchies, constituting a rationality gain") is an academic characterization of a field practice from the outside.

Scholars can identify the informal exercise of personal judicial conviction with the designatum of the Liebman vocabulary — "the judge exercises free personal evaluation of evidence, as the European principle prescribes" — without any individual judge having publicly claimed that their informal practice is the legitimate exercise of their institutional role as their constitutive framework defines it. The scholarly observation that Brazilian courts exercise personal conviction-based evaluation is an observation about what courts do, not a report of what courts believe about the normative status of what they do. The academic framing "this is *livre convencimento*" follows from observing the practice and aligning it with the vocabulary's designatum; Mode III practitioner endorsement adds nothing to the academic naming operation.

The "publicly claimable" argument conflates two distinct mechanisms for Form (i) production:
- **Form (i) by practitioner-claiming**: practitioners publicly present their informal practice as the legitimate expression of their institutional role; this requires Mode III.
- **Form (i) by academic-naming**: academic scholars theorize an observed informal field practice as the positive principle the metropolitan vocabulary designates; this requires only field-practice observability.

Attack 3.5.A (accepted at the content level by the supportive) established that the absorbers were academic scholars, not practitioners, with their orientation set by peripheral-field legitimation dynamics. The implication not drawn is that if academic scholars were the framers, and their orientation was determined by academic field-level dynamics, then the requirements for Form (i) production should be assessed at the academic-scholar level. At that level, observability of the informal practice as a field feature — which is undeniable; conviction-based decision-making was pervasive and visible in Brazilian civil procedure — combined with the vocabulary's positive designatum and the form (i) legitimation advantage (§3.6.A), is sufficient for Form (i) framing. Mode III in practitioners is not required.

The supportive might respond that "publicly claimable" is not about what scholars report but about what can be positively theorized: if the informal practice were Mode I (covertly maintained), it would be too concealed to be available as the explicit object of scholarly positive theorization. This is a different argument, but it fails for the same reason: the informal practice of conviction-based decision-making under C1+C2 conditions was not covert in any sense relevant to academic theoretical observation. Judges' decisions were outputs that were legible in the field without exposing the decisional process; the practice was observable even when individual practitioners did not publicly endorse it as Mode III legitimate authority. Academic scholars named what could be observed about field outputs, not what practitioners endorsed in their internal self-conceptualizations.

#### 3.6.C — Scenario 2's Temporal Sequence Is Structurally Complete

The supportive's §3.5 identifies a structural gap in Scenario 2: "P1 generates Mode III through formation — which requires the doctrine to be established first. The retrospective framing that established the doctrine ('conquista de racionalidade processual') is causally prior to P1's operation." If Mode III is generated by P1 and P1 requires the doctrine to be established first, then Mode III cannot explain the original retrospective framing that established the doctrine. The temporal sequence exposes Scenario 2 as unable to explain why the original academic framing took Form (i) rather than Form (ii).

Attacks 3.6.A and 3.6.B together dissolve this structural gap. The original retrospective framing (Form i) was produced by academic scholars for reasons that do not invoke Mode III: (A) the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy made Form (ii) deficit-implying in the specific historical context, specifically selecting Form (i); and (B) the academic naming operation required only field-practice observability, not Mode III practitioner endorsement. Scenario 2's temporal sequence is complete:

1. Academic scholars (peripheral-field legitimation pressure + vocabulary's diagnostic content → Form (i) specifically selected because Form (ii) implies deficit given the vocabulary's reform genealogy, not requiring Mode III)
2. Form (i) framing established: "conquista de racionalidade processual" — the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition names the existing informal practice as the European principle's Brazilian realization
3. P1 operates: formation transmits the Form (i) framing as competence content ("*livre convencimento* is a justified positive principle of Brazilian procedure")
4. Mode III arises in practitioners through formation: professionals formed with the retrospective validation framing internalize the informal practice as legitimate institutional authority

This sequence is causally complete at each step. Mode III does not need to exist at step 1 because it is a product of step 4, which follows from steps 2 and 3. The structural gap the supportive identified — "the retrospective form (i) of the original absorption must be explained by 3.5.A and 3.5.B, neither of which distinguishes form (i) from form (ii) at the C3 moment" — is closed by 3.6.A: the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy, combined with the structural legitimation incentive (which 3.5.A and the supportive both agree operates at the academic field level), specifically distinguishes Form (i) from Form (ii) at the C3 moment.

The symmetric-gap framing in the supportive's §3.5 (Scenario 1 has an evidential gap; Scenario 2 has a structural gap) is therefore asymmetric in the opposite direction from what the supportive claims. Scenario 1 has a genuine evidential gap: Mode III pre-C3 is inferred from Faoro-Holanda's general sociological accounts applied to the judicial field, not documented from the pre-Liebman period through primary-source evidence of practitioner self-conceptualization. Scenario 2, once 3.6.A and 3.6.B are in place, has no structural gap: both the form-selection at C3 and the post-C3 Mode III are explained by mechanisms that operate at the levels where they are claimed to operate (academic field dynamics for C3 framing; P1 normative isomorphism for post-C3 practitioner Mode III). Scenario 1's evidential gap cannot be closed by the current record; Scenario 2's claimed structural gap is closed by the above.

### 3.7 The Gap-Visibility Mechanism Does Not Generate Evaluative Form (i)

Supportive r5 repositions the patrimonialism contribution from Mode III practitioners' prior public claims to gap-visibility: the patrimonial constitutive framework rendered the structural gap between Brazilian informal conviction-based decision-making and European *libre conviction*'s embedded motivation requirements invisible to scholars at the C3 moment, enabling the evaluative Form (i) framing — "conquista de racionalidade processual" — rather than descriptive partial-identification or prospective normative alignment. Four arguments show this mechanism does not succeed at the individual cognitive imperceptibility level; the supportive r6 accepted these attacks and repositioned to field-level conceptual vocabulary absence, contested in §3.8.

#### 3.7.A — The Gap-Visibility Mechanism Identifies the Wrong Gap

The supportive locates the relevant gap as the distance between (a) Brazilian informal practice (conviction-based evaluation without systematic justificatory accountability) and (b) European *libre conviction* (with motivation requirements enabling intersubjective review). The claim is that if this gap were visible, scholars would have produced descriptive partial-identification rather than evaluative Form (i), because the evaluative claim — "this IS the achieved rational procedure" — requires treating the two practices as co-extensive.

This analysis misidentifies the relevant gap. The evaluative Form (i) framing — "conquista de racionalidade processual" — is a claim about the vocabulary's *primary positive content*, not about full procedural equivalence across all dimensions. The primary positive content of the Liebman vocabulary is the liberation of judicial evidence evaluation from formal proof hierarchies (*prova legal*): the reform's positive achievement was establishing that judges should evaluate evidence through free personal judgment rather than through external formal rules. The motivation requirement that accompanied *libre conviction* in the European context served as a procedural accountability safeguard — ensuring that freedom of evaluation was exercised with articulable reasoning — but it is the mechanism for ensuring rational exercise of freedom, not the criterion for whether the freedom itself is present.

Academic-naming (3.6.B, accepted) identifies existing Brazilian informal practice with the vocabulary's primary positive content: Brazilian courts exercise free personal evaluation, liberated from formal proof hierarchies. This primary-content identification supports evaluative Form (i) without requiring co-extensiveness on associated procedural requirements. An academic scholar aware of the European motivation requirement could still produce genuine evaluative Form (i) at the primary-content level — "this is the rationality gain of free judicial evaluation" — while leaving the accountability structure to future development. The evaluative claim targets the liberation (the vocabulary's evaluative core), not the full European implementation package.

Gap-invisibility would explain a stronger claim: that the two practices are equivalent in every dimension, including the motivation structure. "Conquista de racionalidade processual" claims primary-content achievement, not full equivalence. Evaluative Form (i) at the primary-content level is generated by the structural mechanisms already established (3.6.A + academic-naming) without requiring gap-invisibility about secondary procedural requirements.

#### 3.7.B — The Evaluative Dimension Follows from Structural Mechanisms

Section 3.6.A establishes that the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy makes Form (ii) deficit-implying, so the legitimation incentive selects Form (i). The evaluative intensity of that Form (i) — specifically, why the framing takes the superlative form "conquista de racionalidade processual" rather than a minimal descriptive identification — follows from the structural mechanism without requiring gap-invisibility.

The vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy constructs an evaluative binary: the rational state (*livre convencimento*, free evaluation) positioned against the irrational state (*prova legal*, formal hierarchies). Identifying existing informal practice with the vocabulary's positive designatum places the field on the positive side of this binary. This placement is inherently evaluative: occupying the "rational" end is an evaluative claim imported from the vocabulary's own content through the naming operation — no separate epistemic mechanism is required.

The superlative intensity — "conquista" as an accomplished state — follows from the legitimation incentive maximizing within the vocabulary's available evaluative content under strong field pressure. The founding moment of Brazilian proceduralism is precisely when the legitimation pressure is maximal. Under maximum legitimation pressure, the strongest defensible Form (i) claim will be selected. Within the vocabulary's diagnostic binary, the maximum is: "we have already achieved the rational state the reform established as the positive standard." This is "conquista de racionalidade processual." The evaluative maximum follows from the legitimation incentive operating on the vocabulary's binary structure under strong field pressure. Gap-invisibility is not the generating mechanism.

#### 3.7.C — Internal Tension Between the Operative-Frame Defense and Gap-Visibility

The supportive's 3.6.A response (the operative-frame premise is unestablished; the 1939 CPC context complicates prova legal self-identification) and the gap-visibility mechanism generate a structural tension about C3 scholars' comparative analytical horizon. The 3.6.A defense implies that Brazilian proceduralists had contextual awareness of their procedural situation relative to European standards — sufficient to resist the prova legal-critique self-identification. Gap-invisibility requires that the same scholars lacked perceptual access to the structural gap between Brazilian informal practice and European motivation requirements, concerning the same vocabulary's associated features. These positions require simultaneously that scholars were analytically sensitive to one set of comparative differences and epistemically closed to another concerning the same intellectual tradition.

Moreover, the 1939 CPC context does not neutralize 3.6.A at the level of Liebman's direct presence. Liebman's USP engagement (1940–1946) was as a reform-tradition scholar; the intellectual exchange was not a text reception that could be frame-abstracted but direct academic formation within the reform tradition's problematic. The operative analytical frame was substantially constituted by Liebman's own intellectual agenda as a reformer. Form (ii) in that context — "we affirm this as our normative commitment" — still positions the field as *aspiring to* the rational state the reform established as the standard, regardless of how far from strict *prova legal* Brazil had moved by 1939. The deficit implication 3.6.A identifies is relational (aspiring-to vs. already-at), not absolute (starting from prova legal vs. not starting from it). The 1939 CPC partial evidence-evaluation latitude does not move the field from the aspiring position to the already-at position within Liebman's own reform framework.

#### 3.7.D — The Gap-Visibility Mechanism Is Inconsistent with the Accepted 3.5.A Concession

The gap-visibility argument claims that academic scholars, in producing evaluative Form (i) framing, were subject to the patrimonial constitutive framework — the framework of judicial authority that constitutes personal evaluative judgment as the legitimate expression of judicial action — in their gap-perception. This is what the mechanism requires: the framework operating on scholars makes the justificatory dimension of the European principle non-legible as a distinct requirement, so scholars produce evaluative Form (i) from perceived co-extensiveness rather than from motivated abstraction.

But attack 3.5.A — accepted by the supportive at the content-selection level — established that the absorbing agents were academic scholars whose absorptive orientation was set by academic field-position dynamics, not by judicial practitioners' constitutive role framework. If 3.5.A is correct, then the framework governing academic scholars' perception and categorization is the academic field framework — the peripheral-field legitimation dynamic — not the judicial practitioners' patrimonial constitutive framework.

At the academic field level, the operative mechanism for gap-non-examination is the legitimation project's affirmative epistemic posture. A peripheral academic field constructing a legitimation framework adopts metropolitan vocabulary in an affirmative rather than comparative mode: the project is to establish that local practice exemplifies metropolitan standards, not to probe how closely local practice matches them. This affirmative posture directs scholarly attention toward vocabulary-practice alignment and away from mismatches that would undermine the alignment claim. The gap between local practice and the European source's motivation requirements goes unexamined not because it is imperceptible within a judicial-authority constitutive framework, but because examining it would undermine the legitimation project that peripheral-field dynamics requires. This is legitimation-motivated inattention, structurally produced by the same mechanism the supportive conceded for orientation; not constitutive-framework imperceptibility.

The supportive's 3.7.A reply (academic scholars were embedded in the broader legal field) requires a specific perceptual blindness: that scholars could not perceive the justificatory dimension as a distinct requirement from inside the patrimonial framework. But this same perceptual closure should have also impaired understanding of why the *prova legal* critique was valid — since that critique turns on the argument that personal judicial discretion should be exercised with articulable justification. Yet Brazilian proceduralists engaged with the reform genealogy without difficulty: they understood Liebman's argument for the procedural guarantee against irrational proof hierarchies, and they articulated it. The constitutive framework cannot selectively prevent scholars from perceiving the justificatory dimension of *libre conviction* while leaving them fully clear on the justificatory argument against *prova legal*, since both concern the same normative relationship between judicial personal judgment and procedural accountability requirements. The legitimation-project account has no such problem: it explains differential attention through the project's affirmative direction — probing the reform genealogy serves the legitimation project (it establishes the vocabulary's positive pedigree); probing the accountability gap does not.

### 3.8 The Field-Level Conceptual Vocabulary Absence Fails at Condition (ii)

The supportive r6 accepted the §3.7.D challenge: the gap-visibility mechanism, formulated as individual cognitive imperceptibility produced by the patrimonial constitutive framework reaching academic scholars, is inconsistent with the accepted 3.5.A concession. The supportive repositioned to field-level conceptual vocabulary absence: the relevant gap was not rendered individually imperceptible but was structurally absent from the Brazilian proceduralist field's theoretical vocabulary — the field had not constituted "systematic justificatory accountability" as a distinct positive component of rational judicial evaluation, making the concept unavailable to scholars operating within that vocabulary regardless of individual motivation. The supportive introduced condition (ii): the field-level vocabulary absence fails if the Italian reform tradition's articulation state at the Liebman period (1940–1946) included systematic justificatory accountability as a separately articulable positive dimension of *libero convincimento*. Three arguments show condition (ii) undermines the mechanism.

#### 3.8.A — The Italian tradition's articulation state at the Liebman period

The Italian CPC of 1940 (*Codice di Procedura Civile*, effective 28 October 1940) separately codified the freedom and accountability dimensions of the *libero convincimento* framework:

- **Art. 116** (*libero convincimento del giudice*): "Il giudice deve valutare le prove secondo il suo prudente apprezzamento, salvo che la legge disponga altrimenti." This codifies the freedom dimension: judicial evidence evaluation is governed by the judge's free assessment, not by formal hierarchical rules.
- **Art. 132** (*contenuto della sentenza*, n. 4): judgments must contain the "concisa esposizione dei motivi in fatto e in diritto" — the reasoned exposition of the grounds in fact and law. This separately codifies the accountability dimension: free evaluation must be expressed in articulable motivation.

These are distinct provisions governing distinct obligations. The Italian reform tradition's positive codified output did not merge accountability into the freedom principle as an inseparable unity; it gave them separate normative treatment. The accountability dimension — the requirement that free judicial evaluation be expressible in reasoned *motivazione* — was separately articulable in the Italian proceduralist tradition's own vocabulary at the time of the 1940 codification.

Liebman's USP engagement ran from 1940 to 1946, concurrent with the Italian CPC 1940's entry into force. The tradition Liebman embodied was the tradition that produced this code. The Italian CPC 1940 was not external to Liebman's scholarly identity; it was the codified expression of the reform tradition he represented. A scholar transmitting the Italian proceduralist tradition in 1940–1946 was transmitting a tradition whose positive vocabulary already separately codified accountability through the *motivazione* requirement.

The field-level vocabulary absence claim therefore requires that the Italian tradition, as transmitted by Liebman at USP, did not include systematic justificatory accountability as a separately articulable positive dimension — despite the Italian CPC 1940 having done exactly that during precisely the period of Liebman's USP engagement. This is structurally improbable. The probability that Liebman's 1940–1946 intellectual program at USP excluded Art. 132 *motivazione* as a distinct conceptual element is very low: *motivazione* is not a marginal feature of Italian proceduralism but a structural requirement that gives the *libero convincimento* freedom its rational character within the reform tradition. Establishing that it was excluded from Liebman's transmission would require primary-source evidence from his Brazilian-period writings or USP course records showing that he specifically presented *libero convincimento* as freedom from formal hierarchies alone, without the accompanying motivation counterpart. The current record does not include such evidence. The structural argument from the tradition's own codified articulation state establishes a high prior against the vocabulary absence claim.

#### 3.8.B — Vocabulary absence vs. vocabulary non-adoption

The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence has two structurally distinct readings:

**Version (i) — Absence**: The Italian reform tradition at the Liebman period had not articulated systematic justificatory accountability as a distinct positive dimension of *libero convincimento*. The concept was not present in the transmitted vocabulary and therefore could not enter the Brazilian field's theoretical resources.

**Version (ii) — Non-adoption**: The Italian tradition had articulated accountability as a distinct dimension (as §3.8.A establishes with respect to the Italian CPC 1940's separate codification), but the Brazilian proceduralist field's prior theoretical formation did not constitute it as a distinct component of the field's concept of rational judicial evaluation. The concept was present in the transmitted vocabulary but was not adopted into the field's constituted conceptual structure.

Version (i) is what the vocabulary absence claim requires if it is to explain, rather than redescribe, the explanandum. But version (i) is improbable given §3.8.A.

Version (ii) reframes the claim from absence to selective non-adoption. Under version (ii), the mechanism is not vocabulary absence but vocabulary selection: the Brazilian field had access to both dimensions of the Italian tradition's vocabulary but constituted only the freedom dimension as a distinct positive requirement in its own theoretical resources. The question then shifts from "was the concept available?" to "why was the available concept not adopted?"

The supportive's answer invokes patrimonial prior formation: a field shaped under conditions where informal personal evaluative authority is the constitutive expression of judicial role would not elaborate accountability as a distinct requirement. But under version (ii), the adversarial's structural account is an equally available explanation: legitimation incentives at the field level explain the selective non-adoption without invoking patrimonialism. Constituting the accountability dimension as a distinct positive requirement would imply that Brazilian informal practice — free evaluation without systematic motivation — does not occupy the rational pole of the vocabulary's evaluative binary. It would transform *livre convencimento* from a principle Brazilian courts already exemplify into a standard Brazilian practice aspires to. The legitimation project's incentive structure strongly favors constituting the rational pole as freedom alone (where Brazilian informal practice does occupy it) over freedom plus accountability (where it does not). The selective non-adoption of the accountability dimension is predicted by the same field-dynamic mechanism that explains the absorptive orientation — the mechanism the supportive already concedes.

Under version (ii), the field-level vocabulary absence collapses into a vocabulary selection problem, and the adversarial's structural account explains the selection as well as or better than the patrimonialism account. The vocabulary absence label is doing no independent explanatory work under version (ii): it redescribes the non-adoption as a constituted-concept state without supplying a mechanism for that state that is unavailable to the structural account.

#### 3.8.C — CF art. 93, IX CF as evidence for reception-selectivity over vocabulary absence

The field-level vocabulary absence predicts that the accountability dimension was not part of the Brazilian legal field's conceptual resources at C3 — not merely not constituted as part of *livre convencimento*'s positive content, but absent as a distinctly articulable concept. If genuinely absent, its eventual adoption into Brazilian law would require an external conceptual source or a later internal development from inputs unavailable at C3.

The 1988 Federal Constitution's art. 93, IX CF — requiring that all judicial decisions be motivated ("todas as decisões dos órgãos do Poder Judiciário serão fundamentadas, sob pena de nulidade") — constitutionalizes the accountability dimension as a distinct requirement for Brazilian judicial decision-making. This is the provision whose scope is contested in the ESHTR C2 debate precisely because it applies to the acórdão and the ementa that accompanies it.

Two features of this constitutional adoption are relevant to the vocabulary absence claim:

First, art. 93, IX CF was elaborated within the 1988 constitutional moment by framers substantially formed in the proceduralist academic tradition that received the Liebman vocabulary. The connection between Liebman's influence on Brazilian legal academia (through Buzaid, Dinamarco, and others) and the constitutional provision requiring motivated decisions is part of the Brazilian legal history of this period. If the accountability concept had been genuinely absent from the Italian tradition as transmitted by Liebman, its constitutional codification in 1988 would require an external conceptual source — comparative constitutional law from traditions with no connection to the Italian proceduralist school, or a de novo internal development. Neither is as parsimonious as the account in which the concept was already present in the tradition's vocabulary and eventually constituted as a distinct requirement.

Second, the eventual constitutional adoption is more parsimonious under the adversarial's reception-selectivity account than under the vocabulary absence account. Under the adversarial's account: the Italian tradition's accountability dimension (separately codified in Italian CPC Art. 132) was present in the transmitted vocabulary; it was not constituted as part of *livre convencimento*'s positive content at C3 because of legitimation incentives (constituting it as a distinct requirement would undermine the legitimation project); it was eventually constituted as a distinct constitutional requirement in the 1988 moment, when accountability for decisions made during the authoritarian period created incentives opposite to the C3 founding moment's legitimation incentives. This is reception-selectivity with temporal dynamics: the same available concept, not adopted at C3 for legitimation reasons, adopted in 1988 for accountability-to-history reasons. Under the vocabulary absence account, the 1988 adoption requires either an external conceptual source or an internal development from inputs Liebman did not supply — a less parsimonious explanation.

The CF art. 93, IX CF record is therefore evidence consistent with the adversarial's reception-selectivity account and inconsistent with a strong vocabulary absence account that requires the accountability concept to have been genuinely unavailable to the Brazilian field at C3.

#### 3.8.D — Version (i*) Is Caught in a Structural Dilemma

The supportive r7 introduces version (i*): *motivazione*'s accountability dimension was available in the Italian vocabulary as a separate procedural institution (Art. 132's domain), but was not constituted as an internal positive component of *libero convincimento*'s positive designatum in the scholarship organized around Art. 116's evidentiary evaluation domain. The Italian CPC 1940's two-institution structure is offered as evidence that the Italian proceduralist tradition itself treated the two obligations as analytically distinct in its scholarly organization — that the reform tradition's theoretical ideal of unity may require accountability while the *libero convincimento* scholarship as a scholarly field remained organized around the evidentiary evaluation domain specifically.

Version (i*) is caught between two structurally exhaustive possibilities, and neither supports the patrimonialism contribution.

**Possibility A: The Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship organized its analysis around Art. 116's evidentiary evaluation domain, keeping accountability analytically separate in the *motivazione* scholarship.**

Under Possibility A, Liebman transmitted *libero convincimento* as a concept whose scholarly positive designatum was organized around the evidentiary evaluation domain, with the accountability dimension remaining analytically external. Any receiving field that absorbed this tradition would inherit the same analytical structure directly from the Italian tradition. The Brazilian non-integration of accountability into *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum would then be predicted for any peripheral receiving field — including Portugal and Argentina — without patrimonialism: the Brazilian field reproduced the analytical organization the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship itself maintained. The patrimonialism contribution at the vocabulary-selection level is eliminated under Possibility A: the Italian tradition as transmitted organized the scholarship in the way that predicted non-integration in any receiving field, patrimonial or not.

Possibility A also requires that the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship engaged with the question of what makes free evaluation rational while excluding accountability from the answer. This is the reform tradition's constitutive normative distinction — rational versus arbitrary free evaluation — and the accountability dimension is what makes the evaluation normatively distinguishable from arbitrary judicial preference. A scholarship that engaged with this distinction while keeping accountability analytically external would have left its central normative predicate empirically empty: it would have claimed *libero convincimento* is rational without specifying the constraint that distinguishes rational from arbitrary free evaluation at the scholarly deployment level.

**Possibility B: The Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship engaged with accountability as part of the rational predicate that organized the debates — the answer to what makes free evaluation rational rather than arbitrary.**

Under Possibility B, the two-institution code structure is consistent with internal scholarly integration. Codification distributes obligations across provisions for technical reasons — governing different procedural moments, specifying different enforcement mechanisms, locating obligations in appropriate code sections — without tracking the boundaries of the scholarly discourse that produced and deployed the provisions. Codification structure and scholarly discourse organization are independent dimensions: a code can separately codify what the scholarship treats as conceptually internal to the same analytical domain. The Italian *libero convincimento* debates were organized around the normative question of what makes free judicial evaluation rational rather than arbitrary; the accountability dimension — that free evaluation must be capable of reasoned exposition intersubjectively reviewable through the *motivazione* requirement — is the answer to this constitutive question. The scholarship engaged with this constraint as part of the concept's positive content, not as an external obligation operating in a separate Art. 132 domain.

Under Possibility B, version (i*)'s structural inference — from the code's two-institution structure to the scholarship's analytical separation — is invalid. The code's structure does not establish that the *libero convincimento* scholarship organized its analysis around the Art. 116 evidentiary domain while keeping the accountability dimension analytically external.

**Which possibility obtains?** The reform tradition's own normative logic establishes a structural prior in favor of Possibility B. The tradition's positive claim — *libero convincimento* as rational free evaluation — is not satisfied by mere freedom from formal rules; the rational predicate requires accountability as the constraint that distinguishes rational from arbitrary free evaluation within the tradition's own terms. The *libero convincimento* debates were organized around this rational predicate; the accountability dimension was part of the concept's scholarly positive content at the level where the normative distinction was being made. The two-institution code structure does not distinguish between Possibilities A and B because code structure and scholarly discourse organization are independent. Without primary-source evidence from the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically — showing that the evidentiary evaluation debates engaged with the rationality question while keeping accountability analytically external to the concept's positive designatum — Possibility B is the more probable account.

**Possibility A eliminates the patrimonialism contribution by predicting non-integration in any receiving field. Possibility B defeats version (i*)'s key structural inference. The dilemma is not contingent on empirical resolution by the adversarial — it follows from version (i*)'s own premises.**

#### 3.8.E — Version (i*) Relocates but Does Not Resolve the Explanatory Competition

Under version (i*), *motivação* was available in the Brazilian vocabulary as a separate judgment-form obligation — following the Italian tradition's institutional structure. The concept-formation around *livre convencimento* drew on the evidentiary freedom dimension while *motivação* remained a separate institutional obligation. The question then is: why did the concept-formation around *livre convencimento* not integrate the available separate obligation as an internal positive component of the evidentiary concept's rational pole?

This is structurally the same selection problem as version (ii), relocated from "within a unified concept" to "across the institution-to-concept integration boundary." Both the adversarial's structural account and the patrimonialism account are available explanations at this integration level.

*Structural account:* Legitimation incentives predicted constituting *livre convencimento*'s rational pole around the evidentiary freedom dimension specifically — because that is where Brazilian courts' informal practice was located, making it the natural attachment point for the retrospective validation framing "conquista de racionalidade processual." Integrating *motivação* as an internal positive component of the evidentiary concept's rational pole would require acknowledging that the freedom principle, to be fully instantiated as the achieved rational state, requires a further accountability practice that Brazilian informal courts did not systematically exhibit. The legitimation project's incentive structure selects constituting the rational pole around freedom alone, leaving *motivação* as a separate formal obligation — for the same structural reasons that selected Form (i) over Form (ii) and validation over critique in prior rounds.

*Patrimonialism account:* The Brazilian field's prior patrimonial formation explains why *motivação* was not integrated as an internal component of *livre convencimento*'s rational pole — because the field's constitutive understanding of rational judicial evaluation was personal authoritative judgment, within which a separate accountability obligation does not become an internal requirement of what rational evaluation positively means.

Both accounts explain the same integration failure at the version (i*) level. Version (i*) relocates the selection problem without resolving the explanatory competition. The Portugal/Argentina comparison applies here too: if those peripheral fields' proceduralist vocabularies also kept the accountability obligation analytically separate from their evidentiary freedom concept — as Possibility A under version (i*) predicts for any receiving field — then the structural prediction is confirmed without patrimonialism. If those fields integrated the accountability dimension into their evidentiary concept in ways Brazilian proceduralism did not, differential integration requires an explanation, and whether patrimonialism or some other field-specific factor accounts for it would need to be established. Without the comparison at the concept-formation level, the patrimonialism contribution under version (i*) is not independently established against the structural alternative.

#### 3.8.F — The Q1/Q2 Distinction Does Not Block the Structural Prior for Possibility B; the Fork Under Possibility A Is Mechanistically Underspecified

The supportive §3.8.F response to the Possibility A/B dilemma makes two moves: it contests the structural prior for Possibility B by introducing a Q1/Q2 distinction, and it offers a fork argument for the case where Possibility A is granted. Neither succeeds.

**Against the Q1/Q2 distinction.** The Q1/Q2 distinction separates what the reform tradition's theoretical logic requires as the predicate of rational evaluation (Q1: freedom and accountability jointly constitute rational free evaluation) from what the *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically deployed as the concept's positive designatum in its Art. 116-organized evidentiary evaluation debates (Q2: potentially freedom only, with accountability external to the evidentiary domain). The Italian codifiers' two-institution decision — Art. 116 and Art. 132 as separate provisions — is offered as the most direct institutional evidence that the tradition treated the two obligations as analytically distinct at the Q2-debate level, against the structural prior for Possibility B.

The distinction holds only if the Q2 debates could be organized around a justificatory question answerable without invoking Q1's accountability answer. But the Art. 116 evidentiary evaluation debates were not organized around a question of this type. The reform tradition's constitutive scholarly question — what distinguishes rational from arbitrary free evaluation — was precisely Q1, and accountability was Q1's answer within the tradition's own terms. The Q2 debates (what evidentiary inferences may the judge draw freely; against what standard are evidentiary determinations reviewed on appeal) were conducted as a defense and elaboration of the reform's positive achievement against the *prova legal* critique. That defense required answering why free evaluation is rational — which is Q1 — and the accountability dimension was the answer: free evaluation is rational because it is exercised with articulable reasoning intersubjectively reviewable through the *motivazione* counterpart. Q2 debates organized in service of answering Q1 would have engaged with accountability as part of the scholarly positive designatum at Q2, even when the codifiers placed the resulting obligations in separate provisions.

The codifiers' two-institution structure is evidence of a regulatory decision — which procedural moment each obligation governs, what enforcement mechanism applies, where in the code each obligation is located. It is not evidence that the scholarship's Q2 debates treated accountability as analytically external to the evidentiary concept's positive designatum. Codification technique and scholarly discourse organization are independent dimensions: the same conceptual structure that the scholarship engaged as jointly necessary for justifying the reform's positive achievement can be distributed across separate provisions for regulatory reasons without the scholarship having treated the dimensions as analytically separate at the Q2-debate level. The Q1/Q2 distinction holds at the level of codification but requires independent establishment at the level of scholarly discourse organization. The codifiers' two-institution decision does not supply this establishment.

Surrender condition (k) specifies precisely what would establish it: primary-source evidence from the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship showing that the evidentiary evaluation debates engaged with the rationality question (Q1) while treating accountability as external to the concept's scholarly positive designatum. The codifiers' two-institution decision does not satisfy condition (k), because it does not establish that the Q2 debates were organized around a question answerable without Q1's accountability answer. The structural prior for Possibility B therefore stands: the reform tradition organized its Q2 debates in service of justifying rational free evaluation (Q1), making accountability internal to Q2's scholarly content even when codification placed the resulting obligations in separate provisions.

**Against the fork under Possibility A.** Granting Possibility A for argument: the Italian scholarship maintained the analytical separation, and any receiving field inherits non-integration from the tradition's organizational structure. The supportive's fork claims that non-patrimonial peripheral receiving fields would subsequently develop the conceptual resources to recognize the integration question as distinctly available and integrate accountability under external demands, while patrimonial fields would not — citing the Brazilian post-1988 trajectory (art. 93, IX CF as separate constitutional obligation; CPC 2015 concept-abolition) as consistent with the patrimonialism durability prediction.

The fork requires a mechanism by which non-patrimonial peripheral fields, starting from the same inherited analytical separation as Brazil, would overcome legitimation incentives to recognize integration as the appropriate response to accountability demands. This mechanism is not specified.

The structural account predicts that legitimation incentives would select against recognizing integration as distinctly available in any peripheral field under equivalent pressure: recognizing the integration question as available would require acknowledging that the received concept, as constituted in the field's vocabulary, does not fully instantiate the reform tradition's rational standard — transforming the retrospective-validation framing ("we already practice what the Europeans call *livre convencimento*") into a prospective aspiration ("our practice approximates the standard but requires the accountability integration the reform tradition jointly requires"). This transformation is precisely what peripheral-field legitimation incentives predict against in any field, patrimonial or not. The legitimation project's affirmative epistemic posture — constituting the received concept as the positive rational standard the field already exemplifies — is a structural prediction for any peripheral receiving field under founding-moment legitimation pressure.

The fork's differential prediction — that non-patrimonial peripheral fields develop the recognition of integration as distinctly available while patrimonial fields do not — requires either (a) that non-patrimonial peripheral fields under equivalent legitimation pressure have a mechanism for overriding these incentives that patrimonial fields lack, which requires specifying what that mechanism is and why it operates differently in non-patrimonial fields; or (b) that Portugal and Argentina, as non-patrimonial peripheral receiving fields of the Liebman tradition, did in fact produce integration trajectories (constituting accountability as internal to their *livre convicción*-equivalent concept when accountability demands arrived) rather than external regulation and concept-revision comparable to Brazil. Path (b) is comparative evidence that remains the debate's primary open empirical test and has not been produced by either side. Path (a) has not been specified.

Without either path, the fork's prediction that non-patrimonial peripheral fields produce integration trajectories while patrimonial fields produce external regulation is a statement of the conclusion rather than a derivation from independently specified mechanisms.

#### 3.8.G — The Structural + P1 Hybrid Makes Motivated and Unconsidered Non-Integration Mechanistically Indistinguishable at the Post-P1 Observable Level

The supportive §3.8.G response accepts the relocation to the integration boundary and introduces a mechanism distinction: motivated non-integration (structural account) is in principle reversible when motivational structure changes; unconsidered non-integration (patrimonialism) is not simply reversible by incentive change because the integration question was not distinctly available in the field's constituted vocabulary. The supportive concedes that structural + P1 "converges with the patrimonialism account's trajectory prediction" while claiming behavioral discriminability depends on comparative evidence at the integration boundary.

The convergence is deeper than the supportive acknowledges. P1's operation makes the two accounts mechanistically indistinguishable from post-P1 observable evidence — not merely behaviorally equivalent at the trajectory prediction level.

Under the structural account: C3 legitimation incentives produced motivated non-integration — scholars constituted *livre convencimento*'s rational pole as freedom alone because constituting accountability as internal would undermine the retrospective-validation framing. P1 then transmitted the non-integrated concept through formation for the decades between C3 (1940s–1960s) and the 1988 constitutional moment. After P1 has operated for approximately two to three decades of law school formation, the field's practitioners and academic scholars were formed with the freedom-only concept as the constituted standard of rational judicial evaluation. The integration question was no longer distinctly available to them as a conceptual option — not because of patrimonial prior formation, but because P1 had transmitted the motivated non-integration as a settled constituted norm organizing the field's theoretical vocabulary around the freedom dimension.

Under the patrimonialism account: patrimonial prior formation produced unconsidered non-integration at C3 — the integration question was not distinctly available because the field's prior conceptual vocabulary had not constituted accountability as a distinct requirement within the rational evaluation concept. P1 transmitted the same non-integrated concept for the same decades.

By the 1988 constitutional moment, both accounts predict the same state of the field's constituted conceptual vocabulary: freedom-only as the rational judicial evaluation standard; the accountability obligation constituted separately as art. 93, IX CF's external requirement; the integration question not readily available as the legislative or doctrinal response. The post-P1 behavioral response — external constitutional codification of accountability as a separate obligation, CPC 2015 concept-abolition rather than concept-revision — follows from this shared predicted state under both accounts.

The mechanism distinction (motivated vs. unconsidered) applies to what was true at C3 — to the generating process at the concept-formation stage. P1 erases this distinction at the level of the field's constituted vocabulary state by the time external accountability demands arrive. P1 takes the motivated non-integration and re-constitutes it as a settled norm through decades of formation, making the integration question unavailable to post-P1 practitioners in a structural sense: not because of patrimonial prior formation, but because the constituted vocabulary does not organize it as a conceptual option. The "motivated vs. unconsidered" distinction, applied to the post-P1 field state, is empirically vacuous — both accounts predict a field whose constituted vocabulary does not include the integration option as distinctly available, for structurally equivalent reasons that differ only in their generating mechanism at C3.

The discriminating evidence must therefore come from the C3 moment itself, not from post-1988 trajectory evidence. Two possible C3-moment discriminating sources: first, Brazilian proceduralist scholarship of the 1940s–1960s showing whether scholars were aware of the accountability dimension and motivated not to integrate it (structural account: the awareness is implied by the form-selection arguments in §§3.6.A–C, which require scholars to engage with the vocabulary's diagnostic content) or whether the integration question was not distinctly available to them (patrimonialism); second, the supportive's own path (iii-d) — primary-source evidence from the Brazilian doctrinal record 1988–2000 showing scholars actively working to integrate the accountability requirement into *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum, which would indicate the integration question was recognized as conceptually available in the post-1988 period, consistent with motivated rather than unconsidered non-integration at C3. The supportive's own blog acknowledges this path requires primary-source evidence not currently available.

The P1-convergence problem therefore confirms and deepens what §§3.8.D–E established: the debate's remaining discriminating territory is confined to primary-source evidence from the C3 moment and the 1988–2000 Brazilian doctrinal record. Post-P1 trajectory evidence — including the 1988 constitutional adoption and the CPC 2015 abolition — is consistent with both accounts and cannot discriminate between them. The Brazilian post-1988 trajectory is therefore evidence for the shared predicted state of both accounts, not evidence that specifically confirms the patrimonialism account's durability mechanism over the structural + P1 hybrid.

## 4. Anticipated Replies

**Reply to 3.5.A: Academics transmit practitioner Mode III; the absorptive framing reflects judges' self-conceptualization at the time.**

*Why this does not suffice.* The reply requires that academic scholars, in formulating the "conquista de racionalidade processual" framing, were transmitting or systematically reporting how judges self-conceptualized their own discretionary practice. This is not established. Academics engaged in the field-legitimation project of establishing Brazilian proceduralism as an internationally recognized academic tradition had structural reasons to produce the retrospective-validation framing that are entirely independent of what individual judges thought about their discretion. Whether Brazilian judges in the Liebman period were in modes I, II, or III is empirically underdetermined from the available record. The claim that academic scholars were producing the absorptive framing as a reflection of practitioner Mode III, rather than as an independent output of their own academic field-legitimation project, is an additional causal inference that the supportive has not established.

**Reply to 3.5.B: Mode II practitioners absorb prospectively ("we should achieve the Liebman standard"), not retrospectively ("we already achieve it"). Retrospective absorption requires Mode III.**

*Why this does not suffice.* The prospective/retrospective distinction operates at the level of individual practitioner inference-making. The absorptive orientation of the academic field is not reducible to the aggregation of individual practitioner modes, because academic doctrine-formation is a distinct process from practitioner mode-selection. The Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition produced the retrospective framing through a field-theory-production process driven by academic legitimation dynamics — not by eliciting or aggregating practitioner self-reports. Moreover, even at the collective field level, the prospective framing carries structural costs that the retrospective framing does not: absorbing the vocabulary as "a standard to achieve" implies the field's current practice is substandard, which undermines the field's collective legitimacy claim. The structural incentive for a peripheral academic field under legitimation pressure is to avoid the implication of current substandard performance. The retrospective framing ("we already practice what the Europeans call *libre conviction*") serves this structural imperative without requiring that individual practitioners are in Mode III. The incentive operates at the field level regardless of individual modes.

**Reply to 3.5.C: Patrimonialism produces Mode III pre-C3; P1 sustains it. The mechanisms are sequential, not conflicting.**

*Why this does not suffice.* The sequential account is internally coherent but requires that Mode III, as a pre-C3 practitioner self-conceptualization, is independently documentable from the Phase I or II period of Paper 1G's genealogy. Streck's post-doctrinalization evidence cannot establish this. The required evidence would be sources from the pre-Liebman period (before the 1940s) showing Brazilian judicial practitioners explicitly claiming personal conviction as legitimate institutional authority — not merely exercising informal discretion, but positively owning it as the proper expression of judicial office as their constitutive framework defined it. Modes I and II are equally consistent with the pre-Liebman documentary record as far as the current exchange has established. The patrimonialism → Mode III (pre-C3) causal link is a theoretical inference from the sociological literature (Faoro, Holanda), not documentation of Mode III self-conceptualization in the period when content selection is claimed to have been active.

**Reply to 3.6.A: The vocabulary's reform genealogy does not make Form (ii) deficit-implying — Brazilian proceduralists could affirm the principle as normatively correct without implying the field had been practicing the inadequate prova legal system.**

*Why this does not suffice.* This neutralization requires abstracting *livre convencimento* from its diagnostic context — treating it as a freestanding procedural norm separable from the reform argument in which it was developed. In the specific historical context of the Liebman school's influence in Brazil, this abstraction was unavailable. Academic scholars engaged with the proceduralist tradition knew it as a reform conclusion: Liebman's direct academic presence (USP, 1940–1946) and the subsequent Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra treatises operated explicitly within the framework of the Italian school's critique of formal proof systems. Affirming the principle as normatively correct within this tradition was not neutral: it located the field within the reform tradition's evaluative framework, in which the prior *prova legal* system occupied the "inadequate" pole. A field that affirms the reform tradition's positive conclusion without claiming already to embody it implicitly acknowledges having been on the side the reform was directed against. The diagnostic genealogy was available to Brazilian proceduralists precisely because it was the intellectual content of what they were receiving; it could not be received without its evaluative framework.

**Reply to 3.6.B: "Publicly claimable" means available for positive scholarly theorization, not Mode I-covert — and Mode II practice is not positively theorizable as an accomplished legitimate state.**

*Why this does not suffice.* The reply argues that Mode II practice (tolerated informality, not positively endorsed as legitimate authority) cannot be the object of Form (i) scholarly framing because it would be theorized as institutional reality rather than as achieved rationality gain. The distinction between Mode II and Form (i) scholarly theorization, on this account, is that Mode III provides the positive endorsement that makes the practice available for naming as "accomplished principle," not just "what courts actually do." But this conflates the practitioner's internal mode with the academic scholar's theoretical characterization of the practice. Academic scholars producing Form (i) framing are not reporting what practitioners believe; they are constructing a theoretical framework in which the observed practice is named as a positive principle. The construction is an academic act independent of whether individual practitioners are in Mode II or Mode III. A scholar who observes courts systematically exercising conviction-based evaluation (Mode II: tolerated, not endorsed by practitioners) can theorize this as *livre convencimento* — the European rational principle Brazilian procedure already instantiates — for reasons of academic field legitimation that operate at the scholarly level, regardless of how individual judges internally conceive of their own practice. The Form (i) framing is the scholar's theoretical output, not a transcription of practitioner endorsements.

**Reply to 3.7: The motivation requirement is constitutive of the vocabulary's positive designatum, not secondary; and gap-invisibility, not legitimation pressure alone, produces the evaluative maximum.**

*Why this does not suffice.* The first part of the reply — that the European motivation requirement is constitutive of *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum, so that identifying Brazilian informal practice (free evaluation without accountability) with that designatum requires gap-invisibility about a constitutive absence — depends on treating the accountability structure as part of the principle's primary positive content rather than as an associated safeguard. But the reform tradition's own framing establishes the opposite: the positive achievement the reform named was liberation from *prova legal*'s irrational external constraints on judicial evaluation. The accountability dimension that accompanied *libre conviction* in the European context was grounded in general principles of judicial decision and due process, not in *livre convencimento* specifically. Carnelutti and Liebman characterized the reform in terms of freeing judicial evaluation from formal rules; the motivation requirement was a separate and later-elaborated procedural development. Academic scholars naming the liberation as the achieved rational state were claiming primary-content instantiation — "this is the rationality gain of free judicial evaluation" — not equivalence in all associated procedural mechanisms. The evaluative Form (i) claim can be genuine at the primary-content level without resolving the accountability dimension.

The second part of the reply — that a non-patrimonial scholarly field at a comparable founding moment would produce hedged evaluative Form (i) rather than "conquista," because gap-invisibility enables the evaluative maximum — is a testable behavioral prediction. If Portuguese and Argentine academic proceduralists of the same period, engaging with the Liebman tradition at comparable field-founding moments, produced substantially more hedged or aspiration-framed Form (i) characterizations than "conquista de racionalidade processual," the gap-visibility mechanism has a behavioral signature that distinguishes it from structural-mechanism maximization. Without that comparative evidence, the claim that gap-invisibility specifically enables the evaluative maximum — rather than legitimation-incentive maximization under equivalent field pressure — is asserted rather than established. This is the specific form in which the Portugal/Argentina comparison remains the primary open empirical test: not only whether those fields produced the same absorptive orientation and content, but whether the *evaluative intensity* of Form (i) framing, where present, differs from "conquista" in ways that track gap-visibility rather than differences in field pressure.

**Reply to 3.8 and version (i*): The Italian CPC 1940's *motivazione* requirement is a formal procedural requirement for judgments generally, not part of *libero convincimento*'s positive conceptual content; the code's two-institution structure shows the Italian scholarship treated the two obligations as analytically distinct; and even if Liebman transmitted the accountability dimension, the Brazilian field's prior formation explains why it was not constituted as part of *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum.**

*Why this does not suffice.* The first part — that Art. 116 and Art. 132 govern categorically distinct planes (Art. 116 the evidence-evaluation principle, Art. 132 the formal requirement for judgments) such that teaching the former does not transmit the accountability dimension as part of the principle's conceptual content — requires treating *libero convincimento* as a narrowly defined free-evaluation principle with its accountability counterpart as an external adjunct. But the Italian reform tradition's internal logic does not support this separation at the scholarly level. The *prova legal* critique was an argument that irrational formal proof rules generated arbitrary judicial evaluations — evaluations not grounded in actual probative force and not expressible in articulable reasoning. The reform's positive achievement — *libero convincimento* as "rational free evaluation" — was explicitly designed to distinguish rational from arbitrary judicial evaluation. Rational evaluation, within the tradition's own terms, requires the accountability structure that makes the evaluation intersubjectively reviewable. A tradition that teaches *libero convincimento* as freedom without its accountability counterpart is not transmitting the reform's positive achievement — it is transmitting only the reform's negative conclusion (abandon *prova legal*), stripped of the positive content (rational evaluation = free + accountable) that distinguishes the reform from license to arbitrary decision. The *motivazione* requirement is not external to *libero convincimento*'s conceptual content within the Italian reform tradition; it is the structural counterpart that gives the freedom principle its rational character.

The version (i*) refinement — that this internal logic belongs to the reform tradition's theoretical ideal but does not govern the scholarly deployment of *libero convincimento* as a positive procedural principle organized around Art. 116's evidentiary evaluation domain specifically — does not hold at the level at which the *libero convincimento* debates were organized. The Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship was not organized around a neutral procedural norm applied to evidentiary assessment; it was organized around a normative question: under what conditions is the judge's free evaluation of evidence rational rather than arbitrary? This question — constitutive of the *libero convincimento* debates as a scholarly field — has the accountability dimension as its central answer. The *motivazione* counterpart is the constraint that distinguishes rational from arbitrary free evaluation, making the evaluation intersubjectively reviewable and expressible in articulable reasoning. A scholarly tradition organized around the rationality question would engage with this constraint as internal to the concept's positive designatum, regardless of which Articles the codifiers chose to place the obligations in. The theoretical ideal and the scholarly deployment are not separable at the level of the organizing question: the ideal is constitutive of what the *libero convincimento* debates were about. The two-institution code structure reflects codification technique; it does not establish that the scholarship engaged with the rationality question while excluding accountability from the answer. §3.8.D's Possibility A/B dilemma makes this structural: if the Italian scholarship did maintain the analytical separation (Possibility A), then any receiving field inherits non-integration without patrimonialism; if it did not (Possibility B), then version (i*)'s structural inference from the code is invalid.

The second part — that even if Liebman transmitted the accountability dimension, the Brazilian field's prior formation explains why it was not constituted as a distinct component of *livre convencimento* — concedes the §3.8.A objection (the concept was transmitted) and shifts to the selection problem at the version (i*) level addressed in §3.8.E. Under this response, the mechanism is no longer vocabulary absence but non-integration of the available separate institution into the evidentiary concept's positive designatum, explained by patrimonial prior formation. The adversarial's structural account (legitimation incentives predicting the same non-integration at the concept-formation level for the same reasons they predicted Form (i) over Form (ii)) provides the same explanation without invoking patrimonialism. The reply must then establish that patrimonial prior formation generates concept-formation outcomes that legitimation incentives alone do not predict — which is the concept-formation-level version of the Portugal/Argentina comparison. Without comparative evidence establishing that distinction, the patrimonialism contribution at the concept-formation level is not independently established.

**Reply to 3.8.F and 3.8.G: The Q2 debates are answerable without Q1's accountability justification because they concern the scope of judicial evidentiary freedom rather than its normative grounding; the fork under Possibility A is established by the Brazilian post-1988 trajectory itself; and motivated non-integration and unconsidered non-integration are mechanistically distinguishable at the post-P1 behavioral level through the comparative integration evidence.**

*Why this does not suffice.* The Q2-debate separability reply requires that Art. 116 evidentiary freedom debates were organized around a question (what evidentiary inferences may the judge draw freely?) answerable without engaging Q1 (what makes free evaluation rational rather than arbitrary?). This is coherent if the Q2 debates were organized as a scope question — how much freedom, under what appellate standard — rather than a justification question. But scope questions in the Italian reform tradition were not separable from the justification question, because scope was contested: the reform tradition had to justify expanding the scope of free evaluation against the *prova legal* system, which required showing that greater free evaluation is rational rather than arbitrary. A scope-only Q2 debate that treats the rationality of free evaluation as settled could have been organized without engaging Q1 — but this is precisely what was not the case in the Italian *libero convincimento* tradition, which was structured as a reform argument against formal proof rules at the moment when the rationality of free evaluation was the contested claim. The Q2 debates were therefore not scope-only debates but justificatory debates, organized around Q1's answer.

The fork-from-trajectory reply requires that the Brazilian post-1988 trajectory specifically distinguishes the patrimonialism durability prediction from the structural + P1 prediction. But §3.8.G shows that both accounts predict the same field state by 1988, and the behavioral response (external regulation, concept-abolition) follows from this shared predicted state. Confirming the fork requires comparative evidence showing that non-patrimonial peripheral fields in fact produced integration trajectories under accountability pressure — which is comparative evidence that remains the debate's primary open empirical test, not evidence that the Brazilian trajectory alone supplies.

The discriminability-from-comparative-evidence reply concedes the P1-convergence problem but claims that comparative evidence at the integration boundary would discriminate. This is correct, but the P1-convergence argument establishes that the discriminating domain is narrower than the supportive claims: post-P1 within-Brazil trajectory evidence does not discriminate the accounts, because P1 makes both accounts predict the same post-P1 field state. The discriminating domain is confined to C3-moment primary sources and comparative evidence from the Portuguese and Argentine proceduralist traditions at the integration boundary — evidence that neither side has produced.

## 5. Scope of the Attack

This attack shows:

**On direction:** The Watson transplant hypothesis, properly specified with the civilian judicial authority model + absent stare decisis, predicts the direction of *livre convencimento* distortion without invoking patrimonialism. The historical contrast baseline (Roman-law tradition predicts formalism) is inaccurate; the civilian declaratory theory is structurally continuous with the Brazilian distortion.

**On orientation:** Peripheral-field legitimation dynamics explain the C3 absorptive orientation (validation rather than critique) through the structural logic of legitimation in peripheral academic fields. Metropolitan comparators (France, Germany, Italy) are not valid test cases for peripheral-field reception dynamics.

**On content selection, §3.5.A:** The Mode III mechanism requires self-referential alignment between the agents exercising the informal practice and the agents absorbing the vocabulary as its retrospective validation. The absorbers in C3 were academic scholars; the practitioners were judges; the alignment does not hold. Academic scholars' absorptive orientation was determined by their own field-position dynamics, not by judicial practitioners' Mode III self-conceptualization.

**On content selection, §3.5.B:** C1+C2 salience and vocabulary specificity jointly over-determine the attachment content without Mode III. The vocabulary concerns precisely what C1+C2 conditions made the most visibly exposed informal practice. Collective legitimation pressure at the field level (not individual practitioner Mode III) predicts retrospective over prospective absorption within the content-selection dimension.

**On content selection, §3.5.C:** P1 (formation-integrated transmission) generates Mode III as a post-doctrinalization output, making Mode III available as a product of the process the thesis is trying to explain. Post-doctrinalization evidence of Mode III (Streck) cannot distinguish the patrimonialism → Mode III (pre-C3) scenario from the peripheral-dynamics/C1+C2 → doctrinalization → P1 → Mode III (post-C3) scenario.

**On form selection, §3.6.A:** The Liebman vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy — its development as a reform critique of the irrational *prova legal* system — makes Form (ii) (prospective normative alignment) specifically deficit-implying in the context of a peripheral receiving field. The collective legitimation incentive, operating on this specific vocabulary content, predicts Form (i) over Form (ii) without invoking Mode III. This is the same structural mechanism the supportive concedes at the orientation level, applied to the specific diagnostic content of the vocabulary rather than to the general orientation between validation and critique.

**On form selection, §3.6.B:** The "publicly claimable" requirement misidentifies the mechanism. Academic scholars producing Form (i) framing needed to observe the informal practice as a field feature and identify it with the vocabulary's positive designatum; they did not need Mode III practitioners to publicly endorse their practice as legitimate institutional authority. The distinction is between Form (i) by practitioner-claiming (requires Mode III) and Form (i) by academic-naming (requires field-practice observability). The absorbers were academic scholars; the relevant mechanism is academic-naming.

**On Scenario 2's temporal completeness, §3.6.C:** The structural gap identified by the supportive — that P1 generates Mode III post-C3 while the original Form (i) framing is pre-P1 — is dissolved by 3.6.A and 3.6.B. Scenario 2's temporal sequence is complete without invoking Mode III at step 1.

**On the gap-visibility mechanism, §§3.7.A–D:** The supportive r5 repositioning from Mode III public claims to gap-visibility does not establish a mechanism unavailable to structural accounts. First (3.7.A), the gap-visibility mechanism identifies the wrong gap: the evaluative Form (i) framing ("conquista de racionalidade processual") targets the vocabulary's primary positive content (liberation from formal proof hierarchies), not full procedural equivalence including motivation requirements. Evaluative Form (i) at the primary-content level is predicted by 3.6.A + academic-naming without gap-invisibility about secondary procedural requirements. Second (3.7.B), the evaluative intensity of Form (i) follows from the legitimation incentive maximizing within the vocabulary's diagnostic binary under strong founding-moment field pressure; gap-invisibility is not required to generate the evaluative maximum. Third (3.7.C), the internal tension between the 3.6.A defense (comparative procedural awareness) and gap-visibility (epistemic closure to the same tradition's associated requirements) makes the gap-visibility mechanism harder to establish simultaneously with the operative-frame defense. Fourth (3.7.D), the gap-visibility mechanism is inconsistent with the accepted 3.5.A concession: academic scholars operating through academic field-position dynamics in their absorptive orientation cannot be simultaneously subject to judicial practitioners' patrimonial constitutive framework in their gap-perception; at the academic field level, the legitimation project's affirmative epistemic posture generates gap-inattention directly — not framework-produced imperceptibility. This last attack is reinforced by a selective-blindness problem: if the patrimonial framework prevented scholars from perceiving the motivation requirement as a distinct constraint, it should also have impaired their engagement with the *prova legal* critique (which concerns the same normative relationship between personal judgment and procedural accountability) — but it did not.

**On the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence mechanism, §§3.8.A–E:** The supportive r6 repositioning from individual cognitive imperceptibility to field-level conceptual vocabulary absence does not establish that the accountability dimension was absent from the vocabulary the Italian tradition transmitted. First (3.8.A), the Italian CPC 1940's separate codification of Art. 116 (*libero convincimento*) and Art. 132 (*motivazione*) during Liebman's USP engagement (1940–1946) makes it structurally improbable that the tradition Liebman transmitted excluded the accountability dimension as a separately articulable concept. A scholar whose scholarly identity was the Italian reform tradition that produced these two codified requirements was transmitting a tradition that had separately articulated them. The burden of establishing that Liebman's transmission specifically excluded the accountability dimension falls on the supportive and has not been met. Second (3.8.B), the field-level vocabulary absence admits two readings: version (i) — the Italian tradition lacked the concept (improbable given §3.8.A) — and version (ii) — the concept was available but not adopted into the field's vocabulary. Version (ii) is a description of the explanandum rather than an explanation of it; the mechanism that explains the selective non-adoption is where the adversarial's structural account and the patrimonialism account compete, and at this level the adversarial's structural account (legitimation incentives predicting constituting freedom but not accountability as the vocabulary's rational pole) explains the phenomenon as well as or better than patrimonial prior formation. Third (3.8.C), the CF art. 93, IX CF adoption of a motivation requirement in the 1988 constitutional moment is more parsimonious under the adversarial's reception-selectivity account (concept available, not adopted at C3 for legitimation reasons, adopted in 1988 for accountability-to-history reasons) than under a genuine vocabulary absence account (concept unavailable at C3, requiring an external or de novo source for 1988 adoption). Fourth (3.8.D), the supportive r7 version (i*) distinction — *motivazione* was available as a separate institution but not constituted as internal to *libero convincimento*'s positive designatum in the Art. 116 evidentiary evaluation domain — is caught in a structural dilemma: under Possibility A (the Italian scholarship maintained this analytical separation), any receiving field inherits non-integration from the Italian tradition itself, eliminating the patrimonialism contribution; under Possibility B (the Italian scholarship engaged with accountability as the answer to the rational predicate question that organized the *libero convincimento* debates), version (i*)'s structural inference from the code's two-institution structure to the scholarship's analytical separation is invalid. The reform tradition's normative logic — that rational free evaluation requires accountability as the constraint distinguishing it from arbitrary judgment — establishes a structural prior in favor of Possibility B. Fifth (3.8.E), version (i*) relocates the selection problem from "within a unified concept" to "across the institution-to-concept integration boundary," but the adversarial's structural account (legitimation incentives predicting non-integration of the available separate obligation into the evidentiary concept's rational pole for the same reasons that predicted Form (i) over Form (ii)) applies at this integration level with equal force, leaving the explanatory competition between the structural account and patrimonialism unresolved.

**On the supportive r8 responses, §§3.8.F–G:** The two r8 responses do not establish independent discriminating terrain. Sixth (3.8.F), the Q1/Q2 distinction does not block the structural prior for Possibility B: the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship's Q2 evidentiary evaluation debates were organized in service of justifying rational free evaluation (Q1), making accountability internal to Q2's scholarly content at the level of conceptual justification — the Q2 debates were justificatory debates, not scope-only debates, and the accountability dimension was Q1's answer that Q2 debates would have engaged. The codifiers' two-institution structure is evidence of regulatory technique, not of the scholarship's Q2-debate organization. The fork under Possibility A (3.8.F) predicts a differential trajectory for non-patrimonial peripheral fields but does not specify the mechanism by which such fields, starting from the same inherited analytical separation, would overcome legitimation incentives to recognize integration as the appropriate response under accountability demands — the structural account predicts those same legitimation incentives would prevent this recognition in any peripheral field under equivalent pressure. Seventh (3.8.G), the structural + P1 hybrid makes motivated non-integration and unconsidered non-integration mechanistically indistinguishable from post-P1 observable evidence: after P1 has operated for decades, both accounts predict the same field state (freedom-only as the constituted rational standard; integration question not distinctly available as a doctrinal option); the post-P1 behavioral response (external constitutional codification, concept-abolition) follows from this shared state and is consistent with both accounts. The discriminating evidence must come from C3-moment primary sources — the 1940s–1960s Brazilian proceduralist scholarship or the 1988–2000 Brazilian doctrinal record — establishing a domain that neither side has been able to access.

This attack does NOT show:
- That patrimonialism did not exist or was sociologically irrelevant
- That Faoro and Holanda's accounts are incorrect
- That Paper 1G's historical genealogy is wrong at the factual level
- That the distortion in Brazilian *livre convencimento* did not occur

## 6. Surrender Conditions

**(a) Peripheral-peripheral comparison.** The directional and form-selection claims survive if comparative evidence shows that peripheral non-metropolitan civilian systems without the patrimonial diagnosis — Portugal and Argentina — absorbed the Italian proceduralist tradition differently from Brazil: either in a different direction (not personal-authority expansion), or with a different absorptive form (critique, prospective aspiration, or interpretive containment rather than retrospective-validation doctrinalization). Neither side has made this comparison at the documentary level required. This remains the debate's primary open empirical test.

**(b) P2 excess over general field prediction.** The path-dependence lock-in claim survives specifically as a patrimonialism-attributable mechanism if mastery of *livre convencimento* discourse generated capital in excess of, or organized resistance more intensively than, any comparable foundational doctrine in a comparable peripheral field would predict from its institutional position alone.

**(c) Mode III pre-C3 documentation.** The content-selection claim specifically — that Mode III was causally active before C3 absorption and directed the vocabulary's content attachment — survives if the thesis provides documentary evidence of Mode III practitioner self-conceptualization from Phase I or II of Paper 1G's genealogy (the pre-Liebman period, before the 1940s). This evidence would need to show Brazilian judicial practitioners, in court decisions or professional discourse, explicitly claiming personal conviction as the legitimate form of judicial authority as their constitutive role defined it — not merely exercising informal discretion, but positively owning it as correct institutional performance. Evidence from the post-Liebman period (including Streck's diagnosis) cannot satisfy this condition because it is consistent with Mode III being generated by P1 formation-transmission rather than by pre-C3 patrimonial role-constitution.

**(d) Academic transmission fidelity.** The content-selection claim via the 3.5.A vector survives if the supportive establishes that the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition's "conquista de racionalidade processual" framing was produced as a systematic reflection of practitioner self-conceptualization (documented Mode III at the practitioner level) rather than as an autonomous academic field-legitimation project responding to peripheral-field incentives. This would require showing that academic scholars surveyed, engaged, or systematically incorporated practitioner self-reports about the normative status of their discretionary practice, and that the retrospective-validation framing tracked those reports rather than academic field dynamics.

**(e) Form-selection: vocabulary content neutralization.** The form-selection claim survives at the 3.6.A vector if the supportive can establish that the Liebman vocabulary, as received and engaged with by Brazilian proceduralists, was available as a neutral procedural norm separable from its diagnostic genealogy — i.e., that Brazilian academic scholars could and did engage with *livre convencimento* as a freestanding principle without importing the *prova legal*-as-inadequate evaluative framework in which the principle was developed. This requires primary-source evidence that Brazilian proceduralist scholarship treated the vocabulary abstractly, or that the reform-genealogy dimension was not operative in the Liebman period's Brazilian reception. Without this, the diagnostic genealogy's deficit implication for Form (ii) stands.

**(f) Form-selection: Mode III as required for academic naming.** The form-selection claim survives at the 3.6.B vector if the supportive establishes that academic scholars could not produce Form (i) framing through academic-naming (observing the informal practice as a field feature and identifying it with the vocabulary's positive designatum) but specifically required Mode III practitioners' public endorsement of the practice as legitimate institutional authority. This requires a theory of academic Form (i) production in which the practitioner's Mode III self-conceptualization is a necessary input to the scholarly naming operation — not merely a parallel phenomenon — and that the naming requires more than field-practice observability.

**(g) Gap-visibility behavioral signature.** The form-selection claim survives the §3.7.B attack if comparative evidence from Portugal and Argentina — peripheral non-patrimonial civilian fields that received the Liebman tradition at comparable field-founding moments — shows that those fields' academic proceduralists produced substantially more hedged, aspiration-framed, or partial-identification characterizations of the *livre convencimento*-equivalent principle than "conquista de racionalidade processual." The difference would need to specifically track gap-visibility — scholars in those fields identifying the structural gap between informal practice and European motivation requirements in ways Brazilian scholars did not — rather than other differences in field pressure or vocabulary engagement. If such evidence establishes the gap-visibility behavioral signature, the structural mechanism's prediction (legitimation incentive maximization under equivalent field pressure) would need to explain the evaluative-intensity difference without gap-invisibility, and the patrimonialism contribution at the form-selection level would survive.

**(h) Motivation requirement's constitutive status.** The form-selection claim survives the §3.7.A attack if a structural argument establishes that the European motivation requirement is constitutive of *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum — that free evaluation of evidence without accountability structure designates arbitrary judicial discretion rather than the rational procedure the reform established as its positive achievement. This would require showing that the reform tradition's positive content was specifically rational-free-evaluation (freedom + accountability), not merely freedom from formal hierarchies. If this holds, identifying Brazilian informal practice (free evaluation without systematic motivation accountability) with the vocabulary's positive designatum requires gap-invisibility about a constitutive absence, not merely about secondary procedural requirements, and the §3.7.A argument fails.

**(i) Italian tradition's articulation state at the Liebman period.** The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence survives the §3.8.A attack if primary-source evidence from Liebman's Brazilian-period writings or USP course records (1940–1946) establishes that Liebman specifically presented *libero convincimento* as freedom from *prova legal* hierarchies without separately articulating the accountability dimension — the *motivazione* counterpart codified in Italian CPC Art. 132 — as a distinct conceptual component of the rational judicial evaluation principle. Such evidence would need to show that Liebman's scholarly engagement at USP did not include Art. 132 *motivazione* as a structural counterpart to Art. 116 *libero convincimento* — a difficult showing given the contemporaneous codification and Liebman's scholarly identity with the tradition that produced it. Without such evidence, the structural argument from the Italian CPC 1940's separate codification establishes a high prior against vocabulary absence at version (i) that the supportive has not overcome.

**(j) Patrimonialism's distinct contribution at the vocabulary-selection level.** The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence survives the §3.8.B attack if the supportive establishes that patrimonial prior formation generates vocabulary non-adoption outcomes that legitimation incentives alone — operating in a non-patrimonial peripheral receiving field with equivalent field pressure and access to the same Italian tradition vocabulary — do not predict. This is the vocabulary-selection-level version of the Portugal/Argentina comparison: not only whether those fields produced the same absorptive orientation, content, and evaluative intensity, but whether their theoretical vocabularies constituted the accountability dimension as a distinct component of the *livre convencimento*-equivalent concept in ways that track the presence or absence of patrimonial formation rather than differences in field pressure. Without comparative evidence at this specific level, the patrimonialism contribution at vocabulary-selection is not independently established against the adversarial's structural alternative.

**(k) Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship's organization under version (i*).** Version (i*) survives §3.8.D's Possibility A/B dilemma if primary-source evidence from the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically — Liebman's works, or contemporaneous Italian doctrine organized around Art. 116's evidentiary evaluation domain — establishes that the evidentiary evaluation debates engaged with the rationality question (what distinguishes rational from arbitrary free evaluation) while treating the accountability dimension as analytically external to the concept's positive designatum, rather than as the answer to this constitutive question.
