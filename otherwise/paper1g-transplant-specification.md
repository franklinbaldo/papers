# The Underspecified Transplant: Why Structural Mechanisms and Peripheral Field Dynamics Already Account for the Direction and Doctrinalization Form of *Livre Convencimento*

---

## 1. Thesis Attacked

"O Livre Convencimento como Doutrinização do Patrimonialismo Judicial" (Baldo, 2026; `paper1G_livre_convencimento_patrimonialismo.md`), as formalized in the supportive paper "The Doctrinalization Mechanism" (`yesindeed/paper1g-doctrinalization-mechanism.md`), advances the following as the thesis's diagnostic contribution:

> "The transplant hypothesis predicts distortion but not its direction; the patrimonialism thesis predicts the specific direction of the distortion." (supportive paper, §3.4, original formulation)

Following adversarial engagement, the supportive paper has reformulated the thesis's surviving contribution as follows:

> "Patrimonialism does not uniquely predict the general direction of informal personal-authority drift (where the structural civilian architecture mechanism is correct and sufficient). Patrimonialism specifically explains the specific FORM of doctrinalization: the absorption of C3's vocabulary as positive legitimization rather than critique, and the subsequent P1-P2 lock-in." (supportive paper §3.4, revised formulation)

The thesis being attacked, in its current strongest form, has two components: (1) the claim that the patrimonial habitus adds directional content over the transplant hypothesis's structural baseline; and (2) the claim that the patrimonial field configuration specifically explains the C3 absorptive direction — why Brazil absorbed the Italian proceduralist tradition as positive validation of existing practice rather than as critique or formal constraint. This paper attacks both components.

---

## 2. Faithful Reconstruction

The strongest current form of Paper 1G's argument combines the original directional claim with its surviving reformulation as a C3 absorptive-direction thesis.

Watson's (1974) transplant hypothesis holds that legal rules lose context in transit; the receiving context's available interpretive resources shape how the transplanted rule is reconstructed; and the outcome differs from the source. The thesis claims patrimonialism adds two elements beyond what the transplant hypothesis alone predicts:

**Element 1 — Direction.** The transplant hypothesis leaves the direction of distortion underdetermined without specifying the receiving context's interpretive resources. The patrimonialism thesis specifies those resources as the patrimonial habitus: a field disposition toward treating judicial authority as personal and formal norms as optional instruments. This habitus predicts a specific directional output — expansion of the personal-liberty dimension of *livre convencimento* (freedom from external constraint on personal judgment) over its rational-engagement dimension (requirement to expose reasoning to intersubjective scrutiny). A receiving context with a patrimonial habitus will naturally expand personal judicial authority; a receiving context without it might instead produce formalism, collectivism, or some other reconstruction.

**Element 2 — Doctrinalization form.** The element now foregrounded as the thesis's distinctive contribution: patrimonialism does not merely predict the direction of drift but explains why that drift was DOCTRINALIZED — named, positively valued, and absorbed into professional formation as orthodoxy. The structural mechanism predicts informal personal-authority expansion; it does not predict that this expansion would acquire a technical name (*livre convencimento motivado*), a principled genealogy framing it as an Enlightenment rationality gain, and a legitimizing apparatus within the academic field that reproduced it through the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra formation tradition.

The diagnostic claim for Element 2 is the C3 absorptive direction: the Italian proceduralist tradition (Liebman) was available across civil law jurisdictions; what varied was how receiving fields absorbed it. France (terse form), Germany (*Begründungspflicht*), and Italy (reform response) absorbed it as constraint or critique of informal practice. Brazil absorbed it as positive validation. The patrimonial field configuration — a habitus organized toward treating authority as personal and norms as optional — is claimed to specifically determine this absorptive direction: toward validation rather than critique.

The thesis is not ad hoc. Faoro and Holanda established the patrimonialism account independently across political, bureaucratic, and economic domains; applying it to the judicial field through Bourdieu's habitus mechanism is standard explanatory extension. The C3 absorptive-direction argument is, on the support's own analysis, the thesis's strongest surviving contribution after conceding directional redundancy over the structural baseline.

Both elements are contested below. Sections 3.1–3.2 contest Element 1 and the historical baseline on which it depends. Section 3.3 shows that the missing comparative evidence applies now to doctrinalization form rather than direction. Section 3.4 contests Element 2: the C3 absorptive direction is predicted by peripheral field dynamics without patrimonialism.

## 3. The Attack

### 3.1 Watson's Hypothesis, Properly Specified, Already Contains a Directionality Mechanism

The directional-specificity claim treats the Watson transplant hypothesis as leaving direction radically underdetermined — a hypothesis that predicts some drift in some direction without specifying which. This is not the hypothesis Watson articulates. Watson's account specifies that the direction of drift depends on the receiving context's "available interpretive resources." The question is what these resources include.

The supportive paper's treatment assigns directional determination to *cultural* resources — the patrimonial habitus — but the transplant hypothesis's "available interpretive resources" are not limited to cultural dispositions. They include the structural features of the receiving legal system itself: the institutional organization of judicial authority, the role-definition of the judge, the operational relationship between the judge and the legal text. These structural features are interpretive resources in exactly Watson's sense.

In the Brazilian case, the most prominent structural feature of the receiving legal system is the civilian judicial authority model. In civilian legal systems, the judge is constituted as the authoritative interpreter of the law — *iura novit curia* (the court knows the law). This structural feature is not a cultural disposition; it is an institutional role-definition. When the *iura novit curia* model operates *without stare decisis enforcement*, the structural consequence is directional: the judge's authority to know the law expands without the precedent constraint that would check it. A judge whose authority is constituted as "authoritative legal interpreter" will, absent external precedent constraints, exercise that authority in its natural direction — toward expansion of personal interpretive latitude.

The transplant hypothesis, properly specified with this structural feature, already predicts the direction of distortion: in any civilian legal system operating without stare decisis enforcement, the transplantation of a "free judicial conviction" principle will expand the personal-authority dimension and contract the rational-engagement dimension. The direction is the natural trajectory of civilian judicial authority unbound by precedent constraint, not the specific output of patrimonial cultural formation.

### 3.2 The Claim That Roman-Law Tradition Predicts Formalism Is Historically Inaccurate

The directional-specificity argument depends on the contrast claim: "a receiving context with a strong Roman-law tradition of systematic rule-application would be expected to produce drift toward formalism, not personal discretion." If this contrast claim is false — if Roman-law tradition does not characteristically predict formalism — then the directional-specificity argument loses its comparative baseline.

The claim is historically inaccurate in two distinct ways.

**First**, Roman legal practice was not characterized by formalist rule-application. Classical Roman law was significantly judge-mediated: the praetor's edict and formulary system gave the magistrate substantial discretion in defining the framework for each dispute; the iudex operated with genuine latitude in factual assessment; the jurists whose opinions constitute the Digest were authoritative interpreters whose views the judge could cite but was not mechanically bound by. The equation of "Roman-law tradition" with "systematic rule-application" producing "formalism" is a reconstruction of a specific period (the late Pandectist tradition of 19th-century Germany) applied backward to a more complex tradition.

**Second**, even the Pandectist tradition did not produce judicial *formalism* in the relevant sense. The Pandectist tradition systematized categories but produced authoritative judicial interpreters, not mechanical rule-appliers. The operational practice of civilian legal systems, examined in the comparative law literature (Dawson, Merryman, Lasser), does not consistently produce judicial formalism. What it produces is the **declaratory theory of judicial decision**: the judge declares the law authoritatively. Under this theory, judicial decisions are not primarily reasoned justifications — they are authoritative declarations by the institutional holder of legal knowledge. The French *arrêt* in its classical form reflects this: brief, laconic, structured from the conclusion rather than toward it. This is not formalism; it is the declaratory theory of judicial authority, under which the judge's personal judgment is authoritative precisely *because* it is the judge's personal judgment.

The declaratory theory is structurally very close to what Paper 1G describes as the Brazilian distortion. The distinction between classical civilian declaratory form and the Brazilian distortion is therefore one of degree within the same structural logic, not one between structural civilian authority and patrimonial cultural deviation. This matters for the directional-specificity claim: if the Brazilian drift is an extension of civilian judicial authority's structural logic, the transplant hypothesis already predicts the direction.

### 3.3 The Comparative Prediction Is Made but Not Tested — and Its Scope Has Shifted

The directional-specificity argument entails a comparative prediction: that legal systems receiving *libre conviction* doctrine without patrimonial cultural substrates would have shown different drift directions. This prediction is stated in Paper 1G and formalized in the supportive paper's §3.4. It is the basis for the claim that patrimonialism adds directional content the transplant hypothesis alone lacks.

The prediction was not tested in Paper 1G. Portugal, Argentina, and Italy are the most informative comparators for a test of the directional claim; none are examined.

The comparative question has since shifted. The support concedes directional redundancy over the structural baseline and redirects: the relevant test is now about *doctrinalization form* rather than drift direction. The claim that requires testing is whether peripheral civilian systems without patrimonial diagnosis absorbed the Italian proceduralist tradition in a different form — as critique or formal constraint rather than positive validation of existing practice. Portugal and Argentina remain the most informative comparators: both received the Liebman tradition, both operated as peripheral importing fields under similar structural conditions (civilian judicial authority + weak stare decisis), and neither is attributed the same Faoro-Holanda patrimonialism diagnosis.

These comparators are not presented as confirming the adversarial prediction for the doctrinalization-form question. What they establish is that the test the C3 absorptive-direction argument requires — peripheral non-patrimonial field vs. peripheral patrimonial field, with the metropolitan-field distinction controlled for — has not been made by either side. The comparison the thesis's current strongest claim requires is specifically within the peripheral-field category, not between peripheral and metropolitan fields. Section 3.4 develops this point.

### 3.4 Peripheral Field Dynamics Explain the C3 Absorptive Direction Without Patrimonialism

The C3 absorptive-direction argument is the support's surviving diagnostic claim after conceding directional redundancy: the patrimonial field configuration specifically explains why Brazil absorbed the Italian proceduralist vocabulary as positive *validation* of existing judicial practice, while France, Germany, and Italy absorbed it as *critique* or formal constraint. The patrimonial habitus — treating authority as personal and norms as optional — is claimed to be the mechanism that organized the receiving field's absorptive direction toward validation.

This claim has a prior structural explanation that does not require invoking patrimonialism: **the position of the receiving legal academic field in the international hierarchy of academic production**.

Watson's "available interpretive resources" include not only the structural organization of judicial authority but the position of the receiving field relative to the source of the vocabulary being imported. A field that is (a) in the process of institutionalization, (b) dependent on metropolitan intellectual authority for internal legitimation, and (c) already operating with informal practices that an arriving metropolitan vocabulary can be made to validate — will absorb that vocabulary as validation. The mechanism is not cultural habitus; it is the structural logic of legitimation in peripheral academic fields.

**The legitimation dynamic.** A peripheral legal academic field imports metropolitan intellectual traditions partly to establish the claim that its practitioners are doing legitimate work. A peripheral field that absorbed Liebman's *processualismo* as a critique of its own judicial practice would be importing a condemnation of its practitioners — undermining the claim to expertise that justifies the field's institutional position. A peripheral field that absorbed the same vocabulary as confirming the rationality of existing practice ("*livre convencimento* is what the Europeans call *libre conviction*; we were already doing rationalized evidentiary procedure") establishes the field's claim to have been operating legitimate procedure all along. This absorptive direction — validation rather than critique — is predicted by the receiving field's need for metropolitan confirmation, not by a specifically patrimonial disposition toward treating authority as personal.

This is not a general claim that peripheral fields absorb all metropolitan vocabularies as validation. It is a structural prediction about the absorptive direction when the arriving vocabulary maps onto existing practices: when a vocabulary can be recruited to validate what practitioners are already doing, a peripheral field will recruit it in that direction because the alternative — absorbing it as a condemnation of existing practice — threatens the field's collective legitimacy claim. The *processualismo* vocabulary explicitly valorized procedural rationality as liberation from the irrationality of legal proof hierarchies; this vocabulary could be absorbed as a critique (you are not yet rationalized) or as a confirmation (you have achieved rationalization). A peripheral field under legitimation pressure will absorb it in the confirming direction.

**The metropolitan/peripheral distinction explains the European comparators — not the absence of patrimonialism.** The support cites France (terse form), Germany (*Begründungspflicht*), and Italy (reform response) as evidence that non-patrimonial fields absorb C3 differently from Brazil. But these are not the relevant comparators for the C3 absorptive-direction argument. France, Germany, and Italy are metropolitan fields: they are the sources, not the importers, of the intellectual tradition in question.

Italy cannot absorb Liebman as foreign validation of existing practice because Liebman was Italian — his work was a product of Italian processualismo, not an external confirmation of it. The supportive paper's §4.1 characterizes the Italy/Brazil comparison as "both academic communities received the same C3 vocabulary from the same source," with the structural enforcement context as the only differentiating variable. This characterization misidentifies the Italian field's relationship to the Liebman tradition. The Italian proceduralist community did not receive C3's vocabulary; it generated it. The *processualismo* movement was reform-critical from its origin — its scholars developed the tradition with the internal purpose of improving Italian civil procedure by establishing rational standards for evidentiary evaluation and judicial reasoning. The Italian field's orientation toward thin-motivation practice as a pathology requiring reform was not an absorptive decision made by a receiving community choosing which dimension of an imported vocabulary to foreground; it was the originating substantive content of the tradition being produced. To say that both communities "received the same vocabulary from the same source" is to treat the Italian field's own intellectual production as if it were an external import equally foreign to both. Liebman was not an external authority for Italian proceduralists — he was one of them.

The peripheral-field dynamics argument operates specifically on the reception end of intellectual transmission: what determines absorptive direction when a peripheral importing field receives a metropolitan vocabulary for the purpose of academic legitimation. This mechanism makes no prediction about, and cannot be tested by, the generating side of intellectual production. Italy's reform-critical orientation toward its own scholarly tradition is not an instance of peripheral-field reception dynamics; it is an instance of a metropolitan field's internal scholarly orientation toward problems it has itself identified. The two phenomena — internal scholarly orientation toward a self-generated tradition (Italy) and absorptive orientation toward an externally-imported vocabulary (Brazil) — are structurally distinct, and the peripheral-field dynamics argument operates only on the second. Using Italy's internal orientation as a test case for how peripheral fields absorb external vocabulary is a category error: Italy was not absorbing C3 externally; it was producing C3 internally.

Germany's *Begründungspflicht* requirements arose through Bismarckian state-building institutional logic — an independent institutional development generated by German administrative constitutionalism, not an absorptive orientation toward an imported vocabulary. France's terse declaratory form reflects the institutional adaptation of the appellate architecture to the Cour de cassation's review jurisdiction — a structural response to cassation review, not an absorptive decision about an external intellectual import. None of the three European comparators the supportive advances is a case of peripheral-field reception. All three are cases of internal metropolitan field development, each with institutional logic specific to its own historical context.

Metropolitan fields have the structural capacity for self-critique because their legitimacy does not depend on external validation. France, Germany, and Italy do not need Italian proceduralism to confirm their procedures are legitimate — they are themselves sources of legitimacy in the international hierarchy of academic production. A peripheral field cannot afford the same critique because its legitimacy claim depends on confirmation from the metropolitan center. The relevant comparison is not "non-patrimonial metropolitan field" vs. "patrimonial peripheral field" — it is "peripheral non-patrimonial field" vs. "peripheral patrimonial field," with the metropolitan-field distinction held constant.

**The relevant comparators are peripheral civilian systems.** Portugal and Argentina are the fields where the peripheral-field dynamics hypothesis predicts similar absorptive outcomes to Brazil, regardless of patrimonialism. Both are peripheral relative to the Italian proceduralist tradition, both are civilian systems with weak stare decisis, and neither is attributed the same Faoro-Holanda patrimonialism diagnosis. If the absorptive direction in Portugal and Argentina was validation — if those fields absorbed Liebman as confirming the rationality of existing practices rather than as critiquing them — then the peripheral-field mechanism explains the absorptive direction without patrimonialism. If Portugal and Argentina produced critique-oriented or formally constraining absorptive outcomes under similar structural and field-position conditions, the adversarial's peripheral-field prediction fails and the patrimonialism account gains in residual explanatory scope. The comparison has not been made by either party.

**P2 (capital-generating function) does not require patrimonialism.** The capital-generating function identifies that mastery of *livre convencimento* discourse positioned certain agents as authoritative interpreters of the field's core practices, generating symbolic capital that structural reform would devalue. This mechanism is Bourdieu's general field theory applied to any doctrinalized academic discourse in any field, not a patrimonialism-specific prediction. The Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra tradition generated capital because it was foundational in Brazilian civil procedure formation — not because the patrimonial habitus specifically organized that capital structure. Any foundational doctrine produces the same capital structure and the same resistance to reform. P2 is a patrimonialism-specific lock-in mechanism only if mastery of *livre convencimento* generated *more* capital, or *more* organized resistance, than any other comparable foundational doctrine would predict from its institutional position alone — and that the excess is attributable to patrimonial cultural substrate rather than to the doctrine's foundational position in formation.

## 4. Anticipated Replies

**Reply 1: The patrimonialism thesis is independently established.** Faoro and Holanda's accounts are not auxiliary hypotheses constructed to explain *livre convencimento*; they describe features of Brazilian state formation with broad empirical support across political, bureaucratic, and economic domains. Applying an independently-grounded general account to the legal field is not ad hoc; it is standard explanatory extension.

*Why this reply does not suffice.* The challenge in §§3.1–3.4 is not that patrimonialism is invented or ad hoc. It is that patrimonialism, though real and independently established, provides no directional or absorptive-form content beyond what the structural mechanism (§3.1) and the peripheral-field dynamic (§3.4) already predict. An account can be independently established AND causally redundant. The direction of personal-authority drift is predicted by civilian judicial architecture; the C3 absorptive direction is predicted by the peripheral-field legitimation dynamic. Whether patrimonialism is independently established is orthogonal to whether it provides explanatory content not already supplied by structurally prior mechanisms.

**Reply 2: The peripheral-field dynamic does not specifically predict validation — the patrimonial habitus is what enables the distorted absorption.** Peripheral fields do not uniformly absorb all metropolitan vocabularies as validation. The absorptive direction depends on whether the vocabulary can be recruited to validate existing practice without distortion. The *processualismo* vocabulary explicitly valorized procedural rationality as liberation from legal proof hierarchies — a vocabulary with reform-oriented content. Absorbing it as validation of existing informal practice required treating its normative demands as optional. The patrimonial habitus is what enables this: a field that treats formal norms as optional instruments absorbs a reforming vocabulary as validation of existing practice precisely because it does not take the vocabulary's normative demands at face value.

*Why this reply does not suffice.* The reply correctly identifies that the vocabulary's reform-oriented content creates an absorptive challenge: absorbing it as validation requires treating its normative demands as optional. But the reply locates the mechanism in the patrimonial habitus — individual practitioners' disposition toward treating formal norms as optional. An alternative mechanism at the collective level does the same work: a peripheral academic field under legitimation pressure has collective structural incentives to absorb reforming vocabulary as validation, not because individual practitioners treat norms as optional, but because admitting the vocabulary condemns existing practice would undermine the collective claim to have been doing legitimate procedure. The individual habitual disposition and the collective legitimation incentive are distinct mechanisms; the peripheral-field dynamic operates at the collective level regardless of whether individual practitioners are individually patrimonially disposed.

The reply may further invoke the Italian case to argue that field orientation toward vocabulary is determined by internal scholarly purposes rather than by reception dynamics — Italian proceduralists generated a reform-critical tradition because their internal scholarly purposes were reform-critical, which shows that substantive scholarly orientation sets absorptive direction regardless of peripheral-field legitimation pressure. This argument supports the adversarial's position rather than the supportive's. What the Italian case establishes is that the originating field's internal scholarly purposes determined that field's orientation toward the tradition it was producing. This is not a case of a receiving field setting absorptive orientation; it is a case of a generating field setting production orientation. The argument confirms that generation and reception are different relationships to intellectual material — exactly the distinction §3.4 requires. What remains unaddressed is how a peripheral non-patrimonial field that received the Italian tradition from outside would have oriented toward it absent patrimonial habitus. Italy's internal orientation, however explained, cannot substitute for that comparison. The Portugal/Argentina comparison is required to address this, and remains the primary surrender condition.

## 5. Scope of the Attack

This attack shows:

**On direction:** The Watson transplant hypothesis, properly specified with the structural features of the receiving legal system (civilian judicial authority model + absent stare decisis), already predicts the direction of *livre convencimento* distortion — personal-conviction expansion — without invoking patrimonialism. The claim that the transplant hypothesis is directionally underdetermined treats Watson's "available interpretive resources" as limited to cultural dispositions rather than including institutional structural features.

**On the historical baseline:** The contrast claim ("Roman-law tradition predicts formalism") on which the directional-specificity argument depends is historically inaccurate. The civilian judicial authority model in its operational form — the declaratory theory — is structurally continuous with the Brazilian distortion rather than directionally opposed to it.

**On the C3 absorptive direction:** The claim that the patrimonial field configuration specifically explains why Brazil absorbed the Italian proceduralist vocabulary as validation (rather than critique) has a prior structural explanation in peripheral field dynamics. A peripheral academic field in the process of institutionalization, dependent on metropolitan validation, operating with informal practices the vocabulary can be recruited to confirm — will absorb the vocabulary as validation through the legitimation dynamic, not through patrimonial habitual disposition. The European comparators (France, Germany, Italy) do not establish the non-patrimonial alternative because they are metropolitan, not peripheral; their absorptive outcomes are explained by institutional mechanisms specific to their field positions, not by the absence of patrimonialism.

**On P2:** The capital-generating function that creates path-dependence lock-in is predicted by Bourdieu's general field theory for any foundational doctrinalized discourse, not specifically by patrimonialism.

**On the Italy test:** The supportive's §4.1 argues that structural enforcement factors (cassation benchmark present in Italy; C2 enforcement deficit in Brazil) explain why deviation was legible in Italy but not why the Italian field oriented toward critique while Brazil oriented toward validation — and that the adversarial's peripheral-field account therefore requires explaining this orientation gap without reference to structural enforcement. The attack here misidentifies what the peripheral-field dynamics argument is contesting. The argument does not claim that structural enforcement explains the orientation; it claims that the legitimation structure explains it. More fundamentally, the Italy test cannot test the peripheral-field dynamics argument at all because Italy is the generating source of the Liebman tradition, not a peripheral receiving field. The adversarial argument requires a comparator that received C3 from outside in the same peripheral-field position as Brazil. Italy does not occupy that position. The Italy test case is neither a confirmation nor a refutation of the peripheral-field dynamics argument; it is a case in the wrong category.

This attack does NOT show:
- That patrimonialism did not exist or was sociologically irrelevant
- That Faoro and Holanda's accounts are incorrect
- That Paper 1G's historical genealogy (§§3–4) is wrong at the factual level
- That the distortion in Brazilian *livre convencimento* did not occur
- That patrimonialism played no role in the process — only that it may not have been causally necessary for the outcomes the thesis claims it explains

## 6. Surrender Conditions

**Condition (a): Peripheral-peripheral comparison.** The directional-specificity and C3 absorptive-direction claims survive if the author shows that peripheral non-metropolitan civilian systems without the patrimonial cultural substrate diagnosis produced *different* absorptive outcomes from Brazil when receiving the Italian proceduralist tradition. Portugal and Argentina are the most informative comparators: peripheral relative to the Italian source, civilian with weak stare decisis, not attributed the Faoro-Holanda patrimonialism diagnosis. If those fields absorbed Liebman and the *processualismo* tradition as critique or formal constraint rather than positive validation of existing judicial practice — producing different doctrinalization forms from Brazil under similar structural and field-position conditions — the peripheral-field mechanism does not predict Brazil's outcome and patrimonialism has residual explanatory scope for the C3 absorptive direction.

The Italy test does not constitute and cannot substitute for this comparison. The Italian field generated the Liebman tradition; it did not receive it from outside. A comparison between a peripheral recipient (Brazil) and a metropolitan generator (Italy) does not hold the generating/receiving distinction constant and cannot test whether peripheral non-patrimonial fields orient differently from peripheral patrimonial ones when receiving C3. Condition (a) requires peripheral-peripheral comparison with the external-reception relationship held constant across the compared cases.

**Condition (b): P2 excess over general field prediction.** The path-dependence lock-in claim (P2) survives specifically as a patrimonialism-attributable mechanism if the author shows that mastery of *livre convencimento* discourse generated capital in excess of, or organized resistance more intensively than, what any comparable foundational doctrine in a comparable peripheral field would predict from its institutional position alone. This would require showing that the capital structure or the resistance to the CPC 2015 reforms was specifically shaped by the patrimonial habitus beyond what the doctrine's foundational formation position predicts for any foundational doctrine in any peripheral field.

**Condition (c): Directional excess over the structural baseline.** The directional claim survives in weakened form if the author shows that the Brazilian drift toward personal judicial discretion was qualitatively or quantitatively different — that there was excess personal-discretion expansion beyond what the civilian-without-stare-decisis baseline predicts. The path-dependence conditions (P1-P2) formalized in the supportive paper are the natural locus for this argument, since they describe mechanisms (formation-integrated transmission, capital-generating function) that would intensify or sustain a distortion beyond what the structural mechanism would produce alone.

Condition (a) is the primary surrender condition. The peripheral-field mechanism predicts that Brazil and Portugal and Argentina would all absorb the Italian proceduralist vocabulary as validation, absent the patrimonial differential. If Portugal and Argentina produced different absorptive forms, the peripheral-field mechanism fails the comparative test; if they produced similar forms, patrimonialism is directionally redundant for C3 absorptive direction as well as for initial drift direction.
