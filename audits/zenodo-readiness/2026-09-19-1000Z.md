## 3. Claims and prior-art synchronization

The umbrella presents two headline dogmatic contributions. Both need to be synchronized with later repository evidence before an archival freeze.

### 3.1 Embargos de declaração

The manuscript currently presents as a contribution both the proposition that declaratory defects are defined by their mode rather than by whether the affected matter is procedural or merits-based, and the proposition that modification of the judgment is a consequence of curing a genuine defect rather than an autonomous subsidiary request.

`audits/prior-art/paper1a-embargos-declaracao-2026-09-18.md` materially changes that boundary. The audit identifies pre-cutoff doctrine and case law for the operative merits-defect proposition and direct prior art for modification as consequence/necessary consequence, including Nery Jr. & Nery, Monteiro Neto, STJ decisions and Talamini. It also shows that the consequence-not-autonomous-request architecture predates CPC 2015.

The umbrella may still contribute a useful synthesis, a specific drafting prescription, and — through the later Paper 1A refinement — the narrower unique-determination boundary between integrative completion and genuinely generative cognition. It should not archive the broader component propositions as autonomous doctrinal discoveries or as innovations introduced by CPC 2015.

### 3.2 Five exits / precedent

The umbrella also presents a five-exit taxonomy and lower-court `superação racional` as a second autonomous contribution.

`audits/prior-art/paper1b-five-exits-precedents-2026-09-18.md` finds direct pre-cutoff Brazilian antecedents for the broad proposition that an inferior judge may, under specified conditions and a heightened argumentative burden, decline application of a binding precedent in a concrete case, and for the distinction between concrete-case nonapplication and formal revision/replacement by the source court. The exact five-item organization and the exact category `aplicação parcial silenciosa` remain plausible narrower synthesis contributions in the bounded searches, but broad Exit-4 firstness does not.

The archival contribution therefore needs to move from “two autonomous new dogmatic propositions” toward a bounded synthesis: a practical drafting/cognition refinement for embargos plus a five-part analytical taxonomy whose components are explicitly credited and whose institutional scope is competence-sensitive.

## 4. Domain review: concrete legal corrections

This gate is `fail` because the current manuscript contains at least three material publication-integrity problems independent of scientific tier or empirical evidence.

### 4.1 Wrong CPC pinpoint for modification after embargos

The paper repeatedly attributes the modification rule to **art. 1.024, §3º**. In the current official compiled CPC, art. 1.024 §3º concerns treating embargos as agravo interno when that is the proper appeal. The rule that, when granting declaratory motions changes the challenged decision, the opposing party may complement or alter an already-filed appeal is **art. 1.024, §4º**.

Primary source checked in this round:

- Lei 13.105/2015, current compiled text, arts. 1.022–1.024: <https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2015/lei/l13105.htm>.

This is a direct statutory-citation error and must be corrected everywhere before freeze.

### 4.2 Exit 4 is too categorical about competence

The manuscript says that an inferior court can `superar` a binding precedent by demonstrating error in its ratio and states that the restriction is “argumentative, not competence-based.” The later Paper 1D audit establishes a necessary correction: art. 489 §1 VI imposes a reason-giving burden but does not, by itself, establish that every reasoned contrary result is legally legitimate across every class of binding precedent. Binding effect, distinguishing, anticipatory overruling or other legally supported nonapplication, reasoned criticism/signaling, and formal revision/cancellation by the competent/source court must be separated.

`audits/prior-art/paper1d-vinculacao-racional-dialogo-institucional-2026-09-18.md` also records that the constitutional/statutory complaint regime preserves real competence rules even while allowing reasoned disagreement to travel institutionally.

The current compiled CPC confirms that art. 927 says judges and courts **shall observe** the listed sources, while §1 requires arts. 10 and 489 §1 to be observed when deciding on that basis. Reason-giving and binding effect therefore coexist; one does not erase the other.

Primary source checked in this round:

- Lei 13.105/2015, current compiled text, arts. 489 §1 V–VI and 927: <https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2015/lei/l13105.htm>.

### 4.3 Exit 5 cannot make binding effect depend on self-invocation

The paper's fifth exit says, in substance, that a court which simply does not invoke a precedent does not enter the space of obligations generated by that precedent: “quem não invoca não entra no espaço das obrigações.” That proposition is too broad for an archival legal paper.

For sources within art. 927's binding baseline, applicability is not created by the deciding court choosing to cite the precedent. A court may of course resolve a case on an independent dispositive issue that makes a purported precedent legally irrelevant; that is an applicability/scope proposition. But silence about an otherwise applicable binding source is not, merely because it is silent, a safe fifth route around the binding rule.

The taxonomy can likely preserve a fifth category if it is reformulated around **legal non-incidence/irrelevance** rather than **non-invocation as such**. The current statement should not be frozen.

## 6. Limitations and archival scope

A dogmatic paper need not become an empirical paper to be Zenodo-ready. Its limitations need to be epistemically honest for the artifact it is.

The current umbrella should add at least these boundaries:

1. the component ED rules are established doctrine/case law; the paper's residual contribution is synthesis/refinement, not discovery of the broad rule;
2. the generic lower-court-supersession controversy is established and contested; the paper's residual contribution is the exact taxonomy/analytical synthesis, not first discovery of the controversy or of case-specific nonapplication;
3. the taxonomy depends on the class of precedent and the competent procedural channel; it is not a single competence-free rule for all art. 927 sources;
4. `aplicação parcial silenciosa` is a useful proposed analytical label only to the extent that the concrete reasoning defect is independently established under the applicable legal source;
5. the Marilene/TJRO example is a worked legal application, not empirical validation of frequency, prevalence or system-wide effects.
