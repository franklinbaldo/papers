## 3. Claims and contribution boundary

The abstract and introduction expressly deny an innovative dogmatic contribution and instead promise descriptive precision sufficient for computational formalization. That is a sensible archival scope, but it raises the accuracy bar: rules used as candidate formal primitives cannot materially misstate the CPC.

There is also a scope mismatch inside §5.3. After discussing fragmented majorities, the manuscript introduces an explicit `quadro de rationes candidatas`, including a procedure for clustering votes and selecting the candidate ratio whose argumentative frame is closest to a later case. The text describes this as an extension adopted by the paper after adversarial critique. That is not merely a neutral restatement of CPC categories; it is at least a proposed interpretive/computational heuristic. Before freeze, the paper should either label, delimit and prior-art-audit it as a proposal, or remove it from a manuscript whose declared contribution is purely descriptive mapping.

## 4. Domain review — material CPC corrections

Primary statutory check used the current compiled text of Lei 13.105/2015 at the Planalto: <https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2015/lei/l13105.htm>.

Material blockers found:

1. **Arts. 322/324.** The paper says art. 322 requires a pedido to be “certo e determinado” and art. 324 requires it to be “expresso, vedada a interpretação extensiva ou restritiva”. The compiled CPC instead states separately that the pedido must be **certo** (art. 322) and **determinado** (art. 324), with statutory exceptions for generic requests. The current mapping should be replaced rather than carried into a formal schema.
2. **Art. 485 II/III.** The manuscript reverses/misdescribes these hypotheses. Art. 485 II concerns a process stopped for more than one year by negligence of the parties. Art. 485 III concerns the author's failure to promote acts/diligences incumbent on the author, abandoning the cause for more than 30 days.
3. **Art. 487 II/III.** The manuscript labels art. 487 II a homologatory decision on prescription/decadence. The compiled CPC makes II a decision on prescription/decadence; the homologatory cases are in III.
4. **Art. 356.** The manuscript lists revelia as if it were a free-standing trigger for partial judgment on the merits. Art. 356 II instead incorporates the immediate-judgment conditions of art. 355; revelia matters only through the conditions stated there.
5. **Arts. 503/504.** §5.2 says necessary grounds of a judgment become res judicata “nos limites do art. 504, II”. The statute says the opposite: reasons do not make res judicata even when important to the dispositif, and factual truth established as a ground does not either. A prejudicial issue can receive the extended treatment of art. 503 §1 only if its express statutory conditions are met.
6. **§5.5 propagation/preclusion.** Claims such as “assertions not challenged in embargos de declaração are accepted” and the suggestion that a contingent assertion may later “transit in res judicata as a ground” are too categorical for the cited basis. Preclusion, devolutive scope, issues actually decided, prejudicial issues, reasons and factual grounds should be separated rather than collapsed into one propagation rule.
7. **Art. 1.013 framing.** The paper should separate the devolutive extent/depth of appeal, matters cognizable ex officio, the mature-case situations of §3, and the distinct prohibition on reformatio in pejus. They are not interchangeable labels for the same exception.

Because the paper's practical purpose is to stop incorrect legal categories from becoming compiling-but-wrong formal axioms, these are publication-integrity blockers rather than cosmetic editorial corrections.

## 6. Adversarial review, evidence and limitations

The manuscript does have meaningful adversarial-quality strengths. It records a prior critique that the earlier treatment of fragmented majorities was incomplete, and §5.4 now expressly states a tractability gap: structural signals can help human formalizers while difficult necessity/status judgments remain unreliable for fully automated classification. That limitation should be preserved.

No empirical result is claimed, so empirical reproducibility is `n-a`. For this paper type, the relevant reproducibility requirement is source-to-rule traceability: a third party should be able to identify the primary/doctrinal source for each legal primitive and see which parts are descriptive law versus proposed computational heuristics.
