## 4. Findings and actionable issues

### 4.1 Paper 1E — Custos Argumentativos e Equilíbrio Institucional

Issue: #581 — labels `zenodo-readiness`, `paper:paper1e-custos-argumentativos`.

The current abstract still presents as the central contribution a broad comparative static in which technological reduction of the cost of producing good legal arguments changes the strategic equilibrium. The dated prior-art audit shows that the generic pieces are occupied: private/social litigation-cost divergence; costly production/evolution of precedent; strategic interaction between higher and lower courts; legal-AI productivity; and, most importantly, a pre-cutoff GenAI/law-and-economics account in Henry A. Thompson's 2024 `AI and the law` linking lower legal/litigation costs to increased court use and faster legal evolution.

The strongest residual is narrower and more interesting: the Brazil-specific C5+C6 conjunction — cheaper **auditable, high-quality** argument production, transmission through the reclamação/precedent architecture, CPC 2015 reason-giving duties and a predicted change in institutional engagement. The paper should state that as a hypothesis, not as an established empirical shift.

A second blocker is legal-domain precision. The paper's model sometimes speaks of lower courts `superando` precedent. The frozen version must distinguish distinction/nonapplication/challenge or signaling from formal overruling competence over an otherwise applicable binding STF source. This is consistent with the competence-sensitive caution already surfaced by the Paper 1D audit rather than a new claim of doctrine invented in this round.

Finally, the manuscript needs the competing channel already identified by its prior-art audit: cheaper drafting can increase noisy or unreliable filing volume and raise screening costs. Filing volume alone cannot validate the proposed mechanism; future evidence must separately observe argument quality/auditability and institutional engagement.

### 4.2 Paper 1F — Reputação como Mecanismo de Coordenação

Issue: #582 — labels `zenodo-readiness`, `paper:paper1f-reputacao-sistema-juridico`.

Paper 1F is closer to a clean conceptual archive than its broad opening suggests because adversarial work has already done useful narrowing. The English abstract now limits the strongest mechanism to textually identifiable compliance violations in high-volume/low-review dockets and explicitly distinguishes a local court-practitioner repeated-observation channel from unestablished network-wide reputational democratization.

The prior-art audit nevertheless occupies the generic components. Legal-service reputation as a substitute for difficult quality verification, prestige/certification effects, incumbent entrenchment under information restrictions, and objective/data-driven quality signals displacing prestige all have pre-cutoff antecedents. Mojon–Mahari–Lera's 2024 data-driven law-firm ranking work is especially close to the broad comparative static `better empirical quality information → less dependence on prestige/reputation → more equal access to quality signals`.

The residual candidate is therefore narrower: direct verification of **argumentative compliance/quality** as a shock to reputational capital inside ongoing legal interactions, plus the bounded local repeated-observation channel. The Portuguese abstract/introduction/conclusion remain broader than the English abstract and need to be synchronized before freeze. The institutional scope conditions — attribution, repeated observability, docket structure, assessor mediation, distribution/rotation and incentives — are part of the claim, not optional caveats.

### 4.3 Paper 1G — Livre Convencimento e Patrimonialismo

Issue: #583 — labels `zenodo-readiness`, `paper:paper1g-livre-convencimento-patrimonialismo`.

Paper 1G has the clearest publication-integrity blocker of this batch. Its present central genealogy can be read as if the later Brazilian processualist/Liebman tradition converted a patrimonial personal discretion into the technical legal principle of `livre convencimento motivado`. The prior-art audit establishes a chronology the manuscript must confront: CPC 1939 art. 118 already codified free conviction in evidence assessment together with a duty to state reasons; the historical source used by the audit dates Liebman's arrival and Brazilian teaching to 1940.

That does not make the whole paper untenable. It changes the causal burden. A post-1940 processualist role may still be argued as interpretation, rationalization, broadening, transmission or legitimation of an already codified principle. A stronger claim that patrimonialism explains the **origin** of the technical legal form would require evidence tying the 1939 codification itself to that substrate or showing a later transformation not adequately explained by the publicist/Italian procedural lineage.

The audit also shows that broad ingredients are occupied: patrimonialism/personalism applied to Brazilian civil procedure, empirical criticism of `livre convencimento`, patrimonial culture linked to weak precedent discipline, the free-conviction/precedent conflict and CPC 2015's anti-solipsist turn. The later narrowed `absorptive form-selection` claim remains the more plausible residual, but it must be separated from generic legal-transplant/reception effects and presented with explicit causal uncertainty.
