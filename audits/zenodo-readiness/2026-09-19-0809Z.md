## 3. Claims and current contribution boundary

The manuscript is already carefully scoped. It explicitly says it reports no new empirical measurements, does not identify one bit with a minimum physical quantum, separates primitive edge existence from response agreement, and treats manifold/Torus structure as a downstream falsifiable hypothesis rather than a graph axiom.

The new claim-specific audit materially improves the evidence base. It finds pre-cutoff antecedents for the generic components: cross-model intervention correspondence, intervention mappings in causal abstraction, multiple contexts/entities under intervention, atomic/binary interventions, the generic fact that heterogeneous systems may respond differently, and ordinary multigraph/path/cycle machinery. Those components should not be presented as isolated novelty.

The narrower conjunction for which the audit did not locate a material pre-cutoff antecedent is the particular ILG layering: whole representation systems as vertices; each admitted shared-intervention token as one primitive multigraph edge; primitive adjacency independent of response agreement; response comparison layered above the edge; paths as intervention histories; learned transports/closure as downstream measurements; and global manifold/Torus structure as a falsifiable hypothesis rather than part of the definition.

This is a bounded negative-search result, not a priority proof. The main manuscript still says that a systematic novelty review “remains necessary”; that sentence is now stale and should be replaced by the audited boundary before archival freeze.

## 5. Adversarial review, evidence and limitations

The adversarial and limitations gates are strong for a position paper. The manuscript explicitly lists failure modes for circular intervention identity, binary refinement, graph coarseness, path-length/information confusion and topology over-interpretation. The new audit adds two especially important controls:

- endpoint-independent intervention-admission calibration, including label-collision and adapter-perturbation negatives;
- capacity-matched/random endpoint nulls and out-of-sample cycle closure, because flexible nonlinear alignment/transport maps can manufacture apparently strong structural agreement.

Those experiments are tracked in #617. They are required before upgrading later empirical claims such as “cycle closure evidences global topology.” They are **not** required merely to archive a manuscript whose current claim is “here is a falsifiable ontology/research programme.” Publication Readiness is therefore not being conflated with empirical confirmation.
