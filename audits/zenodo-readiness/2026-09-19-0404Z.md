## 3. Findings and actionable issues

### 3.1 Semantic Atlas Manifolds

Issue: #591 — labels `zenodo-readiness`, `paper:semantic-atlas-manifolds`.

The manuscript already does several things right for an exploratory archive: it labels its claims as hypotheses/proposed experiments, retains point-space baselines, defines explicit rejection criteria, separates sampled-support spectral observables from measured control/escape cost, and explicitly rejects any identification of a graph/Laplacian spectral gap with a physical mass gap.

The dedicated prior-art audit nevertheless narrows the novelty boundary materially. A broad LLM semantic-manifold hypothesis, concept-specific manifolds, multiscale global/local semantic geometry, controlled manifold dynamics, geometry-aware/geodesic steering, local chart atlases, Procrustes alignment, and graph-Laplacian/diffusion/Fiedler/conductance machinery all have pre-cutoff antecedents. In particular, Zhang & Dong's 2025 Multi-Scale Manifold Alignment is unusually close to the global/local semantic-manifold and alignment layer, and their Dynamic Manifold Evolution Theory is a close antecedent for controlled semantic-manifold dynamics.

The surviving candidate contribution is narrower and compositional: a separately calibrated external SRF/quasar macroscopic frame; concept-specific local chart state nested inside it; explicit inter-manifold versus intra-manifold navigation; separately falsifiable cross-model chart alignment rather than assuming it follows from global calibration; explicit separation of geometry/spectral support from measured control cost; and matched point/direction rejection tests.

Issue #357, which proposes a further gauge/quotient/symmetry-reduction extension, is useful research work but is not made a prerequisite for archiving the present bounded position paper. If that extension lands before freeze, its new claims should be audited before being included in the archived version.

### 3.2 Semantic Observers

Issue: #592 — labels `zenodo-readiness`, `paper:semantic-observers`.

The paper has a strong measurement-oriented contract: observer language is operational rather than ontological; resolution is tied to preregistered structural predicates; Blackwell-style ordering is partial; semantic parallax must beat nuisance explanations; multi-observer reconstruction must beat the best single observer against external structure; and the experimental programme has staged kill criteria.

Its current prior-art map is nevertheless incomplete. The dedicated audit shows that the hyperalignment/shared-response lineage already maps idiosyncratic observers into common representational spaces, separates common from observer-specific structure, and — critically for `semantic parallax` — contains work showing reliable, behaviorally predictive individual residual structure after alignment. Shared/private multi-view latent factorization, topology-aware cross-observer comparison, semantic complementarity/fusion, and cross-subject semantic decoding also predate the paper's public cutoff.

The archival contribution should therefore not rest on the observer metaphor, common-space alignment, shared/private decomposition, aligned residual utility, topology comparison, or the word `parallax` individually. The unresolved candidate is the full registered semantic-observability protocol: local multiscale resolution profiles; directional Blackwell-style garbling/refinement tests; controlled topology-changing resolution failures; nuisance-controlled residual semantic utility; reliability-aware fusion that must outperform the best single observer on external structure; and staged falsification.

### 3.3 Semantic Tokenization Transformers

Issue: #593 — labels `zenodo-readiness`, `paper:semantic-tokenization-transformers`.

STT correctly states that it is a position paper and that its compression/fidelity/downstream quantities are predictions or design targets rather than measurements. That makes empirical absence compatible with a Zenodo preprint in principle.

The concrete integrity blocker is its Related Work boundary. The current text says that prior alternatives do not shift to semantic chunks as the fundamental unit or use pre-trained embeddings for offline codebook construction. The dated audit establishes that Large Concept Models / Quant-LCM was public in 2024 and already models language in a pre-trained sentence-level semantic space, including Residual Vector Quantization and discrete semantic-unit prediction. Ippolito et al. had already modeled story progression over pre-trained sentence embeddings in 2020, while SemToken and H-Net occupy additional pre-cutoff semantic/content-aware compression territory.

The defensible unresolved boundary is therefore the specific reconstruction composition rather than semantic/RVQ language modeling itself: a fixed offline semantic-code stream combined with corpus-grounded representatives/medoids, coherent path selection, overlap stitching, and constrained normalization. The evaluation protocol should make LCM/Quant-LCM first-class controls and ablate the reconstruction stages independently. A position paper can be archived before those experiments run, but it should not imply that occupied central components are the novel architectural departure.
