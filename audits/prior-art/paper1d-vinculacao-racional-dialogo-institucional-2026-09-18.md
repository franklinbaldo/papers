# Paper 1D — vinculação racional, fundamentação simétrica e diálogo institucional prior-art audit — 2026-09-18

> **Status:** first claim-specific temporal prior-art audit of [`paper1D_vinculacao_racional_dialogo_institucional.md`](../../paper1D_vinculacao_racional_dialogo_institucional.md). This audit reconstructs the public date of the claims from repository history, distinguishes legal constraints from scholarly novelty, and records two material doctrinal corrections. It does **not** infer copying, plagiarism, bad faith, patent novelty, or causal dependence from chronology alone.

## 1. Claims audited

For temporal and substantive comparison, the current paper is decomposed into six claims:

- **C1 — form rather than result:** the Brazilian binding-precedent system, including binding súmulas, does not primarily bind the lower court to the result; it binds the *form of departure*. A lower court that departs without reasons commits a procedural defect, while a court that departs with reasons operates within a legitimate channel supplied by the system.
- **C2 — symmetric reasoning duty:** the reasoning duty in art. 489, §1, CPC is symmetric. It burdens the court applying or departing from the precedent and also the STF when it judges a constitutional complaint against that departure; the STF must engage the reasons rather than merely restate authority.
- **C3 — superior-court response to reasoned disagreement:** if the lower court's reasons are persuasive, the STF should distinguish or revisit the binding proposition; if they are not, it should refute them with reasons.
- **C4 — complaint as rational dialogue:** the constitutional complaint is not merely an enforcement/silencing mechanism; it can transmit a concrete disagreement to the court competent to preserve, distinguish or evolve its precedent, thereby creating a rational institutional-dialogue structure.
- **C5 — symmetric burden in application and departure:** art. 489, §1, V–VI requires analytic fit when a precedent is applied and distinction/superation when it is not followed; citing authority is not a substitute for identifying the controlling reasons and their fit to the case.
- **C6 — full synthesis:** reasoned lower-court disagreement + analytic superior-court engagement + a channel for precedent preservation/evolution produces a non-purely-authoritative model of vertical precedent that the paper calls rational binding/institutional dialogue.

## 4. Primary legal baseline: a material constraint on C1 and C3

Before comparing scholarship, the statutory/constitutional substrate has to be separated from novelty.

### 4.1 Binding effect and complaint

**Sources:**

- EC 45/2004 / Constitution art. 103-A: <https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/emendas/emc/emc45.htm>;
- Lei 11.417/2006: <https://www4.planalto.gov.br/legislacao/portal-legis/legislacao-por-assunto-OLD/acesso-a-justica-e-processo-judicial/tribunais-superiores/lei-no-11-417-de-19-de-dezembro-de-2006>.

The constitutional text gives a binding súmula **binding effect** over the lower Judiciary and Administration. Lei 11.417/2006, art. 7, expressly allows a constitutional complaint against a judicial decision that contradicts, denies effect to or improperly applies a binding súmula. If the complaint is granted, the STF may quash the judicial decision and order another decision **with or without** application of the súmula, depending on the case. The same statute separately regulates edition, revision and cancellation of the súmula and preserves the STF's formal competence over those acts.

This is not a `prior_art` classification; it is the legal baseline against which the paper's novelty claim must be valid.

**Material correction 1:** art. 489, §1, VI does not by itself establish that a reasoned lower-court disagreement with an otherwise applicable binding súmula is legitimate merely because reasons were supplied. The legal materials distinguish at least: (a) correct application; (b) distinguishing; (c) a claim that the precedent should be overcome; and (d) actual revision/cancellation by the competent court. Reason-giving is necessary to some forms of departure, but it is not automatically sufficient to convert a contrary result into a lawful one.

**Material correction 2:** the statement that, when persuaded by the lower court, the STF *must revise the súmula* is too strong as a procedural proposition. A complaint can expose the need to distinguish, revisit or evolve the precedent, and Lei 11.417 even permits a new decision without application of the súmula in the case; formal revision/cancellation of the enunciation, however, has its own competence/procedure. The defensible claim is about a duty to engage material arguments and the possibility of triggering or motivating precedent revision, not an automatic duty to formally revise the enunciation inside every complaint.

## 5. Pre-cutoff findings

### 5.1 Mendes (2015): the reasoning duty and binding precedent already operate as a two-way structure

**Work:** Anderson Cortez Mendes, **“Dever de Fundamentação no Novo Código de Processo Civil e os Precedentes Vinculantes.”** *Revista Eletrônica de Direito Processual*, v. 16, 2015.

- first verified journal publication date: **2015-12-04**;
- journal page: <https://www.e-publicacoes.uerj.br/redp/article/view/18434>;
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.12957/redp.2015.18434>.

The paper predates Paper 1D by more than a decade. Its abstract already links art. 489 to the adequate **formation and application** of binding precedents, including ratio decidendi, distinguishing and overruling. In the full text it describes the reasoning duty as acting in two directions and expressly calls it a **“via de mão dupla”**: it matters in precedent formation and in subsequent application by lower courts and by the issuing court itself.

**Compared claims:** C2, C5, C6.

**Classification:** `partial_prior_art` for C2; `prior_art` for the generic two-sided formation/application reasoning burden in C5; `partial_prior_art` for C6.

**Why not full anticipation of C2:** Mendes does not, in the located material, formulate Paper 1D's exact dyad “lower court gives departure reasons → STF, while judging a complaint, owes a symmetric art. 489 response to those reasons.” The broader proposition that precedent reasoning is bidirectional, however, was already explicit.

**Material consequence:** Paper 1D should not ground novelty in the word or intuition of *symmetry* alone. The residual claim has to be the specific **vertical complaint-stage wiring** of the two reasoning burdens.

### 5.2 STF Rcl 24.026/DF (2016): constitutional complaint was already described as an instrument of dialogue with binding precedents

**Work/source:** STF, **Reclamação 24.026/DF**, Min. Dias Toffoli, decision signed **2016-06-01**.

- full decision reproduction: <https://www.jusbrasil.com.br/jurisprudencia/stf/876146876>.

The decision is unusually close to Paper 1D's language. It states that constitutional complaint can emerge as an **“instrumento de promoção do diálogo”** in the Supreme Court between the concrete case and binding precedents. It then distinguishes situations involving erroneous application, case peculiarities preventing adequate application (**distinguishing**) and exceptional need to revisit a precedent because legal or factual circumstances changed, with possible **overruling**. It also emphasizes preserving the STF's last word while avoiding jurisprudential petrification.

The specific complaint in Rcl 24.026 was ultimately not admitted because the ordinary instance had not been exhausted. That limitation matters: the decision treats complaint as an exceptional and procedurally conditioned dialogue channel, not as a general substitute for appeal.

**Compared claims:** C3, C4, C6.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the broad C4 proposition that constitutional complaint can serve as a dialogue channel between a concrete case and binding precedent; `partial_prior_art` for C3 and C6.

**Material consequence:** the sentence “reclamação is not only enforcement but a dialogue channel capable of exposing distinguishing or a reason for precedent evolution” cannot carry originality. The STF itself articulated that architecture in 2016.

### 5.3 Macêdo (2016): anticipatory overruling already occupied the lower-court-signaling space

**Work:** Lucas Buril de Macêdo, **“Transformação, Sinalização e Superação Antecipada e sua Pertinência ao Sistema de Precedentes Brasileiro.”** *Revista de Processo Comparado*, vol. 3, 2016, pp. 89–120.

- verified venue/year: **2016**, vol. 3 (June–November issue);
- bibliographic record: <https://www.sumarios.org/artigo/transforma%C3%A7%C3%A3o-sinaliza%C3%A7%C3%A3o-e-supera%C3%A7%C3%A3o-antecipada-e-sua-pertin%C3%AAncia-ao-sistema-de-precedentes>;
- archived issue copy: <https://www.mpsp.mp.br/portal/page/portal/documentacao_e_divulgacao/doc_biblioteca/bibli_servicos_produtos/bibli_boletim/bibli_bol_2006/RProComp_n.3.06.PDF>.

Macêdo evaluates transformation, signaling and anticipatory overruling for the Brazilian precedent system and concludes that a suitably adjusted anticipatory-overruling technique can be received. This is prior work precisely in the conceptual territory where a lower court does not possess ordinary competence to erase the higher precedent but can react to evidence that the precedent should no longer control.

**Compared claims:** C1, C3, C6.

**Classification:** `partial_prior_art` for C1 and C3; `adjacent_prior_work` for C6.

**Material consequence:** the useful distinction is not “reasoned versus unreasoned departure” simpliciter. Paper 1D needs to distinguish **distinguishing**, **reasoned criticism while still respecting the precedent's competence structure**, **anticipatory overruling under constrained conditions**, and **actual overruling/revision by the competent court**. Prior Brazilian doctrine had already developed the lower-court signaling problem.

### 5.4 REsp 1.698.774/RS (2020): art. 489, §1, VI's distinct burden is tied to binding precedents

**Work/source:** STJ, **REsp 1.698.774/RS**, Third Panel, Min. Nancy Andrighi, decided **2020-09-01**, DJe **2020-09-09**.

- reproduced official holding summary: <https://informativos.trilhante.com.br/julgados/stj-resp-1698774-rs>;
- the holding is repeatedly reproduced in later STJ decisions.

The court held that art. 489, §1, VI — which requires demonstration of distinction or superation when a cited proposition is not followed — applies to binding súmulas/precedents, not merely persuasive precedents.

**Compared claims:** C1, C5.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the generic departure-side argumentative burden in C5; `partial_prior_art` for C1.

**Material consequence:** C5's departure half is not novel; it is a statutory/doctrinal feature already interpreted by the STJ. The paper's possible contribution must lie in how that burden is connected to the reviewing court's own burden and to precedent evolution.

### 5.5 Rcl 36.476/SP (2020): complaint is not a universal merits-control channel for every qualified precedent

**Work/source:** STJ, **Rcl 36.476/SP**, Corte Especial, Min. Nancy Andrighi, decided **2020-02-05**, DJe **2020-03-06**.

- STJ-derived/secondary record of the holding: <https://www.jusbrasil.com.br/jurisprudencia/stj/> (search `Rcl 36.476/SP`);
- later commentary registered by STJ BDJur: <https://consorciobdjur.stj.jus.br/vufind/Record/oai%3Abdjur.stj.jus.br.col_2011_168095%3Aoai%3Alocalhost%3A2011-159212>.

The Corte Especial's majority position became that constitutional complaint to the STJ was not available merely to control the concrete application of a repetitive-recurso precedent. Later STJ decisions repeatedly cite Rcl 36.476 for that proposition.

**Compared claims:** C4, C6.

**Classification:** `adjacent_prior_work` plus a **scope constraint** rather than direct anticipation.

**Material consequence:** Paper 1D must avoid generalizing its dialogue architecture from the *súmula vinculante/STF* setting to all qualified precedents and all complaints. The procedural channel differs by precedent type and court. This actually makes the paper stronger if its thesis is narrowed to the constitutional complaint expressly supplied by art. 103-A, §3 and Lei 11.417/2006.

### 5.6 Caminker (1994): the authority of vertical precedent had long been separated from mechanical correctness

**Work:** Evan H. Caminker, **“Why Must Inferior Courts Obey Superior Court Precedents?”** *Stanford Law Review* 46 (1994), pp. 817–873.

- journal record: <https://www.jstor.org/stable/1229094>;
- author/institutional record: <https://michigan.law.umich.edu/faculty-and-scholarship/our-faculty/evan-h-caminker>.

Caminker is comparative/background rather than Brazilian procedural prior art, but it directly interrogates the normative basis for hierarchical precedent obedience and makes clear that vertical authority itself is a separate theoretical question from whether the lower court thinks the precedent correct.

**Compared claims:** C1, C6.

**Classification:** `adjacent_prior_work`.

**Material consequence:** Paper 1D's strongest contribution is not the general philosophical observation that vertical precedent needs justification beyond “the superior court said so”; that debate is old. The relevant novelty question is the specific Brazilian procedural architecture linking argumentative burdens and complaint review.

## 7. Bounded residual novelty after correction

After the sources above, the defensible residual is narrower than the current abstract suggests.

No pre-cutoff source was located, within this run's searches, that states the following **complete and legally qualified** architecture in one claim:

> for a binding súmula, the lower court's analytically reasoned attempt to distinguish or to expose grounds for revisitation acts as an argumentative **signal**, without itself erasing the STF's hierarchical/formal competence; the constitutional complaint transmits the conflict to the STF; the STF owes reasoned engagement with case-distinguishing or genuinely revision-relevant arguments; and the system thereby separates **lower-court signaling** from **higher-court preservation/distinction/formal revision** rather than treating authority as a substitute for reasons.

This is a **bounded negative search result**, not a claim that Paper 1D was first.

The wording “vinculação de forma, não de resultado” should not itself be treated as the surviving novelty. The stronger and safer residual is an **architecture of argumentative transmission under preserved competence**.

## 8. Recommended epistemic revision of Paper 1D

The audit supports a substantive rewrite before external publication:

1. Replace the binary “form, not result” thesis with a competence-sensitive model: binding effect remains real; art. 489 adds analytic burdens; distinguishing and precedent criticism must be separated from actual overruling/revision.
2. Separate four operations that the current paper occasionally compresses: **application**, **distinguishing**, **anticipatory challenge/signaling**, and **formal overruling/revision/cancellation**.
3. Treat STF Rcl 24.026/DF as direct prior authority for the proposition that complaint can promote dialogue with binding precedents. The contribution should build on that authority, not claim to discover the dialogue function.
4. Recast “if the reasons are convincing, the STF must revise or distinguish the súmula” as: the STF must address materially relevant reasons within the procedural scope of the complaint and may distinguish the case or, where warranted and through the competent mechanism, revisit/revise the precedent.
5. Narrow the institutional thesis to the **STF / binding-súmula** complaint channel unless separate support is supplied for other precedent classes. Rcl 36.476/SP shows that complaint availability is not uniform across all qualified-precedent regimes.
6. The highest-value empirical follow-up is a corpus of STF complaints in which the lower court expressly distinguished or challenged a binding súmula, coding whether the STF (a) ignored the argument, (b) engaged and rejected it, (c) distinguished, or (d) treated it as a reason to revisit the precedent. That would test the claimed *symmetry in practice* rather than only its normative plausibility.

## 10. Audit conclusion

This run materially changes the originality and doctrinal assessment of Paper 1D.

The broad **dialogue** claim is not novel: STF Rcl 24.026/DF used essentially that framing in 2016. The generic **two-way reasoning** claim is also substantially prefigured by Mendes (2015), and the apply/depart argumentative burdens are statutory/doctrinal prior art. Brazilian doctrine on **anticipatory overruling** already occupied the lower-court-signaling space.

More importantly, two formulations in the current paper are too strong independently of priority: reason-giving alone is not shown to convert every departure from a binding súmula into a legitimate result, and a persuasive lower-court argument does not automatically create a duty to formally revise the súmula inside the complaint.

The paper's more defensible contribution is therefore an integrated, competence-sensitive thesis: **lower-court argumentative signal → procedurally available complaint → reasoned apex-court engagement → preservation/distinction/possible formal evolution of the precedent**, with the lower court's signal carefully separated from the STF's power to revise or cancel the binding enunciation.
