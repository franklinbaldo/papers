# Paper 1G — livre convencimento, patrimonialismo e precedentes prior-art audit — 2026-09-18

> **Status:** first claim-specific temporal prior-art audit of [`paper1G_livre_convencimento_patrimonialismo.md`](../../paper1G_livre_convencimento_patrimonialismo.md). This audit reconstructs separate public cutoffs for the original broad genealogy and for the later narrowed “absorptive form-selection” claim. It distinguishes component novelty from the full synthesis and temporal overlap from causal dependence. It does **not** establish exhaustive novelty, copying, plagiarism, bad faith, or patent novelty.

## 1. Claims audited

The paper has changed materially through its adversarial history. For temporal purposes, the current argument is best decomposed into six claims:

- **C1 — patrimonial/personalist substrate and precedent rationality:** Brazilian personalism/patrimonialism helps explain irrationality, unpredictability, unequal adjudication, weak impersonality and resistance to precedent discipline;
- **C2 — doctrinalization through `livre convencimento`:** personal judicial discretion is converted into a technically legitimate procedural principle, with doctrine and weak enforcement helping normalize the practice rather than expose it as a defect;
- **C3 — absorptive form-selection:** after later concessions on direction, validation orientation and content selection, the claimed patrimonialism-specific residual is why the receiving field selected retrospective validation of existing practice — form (i) — rather than prospective normative alignment — form (ii);
- **C4 — cultural persistence after legal reform:** CPC 2015 can alter formal doctrine and coercive rules without immediately changing the judicial/professional dispositions formed under the older regime; surface compliance can coexist with persistence of the older decision culture;
- **C5 — CPC 2015 / anti-solipsist transition:** removal of `livremente`, stronger analytical-reasoning duties and the precedent system constrain the older free-conviction/solipsist model;
- **C6 — full Paper 1G synthesis:** patrimonial formation + weak enforcement + technical legitimation of free conviction + institutional path dependence/habitus + later precedent-oriented reform jointly explain the historical trajectory.

The paper's four-phase periodization is treated as part of C6, not as an independent novelty claim.

## 4. Pre-cutoff findings for the broad genealogy

### 4.1 Marinoni & Becker (2003): personal relations/patrimonial culture already applied directly to Brazilian civil procedure

**Work:** Luiz Guilherme Marinoni; Laércio A. Becker, **“A influência das relações pessoais sobre a advocacia e o processo civil brasileiros.”**

- presented at the **XX World Congress of Procedural Law, Mexico City, 2003**;
- later online publication/reproduction: Jus Navigandi, 2004;
- bibliographic confirmation in Marinoni's own later references: <https://www.jusbrasil.com.br/doutrina/secao/referencias-bibliograficas-a-etica-dos-precedentes-justificativa-do-novo-cpc/6849858894>.

The paper explicitly imports the Brazilian literature on patrimonialism/personal relations into civil procedure and legal practice. Its mechanism is broader than Paper 1G: relational networks, personalism, unequal access and deformation of formal/rational bureaucracy. It does not, in the material located here, identify `livre convencimento motivado` itself as the doctrinal carrier of that substrate.

**Compared claims:** C1, C6.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for C1 at the generic civil-procedure mechanism level; `partial_prior_art` for C6.

**Material consequence:** Paper 1G cannot present the move “Brazilian patrimonial/personalist culture → characteristic dysfunctions of Brazilian civil procedure” as novel. Any distinct contribution must be narrower than that move.

### 4.2 Mendes (2012): `livre convencimento` already studied empirically as a mechanism reproducing unequal justice

**Work:** Regina Lucia Teixeira Mendes, **“Verdade real e livre convencimento: O processo decisório judicial brasileiro visto de uma perspectiva empírica.”** *Dilemas*, v. 5, n. 3.

- first publication: **2012-07-01**;
- <https://revistas.ufrj.br/index.php/dilemas/article/view/7400>.

Mendes analyzes legal discourse, doctrinal discourse and judges' representations of `livre convencimento motivado`, including how the principle is actualized in practice and how it influences the reproduction of unequal distribution of justice.

**Compared claims:** C2, C4, C6.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the generic proposition that `livre convencimento motivado`, as a socially actualized judicial practice, contributes to unequal adjudication; `partial_prior_art` for the complete patrimonial genealogy.

This is substantially closer to Paper 1G than a merely doctrinal criticism of judicial discretion because it already treats the principle as an institutional/social practice with distributive consequences.

### 4.3 Marinoni (2014): patrimonialism/personalism already linked directly to unpredictability and weak precedent culture

**Work:** Luiz Guilherme Marinoni, **“Culture, Predictability and Unity of the Law Through the Rule of Precedent.”** SSRN.

- written 2014-08-15; public SSRN posting **2014-08-17**;
- <https://ssrn.com/abstract=2482965>.

The abstract explicitly connects the irrationality/unpredictability of Brazilian law and lack of respect for Supreme Court precedents to Brazilian cultural formation characterized by personalism and patrimonialism. Marinoni's **`A Ética dos Precedentes: Justificativa do Novo CPC`**, first published by Revista dos Tribunais in **2014**, develops the same cultural thesis at book length.

**Compared claims:** C1, C4, C6.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for C1 and for the broad cultural explanation of resistance to an impersonal precedent order; `partial_prior_art` for C6.

**Material consequence:** the broad conjunction “personalism/patrimonialism → irrationality/unpredictability/inequality + weak precedent discipline” was public at least twelve years before Paper 1G. The current Paper 1G bibliography cites Marinoni on precedents but does not cite this 2014 cultural argument.

### 4.4 Nascimento (2023): the free-conviction/precedent conflict was already formulated directly

**Work:** Elisa de Miranda do Nascimento, **“O princípio do livre convencimento motivado sob o enfoque da observância dos precedentes judiciais instituídos pelo Código de Processo Civil.”** *Revista da ESMESC* 30(36), 230–254.

- published **2023-12-14**;
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.14295/revistadaesmesc.v30i36.p230>.

Nascimento asks directly how `livre convencimento motivado` survives in a binding-precedent regime. Her conclusion restricts judicial freedom to evidence evaluation and to technically applying, distinguishing or overcoming precedent; divergent outcomes for materially similar cases are incompatible with the effectiveness of the precedent system.

**Compared claims:** C4, C5.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the doctrinal conflict between free conviction and precedent binding and for the post-CPC narrowing of legitimate judicial freedom; `adjacent_prior_work` to the habitus/cultural-persistence mechanism.

### 4.5 Streck (2015): CPC 2015 as the end of `livre convencimento` and anti-solipsist turn is occupied

**Work:** Lenio Luiz Streck, **“O novo Código do Processo Civil (CPC) e as inovações hermenêuticas: o fim do livre convencimento e a adoção do integracionismo dworkiniano.”** 2015.

The paper and Streck's surrounding work explicitly frame CPC 2015 as repudiating free-conviction/solipsist decision-making and requiring publicly justifiable, non-personalist reasoning.

**Compared claims:** C5.

## 5. A chronology problem material to C2: CPC 1939 precedes Liebman's Brazilian school

This is the most important historical correction found in this run.

### 5.1 The technical legal form already existed in the CPC of 1939

**Primary source:** Brazil, Decreto-Lei nº 1.608, **18 September 1939**, Código de Processo Civil.

The original **art. 118** already stated that the judge would **form his conviction freely in the assessment of evidence**, and its sole paragraph required the decision to indicate the facts/circumstances and grounds supporting that conviction. Primary text: <https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/declei/1930-1939/decreto-lei-1608-18-setembro-1939-411638-publicacaooriginal-1-pe.html>.

This means the national legal coupling `free conviction + stated grounds` was publicly codified in 1939 — decades before CPC 1973 art. 131 and before the later Brazilian processualist school ordinarily associated with Liebman's teaching.

### 5.2 Liebman arrived in Brazil in 1940

**Historical source:** Ada Pellegrini Grinover, **“O magistério de Enrico Tullio Liebman no Brasil.”** *Revista da Faculdade de Direito, Universidade de São Paulo*, 1986.

Grinover records that Liebman came to Brazil in **1940**, in the immediate context of the newly enacted 1939 national procedural code. Source: <https://revistas.usp.br/rfdusp/article/view/67076>.

### 5.3 Classification and consequence

**Compared claim:** C2, C6.

**Classification:** CPC 1939 art. 118 is `prior_art` for the legal institutionalization of the technical free-conviction-plus-motivation form; the 1939 publicist/strong-judge reform rationale is `partial_prior_art` and an **alternative historical mechanism** for C2.

This does **not** show that Liebman and the later Brazilian processualist tradition were irrelevant. It does show that a causal reading in which that school **created the technical legal form** from a pre-doctrinal patrimonial disposition cannot be sustained: the form is already in the 1939 Code before Liebman's Brazilian teaching begins. The defensible later claim is narrower — that the post-1940 processualist tradition may have **interpreted, rationalized, broadened, transmitted or legitimated** an already codified principle in a specifically Brazilian way.

The causal burden therefore changes. To preserve a distinct patrimonial mechanism at the origin of C2, the paper would need evidence that the 1939 codification/rationale itself was shaped by the claimed patrimonial substrate, or that later doctrinal use transformed the meaning of art. 118/its successor in a way not explained by the publicist/Italian procedural lineage alone.

**Work:** Eduardo Cornelius, **“Discursive mismatch and globalization by stealth: The fight against corruption in the Brazilian legal field.”** *Law & Society Review*, version of record online **2023-09-22**.

## 9. Epistemic revision recorded by this audit

This run changes the strongest defensible novelty boundary of Paper 1G in three ways.

1. **The broad cultural thesis is not new.** Brazilian patrimonialism/personalism had already been applied directly to civil procedure by 2003 and to precedent irrationality/unpredictability by 2014.
2. **The doctrinalization chronology needs a hard constraint.** `Livre convencimento + motivation` was already codified nationally in CPC 1939 art. 118 before Liebman arrived in Brazil in 1940. The later Liebman/processualist lineage may still explain interpretation, transmission, broadening or legitimation, but not the first technical legal institutionalization of the principle.
3. **The currently surviving C3 claim remains the most specific unresolved candidate**, but it sits inside a well-developed prior literature on local transformation and selective reception of legal transplants. Evidence of exact historical form-selection — preferably primary Brazilian and comparative reception sources — is therefore necessary before treating patrimonialism as the differential cause.

The appropriate future empirical/historical test is comparative and chronological: reconstruct the 1939 rationale and the pre-/post-1940 doctrinal vocabulary, then compare Brazilian reception with similarly peripheral civilian receiving fields. If the retrospective-validation form appears without the proposed patrimonial conditions, or if the 1939/publicist source already determines the form, C3 loses its differential explanatory force.
