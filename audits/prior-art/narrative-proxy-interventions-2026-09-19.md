## 2. Claims under audit and explicit falsifiers

### C1 — Substrate-native proxy interventions can instantiate a shared causal distinction

**Claim.** A text system and a MaleCNS-derived dynamical system need not receive the same physical object or vector; a prespecified intervention concept may be realized natively in each substrate and then compared experimentally.

**Would falsify or materially weaken it:** independent experts cannot reliably judge the two realizations as manipulating the same causal distinction; equally defensible alternative proxy registries give incompatible conclusions; arbitrary matched proxy locations perform as well as biologically justified ones; or registry choices must be tuned after observing cross-space alignment.

### C2 — The relevant primitive is intervention-in-history, and story boundaries add identifying information

**Claim.** Sequential intervention effects depend on preceding history; explicit initial/terminal conditions should constrain alignment beyond generic extra context.

**Would falsify or materially weaken it:** event order/history has no incremental predictive value after matched-content controls; boundary removal performs like matched interior deletion; or the apparent boundary advantage is fully explained by giving the aligner special endpoint anchors rather than by narrative content.

### C3 — Cross-substrate response geometry can support alignment without direct coordinate matching

**Claim.** Within-space relational structure among intervention responses can align heterogeneous spaces even when their coordinates and dimensions are incomparable.

**Would falsify or materially weaken it:** time-only, generic Gromov/relational alignment, or simple paired baselines match performance; the result is unstable across within-space metrics; shuffled/random response fields align similarly; or high response-geometry agreement fails to predict any held-out correspondence.

### C4 — Monotone narrative transport adds a lawful temporal constraint

**Claim.** A monotone warp may absorb different local rates while preserving causal order.

**Would falsify or materially weaken it:** unconstrained or time-only baselines perform identically; warp capacity can memorize arbitrary pairings; causal-order permutations remain aligned; or the result depends on a narrow warp parameterization without held-out stability.

### C5 — Hidden-correspondence held-out prediction is evidence beyond label matching

**Claim.** If concept identities are hidden from the aligner, learned response structure should recover held-out event neighborhoods/correspondences beyond time and chance.

**Would falsify or materially weaken it:** unsupervised generic structural alignment already obtains the same result without intervention semantics; concept/time leakage remains in features or generation; performance collapses under disjoint stories/vocabularies; or held-out success is not above permutation and capacity-matched nulls.

### C6 — Cumulative-prefix embeddings carry useful history beyond isolated-event embeddings

**Claim.** If narrative history matters, cumulative prefixes should improve cross-substrate correspondence.

**Would falsify or materially weaken it:** the advantage vanishes when lexical overlap, prefix length, participant overlap, or smoothness is matched; shuffled-order prefixes with the same token overlap perform similarly; or incremental response vectors explain the result without any history-specific information.

### C7 — Semantically/anatomically justified MaleCNS proxies should outperform arbitrary matched inputs

**Claim.** Correct native realization matters, not only generic recurrent perturbation.

**Would falsify or materially weaken it:** arbitrary size/magnitude-matched populations perform equally well; results are highly sensitive to one hand-selected proxy registry; or multiple biologically plausible proxy choices fail to agree.

### C8 — MaleCNS topology contributes only if it beats matched recurrent nulls

**Claim.** A topology-specific result requires biological MaleCNS wiring to outperform degree-preserving rewires and matched random recurrent controls.

**Would falsify it:** the matched nulls reproduce the effect. The current paper already treats that outcome as a legitimate negative result rather than redefining success.

## 4. Pre-cutoff novelty findings

### 4.1 Gromov Dynamic Time Warping already aligns ordered trajectories in incomparable spaces from intra-relational geometry

**Source:** Samuel Cohen, Giulia Luise, Alexander Terenin, Brandon Amos, Marc Peter Deisenroth, *Aligning Time Series on Incomparable Spaces*. arXiv v1 **2020-06-22**; AISTATS 2021, PMLR 130.  
Primary: <https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/cohen21a.html>  
Preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12648>

GDTW explicitly starts from the case where two time series live in spaces with no sensible cross-space ground metric and aligns them by comparing **intra-relational geometry** while performing temporal alignment.

**Compared claims:** C3, C4, C5.  
**Priority classification:** `prior_art` for the generic methodological claim "temporally align trajectories in incomparable spaces using intra-space relational geometry"; `partial_prior_art` for the full Narrative Proxy Interventions conjunction.  
**Overlap:** high on relational geometry + temporal alignment; low on causal interventions, native proxies, MaleCNS, story semantics, and the proposed held-out intervention protocol.  
**Required novelty correction:** the paper must not treat the combination of relational geometry and monotone temporal alignment across incomparable spaces as novel. The narrower contribution, if supported, must come from the intervention/proxy/history/held-out experimental composition.

### 4.2 CTW and GTW occupy multimodal monotone spatio-temporal alignment well before the cutoff

**Sources:**

- Feng Zhou & Fernando De la Torre, *Canonical Time Warping for Alignment of Human Behavior*, NeurIPS 2009. <https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2009/hash/2ca65f58e35d9ad45bf7f3ae5cfd08f1-Abstract.html>
- Feng Zhou & Fernando De la Torre, *Generalized Time Warping for Multi-Modal Alignment of Human Motion*, CVPR 2012, DOI `10.1109/CVPR.2012.6247812`. <https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6247812/>

CTW combines learned spatial alignment with DTW; GTW explicitly addresses multi-modal sequences and flexible monotone time warping.

**Compared claim:** C4, with partial relevance to C3.  
**Priority classification:** `prior_art` for multimodal temporal/monotone alignment; `adjacent_prior_work` for intervention-response geometry.  
**Required novelty correction:** narrative time may still be a useful experimental variable, but monotone cross-modal time warping itself is established.

### 4.3 Unsupervised Gromov–Wasserstein human–LLM alignment already matches heterogeneous similarity structures without labels

**Source:** Genji Kawakita, Ariel Zeleznikow-Johnston, Naotsugu Tsuchiya, Masafumi Oizumi, *Gromov–Wasserstein unsupervised alignment reveals structural correspondences between the color similarity structures of humans and large language models*. arXiv v1 **2023-08-08**; *Scientific Reports* 14, 15917 (2024), DOI `10.1038/s41598-024-65604-1`.  
Primary: <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-65604-1>  
Preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04381>

The method aligns human perceptual and GPT similarity structures using only internal pairwise dissimilarities, without predefined label correspondences during alignment.

**Compared claims:** C3, C5.  
**Priority classification:** `partial_prior_art`.  
**Overlap:** high on unlabeled relational structural alignment across radically different representational systems; low on interventions, temporal order, causal stories and MaleCNS.  
**Novelty implication:** hidden labels plus relational geometry alone are not a unique methodological contribution; the interventional/history structure must carry additional predictive value.

### 4.4 Causal abstraction already requires explicit mappings between interventions across levels/models

**Source:** Sander Beckers & Joseph Y. Halpern, *Abstracting Causal Models*, AAAI 2019, DOI `10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33012678`, published **2019-07-17**. <https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/4117>

The causal-abstraction literature treats allowed interventions and their mappings as part of the abstraction contract rather than assuming that similarly named manipulations are automatically equivalent.

Related pre-cutoff work includes interventional causal representation learning (Ahuja et al., ICML 2023), where interventional data support identifiability only under explicit assumptions: <https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/ahuja23a.html>.

**Compared claims:** C1, C7.  
**Priority classification:** `partial_prior_art` for the generic intervention-mapping idea; `adjacent_prior_work` for the exact text↔MaleCNS proxy construction.  
**Novelty implication:** "same abstract intervention, different low-level realization" is not novel in isolation. The paper's contribution must be the concrete heterogeneous-substrate protocol and its tests, not the existence of intervention maps as such.

### 4.5 Narrative/event boundaries are an established object in cognitive and neural studies

**Sources:**

- Jeffrey M. Zacks & Khena M. Swallow, *Event Segmentation*, *Current Directions in Psychological Science* 16(2), first published **April 2007**, DOI `10.1111/j.1467-8721.2007.00480.x`. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8721.2007.00480.x>
- Nicole K. Speer, Jeffrey M. Zacks, Jeremy R. Reynolds, *Human Brain Activity Time-Locked to Narrative Event Boundaries*, *Psychological Science* 18(5), first published **May 2007**, DOI `10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01920.x`. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01920.x>

These works establish that event segmentation and narrative boundaries have cognitive/neural consequences.

**Compared claim:** C2.  
**Priority classification:** `adjacent_prior_work`; it does not anticipate cross-substrate Pontifex alignment.  
**Novelty implication:** the generic proposition "boundaries matter" is old; the falsifiable claim here must remain specifically about incremental cross-substrate alignment information under matched-content and matched-anchor controls.

## 5. Contrary evidence and boundary conditions

### 5.1 Flexible alignment can manufacture apparently perfect causal correspondences

**Source:** Denis Sutter, Julian Minder, Thomas Hofmann, Tiago Pimentel, *The Non-Linear Representation Dilemma: Is Causal Abstraction Enough for Mechanistic Interpretability?* arXiv v1 **2025-07-11**; NeurIPS 2025.  
Primary: <https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/dbb98528c9870377f3f0d133aae6050b-Abstract-Conference.html>  
Preprint: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08802>

The authors prove that sufficiently powerful alignment maps can make causal abstraction vacuous under broad conditions and empirically obtain **100% interchange-intervention accuracy on randomly initialized language models incapable of the target task** in one setting.

**Compared claims:** C3, C4, C5; interpretation of any future positive result.  
**Truth-status classification:** `contrary_evidence`.  
**Strength:** **strong**.  
**Target:** mechanism / interpretation / map identifiability, not the mere possibility of predictive alignment.  
**Required action:** `narrow_claim` + `add_control` + `downgrade_confidence`.

A successful alignment cannot, by itself, establish shared causal mechanism or shared latent ontology. The confirmatory protocol should preregister map/warp capacity and require performance against capacity-matched null systems, including random/untrained observers and response-structure shuffles, preferably with disjoint story families or vocabularies at test time.

### 5.2 High representational alignment does not imply similar encoding organization or computation

**Source:** Johannes Bertram, Luciano Dyballa, T. Anderson Keller, Savik Kinger, Steven W. Zucker, *Decoding Alignment without Encoding Alignment: A critique of similarity analysis in neuroscience*, arXiv v1 **2026-05-07**, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05907>.

The paper shows that high alignment can arise from small non-representative neural subpopulations and can remain high while encoding topology is causally manipulated. It explicitly concludes that decoding/representational alignment does not imply similarity in function or computation.

**Compared claims:** C3, C5, and the final causal interpretation.  
**Truth-status classification:** `contrary_evidence`.  
**Strength:** **strong** for the inference from alignment to common computation; **moderate** for the narrower held-out prediction task.  
**Target:** mechanistic interpretation/generalization.  
**Required action:** `narrow_claim` + `add_control`.

A positive Pontifex result should therefore be described first as **predictive structural correspondence under the declared interventions**. A stronger statement about shared causal computation requires additional evidence about encoding organization and intervention semantics.

### 5.3 Proxy semantics are a construct-validity problem, not merely a registry-format problem

C1 assumes that independently declared text and fly manipulations instantiate the same intervention concept. The causal-abstraction literature above shows why intervention mappings are part of the theorem/contract rather than a free label.

A single expert-authored registry can still create a hidden semantic bridge: if the experimenter chooses both realizations to make the same conceptual story plausible, alignment can reflect registry construction rather than a substrate-discovered equivalence.

**Truth-status classification:** `boundary_condition`.  
**Strength:** **strong**.  
**Target:** C1/C7 construct validity.  
**Required action:** `add_control` + `narrow_claim`.

Recommended confirmatory controls:

1. multiple independently designed proxy realizations for the same concept;
2. blinded expert ratings of intervention equivalence before response data are inspected;
3. negative proxy pairs that share a label but manipulate different mechanisms;
4. arbitrary size/magnitude-matched neural targets;
5. preregistered exclusion of any renderer tuning on cross-space alignment scores.

If independently plausible proxy registries yield incompatible conclusions, the shared-intervention claim must be narrowed to the particular renderer rather than attributed to the concept.

### 5.4 The proposed boundary advantage is confounded by endpoint-anchor advantage

The current strongest regimes tell Pontifex which observations are the beginning and end. Therefore deleting boundary **content** can also alter the quality of the very anchors used by the monotone map. A positive P2 could be an alignment-constraint effect rather than evidence that beginnings/endings carry special causal-story information.

**Truth-status classification:** `boundary_condition`.  
**Strength:** **strong**.  
**Target:** C2 / P2 mechanism.  
**Required action:** `add_control` + `narrow_claim`.

Discriminating controls should separate content from anchor status:

- preserve abstract endpoint anchors while removing boundary narrative content;
- retain boundary content but hide which points are endpoints;
- reveal matched interior or pseudo-boundary anchors;
- shift endpoint labels while preserving content.

Only an incremental effect of boundary **content** after matched anchor information supports the stronger narrative-boundary mechanism.

### 5.5 Cumulative prefixes are mechanically more similar even without useful narrative memory

**Source:** Dmitry Nikolaev & Sebastian Padó, *Representation biases in sentence transformers*, EACL 2023. <https://aclanthology.org/2023.eacl-main.268/>

The study finds strong nominal-participant-set bias in sentence-transformer cosine similarity: overlap in noun participants can dominate predicate/thematic-role differences.

The paper's cumulative prefixes are also nested by construction: `P_k` literally contains most of `P_{k-1}`. Smoother trajectories and apparently better temporal correspondence can therefore arise from shared tokens/participants and length, not accumulated causal history.

**Compared claim:** C6 / P4.  
**Truth-status classification:** `boundary_condition`.  
**Strength:** **moderate-to-strong**.  
**Target:** proposed history mechanism.  
**Required action:** `add_control` + `downgrade_confidence` until tested.

Required controls should include token/participant-overlap matched sequences, shuffled-order cumulative prefixes preserving most lexical overlap, fixed-window histories, and incremental response vectors such as `E(P_k)-E(P_{k-1})`. If the prefix advantage disappears under those controls, P4 should be reinterpreted as representation-overlap smoothness rather than narrative memory.

### 5.6 Generic GDTW/GWOT are stronger baselines than time-only DTW

Because GDTW already performs relational temporal alignment in incomparable spaces and GWOT already performs unlabeled structural matching, a time-only baseline is necessary but no longer sufficient as the strongest conventional control.

**Truth-status classification:** `boundary_condition`.  
**Strength:** **strong** for C3/C5 comparative advantage.  
**Target:** advantage over alternatives.  
**Required action:** `add_control`.

The baseline ladder should include at least:

1. time-only monotone matcher;
2. CTW/GTW-style multimodal temporal alignment where applicable;
3. GDTW on the same trajectory representations;
4. static/temporally stratified GWOT on response dissimilarity matrices;
5. direct paired translator;
6. Pontifex interventional response-geometry method.

If Pontifex does not outperform or add held-out generalization beyond GDTW/GWOT under the same information budget, the novelty and mechanism claims must narrow even if the method remains a useful application.

## 7. Priority synthesis

The search materially narrows the novelty boundary:

- **not novel in isolation:** multimodal monotone temporal alignment; alignment of time series in incomparable spaces; intra-relational/Gromov geometry for cross-space matching; unlabeled structural alignment between humans and LLMs; explicit intervention mappings in causal abstraction; the general importance of narrative/event boundaries;
- **not located as an earlier full conjunction after the bounded searches:** a preregistered ordered causal story whose concepts are realized through text-native and MaleCNS-native proxies, with hidden internal correspondences, interventional response-field geometry, monotone history, held-out intervention prediction, proxy-location nulls, and MaleCNS topology nulls in one protocol.

The defensible language is therefore:

> No pre-cutoff work was located in the searches above that contains the full Narrative Proxy Interventions experimental conjunction. Several central methodological components are established prior art, especially GDTW/GWOT-style relational alignment and multimodal time warping. Any novelty claim should attach to the specific experimental composition and its discriminating controls, not to relational geometry, hidden-label structural alignment, intervention mappings, or monotone temporal alignment individually.

This is a bounded search result, not a claim of being first.

## 9. Bottom line

**Priority:** the full text↔MaleCNS ordered-proxy-intervention protocol was not located in earlier literature in this bounded round, but several of its most important algorithmic components are already occupied. The strongest novelty boundary is the **specific experimental composition and falsification ladder**.

**Validity:** the central risk is not that the protocol cannot produce an alignment. The stronger risk is that it can produce an alignment for reasons weaker than the intended causal interpretation: generic Gromov structure, temporal constraints, renderer choices, endpoint anchors, lexical overlap, or excessive map capacity. Those alternatives are testable and should be promoted to first-class controls.

**Current action:** `narrow_claim + add_control + downgrade_confidence` for mechanistic interpretation; no empirical claim is retracted because the paper currently reports no new empirical result.
