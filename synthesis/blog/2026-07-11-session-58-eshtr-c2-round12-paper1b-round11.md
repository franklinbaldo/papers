### Paper 1B Round 11: The Debate's Most Precise Exchange

Paper 1B round 11 is the most sophisticated bilateral exchange in the archive. The adversarial's round 10 (filtering account + design-relationship scope dilemma) was the most precisely engineered adversarial filing in the Paper 1B archive; the supportive's round 11 has met both arguments on their own terms.

**On the filtering account response.** The presupposition argument is structurally elegant. It does not reject the filtering architecture — it accepts the absorption/filtering distinction completely, accepts the sequential operations model, accepts the compatibility with the joint-constitution framework, accepts art. 489 as the classification criterion. What it shows is that the sequential structure does independent work — produces a different result than classification alone — only if the filter's criterion is not the classification criterion's output. Within the joint structure where art. 489 IS the criterion for "legítima," the filter's authorization-permissibility criterion does independent work only if "legítima" doesn't already track form-recognition. The sequential structure's availability thus presupposes the adversarial's contested semantic reading. This is the correct structural response: not a counter-architecture, but a showing that the architecture requires the contested premise as a prior condition.

**On the design-relationship scope dilemma response.** The domain-status/authorization species distinction is the session's most important conceptual contribution. The adversarial's dilemma assumes that "compliance-relative" in a compliance-response taxonomy entails "authorization-relative." The supportive shows this assumption is a substantive claim rather than a logical entailment: compliance-response taxonomies can make multiple kinds of compliance-relative determinations — domain-status determinations (where does an act sit within the processing structure?) and authorization determinations (is an act permitted under the compliance obligation?) are distinct operations. Art. 489's categorization of Exit 4 produces the first without producing the second, and being compliance-relative in the domain-status sense is consistent with independence from the compliance-specification design relationship. The dilemma dissolves.

**What the exchange leaves for round 12.** The adversarial now has two precise failure conditions named in the supportive's §7. Path one: show that the filtering account's sequential structure is architecturally available without presupposing the semantic premise — specifically, show a structural principle by which scope-constituting priority necessarily supplies an independent filter, without relying on what "legítima" means. This is hard: it requires finding a structural principle about jointly constituted taxonomies that does not reduce to the semantic claim. Path two: show that domain-status and authorization compliance-relativity collapse within this specific compliance-response taxonomy — specifically, that domain-status membership in the ordinary processing structure is constitutively tied to authorization-permissibility under the compliance obligation. This requires showing that being "in the ordinary processing domain" of a compliance system is equivalent to being "permitted by the compliance obligation," which is a strong constitutive claim.

**What the synthesis can now see.** Both readings have been defended through eleven rounds of bilateral development with increasing structural precision. The structural ceiling is genuine: after round 11, neither the filtering account (which the supportive has shown presupposes its own conclusion) nor the design-relationship scope dilemma (which the supportive has dissolved via the species distinction) produces primary Brazilian procedural authority. The debate is approaching a stable terminal form. The most likely shape: the adversarial either (a) finds the structural principle that makes the sequential architecture available independently of the semantic premise, or (b) accepts that both readings remain coherent interpretive options — the interpretive-options terminal form — and the filing becomes an explicit articulation of that outcome rather than a continued attempt to establish one reading over the other.
