### ESHTR C2: The Ementa-as-Judicial-Document Move

The statutory grounding dispute in ESHTR C2 r18 turns on a question of Brazilian procedural law: what is the ementa, formally?

The adversarial r18 assumed it is a secretariat document — a summary produced administratively, separate from the deliberating court's decision — and on that assumption, art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentação* obligation applies to the reasoning in the acórdão's body, not to the secretariat's characterization of it. The argument was then: no independent CPC provision establishes an accuracy obligation for the secretariat's ementa.

The supportive r18 contests the assumption, not the argument. Under CPC art. 943, the ementa is specified as a formal component of the acórdão *lavrado pelo relator* — authored by the relator, not the secretariat, and signed as part of the acórdão. If this is correct, the ementa is a judicial document under the relator's authorship, and art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentação* requirement extends to it directly.

**Editorial assessment:** This is a potentially decisive move, but its force depends entirely on whether CPC art. 943 is accurately characterized. The adversarial r19 has at least two available responses:

1. **Contest the art. 943 reading.** Argue that art. 943's "ementa" provision is merely a formal listing requirement — that the acórdão must *contain* an ementa as a structural element (a heading summary), but that "containing" it does not mean the relator is subject to an accuracy obligation for the summary as a judicial utterance. The relator authors the ementa in the sense of drafting it, but the *fundamentação* obligation under art. 93, IX CF attaches to the reasoning expressed in the body, not to a structural heading element.

2. **Accept art. 943 but narrow the *fundamentação* obligation.** Accept that the ementa is authored by the relator; argue that art. 93, IX CF requires that the reasoning *in* the acórdão be expressed and accessible, not that summary characterizations of that reasoning (even if relator-authored) meet an accuracy standard. The *fundamentação* obligation is about the reasoning being present and exposed, not about the ementa's accuracy in characterizing it.

Either response is available. Response (2) is stronger because it does not require contesting the statutory provision's formal application; it contests the *scope* of the obligation. If the adversarial r19 takes this path, the exchange will sharpen around what "fundamentação" requires — whether it generates an accuracy obligation for summary characterizations in addition to a presence-and-exposure obligation for the underlying reasoning.

The correction-revision absorption is clean. The (a)/(b) structure handles it: if the accuracy-recognition trigger is a matter for obligation (a) (art. 93, IX CF), and obligation (b) assesses §1º, V compliance, then the compliance-assessment framework under (b) need not distinguish correction revisions from changed-circumstances revisions. Both produce a new constitutive reference; that is what (b) tracks. The adversarial's correction-revision argument presupposed that the constitutive account had to absorb the triggering mechanism; the (a)/(b) structure assigns it elsewhere. Whether the adversarial r19 has a response to the CPC art. 943 argument is the live question.
