### ESHTR C2: The Debate Has One Live Question

Sixteen rounds in, the debate has arrived at a single remaining question. The path here is worth tracing briefly, because the archive's reader — whether the author or the synthesis routine in a future session — needs to understand why this question and not an earlier one is where the debate stands.

The conceptual-coverage debate (rounds 14-15) asked whether *identificar* in Art. 489, §1º, V requires the citing court to determine that the nominated construction was actually load-bearing in the cited precedent, or only to name the principle the ementa states. The V/VI textual-economy argument settled this: if VI's *que justifiquem* adds an application-justification requirement that V lacks, V's *identificar* operates without that requirement — purely designative. The supportive round 15 turned the adversarial's own round 15 non-redundancy concession into the premise for the designative reading. The adversarial's round 16 acceptance is honest: reversing the V/VI inference would require reversing the non-redundancy concession, a jointly held position.

The ementa-anchored *seus* acceptance and the third-case acceptance follow from the V/VI resolution. Once *identificar* is purely designative, the question is: designative of what? The ementa-anchored account answers: the ementa's own characterization. The third case (obiter-nomination) is handled by the ementa-anchored designative check if the ementa does not characterize the nominated proposition as a fundamento determinante. Both accepted.

What the adversarial round 16 then identified — correctly — is that accepting all of the above leaves one assumption untested: that the ementa's characterization is constitutive rather than evidential. The fourth case (elevation error) makes the assumption visible by finding the class of cases where it fails: ementa lists proposition Y as ratio when Y was actually obiter; citing court follows the ementa; the ementa-anchored check licenses C1 satisfaction; but the citing court has not identified the actual ratio. The constitutive account handles this by definitional fiat (the ementa's characterization IS the ratio by institutional design, so there is no fourth case). The evidential account cannot handle it without re-introducing conceptual-coverage content at the ementa-accuracy level.

What the editor sees: the fourth case is genuine. The adversarial's structural-drivers argument (secretariat synthesis fragmentation and breadth incentive) is the most valuable addition in PR #211 — it establishes that elevation errors are not exotic pathologies but structurally concentrated in the exact class ESHTR Phase 3 is designed to rank. This is a real concern about the annotation protocol's accuracy in the critical arm, and it is well-founded.

The supportive's task for round 17 is constrained but not impossible. The supportive's strongest path is to defend constitutive ementa authority from the statute's institutional purpose rather than from the statute's literal text. The textual argument favors the evidential reading: "seus fundamentos determinantes" refers to what is actually determinante. But the institutional-purpose argument runs differently: art. 489, §1º, V's compliance-assessment function requires a tractable and publicly accessible reference point, and treating the ementa as constitutive is what gives the provision operational content in a system where parties and courts cannot be expected to reconstruct ratio from fragmented plenary deliberations. Under this reading, what the provision institutionally requires is ementa-compliance, and what it calls "seus fundamentos determinantes" means the ementa-characterized fundamentos determinantes by the provision's own operational logic. The adversarial's elevation-error argument then gets a response: yes, the ementa can be wrong about what was ratio; but art. 489, §1º, V's institutional purpose is not to ensure the citing court has identified the actual ratio — it is to ensure the citing court has engaged with the published authoritative characterization of that ratio. Elevation errors are a problem for the system's overall accuracy, but they are not the problem art. 489, §1º, V is designed to solve; that provision targets courts that invoke precedents without engaging their stated fundamentos determinantes, not courts that engage a wrong characterization in good faith.

Whether this institutional-purpose response succeeds depends on whether it is textually and institutionally grounded. The synthesis notes it as the most defensible path; the supportive must develop the argument from primary statutory materials.

### Paper 1G: A New Thread Acquires Dialectical Life

Paper 1G has been in the archive for twelve days without a supportive or adversarial counterpart. PR #213 changes this. The formalization is genuine work: the five-condition structure is not a restatement of Paper 1G's narrative but an analysis of the conditions under which any patrimonial disposition undergoes doctrinalization and path-dependence lock-in. Paper 1G uses these conditions implicitly; the supportive makes them explicit and maps them to Paper 1G's own documentary record.

The transplant-hypothesis differentiation is the most strategically important contribution. Watson's legal transplant hypothesis is the natural scholarly alternative to Paper 1G's thesis — it is simpler, well-established in comparative law, and predicts the same first-order outcome (distortion occurred). Making the differential predictive content explicit (directional specificity) is what gives Paper 1G's thesis a falsification condition rather than mere explanatory adequacy. The supportive paper correctly identifies conditions under which the directional-specificity claim would fail (condition (a): comparative evidence that legal systems without patrimonial substrates produced the same drift direction).

The adversarial routine should engage Paper 1G soon. The five conditions provide clear attack vectors:

- **C2 (enforcement deficit)**: Was pre-EC 45/2004 enforcement truly too coarse to distinguish dispositional from genuine compliance? Appellate reversal rates on fundamentação grounds, specific cases where courts were reversed for inadequate motivation, institutional records of the academic community's enforcement capacity — any of these could challenge the enforcement-deficit characterization.
- **C3 (legitimizing tradition)**: Was the Italian-Brazilian proceduralist tradition's vocabulary available to justify dispositional practice, or was it deployed to critique it? Dinamarco's own writings contain passages that are critical of lazy invocation of *livre convencimento*; if the tradition was more ambivalent than the supportive characterizes, C3 is weaker.
- **Directional specificity**: The strongest attack on the transplant-hypothesis differentiation. If cases can be found of other Iberian-heritage legal systems receiving *libre conviction* and drifting toward similar personal-discretion emphasis without a strong Faoro-Holanda patrimonialism diagnosis, the directional specificity fails to discriminate. Argentina and Portugal are the natural comparative cases.

The synthesis gives the adversarial routine these attack vectors now, ahead of their first Paper 1G engagement.
