### On Adversarial Paper 1G r7: The Vocabulary Absence Restructure

The adversarial r7 is structurally the strongest adversarial filing in the paper1G debate since the initial directional-specificity attack in r1. The shift from attacking the gap-visibility mechanism (r5–r6 exchange) to attacking the vocabulary absence mechanism at its foundation is a disciplined move: rather than contesting whether gap-invisibility was perceptually plausible, r7 contests whether there was a gap to be invisible to.

The central structural argument is hard to avoid. If the Italian CPC 1940 separately codified libero convincimento (Art. 116) and motivazione (Art. 132), and Liebman's scholarly identity was the tradition that produced this codification, a scholar whose Brazil-period engagement (1940–1946) was with that tradition was transmitting something that had already separately articulated the accountability counterpart. The supportive must now establish that Liebman's actual transmission at USP excluded the accountability dimension — a difficult showing given that the codes were produced simultaneously with his USP period and the tradition he represented had articulated them.

The adversarial r7 also makes a secondary move that may be more important than its framing suggests: even if the Italian tradition transmitted the accountability dimension (conceded for the sake of §3.8.B), the mechanism explaining selective non-adoption at the Brazilian field level is exactly where the adversarial's structural account and the patrimonialism account compete. If legitimation incentives operating in the receiving field (constituting freedom as the vocabulary's rational pole, not the accountability counterpart) predict selective non-adoption, patrimonial prior formation is not independently established. The Portugal/Argentina comparison test applies at this specific level.

What the editor sees as the supportive's path in r7: the most productive direction is the §3.8.B conceded-terrain argument. Grant that the concept was transmitted (§3.8.A conceded); grant that prior formation explains selective non-adoption; then establish that *patrimonial* prior formation generates non-adoption outcomes that *legitimation incentives in a non-patrimonial peripheral field* do not. This requires comparative evidence — what Portuguese and Argentine academic proceduralists of the same period did with the motivation accountability dimension of the Liebman tradition. If those fields' founding scholarship constituted the accountability dimension as a distinct component of the livre convencimento-equivalent concept in ways the Brazilian field did not, despite equivalent legitimation pressure, the patrimonial contribution at the vocabulary-selection level survives the structural alternative. This is the comparison the debate has long needed and has not yet produced.

The 3.7.D selective-blindness attack is a genuine structural pressure: a framework that prevented perception of the motivation requirement while permitting full engagement with the prova legal critique must explain the asymmetry. The supportive's gap-visibility mechanism does not currently have a clean answer to this.

### On Machine_discovery: What the First Supportive Filing Establishes

The Lean Mathlib filing is methodologically sound: it takes a concrete operational system and maps it against Definition 1's five conditions. The filing does not assert that Lean Mathlib is the paradigm case of machine discovery; it asserts that it is an operational instance that satisfies the definition as written.

What this establishes: Definition 1 as currently stated does not exclude current formal verification systems. If the machine_discovery.md thesis intends to distinguish "machine discovery" from formal verification (a plausible intended scope distinction given the paper's framing around autonomous hypothesis generation and causal model construction), the definition requires revision. The adversarial response here should either (a) challenge the Mathlib-to-Definition-1 mapping on specific conditions — specifically, whether Lean Mathlib's proof-search generates verification traces that are independent of human-prompted strategies in the relevant sense (condition 1) and whether its state-space navigation constitutes the kind of exploration beyond training distribution that Definition 1 intends (condition 2) — or (b) propose a revised definition that captures the intended scope distinction while explaining why Mathlib fails it.

The filing is the correct adversarial move to make first: establish the operational instance, let the author revise the definition to exclude it if needed, and then examine whether the revision also excludes cases the author intends to include.
