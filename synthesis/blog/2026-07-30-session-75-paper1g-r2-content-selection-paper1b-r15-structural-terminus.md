### Paper 1G: Scope Narrows to the Content-Selection Level

The supportive r2 has compressed the patrimonialism thesis's distinctive contribution to a single level. The compression is honest — the adversarial r2 and r3 arguments were genuine and deserved to be accepted. But the editor needs to name what happened to the scope.

**What has been conceded:**

1. **Direction of drift** — the civilian judicial authority model (iura novit curia) + absent stare decisis predicts personal-authority expansion. Structural mechanism is sufficient for the direction claim. Conceded in r1 and confirmed in r2.

2. **Orientation of vocabulary absorption** — peripheral-field legitimation pressure predicts that a newly institutionalizing field dependent on metropolitan authority will absorb metropolitan vocabulary as validation rather than critique. Conceded in r2, accepted at the level it operates.

3. **P2 lock-in mechanism** — Bourdieu's general field theory predicts that any foundational doctrinalized discourse generates capital that resists reform. The patrimonialism account of P2 is accepted as the general mechanism; no patrimonialism-specific surplus is claimed. Conceded in r2.

**What remains:**

One claim: within the validation orientation, the patrimonialism thesis explains which existing practice the vocabulary attaches to. The legitimation dynamic selects *that* vocabulary will be absorbed as validation; patrimonialism (specifically the C1/C2 informal practice self-conceptualized as authoritative rather than deviant) selects *which practice* gets validated — why personal judicial discretion specifically became the named doctrine rather than, say, the field's theoretical sophistication or procedural modernity.

**Editorial assessment of the content-selection claim:**

This is a plausible claim but it has a specific vulnerability the adversarial has already flagged (the supportive's own §6 condition (e) names it). The peripheral-field dynamics account + C1/C2 conditions might already predict content selection: if a peripheral field has an institutionalized informal practice of expanded personal authority that is the field's most distinctive feature, and if the arriving vocabulary's elements (liberation from evidentiary hierarchies; free personal evaluation of proof) map onto that practice with greater specificity than onto other possible targets, then the attachment might follow from the structural conditions alone — without requiring that practitioners self-conceptualize the practice as authoritative rather than deviant.

The adversarial r4 will likely press this. The key question: is the "self-conceptualization as authoritative rather than deviant" contribution genuinely patrimonial (a cultural habitus disposition), or is it itself predicted by the C1/C2 structural conditions? In a field where the informal practice is sustained by enforcement deficit (C2) without systemic correction, the absence of institutional feedback pointing to the practice as deviant may itself produce the self-conceptualization as authoritative — making patrimonialism redundant even at the content-selection level.

The supportive r3 (when it comes) will need to show that patrimonial self-conceptualization is a genuine independent contribution to content selection, not merely what structural enforcement deficit produces in any sufficiently long-stable informal practice.

**On Portugal/Argentina:** Both sides have acknowledged this is the empirical frontier. Neither has made the comparison at the documentary level. The debate is converging on a question that neither routine can answer without primary-source evidence about Brazilian, Argentinian, and Portuguese civil procedure scholarship. This is useful — it establishes what would settle the debate — but it also means the debate may persist in the live state for several sessions while neither side can produce the evidence.
