### ESHTR C2: Ementa-Anchored Account Changes the Shape

Fifteen rounds in, the supportive has filed what may be the debate's decisive statutory response. The ementa-anchored account of *seus* is not a retreat from the purely designative reading — it is a precise specification of what the purely designative reading requires. *Seus* is conceded to denote an objective reference set; the ementa is identified as what constitutes that set. The designative check then becomes: does the citing court's statement match what the ementa characterizes as fundamentos determinantes? That is an ementa-level text-to-text comparison, not an independent ratio reconstruction from the underlying decision.

The V/VI textual-economy argument is the most elegant move in the round 15 filing. The adversarial's round 15 argued from the non-redundancy of V and VI to the conclusion that V's *identificar* carries substantive content (else V and VI test the same thing at different thresholds). The supportive takes the same non-redundancy premise and inverts the inference: if VI's *que justifiquem* adds an application-justification requirement that V lacks, V's *identificar* is precisely what lacks that requirement — i.e., V's *identificar* is purely designative. The non-redundancy requires that V and VI target different things; the *que justifiquem* clause in VI is what adds the substantive requirement; therefore V operates without that requirement. The adversarial's non-redundancy concession becomes the supportive's strongest premise.

The obiter-nomination third case is handled by the ementa-anchored reading, but this resolution depends on the ementa's own characterization of fundamentos determinantes being authoritative and correct. This is the adversarial's remaining productive path.

What the adversarial must press in round 16: contest whether the ementa constitutively defines the fundamentos determinantes. The adversarial's best argument is that the ementa's characterization is itself fallible — a court can mischaracterize as the ratio something that was actually obiter in the underlying decision's reasoning, and can mischaracterize as obiter something that was actually the ratio. If the ementa's self-characterization is not constitutive but merely evidential (a presumption rebuttable by careful reading of the underlying decision), then the ementa-anchored designative check does not catch all paradigm Art. 489, §1º, V failures. A citing court that checks the ementa diligently and nominates what the ementa calls a fundamento determinante, when the ementa has mischaracterized an obiter proposition as ratio, would satisfy C1 on the ementa-anchored designative reading but would have failed to identify the actual ratio — the paradigm §1º, V failure.

If this argument is available, it contests the ementa-anchored account's ability to handle the third case without conceding conceptual-coverage content. The adversarial would then be arguing not that *identificar* requires conceptual-coverage work by the citing court, but that the ementa cannot serve as the constitutive reference set because the ementa can be wrong about its own ratio.

This is the productive path. The adversarial should not contest the V/VI textual-economy inference directly — the supportive's use of the non-redundancy concession is genuinely sound, and contesting it would require reversing an accepted position. The ementa-authority question is the live issue.
