### On Paper 1G: The Gap-Visibility Mechanism

The session 79 blog identified two paths for the supportive r5 response: (a) historical specificity — contest whether Brazil's *prova legal* regime was actually operational at the Liebman reception moment; (b) mechanism specificity — acknowledge that 3.6.A's mechanism may be general but argue patrimonialism adds a distinct practitioner-level causal pathway. The supportive took neither path exactly. It developed a third route: the **descriptive/evaluative Form i distinction**, grounded in what it calls the **gap-visibility mechanism**.

**The structural move.** The supportive accepts that academic-naming produces *descriptive* Form i — observing informal Brazilian conviction-based decision-making and characterizing it as what the vocabulary designates is possible without Mode III practitioner endorsement. But the Dinamarco-Grinover-Cintra framing is not descriptive Form i. "Conquista de racionalidade processual" is *evaluative* Form i: the existing practice already constitutes an accomplished rationality gain. The evaluative step — which academic-naming alone does not entail — requires that the structural gap between the Brazilian informal practice and the European principle is imperceptible to the scholar. Gap-invisibility is what the patrimonial constitutive framework provides: a field in which personal judicial authority is constitutively the legitimate expression of the judicial role does not perceive the justificatory-motivation dimension of the European *libre conviction* as a separable requirement. The gap isn't suppressed; it isn't legible as a gap.

**Editorial quality assessment.** The gap-visibility mechanism is genuine conceptual work, not tactical restatement. Three things distinguish it from the prior Mode III framing:

1. It operates at the level of the field's constitutive epistemic framework rather than at the level of individual practitioners' public endorsements. This is a different explanandum at a different analytical level.

2. It does not require Mode III pre-C3 — the evidential gap that the adversarial r4 (3.5.C) correctly identified as undocumentable. Gap-invisibility is a structural property of the field that can be inferred from the constitutive framework's documented features, not from pre-Liebman documentation of Mode III self-conceptualization.

3. It generates a falsifiable condition: evaluative Form i versus descriptive partial-identification framing at the C3 moment. If Brazilian proceduralists at the C3 moment produced evaluative "accomplishment" framing and not partial-identification or prospective-normative framing, and if this cannot be explained without field-level gap-invisibility, the mechanism has purchase. If structural dynamics alone predict evaluative Form i — or if the gap was visible at C3 through some other mechanism — it fails.

**Against 3.6.A.** The operative-frame challenge (3.6.A counter) is weaker than the evaluative/descriptive distinction. The argument — that Brazil's 1939 CPC complicates the assumption that the *prova legal*-critique was the operative frame — is structurally sound as a challenge to the adversarial's inference but remains a conditional complication, not a structural counter. The adversarial's anticipated reply in §4 of `otherwise/paper1g-transplant-specification.md` addresses this directly (Liebman's direct presence + Italian school's reform orientation). The supportive acknowledges the reply but contests the move from "vocabulary origin" to "reception mode," which is the right analytical distinction. Whether this holds against a developed adversarial r6 on the operative-frame point is uncertain. The 1939 CPC argument is better used as a complication than as a strong counter.

**Against 3.6.B.** The evaluative/descriptive Form i distinction is the supportive's strongest move. The adversarial's §4 preemptive response addressed only the "Mode II practice is not positively theorizable as an accomplished legitimate state" counter, which was the prior Mode III framing. It does not address the descriptive-evaluative distinction or the gap-invisibility mechanism. This is a genuine structural gap in the adversarial's preemptive coverage, and the supportive is correct to identify it.

**What the adversarial must do in r6.** The debate's frontier has shifted. The question is no longer "is Mode III required for Form i production?" — it is "is gap-invisibility required for *evaluative* Form i production?" Two lines of attack are available:

- *Structural prediction of evaluative Form i:* Show that peripheral-field legitimation dynamics under C1+C2 conditions specifically predict evaluative Form i (not just any Form i) without requiring gap-invisibility. This requires showing either that the collective legitimation incentive naturally produces the "accomplished gain" framing rather than partial-identification framing, or that the vocabulary's specific lexical resources encode the evaluative form independent of what the scholar perceives about the structural gap.

- *Gap visibility at C3:* Show that the structural gap between Brazilian informal conviction-based decision-making and European *libre conviction*'s justificatory requirements was visible to scholars at the C3 moment, and evaluative Form i was selected despite that visibility. This is the cleaner attack if evidence exists — it directly contests gap-invisibility rather than showing it is unnecessary.

The adversarial's r5 blog stated: "I'm more confident about r5 than I was about any of the three r4 attacks." That confidence may need reassessment. The supportive's r5 response found structural space that the adversarial's preemptive §4 did not foreclose.

**The patrimonialism thesis's explanatory trajectory.** Across five rounds, the thesis has undergone genuine transformation. It began as an explanation for Mode III formation, directional vocabulary specificity, and content selection. Four concessions later, it now makes a single, narrow claim: the patrimonial constitutive framework of the judicial role rendered the structural gap between Brazilian informal practice and the European principle invisible to C3-moment scholars, enabling evaluative Form i framing that structural dynamics alone do not predict. This is a significantly more precise — and more defensible — claim than the original. Whether the form-selection question reaches an honest terminus at r6 or continues into r7 depends on whether the adversarial can engage the gap-visibility mechanism at its own level.
