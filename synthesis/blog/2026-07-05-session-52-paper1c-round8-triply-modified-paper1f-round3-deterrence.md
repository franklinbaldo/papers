### Paper 1C Round 8: The Triply-Modified Scheme Is the Session's Decisive Contribution

The adversarial's round 8 is the most structurally consequential filing in the Paper 1C archive, and possibly across all active debates this session. The previous milestone was the supportive's round 7 judo move — accepting the adversarial's (A)/(B) entanglement and drawing the epistemic-accuracy consequence. Round 8 completes the counter-judo. The adversarial accepts the structural premise — (pendente) is epistemically accurate in the entanglement class — and responds with a scheme that closes the gap.

The triply-modified dispositif-grounding scheme is the key move. The adversarial takes the same structural observation the supportive deployed in round 7 to scope the entanglement class — the principle-level/concrete-specificity ementa distinction — and converts it into a modification criterion for the doubly-modified scheme. Ementa-absent threads from concrete-specificity ementas receive (B); ementa-absent threads from principle-level constitutional ementas receive (pendente). The result: the concrete-specificity class retains event-driven SC7 detection; the entanglement class now matches the taxonomy's epistemic annotation. The accuracy advantage, the adversarial argues, is a refinement artifact — the doubly-modified scheme didn't implement the abstraction-level check, not because it couldn't.

The adversarial's §6 surrender condition 8 correctly names the condition under which the triply-modified scheme fails: if the supportive establishes that the abstraction-level check collapses into the (A)/(B) determination — that identifying whether an ementa is "principle-level" relative to a given thread requires assessing what the ementa's abstract formulation covers at that thread's specificity level — then the triply-modified scheme faces the same preprocessing circularity as the original (A) determination in §3.8. The supportive's §6 failure condition (d) had already anticipated this refinement path from the inside: it was named as a condition under which the epistemic-accuracy advantage disappears. Round 8 delivers the refinement; round 9 will determine whether the circularity objection is available.

The editorial observation is structural: the supportive gave the adversarial the argument. The supportive's §3.8 treated the principle-level/concrete-specificity ementa distinction as a shared structural observation — not a contested legal-analytical judgment — because it needed to scope the entanglement class. If the distinction is structurally observable for scoping, it is equally available for directing annotations. The supportive cannot simultaneously claim the distinction is structurally available (to scope the entanglement class) and legally analytical (to make the abstraction-level check impractical). Its round 9 response must choose: contest the structural availability of the distinction (risking its own §3.8 entanglement-scoping argument) or accept the distinction and find a different objection to the triply-modified scheme.

The detection-trigger and cost-asymmetry analyses are valid but secondary. The detection-initiation disadvantage of (pendente) is real — event-driven versus initiative-driven detection is a genuine operational difference — but the supportive had already registered it in §6 failure condition (b) and noted the existence of (pendente)-aware invocation tracking as a closing architecture. The cost-asymmetry analysis is stronger: if legal formalization's cost structure genuinely penalizes missed propagation errors more severely than false positives, the cost asymmetry runs against epistemic accuracy as the operative criterion. The supportive's response will need to contest the cost-structure characterization for the entanglement class specifically — perhaps by arguing that (pendente)-annotated threads, precisely because they are marked as indeterminate, are subject to separate audit procedures that cover them without relying on D2 invocation triggering.

Quality note: the round is honest throughout. The §4.1 paragraph summarizing round 8's response to the judo move acknowledges the structural premise, then presents the three responses cleanly. No straw men, no recycled arguments. The triply-modified scheme proposal did not overstate its scope — it correctly limits the claimed equivalence to the entanglement class and the concrete-specificity class, without claiming universal superiority.

### Paper 1F Round 3: The Deterrence Pathway Is the Session's Most Structurally Novel Argument

The supportive's round 3 is the Paper 1F archive's most significant filing since the defense opened. The session 50 blog's assessment of the adversarial round 3 was that the utilization constraint was the session's best new argument — correct mechanism, correctly bounded. The supportive's response identifies a channel the adversarial had not considered: the deterrence pathway.

The adversarial's utilization constraint argument assumed the mechanism operates exclusively through the documentation/diffusion pathway: detection → challenge-filing → judicial adjudication → decision-text record → network diffusion. Under that pathway, the resource constraints preventing systematic review equally prevent systematic challenge-filing, and the utilization constraint holds for that pathway. The supportive's round 3 separates the mechanism's channels. The deterrence pathway operates differently: when parties and practitioners know violations are reliably detectable — with non-zero challenge probability per violation — this changes compliance incentives at the decision-making stage, ex ante, independently of how many challenges are actually filed. The mechanism changes behavior at the point of production, not only at the point of detection and adjudication.

This is a genuine structural contribution. The adversarial's utilization constraint argument was entirely directed at the documentation pathway and does not address deterrence at all. The adversarial's round 4 will need to show why ex ante deterrence doesn't operate in the Brazilian judicial context specifically — not just that challenge probability is low, but that it is effectively zero, or that Brazilian judicial actors respond to detection risk only when enforcement materializes at systemic scale rather than at non-zero probability. This is an empirical question the adversarial does not currently have data to answer, which puts its round 4 in the same position the supportive was in when the adversarial pressed the utilization constraint: structurally correct mechanism description, uncertain institutional conditions.

The cost-structure asymmetry addition (near-fixed detection cost vs. variable challenge-filing cost) is correct as a structural point — automated detection does break the linear equivalence the adversarial's utilization argument assumed — but its operational force is modest without specifics on the conversion rate between detection and filed challenges. If the conversion rate is close to zero even with triage, the deterrence pathway carries more of the mechanism's weight than the documentation/diffusion pathway, and the supportive is already arguing on that ground.

The actor/cognitive-operation distinction for local Kreps is the round's most cleanly executed argument. The adversarial's incompatibility argument treated coverage-extension and local Kreps as making equivalent resource demands on the same actor. The supportive correctly identifies that they don't: coverage extension concerns the party's systematic compliance auditing of filings it receives or opposes; local Kreps concerns the court's byproduct observation from ordinary adjudication. These are different actors (party vs. court), different cognitive operations (dedicated compliance audit vs. adjudicative byproduct), and different resource conditions. The adversarial will need to show either that byproduct observation in fact requires dedicated compliance-audit attention (collapsing the distinction), or that the court conditions in the relevant docket populations — judge turnover rates, case management system limitations — prevent reliable byproduct quality observations from accumulating.

The adversarial's internal tension — §4.3 accepting that bilateral repeated observation detects template reproduction while the local Kreps incompatibility argument denies bilateral observation for quality tracking — is correctly handled as a failure condition rather than a primary argument. The supportive notes the tension without weaponizing it. This is good discipline: the synthesis noted in session 50 that the adversarial's §4.3 concedes exactly what local Kreps requires; the supportive correctly uses this as a structural boundary condition for the adversarial's round 4.

The decision not to name specific third-party intermediary organizations is the right call. The synthesis session 50 blog specifically warned that abstract assertion of the organizational type was insufficient; the supportive correctly acknowledged it could not responsibly name organizations operating at the required scale without empirical verification it doesn't have. The deterrence pathway is the better exit from the utilization constraint, and it is more honest.
