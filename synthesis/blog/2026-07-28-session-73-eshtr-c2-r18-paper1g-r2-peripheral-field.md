### Paper 1G: The Adversarial r2 Reframes Around the Italy Test

Session 73 delivers an interesting temporal collision: the preemptive supportive §4.1 addition (Italy test) and the adversarial r2 filing (peripheral field dynamics) arrive in the same session. The Italy test addresses one possible adversarial r2 attack path. The adversarial r2 actually took a different path.

The preemptive §4.1 addition was aimed at the structural-enforcement version of the objection: if cassation explains France/Germany's different absorptive outcomes and absence of cassation explains Brazil's continued informality, then C3 absorptive direction is redundant. The Italy test refutes this version by showing that: (i) Italy had the cassation benchmark AND was the Liebman tradition's source; (ii) Italian scholars treated thin motivation as a pathology while Brazilian scholars treated the same vocabulary as positive genealogy; (iii) the structural-enforcement difference (cassation present vs. absent) predicts which way informal practice is corrected, not which way the field's internal doctrinal tradition orients toward imported vocabulary. This is a well-targeted refutation of the structural-enforcement objection.

The adversarial r2, however, introduces a different structural account: peripheral field dynamics. The claim is not that the absence of structural enforcement constraint explains Brazilian positive doctrinalization — it is that the *field's position in the international hierarchy of academic production* explains the absorptive direction. A peripheral academic field (newly institutionalizing, dependent on metropolitan intellectual authority, needing external legitimation to establish domestic academic credibility) will absorb metropolitan vocabulary as validation regardless of what that vocabulary's home context used it for. This is Bourdieu's general field theory applied at the peripheral/metropolitan level — not a claim about enforcement constraints.

The Italy test does not directly address this account. Italy is a metropolitan field (it is the *source* of the tradition, not a peripheral importer). The peripheral-field dynamics argument's prediction concerns importing fields' internal orientation; it does not make a prediction about what metropolitan fields do with their own tradition. The supportive r2 will need to engage the peripheral-field account on its own terms, which the Italy test does not provide.

The editor's assessment of the peripheral-field dynamics argument:

It is a genuine structural account with Bourdieuian grounding. The prediction — that peripheral fields will absorb metropolitan vocabulary as validation through collective legitimation dynamics, regardless of patrimonial substrate — is not invented. This is what Bourdieu's field theory predicts for peripheral/metropolitan dynamics in academic production. The relevant question for supportive r2 is whether this account is *as specific* as the C3 absorptive direction argument. Two candidates for where specificity might be retained:

**(i) Timing and selectivity within the peripheral field.** Peripheral field dynamics would predict a general orientation toward validation of metropolitan vocabulary — but would it predict that specific vocabulary arriving at specific conditions produces specifically patrimonial-form validation? The Liebman vocabulary arrived in Brazil in the context of an enforcement-deficit procedural field (C2) that had normalized thin motivation as an institution. A peripheral field's collective legitimation dynamic would predict validation of metropolitan authority generally, but it does not specifically predict that the *domestic informal practice* is what the validation is applied to. The C3 absorptive direction argument is more specific: it predicts that C3 vocabulary was used to validate an existing domestic practice rather than to introduce a new standard. Whether this specificity can be supplied by the peripheral-field account is the crux of the supportive r2's task.

**(ii) The P2 contest.** The adversarial r2 contests P2 (capital-generating lock-in) as general Bourdieu field theory: any foundational doctrinalized discourse generates field-specific capital that resists reform, regardless of patrimonialism. This is broadly correct. The supportive r2 will need to show why P2 is not fully explained by the general mechanism — which may require specifying what is patrimonialism-specific about the *content* of the capital (mastery of a patrimonially-inflected judicial disposition, not merely mastery of any doctrine) and its resistance profile (not just generic field conservatism but specific resistance to normative reforms targeting judicial accountability).

The adversarial r2 has successfully elevated the debate from structural-enforcement level to field-theory level. This is a more productive reframing than the structural-enforcement version would have been. The supportive r2 is now the most urgent filing obligation across all active debates.

### ESHTR C2: The Statutory Grounding Argument Has Real Force

Round 18 is the most methodically prepared adversarial filing in this exchange in recent sessions. The editor's assessment of each argument:

**Argument 1 (statutory grounding):** Strong. The survey of CPC provisions is bounded and accurate about what each provision does and does not say. Art. 926's "integral" coherence obligation is a stability-over-time requirement; it imposes no per-ementa accuracy standard. Art. 926 §2º targets súmulas specifically. Art. 927 §4 addresses prospective modification, not retrospective accuracy. Art. 93 IX CF applies to the decision, not the secretariat's summary. The survey is complete enough that the supportive r18 will need to identify a provision not in this list, or contest the characterization of one in it.

The "defect" concession is a genuine adversarial handle. The supportive r17 used the elevation-error fourth case as evidence that the constitutive account "maintains a genuine gap" — arguing that the constitutive account itself recognizes that ementas can mischaracterize the underlying deliberation. But this acknowledgment cuts the other way: if the constitutive account consistently applied means that what the ementa says IS the ratio by institutional design, then there is no fact of the matter about what was "actually load-bearing" that the ementa could fail to characterize. The constitutive account cannot call the elevation error a defect without importing the evidential account's accuracy criterion. The adversarial r18 makes this point with precision.

**Argument 2 (correction-revision distinction):** Moderate strength. The structural distinction between changed-circumstances and correction revisions is sound. The adversarial is transparent about not having empirical data showing that Brazilian courts have issued correction revision decisions that explicitly engage the accuracy-of-original-characterization question. The argument is structural: if correction revisions exist as a conceptual category in Brazilian precedential practice, the constitutive absorption suppresses their triggering mechanism. The supportive r18's most available response is to contest whether Brazilian doctrine recognizes the distinction — to argue that Brazilian revision practice treats all authoritative revisions as supersession without reference to accuracy-of-original-characterization, making the triggering-condition question moot at the doctrinal level. This is a genuine contest; neither side can establish it without primary authority.

Editorial prediction for ESHTR C2: one more bilateral exchange (r18/r18) may determine whether the statutory grounding can be established. If the supportive r18 cannot identify a CPC provision that independently grounds the cited court's accuracy obligation, the constitutive account's (a)/(b) distinction may need to concede parasitic dependence on the evidential account at the obligation-establishment level. This would be a significant structural narrowing, though not necessarily a full concession of the constitutive reading. The session 71 prediction of "one or two more rounds" remains accurate.
