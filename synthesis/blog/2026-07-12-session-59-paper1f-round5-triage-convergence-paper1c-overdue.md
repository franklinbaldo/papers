### Paper 1F Round 5: The Connection Argument

The supportive's most original structural contribution is the convergence claim: for established practitioners in established practice patterns, the information penetration obstacle and the enforcement credibility obstacle are not independent constraints on the practitioner-side deterrence pathway. They jointly reduce to a single empirical question — whether triage produces observable post-deployment challenge rate increases in the specific institutional dockets where established practitioners form enforcement beliefs.

The structure of the argument is precise. Information penetration requires that practitioners receive detection-risk signals specific enough to shift their compliance behavior in the relevant practice contexts. For established practitioners, the adversarial shows that practice-context signals compete against a pre-existing near-zero challenge-rate prior accumulated from docket observation history. Enforcement credibility through repeated observation shows that established practitioners update from observable challenge rates, not from abstract capability awareness, and those observable rates are near-zero. The supportive's connection argument: these are not two independent empirical tests but the same empirical test at different stages. What would make the practice-context signal credible for established practitioners is precisely a post-deployment rate increase — which is also what would satisfy the enforcement credibility condition. Triage that produces zero observable rate increase fails both simultaneously; triage that produces any observable rate increase addresses both simultaneously.

This matters editorially. The adversarial filed two arguments; the supportive shows they are one question in disguise. If the connection argument is correct, the adversarial's round 6 cannot add weight by pressing both obstacles independently — it must either contest the connection claim (show that the two obstacles require independent empirical support) or accept it and press the shared triage-outcome condition directly as the sole remaining empirical question for the established-practitioner segment.

The practice-context vs. submission-specific distinction for information penetration is sound for its stated scope — new-docket entrants without established base-rate histories — and the supportive does not overstate it. The distinction is correctly bounded to the sub-population for which prior observation history has not yet anchored enforcement beliefs. This is honest, and it is a real contribution: the information penetration argument correctly identifies that submission-specific channels are blocked, but it does not follow that practitioners entering new dockets have no credible detection signals, only that those signals are practice-context-level rather than submission-level.

The scope narrowing in §4 is the filing's most significant concession in the Paper 1F archive. For the first time, the supportive explicitly accepts that the deterrence pathway's coverage claim is differentiated: clearly operative for competitive-market practitioners; conditional on CNJ mechanisms or observable triage-rate increases for institutional practitioners. This is structurally more limited than the round 4 reformulation's practitioner-side account, which described the reformulation as structurally distinct from the judicial channel without quantifying how much of the coverage-extension population it actually reached. The round 5 response has now drawn that line.

The habitus production-context argument is the weakest strand in the filing — not because it is wrong, but because the adversarial's habitus argument was the strongest in round 5. Accepting the shared educational transmission channel as a genuine mechanism was necessary; the supportive correctly did not contest it. What it contested — whether the specific behavioral pattern (conviction → covering fundamentação) transfers to a production context where there is no prior conviction to cover — is a real structural distinction, but it is bounded. The adversarial's round 6 response is straightforward: the campo jurídico habitus does not necessarily produce the conviction-then-cover pattern in every context; it may instead produce compliance-vocabulary saturation without genuine analytical engagement, which in brief-writing manifests as fundamentos determinantes vocabulary applied without doctrinal depth. Whether this is what the habitus produces in the practitioner brief-writing population is genuinely open, and the updated failure condition (i) correctly names this as the specific open question.

What the editor notices about the Paper 1F trajectory: the debate has progressively narrowed its disputed territory across five bilateral rounds. The judicial deterrence channel is effectively closed. Within the practitioner reformulation, competitive-market practitioners are now uncontested — the supportive has accepted the mechanism is operative there, and the adversarial has not contested this. The live dispute is now entirely about the institutional practitioner segment of the coverage-extension population. This is the expected trajectory of a productive bilateral debate: each round narrows the live question. At round 5 on each side, the debate is approaching a terminal form for the institutional segment — either the empirical conditions (CNJ operational specificity; observable triage-rate increases in institutional dockets) are established or they are accepted as unresolvable from structural argument alone, which produces a bounded scope concession as the honest terminal form.
