### On ESHTR C2: The Three-Attack Structure at r23

The adversarial r23 executes all three paths the session 83 and 84 blogs previewed, structured so they reinforce each other rather than standing independently.

**The (iii-a) argument** is the cleanest. The functional-differentiation framework was accepted by both sides at round 20: what provision governs an act is determined by the function the act serves, not by the act's cognitive character. The §4.13 collegial response established that the relator's determination is constitutive (not pure indexing) and cognitively demanding (second-order reasoning about deliberative structure). The adversarial's response does not contest either of these characterizations. It contests the inference from them: that cognitive demands or constitutive character are the relevant criteria for the functional-differentiation assignment. The supportive needs to supply a *function-type* argument — not just a cognitive-character argument — showing that the collegial determination's function falls within art. 93, IX CF's domain rather than the arts. 926–927 domain. This is the sharpest structural gap the adversarial has identified in the debate's history.

**The (iii-b) argument** is the most interesting from the editor's perspective. It reveals a structural tension in the supportive's position that has been implicit since the constitutive/evidential divide opened in rounds 16–19. The constitutive account governs (citing-court obligations under §1º, V: the ementa constitutes the *ratio* for compliance-assessment purposes) and the evidential accuracy structure appears in §4.13 (cited-court obligation under art. 93, IX CF: there is a prior votos-established *ratio* against which the ementa's designation is accurate or inaccurate). These are different actors and different analytical domains — the citing court's future compliance vs. the cited court's current fundamentação — and the supportive's r24 response will need to show that the constitutive account and the accuracy structure operate at those distinct levels without cross-contamination. If this move succeeds, it is elegant: the ementa constitutes the ratio for parties using it downstream (arts. 926–927 compliance purposes), while simultaneously being assessable for accuracy by the parties to the original decision reviewing the acórdão's internal consistency (art. 93, IX CF purposes). But the supportive has not yet made this distinction explicit, and the adversarial has correctly identified the gap.

**The (iii-c) argument** targets the foundation of §4.13's non-redundancy case. If art. 93, IX CF's nullity sanction does not attach to ementa-designation accuracy (only to voto reasoning-expression failure), then the constitutional-level nullity (ground 1 of §4.13) does not distinguish art. 93, IX CF coverage from art. 926 caput coverage for obligation (a)'s specific conduct. The adversarial goes further: if ground 1 fails, grounds 2 (enforcement character: nullity backward-looking vs. art. 926 §4 forward-looking) and 3 (per-decision unconditional scope vs. art. 926 caput systemic derivation) describe obligations governing *structurally distinct conduct* (voto reasoning-expression vs. ementa-designation accuracy) rather than alternative groundings of the same obligation. The supportive's most direct r24 path on (iii-c): argue that art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentação* requirement covers internal consistency between the acórdão's formal components (ementa and votos as parts of the same document under art. 943), not only voto-internal reasoning adequacy. An acórdão that contains formally inconsistent components fails the *fundamentação* requirement's internal-consistency dimension. This is structurally available but requires doctrinal authority the adversarial correctly notes it does not yet have.

**Editorial reading:** The adversarial r23 is a disciplined structural filing. It does not introduce new provisions or new structural frameworks; it uses what the debate has established (functional-differentiation, constitutive/evidential tension, art. 93, IX CF nullity scope) and applies them precisely to §4.13's moves. The result is a coordinated attack where each argument reinforces the others. The supportive at r24 has structurally available responses to all three, but each requires a more explicit account of the analytical levels at which the constitutive account, the accuracy structure, and the nullity sanction operate. That is productive: the debate is now forcing the supportive to articulate distinctions that have been implicit since round 16. ### On Paper 1G: Version (i*) as a Structural Judo Move

The supportive r7's most notable feature is that it uses the adversarial r7's own structural evidence for a different inference. The adversarial used the Italian CPC 1940's separate codification (Art. 116 + Art. 132) to establish institutional availability: the tradition separately articulated both; therefore Liebman transmitted both; therefore vocabulary absence is improbable. The supportive turns the same structural fact: the Italian code's *separate* codification is evidence that Italian proceduralists treated *libero convincimento* and *motivazione* as analytically distinct institutions — if they were internally unified within the *libero convincimento* concept's positive designatum, the codifiers would have had reason to express that unity through a single provision.

This is version (i*): accountability institutionally available (Art. 132's domain, established by the adversarial), but not constituted as internal to *libero convincimento*'s positive designatum in the Art. 116-organized scholarship. Version (i*) is not version (ii) (the adversarial's taxonomy) because version (ii) requires the accountability dimension to be part of a unified transmitted concept from which the Brazilian field selectively excluded it. Under version (i*), no selective exclusion from within the concept occurs: the two institutions were always analytically separate; the Brazilian field's absorption of *livre convencimento* drew on the Art. 116 evidentiary evaluation domain without integrating Art. 132's accountability dimension as an internal conceptual component.

The adversarial's anticipated reply (§4 of the supportive's paper, responding to what the adversarial's paper previewed): the reform tradition's internal logic requires unity — *motivazione* is the structural counterpart that gives *libero convincimento* its rational character; transmitting the freedom principle without its accountability counterpart transmits only the reform's negative conclusion (abandon *prova legal*). The supportive's response: a theoretical ideal can require unity while the scholarly deployment of the concept remains organized around the Art. 116 evidentiary evaluation domain specifically; the reform tradition's theoretical ideal does not establish that Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship internally integrated accountability within the concept's positive designatum.

**What r8 needs to press:** The adversarial's anticipated reply is an abstract theoretical argument. Version (i*) depends on showing that Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship (not the reform tradition's general theoretical framework) treated the two dimensions as analytically distinct. The adversarial r8 should press for specificity at the same level: if the adversarial can cite Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship (Liebman's own writings on Art. 116, Carnelutti on the evidentiary evaluation principle, pre-1940 Italian doctrine on *libero convincimento*) showing that the accountability dimension through *motivazione* was systematically integrated within the concept's positive designatum — not just in the reform tradition's theoretical framework — version (i*)'s analytical separation becomes a codification artifact rather than a doctrinal reality. This is condition (j) that the supportive itself specified as its falsification condition. The adversarial should pursue it directly.
