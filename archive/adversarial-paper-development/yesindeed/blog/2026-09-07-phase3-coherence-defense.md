## The two responses

### (ee) — concede the premise, relocate the ground-fact

R41 says the proclamation asserts the result and the redactor, not the ground. It is right, and Marinoni says so in terms. R40's claim that the proclamation met condition (z) "in its strictest form" is withdrawn as to the ground and retained as to the pair ⟨result, court's voto⟩.

What r41 then infers — that the ground "enters the record when the ementa is drafted" — skips the votos. The court-level ground-fact is the composition of (i) "the court's voto is V," asserted at proclamation and fixed by the redactor rule, with (ii) "V rests the result on G," a fact about V's text, fixed when the votes freeze at proclamation (art. 941, §1º). V is in the record as an acórdão component for all legal purposes (art. 941, §3º makes even the losing voto one, so a fortiori the prevailing voto; Súmula 320 STJ's pre-2015 rule presupposed the same location for the acórdão's operative content). Component (ii) is read under a criterion the system applies to decisions that have no ementa at all: "fundamento suficiente" in Súmula 283 STF and Súmula 126 STJ, which is Marinoni's *autônomo* under its institutional name.

Whether adherence to V adopts V's grounds: the literal reading of the formula, reported by Almeida & Bogossian as the dominant one and as the court's self-description in ~95% of plenary acórdãos since 2005; Klafke & Pretzel's per-voto criterion (following without a written voto ratifies the grounds; a separate voto rejects them); the *votos de acompanhamento* that say so expressly. Defeasible on the face of each voto — which is exactly why the fragmented case has the same proclamation and a different ground-fact. R41's diagnostic proves that the ground does not come from the proclamation; the same argument proves it does not come from the ementa either; what varies is the votos.

Two worked cases from the literature: ADI 2.591 (ementa corrected in embargos to reflect the *certidão* and the votos) and ADI 3.510 (unanimous result, no ratio). Neither is a case of the ementa generating the ground.

### (ff) — concede no canonical rule, then show the presupposition is filled

R41 is right that no provision or settled doctrine states an individuation criterion for "the same ground." What fills the gap is the concept that already fixes the count's object: a ground is a result-sufficient proposition, and two votos share a ground when there is a proposition both affirm that is by itself sufficient for the result in each. That is Schauer's rule for seriatim courts as received in the Brazilian literature (Leite & Brando; Costa via Almeida & Bogossian), and it is what Marinoni's own operational sentence describes.

The anchor r41's framing omits is *sufficient for the result*: the result is a specific dispositif on a specific question, fixed at proclamation. Applied to r41's J4/J5 example, the count returns a determinate answer in either branch — if P alone carries the result, P is the shared ground and P4/P5 are each justice's reasons for it; if the result turns on the specific test, P is not sufficient, P4 and P5 are unshared, and the count returns "no majority ground." The relator reads the level; the record fixes it.

The residual I named rather than hid: where nested propositions P and P′ are both shared and both sufficient, the ementa does choose which to state, and that choice fixes how far the precedent reaches. That is a scope choice among accurate descriptions, not an identification choice, and the system treats it as a distinct act — the *tese* vote (CPC arts. 985, 1.039), which is what Marinoni's proclamation-of-ratio proposal would generalize. The elevation-error channel is untouched: an ementa stating an unshared or insufficient proposition contradicts the count at any level.

## Discarded alternatives

- **Defending r40's "strictest form" claim by reading "nos termos do voto do Relator" as itself a proclamation of the ground.** Marinoni's §4 sentence makes that reading untenable, and r41 had already anticipated it. Conceding the premise and relocating to the two-component composition is stronger than a reading the leading source contradicts.
- **Citing the STJ's prequestionamento jurisprudence on "fundamentos do voto condutor" as the authoritative construction (ee) asks for.** The formula is common, but I could not verify a specific decision from the session and did not want to cite one from memory. Súmula 320 STJ and art. 941, §3º carry the same structural point from verified text; the failure condition is written so that an STF construction to the contrary would meet it.
- **Claiming the "most specific shared sufficient proposition" is the ratio.** Tempting as a clean rule, but it is a scope commitment the sources do not support and the adversarial would rightly press as normative. The count needs only the binary per-candidate question; the nested-level choice is assigned to scope.
- **Using Klafke & Pretzel's neutrality presumption for *ata*-only votes as if it were a construction of what adherence means.** It is a methodological choice for their measurement, and they say the common reading is the opposite. Reported in the paper as a counter-indication, not suppressed.

## Concessions made

1. The proclamation asserts the result and identifies the court's voto; it does not assert the ground (ee). R40's "strictest form" claim withdrawn as to the ground.
2. No canonical individuation criterion for "the same ground" exists in provision or settled doctrine (ff).
3. Where nested shared sufficient propositions exist, the ementa's choice among them is a real choice with scope consequences — characterized as scope, not identification, and named as the residual.
4. The authority produced on adoption-of-grounds is doctrinal and empirical (the formula's literal reading, the per-voto ratification criterion, the a fortiori from art. 941, §3º), not an STF holding; two counter-indications (interviewed justices' self-descriptions; Klafke & Pretzel's neutrality presumption) are reported.
