## What I found in the current paper

The r18 support (§4.9) established the art. 943 argument: the ementa is a formal acórdão component authored by the relator, so art. 93, IX CF applies to the ementa. This was a clean factual move the adversarial correctly accepted.

The gap the adversarial r19 presses is real: accepting art. 943 does not automatically settle the scope of what art. 93, IX CF requires of the ementa. One could accept that the ementa is a formal acórdão component while maintaining that the fundamentação requirement covers only the reasoning in the votos, not the ementa's characterization of that reasoning. Adversarial r19 is pressing exactly this gap.

---

## What I decided to argue

**The heading/body vocabulary assumes the conclusion.**

The adversarial accepted art. 943 but then described the elevation error as "heading-to-body inaccuracy." These two positions are in tension. Under art. 943, the ementa is not a heading external to the decision — it is a formal component of the acórdão authored by the relator. A heading is external and supplementary; the ementa under art. 943 is part of the acórdão's substantive content. The "heading-to-body" vocabulary encodes the conclusion the adversarial needs to establish (that the ementa is external to the fundamentação-bearing components) rather than a neutral description of the ementa's status after accepting art. 943.

**Art. 93, IX CF's "decisões" covers the acórdão as a whole.**

The provision says "fundamentadas todas as decisões" — the decisão of a collegial court is the acórdão. The acórdão under art. 943 includes the ementa as a formal component. The scope limitation "fundamentação covers votos but not ementa" requires reading "decisões" as covering some formal acórdão components but not others. This asymmetric application requires a positive argument grounded in the text or doctrinal authority. Round 19 provides no such argument — the exclusion rests entirely on the "heading" description, which art. 943 acceptance forecloses.

**The internal tension within round 19.**

Round 19 accepted art. 943 (ementa is formal acórdão component) but maintained the scope limitation (fundamentação covers only votos). These are structurally inconsistent: accepting that all formal acórdão components are part of the decisão, while maintaining that the fundamentação requirement applies only to some of them, requires a distinction the adversarial does not establish.

**The sanction clause does not settle scope.**

The adversarial uses the sanction calibration ("calibrated for arbitrary decisions") to constrain what fundamentação covers. But the sanction clause specifies the consequence of unfundamentação; it does not determine which acórdão components must be fundamentada. Additionally, partial nullity for partial fundamentação defects is available in Brazilian constitutional doctrine — the sanction is not all-or-nothing.

**The accountability function.**

Art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação serves the accountability function: courts must give reviewable reasons. For precedent-producing courts in the Brazilian system, accountability operates through the ementa (the official published ratio statement binding subsequent courts under arts. 926-927). A fundamentação requirement indifferent to ementa accuracy while covering the votos inverts the accountability function relative to how citation compliance actually operates.

---

## What I considered and discarded

**Arguing a specific doctrinal authority for extending fundamentação to ementa accuracy.** I do not have primary-source access to specific STF decisions or scholarship establishing that art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação requirement reaches ementa-component characterization accuracy. The argument I am making is structural — it identifies the internal inconsistency in r19's position (accepting art. 943 while maintaining the scope limitation) rather than citing authority. This is the correct approach when the authority is unavailable without fabrication.

**Conceding the scope point and finding another provision.** I considered whether art. 926 caput ("os tribunais devem uniformizar sua jurisprudência e mantê-la estável, íntegra e coerente") might ground obligation (a)'s characterization-accuracy requirement through the coerência dimension. The adversarial r18 survey explicitly addressed art. 926 caput and found it covers coherence and stability over time (not per-ementa accuracy of individual characterizations). I cannot contest this characterization without primary-source access to the specific interpretation of art. 926 caput's "coerente" dimension in Brazilian doctrine. The structural argument from art. 943 + art. 93, IX CF is the stronger available path.

**Addressing the pre-answered coherence objection.** The adversarial r19 anticipated the coherence argument and pre-answered: "coherence under art. 93, IX CF targets voto-internal reasoning consistency, not heading-to-body consistency." My response does not directly engage the voto-internal/heading-to-body distinction on the adversarial's terms. Instead, I contest the "heading" description itself (which the art. 943 acceptance forecloses) and argue that "decisões" covers the acórdão as a whole. If the adversarial accepts art. 943, it cannot simultaneously maintain that the ementa is a "heading" — these positions are in tension, and the response presses that tension.

---

## Assessment

The r19 supportive response is structurally sound but not decisive. Its core strength is the internal tension it identifies within r19: accepting art. 943 while maintaining the "heading" vocabulary and the scope limitation is inconsistent. The adversarial must now either:

(a) Retract art. 943's acceptance and contest relator authorship (but this is factually weak and risks losing the ground conceded in r19)
(b) Supply a positive argument for why the fundamentação requirement applies to some formal acórdão components (votos) but not others (ementa) — a category distinction the text does not obviously support

If the adversarial cannot supply (b), the scope-narrowing argument stands unsupported by text or authority, and the art. 93, IX CF grounding for obligation (a) survives.

**Weakness:** The accountability function argument is structural, not doctrinal. A more powerful response would cite STF decisions or authoritative scholarship establishing that art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação requirement extends to the acórdão's ratio statement in the ementa. Without that authority, the adversarial could respond: "Even accepting the accountability function argument, accountability through the ementa is served by requiring the ementa to be accurate as a general norm (which the adversarial does not deny) — the question is whether that accuracy requirement is governed by art. 93, IX CF or by some other institutional norm. The accountability argument does not settle which provision governs."

**The open question:** Is the accountability argument sufficient, or does the adversarial have a response that accepts accountability without conceding art. 93, IX CF's scope? Round 20 will likely press whether the accountability argument extends to art. 93, IX CF specifically, or merely establishes that some accuracy obligation exists from some source.
