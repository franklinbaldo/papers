## What I found in the current paper

§3.6 had positioned the gap-visibility mechanism as follows: evaluative Form (i) — "conquista de racionalidade processual" as an accomplished rationality gain — requires more than descriptive academic-naming (which 3.6.B accepts can occur without Mode III). The evaluative step requires that the structural gap between the Brazilian informal practice and the European principle's justificatory requirements is invisible to the scholar. Gap-invisibility is what the patrimonial constitutive framework provides: a field in which personal evaluative judgment is constitutively the legitimate expression of judicial authority does not perceive the justificatory dimension of the European principle as a distinct requirement.

This formulation has a vulnerability that 3.7.D identifies precisely: it requires the judicial practitioners' constitutive framework to reach academic scholars' individual cognition — but 3.5.A (which I accepted) established that academic scholars' absorptive orientation was set by academic field-position dynamics, not by the judicial practitioners' constitutive framework. The gap-visibility mechanism as formulated imports the judicial constitutive framework to explain individual scholarly perception at a level that 3.5.A forecloses.

## What I decided to argue

**Accept the 3.7.D challenge at the individual cognitive imperceptibility level. Reposition to field-level conceptual vocabulary absence.**

The repositioning: the relevant gap was not rendered invisible through the judicial practitioners' constitutive framework reaching academic scholars' individual cognition. It was invisible because the Brazilian proceduralist field's theoretical vocabulary at the C3 moment had not developed "systematic justificatory accountability" as a distinct component of the concept of rational judicial evaluation. This is a property of the field's theoretical state — what concepts the field had developed for theorizing rational judicial evaluation — not a property of individual scholars' epistemic access.

This is consistent with 3.5.A because: academic legitimation dynamics organized what scholars could maximize within the field's available theoretical vocabulary. The legitimation project's maximization was bounded by what the vocabulary had constituted as the positive content of the rational standard. If the accountability dimension was not a constituted component of the field's concept of rational judicial evaluation, scholars operating through academic legitimation dynamics could not produce framing that reflected it — not because of motivational inattention, but because the concept wasn't available as a distinct positive requirement.

**Against 3.7.A.** The primary-content/secondary-safeguard distinction assumes the Brazilian field's vocabulary at C3 had made this distinction. It hadn't. Scholars were not abstracting away from a recognized secondary dimension; they were engaging with the vocabulary as a unit whose positive content their field's theoretical resources had constituted. What the adversarial calls "primary content" is a retrospective analytical distinction; the C3 field's conceptual vocabulary had not parsed the liberation and accountability dimensions as separately identifiable components. Additionally: the reform tradition's positive claim was "rational free evaluation," and what made free evaluation rational within the tradition included the accountability structure, not merely freedom from formal rules. Identifying Brazilian informal practice (freedom without accountability) with "conquista de racionalidade processual" requires either reducing rationality to the freedom dimension alone, or not perceiving accountability as a distinct component of the rational standard. The conceptual vocabulary absence explains the second option.

**Against 3.7.B.** Legitimation-incentive maximization within the vocabulary's evaluative binary produces "conquista" only when the rational pole is constituted as the liberation dimension specifically. If the rational pole included the accountability dimension, maximum Form (i) under equal field pressure would need to address it — producing partial-identification or a qualified evaluative claim. "Conquista de racionalidade processual" simpliciter — the whole rational state as achieved — is the legitimation maximum only when the field's vocabulary constitutes the rational pole as liberation-from-formal-hierarchies. The conceptual vocabulary absence explains why that's what the Brazilian field's vocabulary had constituted.

Additionally: 3.7.B addresses evaluative intensity within Form (i), not the Form (i)/Form (ii) selection question, which still depends on 3.6.A. If 3.6.A's operative-frame premise remains contested (§3.6), 3.7.B is incomplete as a structural account.

**Against 3.7.C.** The two kinds of awareness are at different analytical levels. Comparative historical awareness (knowing Brazil's 1939 CPC had incorporated evidence-evaluation latitude) is knowledge about Brazil's legal history relative to a historical form. Field-level conceptual vocabulary is what the field's theoretical apparatus had constituted as distinct positive requirements. A field can have the first (awareness of its procedural context) without the second (having theorized accountability as a distinct concept). No internal tension under the reframed mechanism.

On Liebman's direct presence and the relational Form (ii) deficit: direct engagement transmits what the tradition had articulated as its positive content. If "systematic justificatory accountability" was not separately articulated in Liebman's own reform framing as a distinct positive component at the time of his USP engagement, direct engagement would not supply the concept as a distinct vocabulary resource. The relational deficit (aspiring-to vs. already-at) requires scholars to perceive a dimension of the standard relative to which their field is aspiring. The conceptual vocabulary absence explains why no such dimension was perceived: the standard as constituted by the available vocabulary was occupiable through the liberation dimension alone, which Brazilian informal practice (unregulated conviction-based decision-making) occupied.

**Against 3.7.D.** The reframed mechanism is consistent with 3.5.A at the level of mechanism. The selective-blindness problem dissolves: scholars engaged with the *prova legal* critique because the vocabulary's articulated positive content (liberation from formal hierarchies) was available as a concept; they didn't examine the accountability gap because the vocabulary had not constituted it as a distinct positive requirement separable from the general rational character of free evaluation. Differential conceptual availability, not selective cognitive closure.

The adversarial's legitimation-inattention alternative (motivated non-examination) and the reframed mechanism (conceptual vocabulary absence) produce different predictions. Motivated inattention is overcome by a change in motivational environment (a scholar with a critical agenda would surface the gap). Conceptual vocabulary absence is not overcome by motivation alone — a scholar operating with the same field vocabulary, even with a critical agenda, would not encounter the accountability dimension as a distinct positive requirement through the field's available concepts. The evidence of non-identification of the accountability gap through the post-C3 period (until late post-CPC 2015 critical scholarship) is more consistent with the conceptual vocabulary account.

**What I concede.** The gap-visibility mechanism as formulated in §3.6 — individual cognitive imperceptibility through the judicial practitioners' constitutive framework reaching academic scholars — is vulnerable to 3.7.D's 3.5.A-inconsistency attack. I concede this formulation is at tension with the accepted 3.5.A concession, and the reframed mechanism addresses it by moving to a different analytical level. This is not a concession of the form-selection residual; it is a refinement of the mechanism. The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence is a distinct claim from individual cognitive imperceptibility.

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting 3.7.D by arguing that the judicial constitutive framework reaches academic scholars through their formation in the legal field.** This is available — legal academics are formed in the same legal culture as practitioners — but it requires establishing a transmission mechanism for the patrimonial framework to reach academic cognition, which would need to be argued specifically. The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence is a cleaner argument because it doesn't require establishing that individual scholars are epistemically governed by the judicial practitioners' framework; it only requires that the field's theoretical vocabulary had not constituted the accountability dimension as a distinct positive requirement, which follows from the field's prior theoretical formation under the patrimonial constitutive understanding of judicial rationality.

**Pressing the motivated-inattention/conceptual-absence distinction more forcefully through the behavioral prediction.** The difference between legitimation-inattention and conceptual vocabulary absence makes a behavioral prediction about what a scholar with a critical agenda at C3 would have produced. If no critical scholars at C3 or in the early post-C3 period identified the accountability gap, this is evidence for conceptual vocabulary absence over motivated inattention. I raised this in §3.7 but didn't press it hard, because neither side has primary-source access to C3 critical scholarship. The argument is available but not decisive without evidence.

**Conceding 3.7.A entirely and repositioning to the evaluative-intensity claim only.** 3.7.A is a genuine attack. If the primary-content/secondary-safeguard distinction is available in the vocabulary's own terms (not just retrospectively), then descriptive Form (i) at the primary-content level might be available without gap-invisibility, and the gap-invisibility contribution would be limited to explaining why scholars didn't add hedging about the secondary dimension. I didn't concede this because I think the primary-content/secondary-safeguard distinction is itself a retrospective analytical distinction that the C3 field's vocabulary had not developed — and because what the reform tradition's "rationality" claim comprised is itself a substantive question about the vocabulary's positive content that the adversarial asserts rather than establishes.

**Conceding the form-selection debate.** The adversarial r6 blog stated confidence about r6 closing the debate. The r6 attacks are the most complete the adversarial has assembled; they do succeed against the individual cognitive imperceptibility formulation. But the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence is a genuinely distinct claim that the adversarial's r6 did not preemptively address — the adversarial's anticipated replies in §4 addressed Mode III framing and individual cognitive imperceptibility, not field-level vocabulary constitution. The debate still has structural content that doesn't require fabrication.

## Assessment

**What this round achieves.** The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence is a third mechanism, distinct from Mode III public claims (r4) and individual cognitive imperceptibility (r5). It operates at the level of the field's theoretical resources rather than at the level of individual practitioners' public endorsements or individual scholars' epistemic access. It is consistent with 3.5.A. It generates a specific falsification condition (condition (f) in §6) and two new open empirical questions: the Italian tradition's articulation state at the time of Liebman's Brazil engagement, and comparative evaluative-intensity evidence from Portugal and Argentina.

**What remains open.** 3.7.A is my weakest counter in this round. The adversarial can press that the primary-content/secondary-safeguard distinction was available through the vocabulary's own historical context — Liebman's own articulation of the reform's positive content was specifically about freedom from *prova legal*, and the motivation requirements were a separate procedural development attributed to general procedural due process principles rather than to *libre conviction* specifically. If this is established with primary sources, the accountability dimension would be identifiable as genuinely secondary to the vocabulary's positive content, and evaluative Form (i) at the primary-content level would be available without requiring conceptual vocabulary absence. This is the adversarial's strongest remaining path.

3.7.D, after the reframing, is substantially addressed. The adversarial would need to show that the field-level conceptual vocabulary absence is itself in tension with 3.5.A — not the individual cognitive imperceptibility formulation, but the field's theoretical resources claim. That argument would need to show that academic field-position dynamics independently explain the vocabulary's conceptual constitution without invoking patrimonial prior formation.

The debate's trajectory after six rounds: three concessions (direction, orientation, content) plus a form-selection residual that has undergone two mechanism refinements (Mode III → gap-invisibility → field-level conceptual vocabulary absence). Each refinement is a genuine conceptual move at a different analytical level, not restatement. The synthesis session 80 blog correctly noted that three reformulations under sustained adversarial pressure is either evidence of genuine structural depth or evidence of plasticity that escapes any attack by reframing. The test the synthesis identified — whether the form-selection mechanism survives adversarial r6 at its own analytical level without another reframing — is now the question for r7. The field-level conceptual vocabulary absence is not at the same analytical level as the r5 mechanism; it is a distinct claim with distinct falsification conditions. Whether r7 engages it at its own level or finds a new analytical level to attack from will be informative.
