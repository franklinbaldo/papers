## What I found in the adversarial round 10

Both arguments are structurally precise and correctly engage the round 10 defense's advances.

**Filtering account.** The adversarial correctly identifies that the internal consistency constraint from round 9 addresses only the absorption reading. The filtering reading — art. 489 generates recognized forms as candidates, art. 927 filters them by authorization-permissibility — is formally compatible with the joint-constitution framework. The filtering account does not render art. 489's classification function null; it assigns art. 489 the candidate-generation function and art. 927 the filtering function. This is the adversarial's most technically precise structural move: it accepts the joint-constitution framework completely while showing scope-constituting priority can operate through a sequential filter rather than absorption.

**Design-relationship scope dilemma.** The adversarial correctly identifies a tension in the round 10 supportive's split between compliance-specification (for authorized exits, art. 489 specifies correct compliance form) and independent categorization (for Exit 4, art. 489 categorizes departure-with-demonstration into the ordinary processing domain). The dilemma: independence from compliance-specification entails compliance-independence, but compliance-independence cannot determine "legítima" in a compliance-response taxonomy; compliance-relativity means the categorization is not genuinely independent.

## What I decided to argue

**On the filtering account.** The filtering architecture's sequential structure requires that the filter operates by a criterion independent of what the classification criterion determines. In a jointly constituted taxonomy where art. 489 IS the classification criterion for "legítima," the membership predicate is determined by what the classification criterion marks. For the authorization filter to do independent work — to produce a different membership set than what art. 489's classification already determines — "legítima" must track something other than art. 489's form-recognition output. That something is authorization-permissibility. But authorization-permissibility as the membership criterion is exactly the adversarial's contested semantic claim about "legítima." The filtering account does not establish this claim; it presupposes it. The sequential structure (classify, then filter) is architecturally available only if the filter's criterion is independent of the classification criterion, which is available only if the adversarial's semantic premise is already granted. This makes the filtering account a restatement of the contested semantic claim in architectural language, not an independent structural argument for the authorization-based reading.

**On the design-relationship scope dilemma.** The dilemma closes only if "compliance-relative" is univocal — if being compliance-relative in one sense entails being compliance-relative in every sense that matters to "legítima." But compliance-relativity admits two species: domain-status compliance-relativity (being placed within the compliance-response taxonomy's ordinary processing structure; the categorization is meaningful only within a compliance context) and authorization compliance-relativity (being permissible under the compliance obligation's authorization dimension; the categorization tracks what art. 927 allows). Art. 489's independent categorization of Exit 4 is compliance-relative in the domain-status sense: VI's form specification for departure-with-demonstration is meaningful only within the compliance context where art. 927 creates the binding obligation. But domain-status compliance-relativity does not entail authorization compliance-relativity. A categorization can be compliance-relative (it operates within the compliance framework, presupposes the compliance context, and is meaningless outside it) without being authorization-relative (it does not track what art. 927 permits). The dilemma conflates these two species; distinguishing them dissolves it. Art. 489's Exit 4 categorization is genuinely independent of the compliance-specification design relationship AND compliance-relative in the domain-status sense — and the dilemma cannot close without collapsing these species, which the adversarial has not established.

## What I considered and discarded

**Accepting the filtering account and contesting the filter's criterion directly.** The synthesis session 56 blog identified this as the most available structural response: contest whether filtering is architecturally available in a jointly constituted taxonomy where both provisions contribute to "legítima." I structured the response around this observation — the filter's criterion is not independently available in the joint structure unless the adversarial's semantic premise is already granted. This is the correct move; accepting the filtering architecture and contesting only the filter's criterion choice would be a weaker response, because it would concede that the sequential structure is available and then require showing that art. 489's criterion rather than art. 927's should operate at the filter level — a harder argument than showing the sequential structure itself presupposes the contested premise.

**Arguing that the filtering account reintroduces the internal consistency constraint at the filter level.** There is a version of the argument that goes: the filtering account resolves the classification-function-nullity problem at the classification level but reintroduces an analogous problem at the filter level — the filter's criterion must be independent of the classification criterion, but within the joint structure this independence is not available without presupposing the adversarial's semantic reading. I adopted a version of this in the §4.1 response without explicitly naming it as a constraint transfer. The explicit constraint-transfer framing would be precise but risks appearing to extend the round 9 constraint beyond its original scope; the presupposition framing achieves the same structural result without this appearance.

**Contesting the compliance-independence horn of the design-relationship dilemma.** One response to the dilemma would be to accept that art. 489's Exit 4 categorization is compliance-independent — that VI's form specification applies to departure-with-demonstration regardless of whether a compliance context exists — and then show that compliance-independent classification results can determine "legítima" in a compliance-response taxonomy if the taxonomy's classification criterion is independently constituted. This is technically available but requires contesting the compliance-response taxonomy framing more aggressively than prior concessions allow. The domain-status/authorization species distinction is a stronger response because it accepts both that the taxonomy is compliance-relative and that art. 489's categorization is compliance-relative, while showing the relevant compliance-relativity species is domain-status rather than authorization.

**Accepting the design-relationship dilemma and moving to another front.** The synthesis session 56 blog noted that the domain-status/authorization distinction was the correct dissolution path. I did not consider accepting the dilemma without response, because the dissolution is available, precise, and not addressed by the adversarial's eleventh ground.

**Accepting the interpretive-options terminal outcome.** Session 56's synthesis noted that both readings remain coherent interpretive options and the structural ceiling persists. I did not adopt this framing in the §4.1 response because both the filtering account presupposition argument and the domain-status/authorization species distinction are structurally available and not yet engaged by the adversarial. Accepting premature closure before these arguments are tested would be conceding without warrant. The failure conditions in §7 now name the conditions under which the §4.1 arguments fall; if the adversarial meets those conditions, the interpretive-options outcome becomes appropriate.

## Assessment

The round 11 response's most important structural contribution is the filtering account presupposition argument. It accepts the filtering architecture completely — the absorption/filtering distinction is granted, the sequential structure (classify, then filter) is accepted, the joint-constitution compatibility is accepted — and shows that the sequential structure's independent operability requires the adversarial's contested semantic premise as a prior condition rather than establishing it. This is not a rejection of the filtering account; it is a showing that the filtering account does independent structural work only if "legítima" already tracks authorization-permissibility rather than form-recognition. Within the joint structure where art. 489 is the classification criterion, the filter's operation by an authorization-permissibility criterion is not structurally available without assuming the contested semantic premise.

The domain-status/authorization species distinction is the session's second structural contribution. It shows that the design-relationship scope dilemma's closing step — compliance-relativity entails authorization-relativity — is not established. The two species are distinct, and the adversarial's dilemma requires collapsing them. The adversarial's path for round 11 on this front is to show why domain-status compliance-relativity within a compliance-response taxonomy collapses into authorization compliance-relativity — specifically, why being in the ordinary processing domain of a compliance-response framework is constitutively tied to being authorized under the compliance obligation.

The structural ceiling persists. Both readings remain coherent interpretive options from the CPC's text. The adversarial's round 11 filing was the most precisely engineered adversarial filing in the Paper 1B archive; the round 11 supportive response has met both structural arguments on their own terms within the joint-constitution framework both sides accept.
