## The three responses

### (bb) — concession of ambiguity, then a definition

R38 wrote "independent" without fixing its sense. R39 read it as *justice-independence* (would J2 have reached G2 without J1's elaboration?) and built the five-justice hypothetical on that reading. The sense §§4.19–4.22 had actually been tracking is *ground-independence* (does the voto rest the dispositif on G2 alone, or on G2-and-G1, or on G2-given-G1?). Marinoni draws exactly this line — grounds "autônomos ou independentes — capazes de, cada um por si, gerar determinado resultado — ou agregados." Justice-independence is a criterion no provision imposes, and the designation rules point the other way: CPC art. 941 ("autor do primeiro voto vencedor") and RISTF art. 135 §4º ("primeiro voto prevalecente") take the first voto of the winning group as the court's voto and count the followers as votes for its ground. Under that reading r39's hypothetical is the paradigm of a majority-backed ground, not a doubtful case.

What I refused to claim: the biconditional r39's (bb) demands — "independent iff counted toward the dispositif threshold." It is false (an aggregated-ground voto counts toward the dispositif but not for G alone) and r38 never asserted it. The correct structure is per-voto textual property → count → threshold. Answering (bb) by accepting its reduction would have won the condition and lost the argument.

### (cc) — accept the adversarial's standard, then meet it from the sources

This is the round's main move. R39 argued that determinability is not existence and that the aggregation act is generative even under full determination. Rather than defend r38's "determinability is what presence tests," I accepted r39's pre-existence standard and withdrew the determinability argument as load-bearing. What the texts show is that r39 misidentified the aggregation act: under CPC art. 941 the President proclaims the result *after* the votes and *before* designating the redactor; §1º fixes the votes at the proclamation; RISTF art. 135 §2º has the President proclaim the decision; RISTF art. 97 makes the *extrato da ata* — containing "a decisão proclamada pelo Presidente" and the redactor's name — a formal acórdão component; RISTF art. 96 §4º has the acórdão and ementa drafted within ten days after the session. So the court-level fact is asserted, by the court, in its own document, before the designation exists. That meets r38's failure condition (z) in the explicit-assertion form r39 said the standard requires.

The verdict analogy fails on its premise (the "proved facts" are the product of the judge's reasoned evaluation under art. 371; judgment intervenes, which is why art. 93, IX reaches it) and on its location (the generative event, if there is one, is the proclamation by the court, not the relator's later drafting).

Honest limit, stated in the paper: in the fragmented case the proclamation asserts the result and the redactor, not a majority ground. What exists at court level before designation there is the *negative* fact that no ground reached the majority. Marinoni's proposal that the proclamation should state the ratio alongside the decision, and the STF's *tese* vote in repercussão geral (presupposed by CPC arts. 985 and 1.039), are the institutional form of the point. For fragmented-no-*tese* plenaries the ementa's ratio designation contradicts a pre-existing court-level fact — an elevation error the count detects — rather than generating one.

### (dd) — withdrawal, then relocation

R38 asserted that the *regimento interno* "specifies how many independently unconditional invocations of an element constitute collective autonomous support for that element." Checked against arts. 135, 143, 173 and 185–187, it does not. Art. 173's six-vote threshold is on the *sentido* (the result). The only provision applying six votes to an *interpretation* is art. 185 (with a §2º runoff between the two most-voted interpretations) — and that governs the *representação interpretativa* of EC 7/1977, not received by the 1988 Constitution. R39's categorical distinction is correct as a description of the *regimento*. Withdrawn.

The threshold is relocated to the majority-on-grounds rule Marinoni states in terms: a ratio "só pode ser formada pela maioria do colegiado"; six of eleven on one ground make a precedent, five do not; a minority-elected ground cannot be the ratio. Its *number* is the dispositif majority the *regimento* fixes; its *object* is each ground, counted over ground-independent votos. Of r39's three "competing accounts": plurality is rejected in terms and inconsistent with the word *determinante*; the independently-sufficient-majority account *is* this rule; the most-broadly-shared account is a scope question (art. 927 application, C4), not an identification criterion, and r39 gave no source for it as one. The convergence (dd)(ii) asked for is restated correctly: same number, different object.

## Discarded alternatives

- **Defending r38's determinability argument on (cc).** I could not make "determinability is what presence tests" survive r39's verdict analogy without contesting the deliberative-fact standard itself, and the sources made the defense unnecessary: the fact is asserted before designation. Accepting the adversarial's standard and meeting it is stronger than winning a dispute about the standard.
- **Using RISTF art. 185 as the "regimento supplies a ground-level threshold" source.** The provision is real and does exactly what (dd)(i) asks — six votes on an *interpretation*, with a runoff — but it belongs to an extinct instrument. Presenting it as live would have been the dishonest defense the protocol warns against. It appears in §4.23 only as evidence that the *regimento*'s drafters distinguished result-thresholds from interpretation-thresholds, which supports r39's distinction, not r38's claim.
- **Claiming doctrinal unanimity for the majority-on-grounds rule.** Not claimed; §4.23 says doctrine is not unanimous and STF practice has extracted *teses* from non-converging plenaries. The support needs only a determinate leading standard.

## Concessions made

1. R38's "independent" was ambiguous (bb) — now defined.
2. R38's determinability-suffices argument is withdrawn as load-bearing (cc) — replaced by the proclamation/ata argument.
3. R38's claim that the *regimento interno* specifies a *fundamento*-level threshold is withdrawn (dd) — r39 is right about what the *regimento* says.

## Remaining uncertainty

- Whether "nos termos do voto do Relator" in the proclaimed decision is authoritatively construed as adoption of the relator's *grounds* (not just the dispositif) — I have relied on the redactor rules and practice, not on a decision saying so. Named in failure condition (cc).
- How large the fragmented-no-*tese* class is among high-adversarial-record STF precedents — an empirical question the pre-calibration pilot's elevation-error flagging would measure. Named in (cc).
- Whether the majority-on-grounds count is indeterminate in practice because of the level-of-generality problem (when do two votos state "the same" ground?). Named in (dd). This is, I think, the adversarial's best route back.
- The RISTF consolidation used runs through ER 49/2014; the articles relied on (97, 135, 143, 173) are long-stable, but a later emenda could have renumbered or amended them.
