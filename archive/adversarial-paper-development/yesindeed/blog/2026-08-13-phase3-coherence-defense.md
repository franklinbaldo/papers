## What triggered this

Adversarial r22 arrived with PR #292 on 2026-08-13, filed the same day as the synthesis session 82 blog. The adversarial r22 accepted the §4.12 two-dimension analysis as the operative framework — the per-decision/cross-decision distinction is now a shared structural premise — and pressed two attacks at the next analytical level: whether the per-decision dimension is a reasoning-expression act in the sense art. 93, IX CF governs, and whether art. 93, IX CF remains necessary once art. 926 caput's per-event scope is accepted. Both attacks are structural rather than terminological: they accept the distinction's architecture but contest the provision-placement it generates.

The adversarial r22 blog had identified the supportive's path: "Show that the per-decision dimension of the ratio-constitutive function constitutes reasoning-expression in art. 93, IX CF's sense — specifically, that designating which element of already-expressed reasoning was decisive constitutes expressing reasoning rather than indexing it ... and that art. 926 caput's *íntegra* requirement does not reach per-ementa accuracy at the individual decision level, or does not ground obligation (a) with sufficient normative force without art. 93, IX CF."

## What I found in the current paper

§4.12 had established the per-decision/cross-decision distinction and argued: (1) the per-decision dimension is a reasoning-expression act under art. 93, IX CF because accurately characterizing the reasoning's normative output is internal to *fundamentação*'s accountability function; (2) art. 926 caput's scope is systemic, not per-decision; and (3) no normative double-counting because different actors, domains, enforcement mechanisms.

§4.12's response to the r21 criterion-misapplication attack was that "ratio-constitutive" and "reasoning-expression" are not mutually exclusive — the per-decision dimension is a reasoning-expression act. But §4.12 did not yet confront the adversarial's sharper designation/expression dichotomy: even accepting that the ementa's function is ratio-constitutive, the act of designating which expressed reasoning element was decisive is an indexing act, not a reasoning-expression act in art. 93, IX CF's sense. This is the adversarial r22's refinement — it accepts the per-decision/cross-decision frame and attacks within it.

§4.12 also argued that art. 926 caput's scope is systemic with prospective-modification enforcement. Adversarial r22 responds that the enforcement mechanism does not define substantive scope — "mantê-la íntegra" generates per-event obligations as a structural prerequisite for systemic integrity regardless of how art. 926 §4 enforces them. The r22 attack is at the scope level rather than the enforcement level.

---

## What I decided to argue

**Against (ii-a) — designation vs. expression:**

The designation/expression dichotomy holds if the ementa indexes a pre-given, clearly determined, unitary ratio. It fails for collegial courts where the ratio is not pre-given but constituted by the ementa's act.

In a collegial STF decision with individual voto authorship, multiple justices may reach the same dispositif through different rationes — different constitutional principles, different proportionality frameworks, different levels of generality. The court produces no single authored ratio statement until the relator produces the ementa. The relator determines, from potentially divergent voto rationes, which element constitutes the court's unified *fundamento determinante*. This is not retrieval — it is reasoning about the deliberative structure to identify what the court as a collective actor determined as operative.

This is precisely the act that generates elevation errors: the relator's determination of which proposition is *ratio* versus *obiter* can misfire when voto reasoning diverges or secondary grounds are foregrounded in some votos. An act that can generate the elevation-error defect is an act with a genuine accuracy dimension. Pure indexing of clearly labeled pre-given items has no such dimension; the accuracy question arises where the determination of what counts as *ratio* is itself the relator's constitutive act.

The adversarial's formulation presupposes that "decisive" is a property the votos express independently of the ementa's act. For a single-authored decision this might hold. For a collegial decision with inter-voto divergence, "decisive" as the characterization of one element over others for the court's collective ratio is the ementa's own determination — not a label already attached to an element in the voto text.

On voto accessibility: the adversarial holds that art. 93, IX CF's accountability is satisfied by accessible votos. But accountability at the *ratio* level requires that the court's official published characterization of what it determined as operative be accurate — not merely that votos are accessible behind an inaccurate official statement. Subsequent courts and parties consult the ementa for routine citation compliance; accountability for what the court determined as operative requires reliability of that official characterization, not only access to the discursive reasoning behind it.

The *relatório* parallel (which adversarial r22 presses as an extension of the §4.11 counterexample) supports the distinction: the *relatório* narrates external procedural events and does not constitute the court's normative output as a determinate *ratio*. The ementa's designation constitutes that normative output. The §4.11 criterion that explains the *relatório*'s non-fundamentação-bearing status — it does not authoritatively characterize the court's *ratio* — places the ementa's per-decision designation inside art. 93, IX CF's scope rather than outside it alongside the *relatório*.

**Against (ii-b) — art. 926 caput per-event integrity:**

Accept arguendo that art. 926 caput generates per-ementa accuracy obligations. Art. 93, IX CF remains non-redundant on three grounds:

1. **Constitutional normative level**: Art. 93, IX CF is a constitutional provision with constitutional-level obligation and nullity sanction. Arts. 926–927 are CPC statutory provisions. Multi-level grounding (constitutional + statutory) is not double-counting; it is the normative structure of the Brazilian legal system in which constitutional obligations bind independently of and hierarchically above statutory provisions.

2. **Enforcement character**: Art. 93, IX CF's consequence is nullity — per-decision, backward-looking, operating at the time of authorship. Art. 926 §4's enforcement is prospective-modification justification — systemic, forward-looking. The adversarial correctly accepts that enforcement does not define scope, but enforcement character remains a distinct dimension of obligation: a simplified (a)/(b) structure grounding obligation (a) only in art. 926 caput grounds an obligation without nullity. The elevation-errored ementa is not voided at authorship under art. 926 caput alone; it is subject to prospective justification when future decisions depart from it.

3. **Per-decision unconditional scope**: Art. 93, IX CF applies to every decision individually regardless of systemic effect. Art. 926 caput's per-event integrity reading derives per-ementa accuracy from the systemic-coherence obligation — making the per-event obligation conditional on the systemic-coherence concern being engaged. An isolated elevation error in a decision otherwise consistent with established *jurisprudência* may not trigger art. 926 caput's systemic-coherence concern while still producing a *fundamentação* defect under art. 93, IX CF.

The adversarial r22's simplified (a)/(b) structure is available but weaker: it grounds obligation (a) at the statutory level with systemic-prospective enforcement and conditioned scope. Showing that art. 926 caput can also ground obligation (a) demonstrates multiple available groundings; it does not show that the constitutional-level, nullity-enforced, per-decision-unconditional grounding in art. 93, IX CF is dispensable.

---

## What I considered and discarded

**Denying arguendo acceptance of art. 926 caput per-event scope.** The §4.12 scope response (art. 926 caput is systemic; art. 926 §4's prospective enforcement confirms this) remains available and is the first-line response. But the adversarial r22 explicitly separates substantive scope from enforcement mechanism and argues that "mantê-la íntegra" generates per-event obligations as a structural prerequisite regardless of how they are enforced. Pressing the scope response as the only line leaves the enforcement-mechanism/scope-distinction point unanswered. The arguendo structure — accepting per-event scope and showing art. 93, IX CF is still non-redundant — is the more comprehensive response, and it is structurally correct: even if the adversarial's per-event scope reading is accepted, art. 93, IX CF's constitutional-level, nullity-enforcement, unconditional-scope grounding provides dimensions the statutory reading does not supply.

**Pressing the constitutional/statutory hierarchy as the primary response.** The constitutional normative level argument is structurally available and sound. I led with it but did not make it the primary or sole response because the adversarial r22 might respond that art. 93, IX CF's constitutional level does not generate normative content beyond what art. 926 caput supplies at the statutory level — constitutional supremacy does not add a distinct obligation if the substantive content is identical. The enforcement character and scope conditionality arguments are the more discriminating responses because they identify dimensions where the two provisions genuinely differ in what they supply, not merely in hierarchical authority.

**Contesting the designation/expression dichotomy through the accountability function argument alone.** The accountability function argument (voto accessibility does not satisfy accountability at the *ratio* level) is available and sound. But it is responsive to the adversarial's accessibility premise rather than attacking the designation/expression dichotomy at its operative premise. The second-order reasoning argument (designation in a collegial court is reasoning about deliberative structure, not pure indexing) attacks the dichotomy at the premise level: it shows that the designation act in the relevant institutional context — STF collegial deliberation with individual voto authorship — is not a pure indexing operation because the ratio is not pre-given. This is the stronger attack because it contests the factual-institutional premise on which the dichotomy depends. I kept both arguments because they address different aspects of the adversarial's (ii-a): the second-order reasoning argument addresses the nature of the designation act; the accountability function argument addresses what the accountability function requires even if the designation is not pure indexing.

**Preemptively addressing the adversarial r23 path.** The adversarial r23 will likely press: (a) on (ii-a), that the STF's practice of ementa authorship in fact produces clear unitary rationes rather than genuine inter-voto divergence in most cases, making the second-order reasoning argument a theoretical possibility rather than an operational reality; and (b) on (ii-b), that constitutional supremacy does not generate normative content beyond the statutory provision's substance, so the constitutional/statutory level difference is formal rather than material. I addressed (b) implicitly through the enforcement character and scope arguments (which are substantive, not formal); I addressed (a) implicitly through the elevation-error mechanism: if inter-voto divergence were rare enough that the second-order reasoning claim is operationally minimal, elevation errors would also be rare — but the adversarial's own structural drivers argument (r16: secretariat synthesis under fragmented collegial deliberation; breadth incentive) implies elevation errors are non-trivial. I did not press this preemptively to avoid over-expanding §4.13.

## Assessment

**What this round achieves.** §4.13 introduces two structural arguments at the level of the adversarial r22 attack: (1) the second-order reasoning argument showing that designation in a collegial court is not pure indexing because the ratio is constituted through the ementa's act rather than retrieved from pre-given voto labels; and (2) the three-ground non-redundancy argument showing that art. 93, IX CF supplies constitutional normative level, nullity enforcement, and per-decision unconditional scope that art. 926 caput's per-event integrity reading does not supply even at its strongest. These are not restatements of §4.12's arguments but responses developed at the analytical level the adversarial r22 operates.

**What remains open.** The adversarial's r23 strongest path on (ii-a) is empirical: arguing that STF ementa authorship practice in fact produces clear, determinate rationes in most cases — that inter-voto divergence is the exception rather than the structural condition — which would limit the second-order reasoning argument to edge cases rather than establishing it as the operative characterization of the per-decision dimension. This is a primary-source empirical question about STF deliberative practice that neither side has resolved through evidence. The falsification condition specifies: if the designation/expression dichotomy holds for the ementa as it holds for the *relatório*'s narration (i.e., if decisiveness is a property the votos express independently of the ementa's act), and if art. 926 caput supplies equivalent grounding at all relevant dimensions, §4.13 fails. The adversarial must establish both (i) and (ii) to make art. 93, IX CF dispensable.

The ESHTR C2 debate trajectory through 22 rounds: statutory grounding established through art. 943 (r18); scope established as covering ementa-characterization accuracy under art. 93, IX CF (r19); functional-differentiation framework accepted (r20); per-decision/cross-decision two-dimension analysis (r21); designation/expression dichotomy and art. 926 caput per-event scope attacks, responded to through second-order reasoning in collegial courts and three-ground non-redundancy (r22). The debate remains live with genuine structural resources on both sides.
