## What changed in the position

### Against (gg) — the concession, then the relocation

R45 pressed a gap in the priority rule (non-coincidence covers contradictions, not compatible expansions). Checking the rule's current text showed there is no rule to have a gap: ER 26/2008 replaced art. 96, and the current regime makes the *voto escrito* released by the justice's office the ordinary voto text, with the audio transcript as fallback. §4.25's "the published elaboration is legally subordinate to the session record" is withdrawn for the STF. Both sides had been reading the 1980 wording from mirrors; r45's diary said "the rule text says what it says." It did not say that any more.

What survives, each with its own source: (1) the *vote* is fixed at proclamation — CPC art. 941, §1º, and now the STF's own Plenary in ADI 2.949-QO; (2) the transfer to the majority is fixed by session adherence (art. 135), which r45 accepts and which needs no priority rule; (3) the regiment sequences voto release (art. 96, §2º) before ementa drafting (§4º), with art. 95, §3º showing the ementa's dependence on the winning voto's text in the limiting case; (4) in the virtual plenary — the large majority of collegiate decisions since 2019 — the session text *is* the lodged voto text, so the composition holds as written for that arm.

The residual class is stated: physical-session plenary, relator's released voto narrows what it presents as result-sufficient relative to the voto as read, relator's vote pivotal for the ground's majority. Where the adherents alone form the majority, the relator's revision cannot move the majority ground; where the revision does not touch sufficiency, it does not touch the count's input. The class is unobservable from the published acórdão and measurable only against the session audio; its size is a pilot measurement.

For the STJ, where RISTJ art. 103, §1º carries the sentence the STF repealed, r45's scope argument is answered on the merits: the STJ applies the rule to determinate divergences on the disputed point (REsp 991.721; REsp 1.107.543/SP) and not to formal contradictions only; a written voto making the result depend on C where the session voto rested it on G alone is such a divergence on the count's point, and r45's example, read for sufficiency, is a contradiction. For STF precedents nothing in that paragraph is claimed.

### Against (hh) — the within-voto press

R45 asks whether J5's ground is P or P4 once J5's premise-transfer theory is fixed. The count's unit — the shared result-sufficient proposition (§4.24) — decides it without an individuation choice: "this case has P4" is not result-sufficient in J5's own chain; "P holds here" is, and so is the conjunction of the norm with the transfer finding, which entails it. J5 contributes the nested chain {P-here, R∧P4-here}, the object §4.25 named for the between-voto case; which element to *state* is the ementa's scope choice under (hh-2), which r45 accepts. The "decisive element" reading confuses what an argument turns on with what carries its result, and the CPC keeps them apart in the V/VI split the exchange settled at round 16: the transfer condition is art. 489, §1º, VI's applicability question; V asks for the norm applied. Conceded: (hh)(a) as r45 wrote it is not met and not needed; a voto that states the transfer finding without stating the norm is a flagged-class voto, pilot-measured with the cumulative-grounds class.

## Discarded alternatives

- **Answering r45's scope argument for the STF on the "não coincidir" wording alone.** It is a good textual answer (coincidence is identity of teor, not consistency of propositions), and it is kept for the STJ. Offering it for the STF would have meant arguing about the trigger of a repealed sentence.
- **Arguing that the audio transcript is still "the" record under new art. 96 caput, so that the session still fixes the text.** The caput does make the audio the primary record, but §§2º–3º make the released written voto the ordinary text of the acórdão and the transcript the fallback, with no ranking between them. The stronger reading was not available honestly.
- **Claiming that revision of a written voto is confined to material corrections (old §3º / new §6º).** That provision governs correction of the *decision*, not the content of a justice's own released voto; no holding read confines revision.
- **Treating the virtual-plenary share of the docket as the virtual share of the calibration arm.** The *destaque* mechanism selects contested cases into physical sessions; the arm-specific share is a pilot number.
- **Citing the 2024 virtual-share figure from the STF's panel.** Search excerpt only; not used.
- **Citing STF Informativo 800's HC (Gilmar Mendes ordering the STJ to join the notes or repeat the judgment).** The item was seen only in search excerpts, without a case number; not used.

## Concessions made

1. §4.25 quoted RISTF art. 96 in its 1980 wording; Emenda Regimental 26/2008 replaced it and the current article has no priority rule. "Fixed by the session record, which prevails over the published text" is withdrawn for the STF.
2. At the STF the released written voto is the regimental voto text; the relator can elaborate it after the session and no rule ranks the reading above the release.
3. A residual class exists where the count's input is not fixed against the relator: physical session, sufficiency-changing revision, pivotal relator vote. Its size is unmeasured.
4. The virtual share of the high-adversarial-record calibration arm is unknown and likely below the docket-wide share.
5. (hh)(a) as r45 formulated it is not met; the individuation level is fixed by the count's unit and chain output, not by the voto's theory.
6. A voto stating only the transfer finding, without the norm applied, is a flagged-class voto.

## Remaining uncertainty
