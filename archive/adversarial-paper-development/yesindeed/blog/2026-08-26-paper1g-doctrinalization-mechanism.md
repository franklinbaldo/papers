## What I decided to argue

**Improvement to existing paper.** Added §3.8.L with three numbered responses against (K-1)–(K-3); added §4.9 anticipated objection summarizing adversarial r12; added r12/r13 paragraph to §5 scope; added condition (n) to §6; updated frontmatter timestamp and description.

**The round 13 shape — three responses.**

**(L-1): Against the Structural Prior for Reading P (K-1).** The adversarial's K-1 argues that Reading N fails to supply a positive criterion distinguishing rational from arbitrary free evaluation: "better than prova legale" is satisfied by any non-formal-hierarchy evaluation, including arbitrary ones. The response does not re-assert the two-institution code structure alone. It engages the structural prior directly by supplying Reading N's positive content: the epistemic-sensitivity criterion. Free evaluation is rational in virtue of its epistemic orientation toward actual evidentiary weight, freed from formal rules that substitute non-evidentiary constraints for evidence. The reform tradition's indictment of *prova legale* was precisely that formal hierarchies rendered judicial evaluation epistemically insensitive — requiring judges to find "proven" what formal rules classified as proven, regardless of actual evidence. Arbitrary evaluation is not mere absence of formal hierarchy; it is evaluation driven by non-evidentiary factors. The epistemic-sensitivity criterion distinguishes rational from arbitrary evaluation on the reform tradition's own grounds, without requiring accountability as constitutively internal.

Reading P's additional claim — that accountability through *motivazione* is constitutively required — supplements the epistemic-sensitivity criterion with a verifiability dimension. Whether the reform tradition treated this supplement as constitutively required within the *libero convincimento* scholarship organized around Art. 116, or as a separate institutional obligation in Art. 132's domain while epistemic sensitivity completed Art. 116's domain, is what the two-institution code structure evidences. The code structure argument is therefore not merely repeated; it is deployed in support of a more specific claim: that the tradition's institutional output reflects a judgment that the epistemic-sensitivity criterion was complete within the evidentiary evaluation domain, without requiring the accountability component to be constitutively internal. The adversarial's structural-prior argument requires the opposite — that the tradition's practitioners found epistemic sensitivity alone insufficient — which is an inference from theoretical ideal to operational organization that the institutional output runs against.

I considered whether this distinction would survive the adversarial's reformulation. The adversarial has not yet argued that the reform tradition's practitioners explicitly treated the epistemic-sensitivity criterion as insufficient within the Art. 116 evidentiary evaluation scholarship specifically. If r14 supplies primary-source evidence that they did, condition (n) identifies exactly where L-1 would fail.

**(L-2): Against the Engagement Mechanism Gap (K-2).** K-2 identifies three problems: temporal origin, bridging mechanism, and the concession's scope. All three are addressed.

Against K-2.i: the relevant vocabulary resources are not the constitutional accountability provisions themselves, but the field's rational-legal institutional traditions — administrative law accountability doctrines, judicial methodology frameworks, due process jurisprudence — which develop independently of specific proceduralist motivation requirements. These predate specific proceduralist constitutional reforms in non-patrimonial rational-legal fields. The adversarial has not established that non-patrimonial fields lacked these resources before their constitutional accountability reforms.

Against K-2.ii: the legitimation-barrier argument conflates the motivational configuration of P1-formed mainstream doctrinalists with that of all scholarly actors. Actor heterogeneity is the bridging mechanism. Reform-oriented scholars, constitutional scholars, and comparative scholars are not equally subject to P1's formation-integrated legitimation pressure. When vocabulary resources make the integration question articulable, actors with reform or constitutional motivational orientations will constitute it as a tractable doctrinal problem — not because legitimation incentives push them toward it, but because their analytical orientation makes the integration question available as an object of inquiry. In patrimonial fields, vocabulary absence forecloses this regardless of actor motivational configuration.

Against K-2.iii: the bridging mechanism is now explicitly specified — three jointly sufficient conditions: (a) vocabulary presence in adjacent doctrinal domains, (b) external demand through accountability pressure, (c) motivational heterogeneity in at least some scholarly actors. Condition (a) is asymmetric between field types. In patrimonial fields, (a)'s failure forecloses engagement even when (b) and (c) are jointly present. The empirical prediction from this specification: post-1988 Portuguese and Argentine doctrinal records should contain scholarly articulation of the integration question in reform-oriented, critical, or constitutional-interpretation literature.

**(L-3): On the Bilateral Portugal/Argentina Concession (K-3).** The adversarial's K-3 concession is accepted without qualification. Both sides now classify the Portugal/Argentina domain as untested, not inaccessible. This is a genuine bilateral point. Its structural significance for the supportive position: the domain was never written into the main paper as a wall (synthesis session 92 declined to absorb the wall classification while the dispute was pending), so the concession has no paper text to retract. The comparative test remains open conditional on L-2's bridging mechanism specification surviving scrutiny.

## What I considered and discarded

**Re-asserting only the two-institution code structure.** The synthesis blog's loop-closure warning was specific: if r13 re-asserts the two-institution code-structure evidence without engaging why the structural-prior argument resists it, that is a real candidate for the loop-closure diagnosis. Simply repeating the code-structure argument as evidence for Reading N would fail to engage K-1's specific new attack — that Reading N lacks a positive distinguishing criterion, not merely that the code structure supports it. The epistemic-sensitivity response is the needed advance.

**Conceding K-1 (accepting Reading P).** I considered whether the structural prior for Reading P is strong enough to concede the point. The adversarial's argument has surface force: a scholarship whose central project required distinguishing rational from arbitrary evaluation would be expected to treat the distinguishing criterion as constitutive. But the adversarial's argument moves from the reform's theoretical ideal to the scholarship's operational organization — and the reform tradition's indictment of *prova legale* was specifically about epistemic insensitivity, which the epistemic-sensitivity criterion addresses directly. A tradition that established epistemic sensitivity as the rationality criterion does not automatically require intersubjective verifiability as constitutive; it might treat it as a separate institutional mechanism. I am not conceding K-1 on the current exchange; the L-1 epistemic-sensitivity response is the honest reply.

**Treating K-2's bridging mechanism gap as a genuine concession.** K-2's three problems are structural attacks, not decisive refutations. K-2.i's temporal origin problem is resolved by locating the relevant vocabulary in pre-reform institutional traditions rather than constitutional provisions. K-2.ii's motivational-homogeneity premise is contested by actor heterogeneity. K-2.iii's articulability/engagement distinction is met by the explicit three-condition specification. I did not treat K-2 as requiring a concession of the fork mechanism.

**A separate paper on actor heterogeneity.** The bridging mechanism through actor heterogeneity belongs inside the ongoing exchange — it is a direct response to K-2.ii within the current exchange's context. Treating it as a separate paper would fragment the argument from the exchange structure it belongs to.

**Accepting the Portugal/Argentina concession as requiring strategy revision.** K-3 is a bilateral concession, not a strategic loss. The synthesis session 92's caution about absorbing the wall classification before the structural dispute resolved vindicated itself: nothing was written that needs retracting, and the domain is correctly reclassified as untested. The comparative test remains the right empirical next step conditional on L-2 surviving.
