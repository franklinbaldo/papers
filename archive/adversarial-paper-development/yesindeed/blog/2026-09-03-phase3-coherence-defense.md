## The Round 37 Shape — Three Level-Shifts

### Against (v/y): Generation/derivation at the institutional-collective level

R37 accepts that the generation/derivation distinction is correctly applied at the individual-justice level — J4's voto and J5's voto pre-exist the designation, and their content is derivable by reading them. But it argues that art. 93, IX CF's "fundamentadas" requirement applies to the court's institutional determination, not to individual-justice positions. No individual voto asserts the court-institutional fact "G2 is this court's *fundamento determinante*." At that level, the generation characterization holds: the designation produces a court-institutional fact that does not exist before the relator's act.

### Against (w/z): Constitutability-versus-presence

R37 accepts §4.21's constitutive-existence framing as the correct question but contests the answer. J4's voto asserts J4's individual-justice position; J5's voto asserts J5's. No voto asserts the court-institutional fact "G2 received this court's autonomous collective support." The individual-justice positions are the evidentiary inputs from which that court-institutional fact can be constituted — not the fact itself. Constitutability from the votos is not the same as presence in the votos.

### Against (x/aa): Normative interpretive judgment in unconditionality and autonomy determination

R37 accepts that within-voto-expressed conditionality is detectable from voto texts. But it presses two dimensions §4.21's threshold-decomposition does not close. First, implicit dependence: whether A's G2 reasoning depends on B's G1 elaboration requires assessment of voto reasoning structure that may not be determinable from A's explicit argument chain. Second, deliberative autonomy: determining whether G2 "alone would have been sufficient for the outcome" is a counterfactual that the *regimento interno*'s majority-resolution threshold does not supply — that threshold governs when a decision can issue, not when a reasoning element has autonomous deliberative support.

## What I Decided to Argue

### Against (y): The Court-Institutional Determination Is Aggregative, Not Generative

R37's level-of-analysis move is correct: the accountability requirement applies to the court's institutional determination, not to individual-justice positions taken separately. The question is whether, at that institutional-collective level, the designation generates new normative content or reports what the applicable aggregation rule, applied to the voto facts, already yields.

The response is that the court-institutional determination is aggregative: count the independently unconditional G2 invocations; check whether the count meets the applicable threshold. Before the relator acts, J4's unconditional G2 invocation, J5's unconditional G2 invocation, and the threshold rule together determine the output with full specificity. The designation formally expresses what the rule-plus-inputs combination already yields — it is derivation at the institutional-collective level, structured as an aggregation over individual-voto facts rather than a derivation from any single voto.

The verdict contrast holds at this level: before jury deliberation, no conjunction of prosecution submissions and applicable rules mechanically determines "guilty." The verdict's reasoning chain is generated through normative deliberation. The ratio-designation's output is determined before the act by applying a counting rule to observable voto facts.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Arguing that the institutional-collective level is simply not the right level for art. 93, IX CF's purposes. This would require contesting the constitutional provision's scope — that "fundamentadas" addresses voto-internal reasoning expression only, not the court's institutional determination. I cannot establish this at r38's stage without primary authority, and the synthesis blog's signal was to engage the institutional-collective level argument on its own terms rather than restate the individual-justice-level position. The aggregative-not-generative response engages on those terms.

Conceding (y) and concentrating on (z) and (aa). I could not persuade myself that the aggregation point is unavailable. If the applicable rule plus the voto facts fully determine the output, the court-institutional determination is derivation under a different description, not generation. This is the correct structural ground.

**New failure condition (y):** If the relator must exercise normative judgment beyond counting-rule application — specifically, if determining which ground is the *fundamento determinante* requires weighing among grounds each endorsed at the applicable threshold with no rule yielding a single output, or if the aggregation rule requires normative interpretation of "independent" invocation — the institutional-collective determination is generative rather than aggregative.

### Against (z): Collective Constitution From Individual Positions Satisfies "Presence"

R37's constitutability-versus-presence distinction is genuinely the sharpest formulation of the (w) challenge — it accepts §4.21's constitutive-existence framing and contests the answer rather than the question.

The response is that "presence" in the deliberative-fact standard tracks what the deliberation produced. Deliberation's output under the aggregation rule is constituted by the individual-justice positions: if J4 unconditionally grounds G2 and J5 unconditionally grounds G2, and the threshold rule says two independent unconditional invocations suffice, then the court-institutional collective-support fact is fully constituted by those inputs under that rule. Asking whether this fact is "present" in the votos is asking whether it is what the deliberation produced — it is.

R37's evidentiary-record/institutional-fact distinction has most purchase when the inputs do not fully determine the product — when the designation requires normative selection among candidates or judgment beyond aggregation. Where the inputs fully determine the product under the applicable rule, constitutability under that rule is what the deliberative-fact standard's "presence" requirement tests.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Contesting whether there is a genuinely distinct court-institutional level at all. This would require arguing that the aggregation of individual-justice positions IS the court-institutional fact without any gap — denying the institutional-collective level that the functional-differentiation framework, and R37's press, require. This is a stronger claim than I need and collapses the analytical structure the debate has built. The collective-constitution-satisfies-presence response accepts the level distinction and contests how the deliberative-fact standard applies at it.

**New failure condition (z):** If the deliberative-fact standard's "presence" requirement demands explicit assertion at the court-institutional level by some pre-designation document — so that facts constitutable from individual positions under an aggregation rule are not "present" absent such an assertion — the §4.22 response fails and R37's constitutability-versus-presence distinction holds.

### Against (aa): Structural-Textual Implicit-Dependence; *Regimento Interno* Operationalizes Autonomy

Two responses, one per sub-press.

**Implicit dependence:** Whether A's G2 conclusion depends on B's G1 elaboration is a logical-structural reading question: do A's stated premises for G2 include propositions drawn from B's voto, or does A's reasoning chain for G2 stand on premises A states independently? This is a dependency-structure analysis on the voto texts. It is more difficult than within-voto-expressed conditionality detection, but difficulty of reading is not generation of normative content. The analysis accesses information in the voto texts; it does not produce content not derivable from them.

**Autonomy criterion:** R37 is correct that the *regimento interno*'s majority-resolution threshold tells the court when a decision can issue, not when a reasoning element has autonomous deliberative support. But the *regimento interno* also specifies how many independently unconditional invocations of an element constitute collective autonomous support for that element as the *fundamento determinante*. That specification, combined with the unconditionality and independence facts textually determinable from the votos, yields the autonomy determination without further normative judgment. The relator asks: did enough justices assert G2's dispositif-sufficiency unconditionally and independently? The answer is read from voto texts (unconditionality, independence) and the applicable threshold rule (number required). R37's counterfactual formulation — "whether G2 alone would have been sufficient" — is operationalized by this textual test, not by constructing a hypothetical deliberation.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Conceding the implicit-dependence sub-press and contesting only the autonomy sub-press. I examined whether implicit-dependence detection systematically requires normative judgment in Brazilian STF practice. The question is whether A's load-bearing premises are identifiable from A's voto's explicit argument chain. In principle, if A's stated reasoning is in A's voto, dependency-structure analysis is textual. The concern is about cases where A's premises are implicit — where A's reasoning relies on B's elaboration without stating that reliance. I cannot establish from structural argument that this is systematically absent. But I also cannot concede it without evidence that it is systematically present. The structural-textual response sets the correct condition: if implicit-dependence detection requires normative architecture judgment not derivable from voto texts, the response fails; if it is a reading operation (harder than explicit-conditionality detection, but textual), it does not generate normative content.

On the *regimento interno* autonomy point: I considered whether the *regimento interno* actually specifies the number of unconditional invocations required for *fundamento determinante* autonomous support as opposed to decision-disposition. If the *regimento interno* only specifies how many justices must agree for a decision to issue, and not how many must invoke a specific element for it to be the *fundamento determinante*, the threshold-rule response requires clarification. I have not resolved this from primary sources. The new failure condition (aa) names this as the condition under which the response fails: if the applicable *regimento interno* or other applicable rule does not supply the number criterion for autonomous-element support (as distinct from majority-decision resolution), the autonomy determination is not mechanically computable.

**New failure condition (aa):** Fails on two grounds: (1) if implicit-dependence detection systematically requires normative judgment about epistemic architecture not determinable from voto logical structure; (2) if the operative sense of "autonomous" support requires counterfactual judgment not derivable from unconditionality + independence of actual voto positions.

## Assessment After R37

**For (y):** R38 argues the institutional-collective determination is aggregative. R39 (adversarial) would need to show either (a) that the aggregation rule itself requires normative interpretation of "independent" invocation at the institutional-collective level — not resolved by within-voto-expressed conditionality detection — or (b) that the applicable threshold rule does not mechanically yield a single *fundamento determinante* when multiple grounds each receive independent unconditional support at the threshold number. Either would establish that the institutional-collective determination is generative rather than aggregative.

**For (z):** R38 argues collective constitution under the applicable aggregation rule satisfies the deliberative-fact standard's presence requirement. R39 would need to show that the standard requires explicit court-level assertion, not just full determination by individual positions plus applicable rule. The clearest route would be a structural argument that the deliberative-fact standard's purpose (tracking what the designation identifies versus what the designation generates) requires a pre-designation document to assert the court-institutional fact, not merely to supply the inputs from which it is constitutable.

**For (aa):** R38 argues structural-textual implicit-dependence detection and *regimento interno*-operationalized autonomy. R39 has two routes. For implicit dependence: establish that Brazilian STF votos in practice exhibit A-reasons-by-silent-reliance-on-B at a frequency that makes the structural-textual response non-operational. For autonomy: establish that the *regimento interno* threshold specifies decision-disposition majority, not element-autonomous-support threshold, and that no applicable rule supplies the latter.
