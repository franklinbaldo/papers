## What triggered this

The synthesis session 87 blog named ESHTR C2 r25 the primary obligation for the supportive routine. Adversarial r25 was the sole adversarial filing outstanding, advancing two challenges: (iv-a) pressing the *relatório*-function-type parallel against §4.14's act-character response, and (iv-c) pressing the doctrinal grounding gap for the internal-consistency reading of art. 93, IX CF. The adversarial accepted (iv-b) — the actor-level (a)/(b) separation — ending any remaining dispute on that front.

---

## What the adversarial r25 pressed

**Against (iv-a) — location-in-process ≠ function-type:** The §4.14 act-character criterion establishes that the relator's collegial ratio-determination is embedded in the art. 943 acórdão-authorship process. But the *relatório* is also embedded there and relator-authored. Location-in-process ≠ function-type. A function-type argument showing why the ementa-authorship act's function falls within art. 93, IX CF's reasoning-expression domain — as distinct from the *relatório*-authorship act's function — was required.

**Against (iv-c) — doctrinal grounding gap:** The §4.14 argument that an acórdão with ementa-voto inconsistency fails art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentação* requirement as an internally inconsistent decision-document is a proposed construction of the provision. No STF precedent or authoritative Brazilian procedural doctrine establishing this as an independent nullity category has been supplied.

---

## What I decided to argue

**§4.15 — two responses, one acceptance, one honest concession with a preservation claim.**

**Against (iv-a):** The function-type argument is not new to §4.14. It was built progressively across §§4.11–4.14. §4.11 established ratio-constitutive function as the operative criterion and distinguished the ementa from the *relatório* by function-type. §4.12 identified the per-decision dimension as the dimension tracking art. 93, IX CF. §4.13 showed the per-decision act involves second-order deliberative reasoning with a genuine accuracy dimension. §4.14 applied the act-character criterion.

The adversarial r25 identifies "location-in-process" as the §4.14 basis. But §4.14's act-character criterion is a function-type criterion — it asks what the deliberating court's act constitutively does within the acórdão-production process, not where in the production sequence the act is formally located. Both the ementa and the *relatório* are located at the same formal position in art. 943's sequence; they differ in function-type. §4.11 already established this: the *relatório* counterexample established functional differentiation as the operative framework, not formal component membership. The function-types — case narration (*relatório*) and per-decision normative output expression (ementa) — are distinguishable under the criterion both sides accepted at round 20. The *relatório* parallel confirms rather than defeats the distinction.

**Against (iv-c):** Accept the gap. The internal-consistency reading of art. 93, IX CF is a proposed construction. Primary authority has not been supplied. But the adversarial's counter-claim is equally ungrounded for the elevation-error scenario specifically. Both readings are textually available constructions of "decisões... serão fundamentadas" for a scenario that appears unresolved in Brazilian constitutional doctrine. The debate reaches an evidentiary wall on the art. 93, IX CF scope question.

The evidentiary wall affects the constitutional grounding of obligation (a), not the (a)/(b) structure. Round 25 accepts the actor-level separation. Art. 926 caput provides statutory-level grounding for obligation (a) — the adversarial's own round 22 structure proposed this. The residual dispute is not whether obligation (a) exists but whether art. 93, IX CF's nullity sanction additionally reaches ementa-designation accuracy as a *fundamentação* defect category. That question is, on current argument, unresolved.

---

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the evidentiary-wall framing for (iv-c) and arguing for the internal-consistency construction.** I could have pressed the §4.14 reasoning more strongly — the provision covers "decisões," the acórdão is the decision, the ementa is a formal component under art. 943, therefore inconsistency between formal components is a decision-level defect. This structural inference is available from the text. But the adversarial's counter-inference (voto adequacy satisfies the provision regardless of ementa-component inconsistency) is equally available from the text. Without primary authority, continuing to press the inference risks converting an honest evidentiary wall into a looping argument about structural probability. Accepting the gap and preserving the (a)/(b) structure through art. 926 caput is more honest and more stable.

**Claiming the function-type argument was already fully made in §4.14.** It was built across §§4.11–4.14, but §4.14 itself emphasized the "act-character criterion" and the embedding-in-the-authorship-process framing that the adversarial reasonably reads as a location argument. §4.15 makes explicit what was implicit: the act-character criterion is a function-type criterion, and the *relatório*'s case-narration function-type and the ementa's per-decision-normative-output-expression function-type are distinguishable under it. This is clarification, not retreat.

---

## Assessment

The (iv-a) response is strong. The function-type argument has been in place since §4.11 — the adversarial r25 is pressing a clarification point, not uncovering a missing argument. §4.15 makes the function-type/location distinction explicit and shows the *relatório* parallel confirms rather than defeats the distinction.

The (iv-c) response is honest but limits the framework. If art. 93, IX CF's nullity sanction does not reach ementa-voto inconsistency, obligation (a) has only art. 926 caput's statutory grounding with systemic-prospective enforcement. That is a weaker basis for the (a)/(b) structure than the constitutional nullity grounding would be. The debate is at an evidentiary wall, and neither side can resolve it by argument alone without primary-source doctrinal authority on this specific defect category.

What survives regardless of (iv-c)'s resolution: the (a)/(b) two-actor structure, the constitutive account for obligation (b), the art. 926 caput grounding for obligation (a) at the statutory level, and the functional-differentiation framework for the ementa's art. 93, IX CF coverage question (which turns on whether the internal-consistency reading is accepted). The debate's center of gravity has shifted from "does obligation (a) exist?" (conceded) to "which provision grounds obligation (a) for the elevation-error defect category?" — a narrower question more tractable in principle with primary-authority evidence.
