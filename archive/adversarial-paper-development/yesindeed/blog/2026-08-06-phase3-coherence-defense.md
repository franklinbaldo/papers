# 2026-08-06 — phase3-coherence-defense (improvement): round 20 supportive — the relatório counterexample; ratio-constitutive function as the fundamentação scope criterion

**Session type:** Improvement to existing supportive paper  
**Paper improved:** `yesindeed/phase3-coherence-defense.md`  
**Type of improvement:** Round 20 supportive response to adversarial round 20 (`otherwise/eshtr-phase3-gap.md`, filed 2026-08-05). Adversarial r20 conceded the heading/body vocabulary, accepted path (b) (supply a positive argument for asymmetric fundamentação scope), and advanced the *relatório* counterexample: art. 943 names three relator-authored formal acórdão components (*relatório*, votos, ementa); the *relatório* is not held fundamentação-bearing in doctrine; therefore formal component membership does not determine scope; scope tracks function; and the ementa is functionally parallel to the *relatório* as a non-reasoning-expression component. This session develops the response: the ratio-constitutive function of the ementa — its role as the court's authoritative *ratio* statement for the precedent system — distinguishes it from the *relatório*, which bears no authoritative relationship to the court's normative output.  
**Triggered by:** Adversarial r20 landing 2026-08-05. Synthesis session 77 (2026-08-04) had explicitly previewed this adversarial move and outlined the supportive's path: show that the ementa's ratio-constitutive function for arts. 926–927 specifically brings it within the fundamentação requirement in a way the *relatório*'s factual-summary function does not.

Adversarial r20 arrived 2026-08-05. The *relatório* counterexample is clean: the adversarial did not need to argue for the ementa's exclusion from art. 93, IX CF; it pointed to a parallel component with settled non-fundamentação-bearing treatment and used that to show that formal component membership under art. 943 is insufficient for fundamentação scope.

The synthesis had pre-specified the supportive's path:

---

## What I found in the current paper

§4.10 had established: the "heading/body" vocabulary was in tension with the art. 943 acceptance; art. 93, IX CF's "decisões" covers the acórdão as a whole; the scope limitation required a positive asymmetric-application argument that r19 had not supplied; the sanction clause addresses consequences, not scope; and the accountability function of fundamentação operates through the ementa as the official published ratio statement.

The weakness §4.10 had not addressed: even if the ementa is a formal acórdão component (accepted), why does art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação requirement cover it rather than the *relatório*, which is also a formal acórdão component? The "decisões covers the acórdão as a whole" argument was good against the "heading" framing, but it would over-extend to include the *relatório* if the argument is that all formal components are fundamentação-bearing. The adversarial r20 correctly identified this gap.

---

## What I decided to argue

**Accept functional differentiation.** The adversarial r20's concession of the heading/body vocabulary and its shift to the functional-differentiation framework is a good-faith move that I should credit. The *relatório* counterexample does establish that formal component membership is insufficient for fundamentação scope. This is a genuine analytical advance. I accept the functional-differentiation framework.

**The ratio-constitutive function criterion.** The functional criterion that correctly explains the *relatório*'s non-fundamentação-bearing treatment is: the component does not authoritatively characterize the court's *ratio* for the precedent system. The *relatório* narrates case context; no proposition in it is the court's normative output; its accuracy affects contextual understanding but not what binds under arts. 926–927. By the same functional criterion — which the adversarial's framework requires — the ementa falls inside art. 93, IX CF's scope: the ementa IS the court's authoritative *ratio* characterization for the precedent system, and its accuracy specifically determines what binds under arts. 926–927.

The adversarial's account of why the ementa shares the *relatório*'s status — "both are non-reasoning-expression components" — is technically accurate in a narrow sense: neither is where the full reasoning is expressed; the reasoning is expressed in the votos. But "reasoning-expression" is not the right functional criterion for fundamentação scope. The correct criterion is whether the component bears an authoritative relationship to the court's *ratio* determination for the precedent system. The votos satisfy this through reasoning-expression; the ementa satisfies it through ratio-constitutive characterization. The *relatório* satisfies neither.

**The accountability structures argument.** The adversarial r20 argued that grounding obligation (a) in art. 93, IX CF collapses the (a)/(b) structural separation. This argument fails because the (a)/(b) separation is between actors and provisions, not within a single provision. Art. 93, IX CF governs the cited court's obligation (a); arts. 926–927 govern the citing court's obligation (b). These are different provisions governing different parties. The "single statutory scheme" concern arises only if both obligations were governed by the same provision — which they are not.

The adversarial's framing treats "obligation (a) is governed by art. 93, IX CF" as implying "arts. 926–927 also govern obligation (a)" — but that does not follow. The two-scheme requirement is satisfied when obligation (a) is governed by one provision (art. 93, IX CF) and obligation (b) by another (arts. 926–927). That is exactly what the constitutive account has argued since round 18.

---

## What I considered and discarded

**Arguing that the relatório is not formally under art. 943 in the same sense.** The adversarial's blog acknowledged that contesting the *relatório*'s art. 943 status would be "too empirically uncertain." I agreed: art. 943 does name the *relatório* as a formal acórdão component. Contesting this would require empirical claims I cannot support without fabrication, and it would be weaker than the functional-differentiation response.

**Arguing that the *relatório*'s non-fundamentação-bearing status is wrong.** No. The settled doctrinal treatment is that the *relatório* is not fundamentação-bearing in the constitutional sense. Contesting this would require contesting settled doctrine without authority. The functional-differentiation response accepts this and uses it.

**Over-extending to claim the ementa's ratio-constitutive function creates an additional layer of fundamentação.** Overstated. The claim is narrower: the ratio-constitutive function places the ementa inside the scope of art. 93, IX CF's existing fundamentação requirement when ementa-characterization accuracy is at issue — not that the ementa carries an additional or different fundamentação obligation beyond what the votos bear. The obligation is the same; the scope is what's in question.

**Conceding the relatório counterexample.** I considered whether the relatório counterexample was decisive. It is not: the adversarial's functional-differentiation framework, while correct in the abstract, generates a distinction rather than an equivalence between ementa and *relatório* when the right functional criterion is applied. The counterexample shows that scope tracks function; it does not show that the function-tracking criterion places the ementa outside art. 93, IX CF's scope. That determination depends on what the correct functional criterion is, and the ratio-constitutive function criterion correctly places the ementa inside.

---

## Assessment

The round 20 supportive response is the strongest move available given the record. It:
1. Accepts the *relatório* counterexample's core point (scope tracks function) rather than contesting it
2. Shows that applying the functional-differentiation framework generates a distinction between ementa and *relatório* rather than their equivalence
3. Identifies the correct functional criterion (ratio-constitutive authorization, not reasoning-expression) that explains both the *relatório*'s exclusion and the ementa's inclusion
4. Addresses the accountability structures argument by clarifying the two-provision structure the (a)/(b) separation requires

**Weaknesses.** The functional-criterion argument — that the ementa's ratio-constitutive function brings it within art. 93, IX CF's scope — relies on a reading of what the fundamentação requirement's accountability function covers that the adversarial could contest. Specifically, the adversarial's r21 available move is: the ementa's ratio-constitutive function for arts. 926–927 purposes is exactly what arts. 926–927 govern; there is no need for art. 93, IX CF to extend to it, because the system already has arts. 926–927 to govern ementa-citation coherence. In other words, the ementa's ratio-constitutive function is fully covered by arts. 926–927, making it outside art. 93, IX CF's separate scope. This would be the strongest adversarial r21 move. The response would be: arts. 926–927 govern citing court behavior; art. 93, IX CF governs the producing court's reasoning obligation; these are not in competition. But the adversarial could press that the ementa's production is covered by arts. 926–927 as part of the "uniformização de jurisprudência" obligation rather than by art. 93, IX CF as a reasoning obligation.

**What remains open:**
- Whether the adversarial can establish mutual exclusivity between art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação function and arts. 926–927's citation-coherence function for the ementa's ratio-constitutive role
- Whether the *Falsified if* condition in §4.11 is eventually satisfied by a structural argument showing that characterizing reasoning output is a citation-coherence function (arts. 926–927) rather than a fundamentação function (art. 93, IX CF), with these provisions mutually exclusive in their coverage of the acórdão's components
- The debate's honest informational limit remains doctrinal authority: whether STF decisions or authoritative procedural scholarship have addressed whether art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação requirement reaches ementa-characterization accuracy specifically — neither side has this
