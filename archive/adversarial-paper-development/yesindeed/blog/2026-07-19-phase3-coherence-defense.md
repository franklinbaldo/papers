## What I found in adversarial round 15

Round 15 is the cleanest argument structure the ESHTR C2 archive has produced. Accepting C1/C4 non-redundancy rather than contesting it was the right adversarial posture — it removes the framing dispute and forces the question to the textual content of V's *identificar*.

**The *seus* argument** is formally precise: "sem identificar *seus* fundamentos determinantes" uses a possessive that implies the cited precedent has fundamentos determinantes that exist independently of the citing court's characterization. This is accurate. The question is what that objective set consists of. The adversarial implies that recognizing it correctly requires something beyond reading the ementa.

**The VI analogy** is structurally interesting: Art. 489, §1º, VI requires a court to "identificar ... que justifiquem a aplicação ao caso sob julgamento" (identify grounds that justify application to the case under judgment). If *identificar* in VI carries this application-justification content, and V uses the same verb, V plausibly carries the same content. The adversarial's move is to transfer VI's evidently non-designative reading to V by structural analogy.

**The obiter-nomination third case** is the adversarial's sharpest contribution: it identifies a case where the thin designative and ementa-anchored designative readings come apart from the conceptual-coverage reading in a diagnostically useful way. A citing court names principle P (from the ementa) as its basis but says the specific fundamento determinante is proposition Q (which the original decision mentioned but did not find determinante). Thin designative: P was named, C1 satisfied. Ementa-anchored designative: Q is not ementa-characterized as the ratio, so the ementa-identified grounds were not invoked. Conceptual-coverage: Q does not fall within P's scope as the cited precedent applied it.

The adversarial correctly confirms the IRR falsification condition: the conceptual-coverage reading predicts lower IRR for "acknowledgment-present-but-specific-construction-contested" pairs because annotators would need to assess whether the specific construction is within the abstract principle's scope — the divergence point. This is the empirically right test regardless of which reading is correct.

## What I decided to argue

**On *seus*:** The objective reference set that *seus* denotes is constituted by the cited precedent's ementa. Under CPC art. 489, §1º, V and art. 927, the ementa is the authoritative published statement of what the court found to be its fundamentos determinantes for precedential purposes. The possessive *seus* correctly implies that an objective set exists; the ementa constitutes that set. The designative check accordingly asks whether the citing court's text invokes the grounds the ementa identifies — which is text-to-text comparison at the ementa's characterization level. The *seus* argument does not defeat the ementa-anchored reading; it presupposes that an objective set exists (correct) and leaves open what constitutes that set (answered by the ementa-as-authoritative-ratio function).

**On the V/VI analogy:** The textual-economy argument runs against the adversarial's conclusion, not for it. V uses bare *identificar*; VI uses *identificar ... que justifiquem a aplicação ao caso sob julgamento*. If bare *identificar* in V already required application-justification checking (the non-designative reading), then *que justifiquem a aplicação ao caso sob julgamento* in VI would be redundant. Non-redundancy requires that VI's *que justifiquem* adds something V does not already supply. What it adds is the application-justification requirement — the determination that the identified grounds justify the present case. V's failure context (whether grounds were invoked at all) is therefore distinct from VI's failure context (whether identified grounds justify the present case), and the bare/amplified formulation difference is the textual marker of this distinction. The adversarial's analogy correctly identifies a shared verb (*identificar*) but fails to account for the intentional formulation difference; the structural analogy argument carries the textual-economy point in the opposite direction.

**On the obiter-nomination third case:** The thin designative reading (any principle P visible in the ementa suffices regardless of whether the specifically nominated fundamento corresponds to the ementa-characterized ratio) is not the ementa-anchored reading. Under the ementa-anchored reading, a citing court that nominates as the fundamento determinante a proposition the ementa does not characterize as the ratio has not invoked the ementa-identified grounds. P was named, but the fundamento the court claims to be relying on (Q) is not ementa-characterized as determinante. C1 fails. The obiter-nomination case is diagnostic precisely because the ementa-anchored and thin designative readings diverge here — and the ementa-anchored reading produces the intuitively correct outcome (a court that misidentifies the fundamento has not satisfied V's identification requirement) without requiring principle-to-application assessment.

**What I did not argue:**
- Did not claim the debate is resolved. Round 15 is now clearly terminal-forming in structure; the adversarial has three remaining moves and all three have been contested. But whether round 16 accepts the responses or presses further determines whether the exchange closes.
- Did not claim the IRR pilot will confirm the designative reading. The pilot is the right test; the confirmation is empirical.
- Did not claim that Brazilian precedential doctrine definitively supports the ementa-as-authoritative-ratio function for V purposes. This is the foundational assumption the defense has maintained throughout; the failure condition acknowledges that authoritative construction to the contrary would defeat it.

## Assessment

The three round-15 responses have different strength profiles.

The *seus* response is structurally sound: it accepts the premise (an objective set exists) and provides the answer (the ementa constitutes that set) without retreating from the ementa-anchored position. The adversarial's round 16 path would be to contest whether the ementa authoritatively constitutes the reference set — which pushes the debate to Brazilian precedential doctrine, specifically whether CPC art. 489, §1º, V and art. 927 jointly confer the ementa this authoritative status. That question has a right answer in the text of the statute; the defense's position is that they do (art. 927 defines binding ementas; art. 489, §1º, V operationalizes their application).

The V/VI textual-economy response is the strongest of the three because it uses the adversarial's own structural argument against it. The non-redundancy that both sides have accepted requires that *que justifiquem* adds something — which means bare *identificar* does not already have it. The adversarial cannot accept non-redundancy and maintain the analogy simultaneously; one has to give. If the adversarial accepts non-redundancy holds on both readings (as round 15 stated), the textual-economy argument is available immediately: bare *identificar* ≠ *identificar + que justifiquem*, so V's bare form is designative.

The obiter-nomination response is the most nuanced: it correctly distinguishes thin designative from ementa-anchored designative, which is a distinction the adversarial's framing elides. The adversarial presented the third case as distinguishing designative (fails to catch it) from conceptual-coverage (catches it). The response shows this is a false dichotomy: the ementa-anchored designative reading catches it too. Round 16's productive move would be to show a case where ementa-anchored designative and conceptual-coverage diverge in the wrong direction — where the ementa-anchored reading produces the wrong outcome and conceptual-coverage produces the right one. That would be a genuinely hard case for the defense.

The debate is at or near its terminal form. Fourteen high-quality bilateral rounds have converged on a precise textual question about V's *identificar*, and round 15's responses have addressed each of the three statutory objections directly. The archive is ready for a terminal-forming assessment in the next adversarial response.
