**The round 15 shape — two responses.**

**(Against M-1): Criterion content and verification mechanism are analytically distinct.**

M-1's attack: the epistemic-sensitivity criterion describes the judge's internal cognitive orientation, which is indistinguishable from the publicly observable judicial act without *motivazione*'s disclosure function; therefore the criterion is only publicly actionable when coupled with *motivazione*, presupposing Reading P's unified accountability structure rather than independently completing Reading N; the code-structure redeployment shows functional interdependence, not analytical independence.

The response makes three moves. First, M-1 conflates criterion content with verification mechanism: legal rationality criteria routinely specify internal standards that require external verification infrastructure without that infrastructure being constitutively internal to the criterion's content. The good-faith analogy: good-faith doctrine specifies an internal cognitive standard requiring external verification evidence without good faith collapsing into whatever verification mechanism applies to it. Second, M-1 proves too much: virtually all legal rationality criteria governing internal states would be "verificationist" by this logic, which cannot be the right conclusion for rationality criteria that are clearly independent of specific institutional verification designs. Third, the Italian CPC 1940's two-institution structure instantiates the criterion/mechanism distinction the adversarial's argument denies — the codifiers operating within the unified reform tradition nevertheless designed Art. 116 and Art. 132 as separate normative domains, which is the most direct available institutional evidence that the reform tradition recognized the criterion as analytically distinct from the verification mechanism.

The core move: functional necessity for external operation (M-1 establishes *motivazione* is needed to make the criterion publicly actionable) does not establish constitutive internal dependence (that *motivazione*'s accountability function is part of what the criterion specifies). M-1 asserts the latter but only establishes the former.

New failure condition: (n) — already named after adversarial r12 — specifies primary-source evidence from the *libero convincimento* scholarship showing practitioners found the epistemic-sensitivity criterion substantively insufficient (not merely operationally dependent on *motivazione* for external verification).

**(Against M-2): Condition (a) requires Type-2 accountability concepts, which are asymmetric between field types.**

M-2's attack: Brazilian administrative law and due process doctrine constitute judicial accountability as a distinct positive dimension of judicial rationality in adjacent domains; all three bridging conditions are jointly symmetric; the mechanism's asymmetric prediction fails; the specific proceduralist vocabulary for constituting the integration question develops through the reform tradition itself (post-C3), not from the pre-constitutional adjacent domains L-2.i identifies — redeploying K-2.i's temporal-origin problem at a more precise target.

The response introduces the Type-1/Type-2 accountability concept distinction. Type-1: accountability as formal compliance obligation (judicial acts must contain X; judges must comply with Y). Type-2: accountability as positive dimension of what judicial rationality consists in (judging well is inherently an intersubjectively accountable practice). Condition (a) requires Type-2 vocabulary — the vocabulary for constituting the PROCEDURALIST integration question as tractable ("should the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive designatum incorporate the accountability dimension?") presupposes that accountability could be internal to rational evaluation rather than merely an external compliance requirement. Scholars with only Type-1 vocabulary can pursue compliance-framework responses without articulating the integration question in the form the bridging mechanism requires.

The Type-1/Type-2 distinction generates the asymmetry M-2 denies. Non-patrimonial rational-legal fields constitute judicial rationality as inherently intersubjectively accountable — judicial authority derives from the quality of legal reasoning — making accountability internal to judicial rationality and supplying Type-2 vocabulary through general institutional formation. Patrimonial fields constitute judicial rationality as personal authoritative judgment — judicial authority derives from institutional position — making adjacent-domain accountability concepts take the Type-1 compliance-constraint form.

The Brazilian post-1988 doctrinal record is diagnostic: Streck's constitutional proceduralism critique takes the form of a compliance-failure diagnosis and constitutional-correction demand (Type-1 in structure), not an integration-attempt engagement constituting accountability as internal to rational evaluation (Type-2). This is consistent with Type-2 vocabulary absence in Brazil's adjacent legal domains.

M-2's temporal-origin argument: correct at the level of vocabulary for answering the integration question (which develops through the reform tradition), but does not apply to vocabulary for constituting the question as tractable (a feature of general rational-legal institutional formation, predating specific proceduralist reform in non-patrimonial fields).

New failure condition (o): evidence from non-patrimonial peripheral fields' adjacent domains showing those domains' vocabulary was also primarily Type-1 prior to their constitutional accountability reforms would defeat the Type-1/Type-2 asymmetry claim.

## What I considered and discarded

**Conceding M-1 and focusing on M-2.** M-1 has surface force — the verificationist objection to internal-state criteria is a recognized challenge in legal theory. But the argument equivocates between two distinct claims: (a) *motivazione* is operationally necessary for making the criterion externally actionable, and (b) *motivazione*'s accountability function is constitutively internal to what the criterion specifies. M-1 establishes (a) and needs (b); the two-institution code structure argument supports the distinction between them. The good-faith analogy and the proves-too-much argument together show that (b) does not follow from (a). I am not conceding M-1 on this exchange.

**Arguing that M-2's temporal-origin point fails entirely.** M-2 correctly identifies that the vocabulary for answering the integration question (resolving it through the Liebman proceduralist framework) develops through C3 and P1 engagement. The stronger response is not to contest this but to distinguish it from vocabulary for constituting the question: the Type-1/Type-2 distinction operates at the constituting level, not the answering level. This move accepts M-2's temporal-origin point as correct at its own level while showing it does not apply at the level condition (a) targets.

**Treating the Type-1/Type-2 distinction as requiring a new paper.** The distinction can be made within §3.8.N because it directly responds to M-2's symmetry attack at the level of condition (a)'s specification. It does not require a separate formal paper — the distinction is operationally tight (compliance constraint vs. positive dimension of rationality) and M-2's own attack sets up exactly the question the distinction answers. Adding a new paper would fragment the argument from its exchange context.

**Accepting that the Brazilian due process record shows Type-2 vocabulary.** M-2 identifies Brazilian administrative law and due process doctrine as adjacent-domain vocabulary sources. The diagnostic move — pointing to the post-1988 Brazilian doctrinal record's compliance-failure structure (Streck) as consistent with Type-1 framing — is the correct response rather than contesting whether these adjacent domains supply accountability concepts at all. M-2 is right that they supply accountability concepts; the contest is over which type.

## Assessment after r15

The Paper 1G debate has now held a clean one-to-one-response shape across the r14/r15 exchange. Both M-1 and M-2 were engaged with new distinctions (criterion/mechanism; Type-1/Type-2) rather than restatements of prior responses.

The three live structural questions after r15:

**(M-1/N-1):** The criterion/mechanism distinction is the response's central move. If r16 shows that the criterion/mechanism distinction is itself unavailable at the Art. 116/Art. 132 level — specifically, that the reform tradition's practitioners did not recognize this distinction and treated the disclosure obligation as constitutively internal to what the evidentiary evaluation criterion specifies — condition (n) is the named failure. The distinction is held by the good-faith analogy and by the Italian CPC 1940's institutional design; r16 would need primary-source engagement from the *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically.

**(M-2/N-2):** The Type-1/Type-2 distinction is the advance over the prior exchange. If r16 shows that non-patrimonial peripheral fields' adjacent legal domains were also primarily Type-1 in structure prior to their constitutional accountability reforms — comparative evidence from Portuguese or Argentine administrative law, judicial methodology, or due process doctrine — condition (o) is the named failure. If r16 presses instead that the Type-1/Type-2 distinction is itself not clear or not asymmetric, the argument would need to show what specific features of non-patrimonial rational-legal institutional formation generate Type-2 concepts at the adjacent-domain level as a feature of that formation specifically.

**Loop-closure condition for r16:** restating M-1's verificationist charge without engaging the criterion/mechanism distinction and the good-faith analogy; or restating M-2's symmetry argument without engaging the Type-1/Type-2 distinction and the Brazilian post-1988 diagnostic.
