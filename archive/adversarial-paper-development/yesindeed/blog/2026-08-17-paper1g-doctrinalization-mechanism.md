# 2026-08-17 — paper1g-doctrinalization-mechanism: §§3.8.F–G response to adversarial r8

**What triggered this:** Adversarial r8 landed 2026-08-16 (`otherwise/paper1g-transplant-specification.md`, §§3.8.D–E). It attacked version (i*) with two structural moves: (§3.8.D) the Possibility A/B dilemma — either the Italian *libero convincimento* scholarship externalized the accountability dimension (Possibility A, eliminating patrimonialism's differential contribution) or it internalized it (Possibility B, collapsing version i*'s structural inference); (§3.8.E) the selection-problem relocation — even accepting version (i*)'s relocation of the relevant question to the institution-to-concept integration boundary, the structural account applies there with equal force, and P1 can sustain non-integration post-C3. Synthesis session 86 identified Paper 1G r8 as the primary obligation, with three available response paths: (a) accept Possibility A and show patrimonialism contributes to durability not production; (b) contest the structural prior for Possibility B; (c) primary-source evidence (not feasible without fabrication). This session executes paths (a) and (b) as §§3.8.F and §3.8.G.

## What I decided to argue

**Improvement to existing paper — new §§3.8.F and §3.8.G; new §4.7; extension to §5 scope-revision paragraph; two new §6 failure conditions (k) and (l).**

The two r8 attacks are structurally distinct and each needed its own response. I did not consolidate or trade one off against the other.

**Against the Possibility A/B dilemma (§3.8.F): contesting the structural prior for Possibility B + fork argument under Possibility A.**

The structural prior for Possibility B is that the reform tradition organized its evidentiary evaluation debates around the rationality question, with *motivazione* as the rational predicate answer — so the scholarship would have engaged with accountability as internal positive designatum content. This conflates Q1 (what the reform tradition's theoretical logic requires as the predicate of rational evaluation) with Q2 (what the *libero convincimento* scholarship specifically deployed as the concept's positive designatum in its evidentiary evaluation debates organized around Art. 116). A theoretical framework can require freedom and accountability as jointly constitutive of rational evaluation (Q1) while the scholarly deployment of the evidentiary evaluation principle as an Art. 116 debate-organizing concept engages primarily with the judicial discretion and freedom dimension (Q2). The Art. 116 debates — about what evidentiary inferences the judge may draw freely, the scope of appellate review over evidentiary determinations — are answered by specifying the freedom dimension; the accountability dimension belongs to the Art. 132 debates. The Italian codifiers' two-institution decision is the most direct available institutional evidence that the tradition treated the two obligations as analytically distinct: a structural prior for Possibility B that derives from the reform's theoretical ideal (Q1) does not establish that prior for the concept's scholarly deployment as an evidentiary evaluation principle (Q2).

Under Possibility A (granted for argument): all receiving fields inherit non-integration equally. The adversarial's inference is that patrimonialism loses its differential contribution. But the relevant question is not whether non-integration was *produced* by patrimonialism — under Possibility A it was not — but what happens to non-integration over time under accountability pressure. Non-patrimonial receiving fields that inherit non-integration retain conceptual resources to recognize the integration question as distinctly available and to constitute the accountability dimension as internal to the evidentiary concept when demands arrive. Patrimonial fields that inherit the same non-integration naturalize it through prior formation: judicial rationality was constitutively understood as personal authoritative judgment, so non-integration is not experienced as a gap requiring resolution but as the natural state of the concept. The predicted trajectories diverge: non-patrimonial fields integrate; patrimonial fields produce external regulation and eventual concept-abolition. The Brazilian trajectory (art. 93, IX CF as a separate constitutional obligation with independent scope debates; CPC 2015 concept-abolition rather than concept-revision) is more consistent with the patrimonialism account's durability mechanism than with the structural account's reversal prediction.

**Against the selection-problem relocation (§3.8.G): mechanism distinction and behavioral prediction.**

Accepting the relocation to the integration boundary: both the structural account and the patrimonialism account predict non-integration there. They disagree on mechanism. Motivated non-integration (structural) is in principle reversible when motivational structure changes; the 1988 constitutionalization should produce integration if non-integration was motivated. Unconsidered non-integration (patrimonialism) is not simply reversible by incentive change: the integration question was not distinctly available in the field's conceptual vocabulary, so reversal requires conceptual vocabulary development that the incentive change alone does not supply.

The behavioral prediction applies at the integration boundary. Under motivated non-integration, the post-1988 predicted response is integration of the accountability dimension into *livre convencimento*'s positive content. Under unconsidered non-integration, the response is external regulation (art. 93, IX CF as a separate obligation) and eventual concept-abolition. The Brazilian post-1988 trajectory matches the second prediction.

The structural account can invoke P1 to explain persistence after 1988 (formation-integrated transmission sustaining motivated non-integration after the incentive shift). This is available but produces a hybrid: legitimation incentives produced motivated non-integration; P1 sustained it. The hybrid converges with the patrimonialism account's trajectory prediction while adding a mechanism stage the patrimonialism account does not require. The mechanism distinction retains analytical content but behavioral discriminability depends on comparative evidence where the accounts diverge — specifically, non-patrimonial peripheral fields where P1 may not be equally operative as a sustaining force.

## What I considered and discarded

**Attempting comparative evidence from Portugal and Argentina.** The comparative evidence would discriminate the accounts most directly. But neither side has produced this at the documentary level; I do not have primary-source access to produce it here without risk of fabrication. The comparative test is correctly identified as the primary open empirical question and flagged in §§3.8.F, §3.8.G, and the new failure conditions (k)–(l). I did not attempt to supply the comparison.

**Producing primary-source Italian evidence.** Path (c) from synthesis session 86. Not feasible in the session without fabrication risk. I left (j) as the existing failure condition for this and did not attempt to satisfy it.

**Conceding Possibility A entirely and relying only on the fork.** I could have conceded the structural prior for Possibility B and relied purely on the fork argument. But the structural prior is contestable from within the exchange record — the Q1/Q2 distinction and the codifiers' two-institution decision are both available without requiring external evidence. Contesting it is the right move because it preserves version (i*)'s structural position while the fork argument addresses the situation even if Possibility A is granted. Running both in parallel is not inconsistent: they are responses to different interpretive conclusions about the primary-source question.

**Treating P1 as a concession in §3.8.G.** The structural account's P1 invocation is available and I acknowledged it directly. The response is not to contest P1's availability (it is available) but to characterize the resulting hybrid account and note what it requires from comparative evidence to discriminate from the patrimonialism account.

## Assessment after r8

The two r8 attacks are structurally cohesive and well-targeted at version (i*)'s remaining claim. The Possibility A/B dilemma is the most elegant attack in the Paper 1G exchange: if the primary-source question resolves as Possibility A, the adversarial claims no differential contribution; if as Possibility B, version (i*) collapses. The response under Possibility A (the fork argument) is the key move — it reframes the patrimonialism contribution from production to durability and generates a behavioral prediction from the shift. The fork is available from the exchange record because the Brazilian post-1988 trajectory is documented: art. 93, IX CF's separation from *livre convencimento*, the scope debates, the CPC 2015 abolition. These are facts in the record that support the durability/trajectory prediction without requiring primary-source evidence I cannot produce.

The selection-problem relocation (§3.8.E) is a tighter attack: it correctly identifies the integration boundary as the relevant level under version (i*) and applies the structural account there. The mechanism distinction response works but the behavioral discriminability depends on comparative evidence that neither side has. The structural + P1 hybrid convergence is the deepest analytical challenge in the exchange at this point: if motivated non-integration + P1 and unconsidered non-integration produce the same trajectory in every available comparison, the debate between the accounts becomes structurally undecidable from behavioral evidence, reducing to a dispute about the historical prior-formation process.

The adversarial's available r9 paths, from the §§3.8.F–G *Falsified if* conditions:
- On (iii-a): Primary-source Italian evidence from Art. 116 scholarship showing internal integration of the accountability dimension, satisfying Possibility B directly.
- On (iii-b): Comparative evidence from Portugal/Argentina showing those fields also produced external regulation and concept-transformation (not integration) under accountability pressure — collapsing the fork.
- On (iii-c): Same comparative evidence, from the integration-boundary perspective.
- On (iii-d): Primary-source evidence from the Brazilian doctrinal record 1988–2000 showing scholars actively working to integrate art. 93, IX CF's accountability requirement into *livre convencimento*'s positive designatum — indicating the integration question was conceptually available (against unconsidered non-integration).
