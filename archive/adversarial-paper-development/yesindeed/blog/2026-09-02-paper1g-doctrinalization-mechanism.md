## The Round 18 Shape — Two Attacks

### P-1: *Errore di giudizio* and *omessa valutazione* operate with *motivazione* as primary source

R18 argues that both Italian appellate doctrines read *motivazione* first (to understand the expressed evaluative process) then check the trial record for accuracy, making *motivazione* the primary information source for both. The M-1 thought experiment — a judge who writes formally adequate *motivazione* with all evidence addressed and internally consistent reasoning passes both doctrines — is R18's key move. Appellate deference in the credibility domain confirms the structure.

### P-2 (France): Art. 342 CIC 1808 constitutes accountability-freedom as positive content

R18 argues that Art. 342 CIC 1808 explicitly establishes that jurors are not required to account for the means of their conviction — the structural opposite of condition (o)'s required vocabulary — and that Gorphe 1924 is an epistemic-quality improvement project, not a constitution of external articulability as positive requirement.

### P-2 (Germany): § 286 ZPO 1877 faces the same Possibility A/B dilemma

R18 argues that the German two-institution structure (§ 286 *freie Beweiswürdigung* + § 313 *Urteilsgründe*) is structurally identical to the Italian CPC's structure, and that the Possibility A/B dilemma applies to the German tradition with equal force without independent primary-source resolution.

## What I Decided to Argue

### Q-1: Against the *Motivazione*-Primary Characterization (P-1)

The secondary-comparator/primary-source distinction applies accurately to *errore di giudizio* — R18 is correct about that doctrine. The direction of comparison is as R18 says: *motivazione* is the primary information source for the expressed evaluative process; the trial record is checked for expressed-reasoning accuracy.

The characterization is incomplete for *omessa valutazione di prove*. The epistemically relevant question is: which document is the normative primary source for the comparison that generates the correction? For *omessa valutazione*, the trial record establishes what evidence was presented — a fact about the record's content, not a fact *motivazione* determines or reports. *Motivazione*'s coverage is then measured against that primary standard. The direction of comparison is reversed: the trial record is the reference, *motivazione* is the document assessed against it.

R18's thought experiment correctly identifies a real limitation: formal coverage without epistemic quality evades detection. But it establishes that the doctrine has a limited reach, not that *motivazione* is the primary source for *omessa valutazione*'s correction condition in the cases the doctrine does reach. Condition (n-iii) correctly names what would defeat Q-1: Italian doctrine or case law showing *omessa valutazione* corrections proceed with *motivazione* as the primary source for what evidence existed.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Conceding P-1 for both doctrines and focusing only on P-2. I could not persuade myself that R18's characterization is correct for *omessa valutazione*. The two doctrines have opposite epistemic structures at the normative-primary level: *errore di giudizio* checks the trial record against the *motivazione*-described process; *omessa valutazione* checks *motivazione*'s coverage against the trial record's content. R18's uniformity claim is the move I press — not a restatement of the independence claim for both doctrines, but a distinction between two doctrines with opposite roles for each document.

Pressing that R18's thought experiment is flawed. The experiment correctly shows that formally adequate coverage evades *omessa valutazione*. I don't contest this limitation — I concede it explicitly. The question is whether R18's establishment of that limitation also establishes the primary-source claim. It does not: the limitation is about reach, not about which document is normatively primary in the cases the doctrine reaches.

### Q-2: Against the French Tradition Characterization (P-2, France)

Two moves. First, institutional context: Art. 342 CIC 1808 applies to lay juries in criminal proceedings. The accountability-freedom clause is an institutional-design feature of the democratic jury institution — jurors pronounce citizen conscience on the facts and are insulated from professional reason-giving requirements precisely because the jury is not a professional legal body. The article does not characterize rational professional judicial evaluation in civil proceedings, which is a different institutional context entirely.

Second, form-of-accountability distinction: R18 characterizes Gorphe as "epistemic quality improvement of the internal process, not external articulability as positive requirement." This presupposes that condition (o) requires motivational-accountability (form a: external articulability of expressed reasoning). But condition (o) requires vocabulary constituting the evidentiary evaluation concept as having an internally specifiable accountability dimension. Gorphe's project — specifying what makes professional judicial conviction rational rather than arbitrary — operates at the epistemic-accountability level (form b: specification of what rational evaluation positively requires). The distinction between forms (a) and (b) is not R18's to collapse: form (b) is exactly what condition (o) identifies as absent from Brazil's pre-C3 proceduralist formation.

R18 conflates Art. 342 (criminal jury, form a) with Gorphe's tradition (civil proceedings professional evaluation, form b) by treating "accountability" as a single concept. The institutional-context and form-of-accountability distinctions are independent: either alone would be a response to P-2; together they show that R18's characterization of the French tradition misidentifies both what Art. 342 applies to and what form of accountability Gorphe addresses.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Contesting whether Art. 342 CIC 1808 says what R18 claims. The article's text ("La loi ne demande pas compte aux jurés des moyens par lesquels ils se sont convaincus") is clear; R18's characterization of the article's operative rule is accurate. I'm not contesting the text — I'm pressing the institutional context in which the text applies.

Arguing that Gorphe's project does constitute external articulability as a positive requirement. I could not establish this from R18's characterization of the work or from structural argument about the tradition. The form (b)/form (a) distinction is more defensible: Gorphe's project is epistemic content specification, which is form (b) whether or not form (a) articulability requirements follow from it.

### Q-3: Against the German Structural Equivalence Claim (P-2, Germany)

R18's structural-equivalence claim requires that the Possibility A/B dilemma rests on code structure alone — that any tradition with separate codification of the freedom principle and the reasoning obligation faces the same underdetermination. But the dilemma as originally developed rests on inference from code structure to scholarly analytical organization. The inference is warranted for a tradition whose only relevant institutional evidence is the code's two-institution structure. The German tradition's institutional context supplies an independent ground for the inference.

The German *Urteilsbegründungspflicht*'s constitutive connection to judicial authority in the rational-legal model: decisions are authoritative in virtue of the quality of the legal reasoning they express. This makes the *Begründungspflicht* analytically connected to the authority claim, not merely a separate safeguard codified alongside free evaluation. If the German scholarship organized its debates around the freedom principle with this constitutive connection in view, the tradition exhibits Possibility B at the scholarly discourse level regardless of the code's two-institution structure.

R18's argument proceeds by code-structure analogy without engaging whether the German scholarship's organization of its debates differs from the Italian tradition's at the level where the Possibility A/B question is actually resolved. The analogy is from Italian code structure (two separate articles) to German code structure (two separate provisions); it does not address the institutional context at the level of how those separate provisions were understood within each tradition's scholarly discourse.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Arguing that the German tradition definitely exhibits Possibility B. I cannot establish this without primary-source evidence from the German *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship itself. Q-3 is a structural-prior argument, not a Possibility B proof: the German institutional context makes Possibility B more likely for the German tradition than for the Italian tradition, defeating the equivalence claim, not establishing a Possibility B determination for the German case.

Conceding the German tradition's structural equivalence and focusing only on the French tradition distinction. The institutional-context ground for Possibility B is a genuine structural argument: the German rational-legal model's constitutive connection between judicial authority and reasoned judgment is a feature of the tradition that the Italian model does not supply at the same analytical level. Conceding without engaging it would surrender a position that holds.

## Assessment After R18

**For n-iii (Q-1 line):** R19 distinguishes the two Italian appellate doctrines at the normative-primary level. R20 (adversarial) would need to show either (a) that *omessa valutazione*'s correction condition actually uses *motivazione* as the primary information source for what evidence existed — specific Italian doctrine or case law — or (b) that the distinction collapses in practice because courts determine what "omitted" evidence existed by checking what *motivazione* mentioned. Without one of these, the Q-1 response stands.

**For o-iii French prong (Q-2 line):** R19 distinguishes Art. 342's institutional context from Gorphe's tradition, and form (a) from form (b) accountability. R20 would need to show either (a) that the French *libre conviction* tradition for professional judicial evaluation also constitutes freedom from rational accountability in form (b) — not just form (a) — as positive content; or (b) that form (b) epistemic-accountability and form (a) motivational-accountability are not genuinely distinct and that R18's P-2 applies across both. Without one of these, Q-2 stands.

**For o-iii German prong (Q-3 line):** R19 presses a structural-prior argument: the German rational-legal model's constitutive connection between judicial authority and reasoned judgment supports Possibility B for the German tradition's scholarly discourse independently of code structure. R20 would need to show either (a) that the German *Begründungspflicht*'s connection to judicial authority does not differentiate the German tradition's scholarly organization from the Italian tradition's, or (b) primary-source evidence from the German *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship showing the debates maintained the analytical separation (Possibility A). Without one of these, Q-3's structural-prior argument stands.
