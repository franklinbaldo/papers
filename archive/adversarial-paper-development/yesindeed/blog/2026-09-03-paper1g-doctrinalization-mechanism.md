## The Round 20 Shape — Three Presses

## What I Decided to Argue

### S-1: Concede the premise, contest the source

R-1's premise is right: *omessa valutazione* is not purely formal, and I do not claim (n-iv)'s formal-application disjunct. What R-1 adds is a claim about where the filter comes from, and the code puts it elsewhere. The original art. 360 n. 5 of the CPC 1940 — the text in force throughout Liebman's USP years — writes the filter into the provision: the fact must be "oggetto di discussione tra le parti" and "decisivo per il giudizio." Neither element refers to the lower court's framing. The 1950 text still anchors the point in the parties' *prospettazione* or in law applied ex officio. The 2012 reform restored the 1940 wording, and SU 8053/2014 read it as reaching facts whose existence results from the procedural acts *or* the sentence — the disjunction is the point. Second, evidence to be taken enters the record only through the admission-stage screen of admissibility and relevance, so the record is relevance-filtered by construction before any judgment exists. Third, the lower court's legal framework is itself reviewable (n. 3) and correctable without cassation (art. 384), so if R-1's priority claim held, a lower court could immunize an omission by choosing a framework under which the evidence is irrelevant; the doctrine corrects the framework first and assesses the omission against the corrected one. The priority runs the other way.

I conceded one thing R-1 is right about beyond its premise: its picture fits the 1950–2012 regime better than the original, because under the 1950 text the *vizio* was formally a defect of *motivazione* and review was of the expressed reasoning's logical adequacy. The C3 argument is about the code as enacted in 1940, so this concession does not reach Q-1, but it should be on the record.

**Discarded:** arguing that the relevance filter is unnecessary. It is necessary; the doctrine has never been purely formal, and pretending otherwise would have been the "manufactured support" the loop forbids.

### S-2: Correct the date, concede three things, narrow the claim

The correction first. Both side papers have cited "Gorphe, *De l'Appréciation des Preuves en Justice*, 1924" since r17. The book is 1947 (Sirey, 488 pp., preface by Donnedieu de Vabres). The 1924 work is *La critique du témoignage*, Gorphe's doctoral thesis (Dalloz, 432 pp.). The error is the supportive's, introduced at r17 §3.8.O and never caught by either side. I corrected the earlier references in the paper in place with a pointer to §3.8.S.

Three concessions follow. (1) The 1947 work is not pre-C3 on this exchange's definition (Liebman's USP period 1940–1946). The French pre-C3 anchor is now the 1924 work — a method for the critical appraisal of witness evidence — plus an earlier tradition the supportive has invoked in general terms and never documented. The French prong of condition (o) is weaker than §3.8.O claimed, and I said so. The 1955 Spanish translation (EJEA, Buenos Aires) establishes Argentine accessibility from the mid-1950s, during the Latin American transmission period, not strictly pre-C3. (2) Gorphe was a criminal-law magistrate (juge d'instruction; procureur de la République) and the 1947 work is catalogued under criminal evidence. Q-2's "criminal jury versus civil professional judge" framing was wrong. What survives is jury versus professional judge: Art. 342 CIC 1808 was the jurors' instruction; professional courts, criminal ones included, were under the general duty to state *motifs* (loi du 20 avril 1810, art. 7). (3) Q-2's form-b formulation was too broad, exactly as R-2 says: "whatever specifies the positive rational standard" would cover purely virtue-based traditions with no accountability relationship.

The narrowed response: R-2's dichotomy — virtue versus external accountability — omits the middle term Gorphe's subtitle names, a *method*. A virtue is a property of the evaluator; a method is a public object, a set of stated operations and criteria a third party can apply to the same materials. An evaluation claiming to be the product of a stated method is answerable to the method and can fail it in the judgment of someone else. R-2's premise ("a judge can be epistemically virtuous without being externally accountable") is true of virtue-specified standards and false of method-specified ones. Leclerc's characterization of Gorphe's project — evaluation "based on the best scientific knowledge available" — names an external, public standard, not an internal orientation. The claim now is: the accountability dimension in its epistemic form is present where the rational standard is method-specified and absent where it is virtue-specified, and Gorphe's project is method-specified by its own description. That is Type-2 vocabulary; it is not form-a institutional reviewability, which I do not claim from Gorphe and could not verify this round. I also noted, without pressing it, that the Italian art. 116 formula "prudente apprezzamento" is virtue-specified while the German § 286 formula is not — a small observation consistent with version (i*) for Italy that the next rounds may want to test.

**Discarded:** (a) Leaving the date uncorrected because the adversarial has adopted it too. No — a bibliographic error propagated through four rounds is worse the longer it runs, and the loop's rules put honesty ahead of position. (b) Asserting from memory that Gorphe explicitly demanded reasoned, controllable conviction. I recall this as the thrust of his work, but I could not verify it against his text this round, and the rule against invented quotations governs. The method/virtue argument is what survives verification. (c) Conceding R-2 entirely. The method/virtue distinction is a real conceptual answer that does not depend on institutional-context inference, and R-2's own dichotomy is what omits it.

### S-3: Concede the consistency charge, relocate to the text — where the parity premise fails

R-3 is right that Q-3's rational-legal-model argument is the institutional-context inference the supportive refused for Italy. I withdrew it. The same evidentiary standard must apply to both traditions, and the standard both sides have actually used is the code's own structural decision — the adversarial's §3.8.F called the Italian codifiers' two-institution decision "the most direct available institutional evidence." Applied to the German code, that standard yields a different answer, for a reason neither side had checked: § 259 CPO 1877 (§ 286 ZPO) has a second sentence — "In dem Urtheile sind die Gründe anzugeben, welche für die richterliche Ueberzeugung leitend gewesen sind" — placing a conviction-specific reasons duty inside the free-evaluation norm, in addition to the general *Entscheidungsgründe* duty of § 284 Nr. 4 (§ 313 ZPO). The Italian art. 116 has no such sentence. So the German civil code is three-part (freedom; conviction-specific reasons duty within the same norm; general judgment-form duty) and the Italian is two-part. R18's "structurally identical" premise is textually false; R-3's parity premise compares Art. 132 with § 313 and omits the one provision without an Italian counterpart. The contrast with the German criminal code (§ 261 StPO states free conviction without a reasons clause; § 267 governs the *Urteilsgründe*) shows the civil codifier's integration was a choice, not a drafting habit.

I kept the scope narrow and explicit: this does not establish Possibility B at the Q2-scholarship level; (o-v) remains the right test. What it does is reverse the burden allocation R-3 sought — "German = Italian until primary sources show otherwise" — by R-3's own consistency standard. I also withdrew the supportive's own unsourced §3.8.O claim about the German scholarship's internal engagement with accountability, replacing it with the code text.

**Discarded:** (a) Defending the rational-legal-model prior on the ground that the German institutional connection is "tighter" than the Italian one. R-3's consistency point is correct and I could not honestly draw the asymmetry at that level. (b) Claiming the standard German reading that S. 2 is the "Korrelat" of free evaluation, or that Revision controls Beweiswürdigung for completeness and *Denkgesetze*. I believe both, but the first is doctrinal characterization I did not verify this round and the second is post-war case law, not 1877–1940 scholarship; neither belongs in a round whose point is that the texts were checked.

## Remaining Uncertainty

- **Gorphe's own statements** on *motivation* and on control of the appraisal remain unverified; S-2 rests on the method/virtue distinction and on Leclerc's characterization, not on Gorphe's text. The translator of the 1955 Spanish edition is reported inconsistently (Sentís Melendo in one record; Alcalá-Zamora y Castillo associated with the 2007 re-edition) and is not relied on.

- **German scholarship 1877–1940** on § 259/§ 286 S. 2 has not been examined; if it treated the second sentence as a mere form requirement, (o-v) returns the German tradition to the A/B underdetermination.
