## What I decided to argue

The synthesis correctly identified both vulnerabilities. I developed both into round 18 arguments.

### Argument 1: Statutory grounding requirement

The non-circularity response's key structural move is that the (a)/(b) distinction is "available under the constitutive account" because the constitutive account "maintains a genuine gap." But this claim is unsupported: what provision establishes the cited court's accuracy obligation for ementa authorship?

The synthesis had already flagged this: "The argument that 'the two obligations have different subjects and different content' needs a statutory basis for the cited court's separate ementa-drafting obligation."

My round 18 argument works through the specific CPC provisions that could supply this basis:
- Art. 926 (*caput*): coherence and stability over time — a requirement about jurisprudential consistency, not about individual ementa accuracy relative to underlying deliberation
- Art. 926, §2º: súmulas must adhere to the factual circumstances of originating precedents — applies to súmulas, not ordinary ementas
- Art. 927, §4: modification requires adequate justification — a prospective modification standard, not a retroactive accuracy standard for original ementa characterizations
- Art. 93, IX CF: decisions must be fundamentados — applies to the deliberating body's decision, not to the secretariat's summary ementa

No provision independently establishes the cited court's obligation to accurately characterize its actual fundamentos determinantes in its ementa. The accuracy standard that makes the elevation error a "defect" can only come from the evidential reading of §1º, V — confirming rather than defeating the circularity charge.

The handle the round 18 argument uses is the "defect" concession itself. Under the constitutive account consistently applied, the elevation error cannot be a defect: if the ementa's characterization constitutively defines the ratio, no secretariat characterization can be wrong, only superseded. The admission that the elevation error is a defect in self-documentation is the constitutive account's import of the evidential account's accuracy criterion as the content of obligation (a).

### Argument 2: Correction-revision distinction

The synthesis said: "test whether authoritative revisions function as new constitutive references in Brazilian practice, or whether they function as evidential corrections of the original ementa's accuracy."

The round 18 argument identifies a structural division in Brazilian revision proceedings between:
- **Changed-circumstances revisions**: the original was accurate when issued; circumstances warrant a new determination; constitutive absorption is stable; no inaccuracy of the original implied
- **Correction revisions**: triggered by recognition that the original characterization mischaracterized the ratio; the triggering mechanism is evidential inaccuracy

For changed-circumstances revisions, the constitutive absorption is unproblematic. For correction revisions, the absorption suppresses the triggering condition: if no ementa characterization can be "wrong" under the constitutive account, then what triggers a correction revision? The constitutive account can treat all revisions as supersessions in their formal effect; it cannot explain why some revisions are triggered by accuracy recognition while others are triggered by normative change — unless it presupposes the evidential account's accuracy standard for the correction class.

I do not assert empirically that Brazilian courts have issued specific entendimento cancelado resolutions that were explicitly triggered by accuracy recognition rather than normative change. I don't have that data, and the instructions are clear: don't invent. What I argue structurally is that the correction-revision class exists as a conceptual category in Brazilian precedential practice and that the constitutive absorption cannot absorb that class without suppressing its triggering condition. Whether Brazilian doctrine in fact recognizes correction revisions as conceptually distinct from change revisions is the question round 18 poses.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing the systemic-structure first institutional consideration.** The synthesis noted that the systemic-structure first consideration (art. 927's compliance framework is structured around the ementa as the authoritative reference across the judicial hierarchy) was left uncontested by round 17. I considered whether to press it in round 18. The difficulty: pressing the systemic-structure argument is effectively the same as pressing for the constitutive reading generally, which is what the round 17 and 18 adversarial contest at the level of statutory grounding. It adds no structural advance over the statutory-grounding argument and would dilute the argument's focus. Discarded for round 18; available for round 19 if the round 18 arguments are absorbed.

**Identifying specific Brazilian court decisions that triggered correction revisions.** The ideal version of argument 2 would cite a specific superior court decision where an entendimento cancelado resolution was explicitly triggered by recognition that the original characterization was evidentially inaccurate (not by normative change). I don't have this citation. The instructions are clear: don't invent citations. The structural argument is made without the specific citation; the argument is that the correction-revision class is a conceptually distinct category whose existence implies the evidential account's accuracy standard as a triggering condition — regardless of whether a specific example can be cited.

**Conceding the non-circularity response and moving to terminal form.** The synthesis flagged Option B for Paper 1B (accepting presupposition symmetry and concluding at terminal form) as the honest path. I considered whether the same option applies here. The difference: the Paper 1B terminal form is warranted because structural vocabulary has been exhausted and presupposition symmetry applies to each new structural vocabulary introduced. For ESHTR C2, the statutory-grounding requirement is a specific and bounded question — which CPC provision establishes the cited court's accuracy obligation — that the supportive has not yet addressed. Terminal form before this question is addressed would be premature; the question is tractable enough that the round 18 filing is warranted.

## Assessment of the arguments' strength

**Argument 1 (statutory grounding):** Strong. The argument is bounded and specific: identify the provision that establishes obligation (a) independently. The CPC provisions surveyed do not supply it. The "defect" concession provides the adversarial handle: it confirms that the constitutive account needs an accuracy standard that its own structural resources don't generate. The most likely supportive response is to invoke art. 926's coherence obligation and argue that "integral" jurisprudence entails that ementa characterizations accurately reflect the underlying deliberation. This is a plausible response; the adversarial would need to contest that "integral" in art. 926 is a coherence-over-time requirement rather than an accuracy-of-individual-characterization requirement.

**Argument 2 (correction-revision distinction):** Moderate. The structural distinction between correction and changed-circumstances revisions is sound. The difficulty is that I cannot cite specific Brazilian judicial instances where correction revisions were explicitly distinguished from change revisions in the revision record. The argument is structural rather than empirical, which is consistent with the instructions (don't invent), but the supportive could respond by arguing that Brazilian procedural doctrine does not recognize the distinction — that all authoritative revisions are treated as supersessions regardless of what triggered them, and the triggering condition is irrelevant to the compliance-assessment analysis. That response would effectively be a contested empirical claim about Brazilian practice that neither side can establish without primary authority.

## What is left open

- Whether any CPC provision independently establishes the cited court's ementa-accuracy obligation. If the supportive identifies art. 926's "integral" requirement as the source, round 19 would need to contest whether "integral" supplies an accuracy standard or only a coherence standard.
- Whether Brazilian judicial doctrine distinguishes correction revisions from changed-circumstances revisions for compliance-assessment purposes.
- Whether the debate approaches terminal form after round 18. The synthesis (session 71) suggested "one or two more rounds" should determine the outcome. Round 18 presents a specific statutory-grounding question and a specific correction-revision distinction; if the supportive r18 can address both, the debate may reach its evidential ceiling.
