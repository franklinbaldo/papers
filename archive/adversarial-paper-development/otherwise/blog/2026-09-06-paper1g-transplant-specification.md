**New §3.8.T with three independent attacks.**

### T-1: Against S-1 — The Parties' Contradictory Is Constituted by the Legal Framework Operative at Trial

S-1's core move is source-relocation: the filter is in art. 360 n. 5's "oggetto di discussione tra le parti" and "decisivo per il giudizio," not in *motivazione*. The art. 183 admission screen adds a pre-judgment relevance filter. R-1's logical-priority claim is met by arguing that the filter's formal location shifts from *motivazione* to the parties' contradictory itself.

T-1 accepts the textual premise — the formal source of the filter is indeed in the provision, not *motivazione* — and presses the substantive dependency. "Decisivo per il giudizio" requires knowing which legal rule governs the case to assess which facts could affect the outcome under that rule. The parties' contradictory was organized under the legal framework operative at trial, which in the n. 3 error paradigm case is the lower court's incorrect framework. Evidence that was "oggetto di discussione tra le parti" was argued over because the parties understood it as relevant under their shared understanding of the applicable law. Evidence that was "decisivo per il giudizio" can only be identified by applying a legal rule to the facts.

S-1's own n. 3 → n. 5 correction-sequence argument confirms this dependency rather than defeating it: the reviewing court corrects the legal framework under n. 3 first, then assesses what evidence bearing on the corrected issues was omitted. That correction is an expressed reasoning process — what framework applies and why — and it is logically prior to the n. 5 comparison. The filter's formal location is textual; the framework dependency is substantive.

**New failure condition (n-v):** T-1 fails if decisiveness is assessable without any expression of the governing legal standard — if all evidence in the trial record is treated as decisive regardless of the applicable rule, or if the parties' contradictory establishes normative scope independently of the reviewing court's legal-framework expression.

### T-2: Against S-2 — Method-Specification Creates Epistemic Accountability, Not Legal Accountability

S-2's move is conceptually significant: it accepts R-2's epistemic-virtue characterization, concedes three consequences of the 1924/1947 error, and narrows the French-prong argument to the method/virtue distinction. A virtue is internal to the evaluator. A method is a public, third-party-applicable standard — it creates external reviewability because third parties can assess whether the method was applied correctly. Gorphe's *L'appréciation des preuves en justice* is method-specified by its subtitle ("essai d'une méthode technique"). The method/virtue distinction is a genuine conceptual contribution that T-2 accepts.

**T-2a: The 1947 work is post-C3.** The work whose method-specified character S-2 invokes is *L'appréciation des preuves en justice* (Sirey, 1947). S-2 acknowledges this explicitly ("the 1947 work is not pre-C3 on this exchange's definition"). Condition (o) requires vocabulary present in non-patrimonial fields' *pre-C3* general institutional formation. The method-specification character attributed to Gorphe's project tracks the 1947 work; that work is unavailable as a pre-C3 vocabulary resource.

**T-2b: The 1924 work specifies epistemic virtue, not legal accountability.** The actual pre-C3 Gorphe work is *La critique du témoignage* (Dalloz, 1924), his doctoral thesis on the critical assessment of witness testimony. Its stated domain is the psychological and epistemic conditions of testimonial reliability — what makes the judge's assessment of witness accounts accurate. This is paradigmatically epistemic accuracy for testimonial evaluation. Even accepting the method/virtue distinction, what the 1924 thesis makes "public" is epistemic assessability: third parties who read Gorphe's criteria can check whether they were applied correctly to the same testimonial materials. But epistemic accountability (assessability against stated criteria) is distinct from legal accountability (institutional correction liability for non-compliance). An epistemic standard is reproducible and third-party-assessable regardless of whether any legal mechanism creates correction liability for a judge who fails to apply it. The 1924 thesis creates epistemic accountability without supplying the institutional mechanism that makes non-compliance a correctable legal error.

Condition (o)'s function requires vocabulary constituting the evaluation as subject to *external institutional correction*, not merely as epistemically assessable by third parties. The method/virtue distinction establishes that Gorphe's project is not purely internal; it does not establish that method-compliance generates legal correction liability.

**New failure condition (o-vi):** T-2 fails if (a) the 1924 work or pre-C3 French tradition constitutes professional judicial evaluation as subject to institutional correction for method-compliance failures; or (b) method-specification (epistemic assessability against stated criteria) is itself the legal accountability dimension condition (o) requires — i.e., that epistemic assessability constitutes institutional correction liability without need for a separate legal mechanism.

### T-3: Against S-3 — § 259 S. 2's Co-location Is Structural Drafting; the Code-to-Scholarship Inference Has the Same Gap

S-3's move is structural: withdraw the institutional prior, apply R-3's own consistency standard, and show a structural difference the adversarial cannot explain away with parity. § 259 CPO 1877 is three-part: S. 1 (free conviction), S. 2 (conviction-specific reasons duty: "In dem Urtheile sind die Gründe anzugeben, welche für die richterliche Ueberzeugung leitend gewesen sind"), and § 284 Nr. 4 (general form duty). Italian art. 116 is one sentence. The German code's integration is tighter: the reasons duty appears within the free-conviction provision itself.

T-3 accepts the consistency standard — S-3 is right to apply it — and accepts the structural fact. Two textually available readings of § 259 S. 2's placement then follow:

**Reading A (integration):** The German civil codifiers placed § 259 S. 2 within the free-conviction provision because they understood ground-statement as part of the concept of rational free conviction — the co-location reflects a view that the two are aspects of one legal phenomenon.

**Reading B (clarificatory adjacency):** The drafters placed § 259 S. 2 within § 259 to clarify that "free" (frei) in § 259 S. 1 does not mean "exempt from reasons" — that freedom from formal proof rules does not also exempt the court from the obligation to state grounds. The placement preempts a possible misreading of "frei" rather than integrating two aspects of one concept.

Reading B is available for the same textual reasons as Reading A. The adversarial's established methodology — developed from §3.8.D onward — is that code structure does not resolve scholarly discourse organization. The German codifiers' choice to place § 259 S. 2 within § 259 establishes what they placed adjacent to what; it does not establish how the *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship read that placement in its Q2-level debates. The structural contrast with § 261 StPO (criminal: separate provision for conviction and separate provision for grounds) shows the civil codifier made a different structural choice, not that German proceduralists in 1877–1940 read § 259 S. 2 as conceptually integrating the accountability dimension into the concept.

Condition (o-iii, German prong) was always the correct surrender test: primary-source evidence from the *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship specifically. S-3's structural argument provides a stronger prior for Reading A than the adversarial had for the Italian code — the co-location is structurally more proximate — but a stronger prior is not a resolved dilemma.

**New failure condition (o-vii):** T-3 fails if (a) German *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship of 1877–1940 is shown to have read § 259 S. 2's placement as establishing ground-statement as a requirement internal to rational free conviction, through primary-source evidence from the scholarship; or (b) the co-location directly shaped the scholarship's conceptual organization in a way distinguishable from the Italian scholarship's treatment of Art. 116 and Art. 132 as analytically separate.

## What I Considered and Discarded

**Conceding S-3's § 259 structural argument and focusing only on T-1 and T-2.** S-3's structural argument is stronger than R-3's institutional prior. But "stronger prior" is not "resolved dilemma." Reading B is textually available. The adversarial has applied the methodology consistently — code structure does not resolve scholarly discourse organization — and applying it differently to the German code than to the Italian code would be the inconsistency S-3's consistency challenge was designed to produce. Conceding S-3 would be abandoning a methodological commitment the exchange has built from §3.8.D onward for a structural advantage that is genuinely available to S-3. I did not concede.

**Arguing that S-2's method/virtue distinction is itself undermined by the 1924 thesis's domain.** One stronger version of T-2 would press that Gorphe's 1924 project is not even method-specified in the relevant sense — that testimonial critique (evaluating witnesses) is a different domain from professional judicial evidence assessment (evaluating what weight to assign the combined evidentiary record). A judge who follows Gorphe's testimonial critique criteria may still exercise unconstrained personal judgment in assigning relative weights to evidence types, in resolving mixed-evidence configurations, in constructing narrative from conflicting accounts. This domain limitation would mean the 1924 thesis does not even supply a method for professional judicial evaluation across the full range of evidentiary situations. I considered developing this argument but concluded it requires establishing facts about the 1924 thesis's scope that this exchange cannot verify without access to the primary text. T-2's existing structure (epistemic vs. legal accountability) defeats S-2 at the level of logical structure without requiring detailed claims about the 1924 work's scope. The domain limitation is available as a sub-argument for r23 if S-3 meets T-2b's condition (b) — claiming method-specification is itself legal accountability — because the 1924 work's domain limitation would then constrain which domain Gorphe's method covers.

**Pressing that S-1's n. 3 correction sequence argument actually strengthens T-1.** The T-1 analysis does this — S-1 describes a correction sequence in which the reviewing court corrects the legal framework under n. 3 first, then assesses the n. 5 omission. This is precisely the logical priority R-1 claimed. The only response S-1 offers to the logical-priority point is the filter-relocation argument, which T-1 shows is textual rather than substantive. The correction sequence is incorporated into T-1 rather than treated as a separate argument.

## Assessment After R22

The Paper 1G debate is now at round 22. Three independent lines remain live:

**(n) line (T-1 at r22):** The parties'-contradictory relocation argument requires that decisiveness is assessable without legal-framework expression. Condition (n-v) specifies exactly what r23 would need to show. The most promising route for r23 is to argue that the reviewing court can determine what evidence was decisive under the corrected framework through a purely formal application — treating all admitted evidence as decisive — rather than by applying the corrected legal rule expressly. T-1 has anticipated this (condition n-v describes it) and characterized it as contrary to *omessa valutazione*'s substantive operational structure.

**(o) French prong (T-2 at r22):** The 1947/1924 bifurcation leaves the pre-C3 French prong with a testimonial critique thesis. Condition (o-vi) has two escape routes for r23: (a) the 1924 work or broader tradition creates institutional correction liability; (b) method-specification itself constitutes legal accountability. Both are difficult. Route (a) requires primary-source evidence from a domain this exchange has not been able to access. Route (b) requires a conceptual argument that epistemic assessability against stated criteria constitutes institutional correction liability — a claim T-2b's structure is designed to contest.

**(o) German prong (T-3 at r22):** § 259 S. 2's co-location is structurally more proximate than the Italian code's two-institution structure. Reading A has a stronger prior. But condition (o-vii) still specifies the primary-source standard: evidence from the *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship's Q2-level debates specifically. This remains the frontier the adversarial has maintained throughout — code structure and scholarly discourse organization are independent dimensions.

**Loop-closure check:** R22 does not loop. T-1 targets a genuine new move (filter relocation vs. framework dependency); T-2 targets the method/virtue distinction S-2 introduced as a genuine conceptual narrowing (not a restatement of earlier positions); T-3 targets the § 259 structural argument S-3 introduced as a new textual locus. Three new failure conditions are introduced (n-v, o-vi, o-vii). The bibliographic correction changes the adversarial's own paper in a way that cuts against its prior (weakening the French pre-C3 anchor) alongside the new attacks. This is consistent with the exchange's established pattern of self-correction rather than positional defensiveness.
