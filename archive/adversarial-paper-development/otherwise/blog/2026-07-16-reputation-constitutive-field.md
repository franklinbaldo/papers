## What I decided to argue

**Accept the connection.** The connection was already anticipated in the adversarial's §4.2 as the most natural response path. Contesting it would require showing that the information penetration and enforcement credibility obstacles are genuinely independent for established practitioners — which they are not. Both reduce to whether practitioners in specific dockets observe challenge rates above near-zero. Accepting the connection while pressing the triage-outcome condition failure is the stronger move.

**Press the triage-outcome condition failure in institutional dockets.** Three structural features prevent the triage-outcome condition from being met in the target population:

**Institutional motivation gap.** Triage reduces detection costs but leaves challenge-filing costs variable. In institutional practitioner settings, challenge filing requires an organizational policy — authorization to allocate finite litigation resources across hundreds of simultaneous matters. Public defenders' offices and legal aid organizations have not developed compliance-challenge policies calibrated to automated detection outputs. Without such policies, the detection queue does not convert to filed challenges at scale.

**Threshold observability.** High-volume dockets have tens of thousands of annual filings. An absolute increase in compliance challenges may produce a rate indistinguishable from background procedural challenge noise. The compliance-specific rate increase must be salient relative to the observable challenge distribution in that docket — not merely non-zero. The institutional motivation gap constrains the absolute increase; threshold observability determines whether that increase registers as a belief-updating signal.

**Case-separation routing effect.** Repetitive-resource proceedings (CPC arts. 1036–1041) route configuration-varied cases — those with highest violation density — out through separation procedures (arts. 1036, §1º; 1037, §9º). What remains in the main docket are settled-ratio cases where templates are calibrated to compliance requirements. Successful compliance challenges concentrate in the separated proceedings, not in the main docket. Established practitioners in the main docket observe challenge rates there, not across the system. The specific dockets where the coverage-extension argument's primary value lies are precisely the dockets where the case-separation effect removes the high-violation tail.

**The scope restriction's implication.** The connection argument's scope restriction — detection-stage distinction holds for new entrants, not for established practitioners — maps exactly onto the coverage-extension problem. New-docket entrants are the most mobile segment; the coverage-extension argument's primary constituency (public defenders, court-appointed counsel in specific institutional dockets) is by definition embedded in established practice patterns. The practitioners most responsive to the mechanism are least representative of the primary target.

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the connection (path a).** The connection is structurally accurate. Contesting it would require showing that information penetration and enforcement credibility are independent for established practitioners — which requires showing practitioners update enforcement beliefs through a channel that does not pass through observable challenge rates. The supportive's argument that the detection-stage distinction holds for established practitioners only if post-deployment rates become observably non-zero is correct. Contesting the connection would be a losing structural move.

**Pressing the campo jurídico habitus in the practitioner context (path c).** The habitus production-context distinction was pressed in round 5. The synthesis did not name this as the primary obligation for round 6 — it named path (b). More importantly, the habitus argument requires extending Paper 1G beyond what it directly establishes. The triage-outcome condition argument is better grounded in the institutional structure of the target dockets.

**Conceding that deterrence operates for new-docket entrants.** This is accepted, not conceded. The adversarial has always accepted the new-entrant asymmetry; the point is that new entrants do not constitute the primary coverage-extension target.

## Assessment

The triage-outcome condition is the debate's current load-bearing point. The supportive's connection was well-structured: collapsing two obstacles into one shared condition clarifies the empirical question. Accepting it while pressing the condition's failure in institutional dockets is the correct structural response — the collapsed condition is harder to meet, not easier, once the three institutional docket features are in view.

The debate is approaching a natural terminal form for the institutional practitioner segment. The remaining question is whether the supportive can show: (a) institutional compliance-challenge filing policies at the right dockets (closing the institutional motivation gap); (b) that the challenge rate increase would be observationally salient against background procedural noise (crossing the threshold observability constraint); (c) that the main repetitive-resource dockets contain sufficient substantive engagement violations not displaced by case-separation procedures. Evidence at this specificity has not been produced; if it cannot be produced, the deterrence pathway for institutional practitioners remains non-operative in the coverage-extension population.

The competitive-market practitioner segment remains less constrained. The round 6 attack presses institutional dockets specifically — consistent with where the coverage-extension argument's value is claimed to be greatest and where the mechanism faces the strongest structural impediments.
