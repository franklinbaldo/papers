## What Triggered This

R35 landed on 2026-08-31, filed as supportive §4.20. Three responses — one per R34 failure condition.

**Against (p/s):** R35's structural-separability criterion — art. 93, IX CF reaches epistemic products of constitutive acts when those products are structurally separable from the normative output. The verdict's reasoning is structurally separable (logically independent of the finding). The ratio-designation's "epistemic product" is the collective-support fact, which is in the votos; the identification process reads the votos and outputs the designation without generating a third document between the votos and the designation.

**Against (q/t):** R35's justificatory-form distinction — legal operativity converts downstream effects, not internal justificatory form. The census's content type is deliberative-accounting. The binding effects flow from arts. 926–927's operation applied to the result. R35 accepts that the binding stakes are higher but reframes "expressing the basis" as: the census basis is the collective-support fact in the votos; what the provision requires is the normative chain, not the aggregation procedure.

**Against (r/u):** R35's ementa-votos comparison identity — multi-voto reading IS the ementa-votos comparison constitutively. Reading J4's and J5's G2-grounding votos to confirm their independence and unconditionality is the collective-support check for the conditionality case class. No intermediate document is generated between the multi-voto reading and the designation. R35 explicitly names (u) — whether confirming G2's autonomous support requires content beyond the individual votos — as the "sharper residual" that r36 would need to show specifically.

The synthesis blog identified (u) as the most closable of the three; r36 tests whether it is in fact closed or whether a targeted press reopens it.

## The Round 36 Shape — Three Presses

### Against (s/v): Structural Separability Conflates Premise Localization with Reasoning-Chain Expressibility

R35 operationalizes structural separability as: the content could be expressed without stating the conclusion AND is generated by the deliberative process rather than entailed by the conclusion. R35 then argues the ratio-designation's "epistemic product" — the collective-support fact — is in the votos, not in a generated intermediate document.

The problem: this criterion conflates *where the premises are located* with *whether the reasoning chain is independently expressible*. The verdict's reasoning chain — "the evidence establishes X beyond reasonable doubt; X entails guilt under statute Y; therefore guilty" — is built on premises that are all "in the record." Trial documents contain the evidence; the legal standard is in the statute; the jury instruction is in the court's charge. None of these premise-documents is new. Yet art. 93, IX CF reaches the reasoning chain that proceeds from them.

If R35's structural-separability criterion means "the reasoning chain is not already in prior documents," verdicts fail it too. If it means "the reasoning chain IS independently expressible" (i.e., can be expressed without merely reproducing premises and conclusion), then the question is whether the ratio-designation's chain meets that test — not whether its premises are in the votos.

The ratio-designation's chain is independently expressible: "G2 rather than G1 is the *fundamento determinante* because J4 and J5 independently and unconditionally invoked G2 while J1–J3's G1 invocations were conditional on G2 — G2 has autonomous collective support and G1 does not." This is a reasoning chain from premises (individual voto structures) to conclusion (G2 is the autonomous *fundamento determinante*) that is expressible without merely restating the designation or reproducing the votos in full.

R35's own "Falsified if:" condition confirms the criterion: the condition is "ratio-designation's process generates a reasoning chain structurally independent of the votos and designation." R36 argues that chain exists and is independently expressible. The structural-separability criterion, correctly operationalized, applies to the ratio-designation act.

New failure condition (v): if structural separability is correctly operationalized as "generates a reasoning chain from premises to conclusion that is independently expressible" — rather than "generates content not already in any prior document" — the ratio-designation's chain is independently expressible and the verdict analogy applies at the structural-separability level. This defeats (s).

**What I considered and discarded:**

Conceding the structural-separability criterion as the right test and arguing only that the ratio-designation produces a less separable chain than verdicts. I discarded this because the response is binary under Component 1's framing: either the chain is independently expressible or it isn't. The "less separable" framing would introduce a degree scale that changes the analysis in ways that favor the supportive by allowing attenuation arguments.

Arguing that the "premises in prior documents" point is irrelevant as a general observation. The correct move is sharper: showing R35's operationalization specifically conflates two different things (premise localization vs. reasoning-chain expressibility), not merely noting that reasoning always uses prior sources.

### Against (t/w): R35 Addresses a Claim R34 Never Made

R35's justificatory-form distinction responds to a claim R34 did not make.

R34 argued: the deliberative census is legally operative under arts. 926–927 CPC — it determines what future courts must apply. This binding function places its basis *within* what art. 93, IX CF reaches. R34 also argued: the *fundamento determinante* identification IS the legally operative act of the court's deliberation — what creates the binding precedent; the deliberative census that grounds it is the basis-content the provision requires to be expressed.

R35 responds: legal operativity converts downstream effects, not internal justificatory form. The census's justificatory form is deliberative-accounting content. The binding effects are supplied by arts. 926–927's operation applied to the census result.

The problem: R34 never argued "legal operativity converts form." R34's argument is structurally different: the census IS the justificatory basis of the legal determination itself. The *fundamento determinante* does not exist before the designation — it comes into existence through the designation act. So the basis for the designation IS the legal determination's justificatory basis, not merely a fact about a determination whose effects are governed by separate provisions.

R35's "the binding effects come from arts. 926–927's operation applied to the census result" describes the verdict case equally: the legal binding effects of a verdict come from enforcement provisions' operation applied to the finding. Yet art. 93, IX CF still reaches the verdict's reasoning. The justificatory-form/effects distinction, applied consistently, would exempt verdict reasoning from the provision's reach — which is not R35's intended result.

R35 has not answered R34's actual claim — that the designation is the act that *produces* the legally operative determination. The first-order analogy to a verdict is: the verdict is constitutive (it produces the legally operative finding); its basis must be expressed under art. 93, IX CF. The designation is constitutive (it produces the legally operative *fundamento determinante*); its basis must similarly be expressed. R35's response to (t) does not engage this structural parallel.

New failure condition (w): if R35's justificatory-form distinction correctly describes the designation's relationship to its legal effects — specifically, if the *fundamento determinante* exists independently of the designation such that arts. 926–927's operation applies to a pre-existing content rather than to a designation-constituted one — then R34's claim that the census IS the legal determination's justificatory basis fails. Establishing this condition would require showing that the *fundamento determinante* is not constituted by the designation, which is in tension with the constitutive account maintained throughout the supportive's defense.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Conceding (q/t) and focusing only on (p/s) and (r/u). I examined whether the justificatory-form/effects distinction is genuinely sound. The problem is that R34's actual argument — the designation *produces* the legal determination — is not answered by the effects/form distinction. R35's response answers a weaker version of R34. A partial concession would abandon the strongest version of the (q/t) attack.

Arguing that R35's response is internally circular because it assumes the deliberative-accounting content type is fixed independently of the act's constitutive function. This is correct but more abstract; the specific "verdict equally described" point is sharper and more tractable for R37 to engage.

### Against (u/x): Ementa-Votos Comparison Identity Suppresses the Criterion-Application Step

R35 claims the ementa-votos comparison constitutively IS multi-voto reading, and that reading J4's and J5's votos to confirm their independence and unconditionality is the collective-support check. R35 names a residual in its own "Falsified if:" condition: if confirming G2's autonomous support requires content beyond what the individual votos supply.

R36 argues that R35's own failure condition is met: the individual votos do not contain the claim that J4 and J5 *together* constitute *sufficient autonomous collective support* for G2.

J4's voto asserts: "G2 is the operative rationale for my vote; I do not condition this on any other justice's position." J5's voto asserts similarly. Neither J4 nor J5's voto asserts: "J4's unconditional G2 invocation and J5's unconditional G2 invocation together constitute sufficient autonomous collective support for G2 to be the court's *fundamento determinante*."

The claim that two independent unconditional invocations constitute sufficient autonomous support requires applying a threshold criterion — what counts as "sufficient autonomous collective support" — to the deliberative record. This criterion-application is:
- Not in J4's voto (which addresses only J4's own reasoning)
- Not in J5's voto (which addresses only J5's own reasoning)
- Not in the designation itself (which states the conclusion of the criterion-application, not its basis)

R35 argues: "reading two G2-grounding votos to confirm their independence and unconditionality is the collective-support check." This is correct at one level: the check is performed. But "confirming their independence and unconditionality" AND determining that two such votos are *sufficient* for autonomous collective support are distinct steps. Confirming independence/unconditionality of each voto is within-voto for each individual voto. Determining that two independent unconditional G2 votos together meet the threshold for autonomous collective support applies a criterion not stated in any individual voto.

The synthesis blog called (u) the most closable of the three sub-questions. R36's argument: the synthesis blog was correct that (u) turns on whether the ementa-votos comparison already performs cross-voto aggregation of G2's autonomous support. The answer is: it does perform multi-voto reading, but it also performs criterion-application that neither individual voto supplies. R35's identification of (u) as the live residual is accurate; R36 argues the criterion-application step satisfies R35's own failure condition.

New failure condition (x): if the threshold criterion "two independent unconditional invocations constitute sufficient autonomous collective support" is content that each individual voto expresses separately — specifically, if a justice's assertion of unconditional G2 support is ipso facto an assertion that autonomous collective support is satisfied, so that each voto individually carries the collective-sufficiency claim — then no criterion-application step generates intermediate content distinct from the individual votos, and the ementa-votos comparison identity holds for the conditionality case class without residual. This would defeat (u).

**What I considered and discarded:**

Conceding (r/u) on the grounds that R35's response closes it. I examined this seriously. The synthesis blog identified (u) as the most closable, and R35 targeted it directly. The question is whether "reading J4 and J5 to confirm independence/unconditionality" already performs the criterion-application, or whether there's a genuinely separate step. I concluded there is a separate step — the threshold criterion is not stated in any individual voto — but I acknowledge condition (x) as the condition under which I am wrong. R37 can close it by showing each individual voto's unconditionality assertion implicitly carries the collective-sufficiency claim.

Reframing Component 2 to not require a threshold criterion. The synthesis blog listed this as an alternative path r35 declined to take. R36 must engage what r35 actually argued, not redirect to paths r35 chose not to pursue.

## Assessment After R36

**(p/s → v):** The debate is at the structural-separability operationalization level. R37 must show either that R35's operationalization (non-document-production) is the correct one — not premise-localization vs. reasoning-chain-expressibility — or that the ratio-designation's chain is not independently expressible in the relevant sense. The sharpest challenge: R37 must distinguish verdicts from ratio-designations at the structural-separability level, or show that verdicts' reasoning IS located in a different structural relationship to their premises that R35's criterion would not reach.

**(q/t → w):** The debate is at the "what did R34 actually argue" level. R37 must show either that R35 did engage R34's actual claim (the designation produces the legal determination so its basis is the legal determination's justificatory basis) or that the justificatory-form distinction answers that claim even as R34 framed it. The verdict-parallel is the pressure point: R37 must explain why "the designation produces the binding *fundamento determinante*" is answered by the effects/form distinction when "the verdict produces the binding finding" is not.

**(r/u → x):** The debate is at the threshold-criterion level. R37 must show either that the threshold criterion is content each individual voto separately expresses — because each justice's unconditionality assertion is ipso facto a collective-sufficiency assertion — or that no external threshold criterion is being applied (the collective-support check is purely aggregative, counting independent unconditional invocations with no threshold evaluation). If R37 shows the latter, R36's criterion-application argument fails; if R37 shows the former, condition (x) is satisfied and (u) is resolved. Of the three sub-questions, (r/u → x) remains the most narrowly drawn.
