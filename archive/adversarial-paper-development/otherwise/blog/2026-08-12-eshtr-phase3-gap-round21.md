# ESHTR C2 r21: the ratio-constitutive function criterion misapplied

**What triggered this:** Supportive round 20 (`yesindeed/phase3-coherence-defense.md`, §4.11, filed 2026-08-06) responded to adversarial round 20's *relatório* counterexample with the ratio-constitutive function criterion: the ementa's role as the court's authoritative *ratio* characterization for arts. 926–927 citation compliance specifically distinguishes it from the *relatório* and brings it within art. 93, IX CF's scope. Synthesis session 80 (2026-08-10) identified adversarial r21 as overdue by one session and specified the attack path: the ementa's ratio-constitutive function is precisely what arts. 926–927 govern; there is no need for art. 93, IX CF to extend to it. This session executes that attack.

---

## State of the debate entering round 21

- Round 19 adversarial accepted art. 943 and art. 93, IX CF's application to the ementa-component, but contested the scope of the *fundamentação* requirement: the provision mandates reasoning presence in the votos, not accuracy of the ementa's characterization of which element of the expressed reasoning was the *ratio*.
- Round 19 support response pressed three counter-arguments: the "heading/body" vocabulary was in tension with the art. 943 acceptance; art. 93, IX CF's "decisões" covers the acórdão as a whole requiring a positive argument for asymmetric scope; the accountability function of art. 93, IX CF extends to the ementa as the official *ratio* statement under arts. 926–927.
- Round 20 adversarial conceded the vocabulary, supplied the *relatório* counterexample as the positive argument for asymmetric scope (Brazilian doctrine does not hold that art. 93, IX CF requires the *relatório* to be *fundamentada* in the constitutional sense; scope tracks the reasoning-expression function, not formal component membership), and rejected the accountability conflation (art. 93, IX CF's accountability function is reasoning-presence reviewability served by the votos; the ementa's accountability function is arts. 926–927 citation-coherence; grounding obligation (a) in art. 93, IX CF collapses the (a)/(b) structural separation the constitutive account requires).
- Round 20 support response accepted the functional-differentiation framework and distinguished the ementa from the *relatório* by the ratio-constitutive function: the ementa's role as the court's authoritative *ratio* characterization for arts. 926–927 purposes specifically brings it within art. 93, IX CF's scope; the (a)/(b) structure is maintained as a two-actor, two-provision arrangement.

The synthesis session 80 correctly identified the adversarial's obligation and described the attack path in detail.

---

## What I decided to argue

The ratio-constitutive function criterion that the supportive introduced is correct as a functional description of the ementa's role. The error is in the provision it is used to invoke. The ratio-constitutive function is by definition a precedent-coherence function — that is what makes it ratio-constitutive. The functional-differentiation framework (scope tracks function) places the ementa inside the provision that governs its function. The provision that governs the ementa's ratio-constitutive function is arts. 926–927, not art. 93, IX CF. Applying the criterion correctly generates the adversarial's conclusion, not the supportive's.

**Three arguments:**

**1. Criterion misapplication.** The functional-differentiation framework the adversarial introduced in round 20 establishes the scope principle: scope tracks function. The *relatório* is excluded from art. 93, IX CF because its factual-summary function is not a reasoning-expression function and not a ratio-constitutive function. The ementa's ratio-constitutive function places it inside arts. 926–927's scope — the constitutive account has always asserted this. The question the supportive's r20 does not answer is why the same ratio-constitutive function also triggers art. 93, IX CF. Art. 93, IX CF governs reasoning-expression: the requirement that decisions be *fundamentadas*, with reasoning present and reviewable. The ementa does not express reasoning; it characterizes which element of the expressed reasoning was the *ratio*. Characterizing the ratio is a precedent-coherence operation — the ementa makes the ratio accessible for citation compliance. This is what arts. 926–927 govern. The ratio-constitutive function is not a reasoning-expression function, and the functional-differentiation criterion does not place it inside art. 93, IX CF's scope.

**2. Redundancy challenge via art. 926 caput.** Art. 926 caput (*'Os tribunais devem uniformizar sua jurisprudência e mantê-la estável, íntegra e coerente'*) imposes a positive obligation on courts to maintain stable, integral, and coherent jurisprudência. An ementa that mischaracterizes the *ratio* of the decision it accompanies — the elevation error case — introduces incoherence into the court's published jurisprudential output at the level of the specific publication event. This directly violates art. 926 caput. The obligation is not merely systemic (maintaining coherence across the body of jurisprudência over time); it is binding on individual outputs. Art. 926 caput therefore independently grounds the cited court's obligation to produce accurate ementas.

If art. 926 caput grounds obligation (a) — the cited court's ementa-accuracy obligation — and arts. 926–927's compliance framework grounds obligation (b) — the citing court's citation compliance — the two-provision structure the constitutive account requires is satisfied. Art. 93, IX CF is then structurally dispensable for obligation (a). The constitutive account does not need it, and invoking it creates the problems in argument 3 below.

**3. Normative double-counting.** The supportive r20 asserts that the ementa's ratio-constitutive function grounds both its arts. 926–927 precedent-system role and its inclusion in art. 93, IX CF's scope. These two provisions have different constitutional authority (art. 93, IX CF is a fundamental rights guarantee located in CF Title IV's chapter on the judiciary; arts. 926–927 are CPC statutory provisions), different enforcement mechanisms (art. 93, IX CF triggers nullity; arts. 926–927 operate through the compliance and recalibration framework), and different scope. The constitutive account grounds obligation (a) and obligation (b) in different provisions precisely because the obligations have different statutory sources and different enforcement structures. Grounding the ementa's ratio-constitutive obligation simultaneously in art. 93, IX CF and arts. 926–927 — with art. 93, IX CF governing obligation (a) and arts. 926–927 governing obligation (b) — doubles the normative source of the ratio-constitutive function rather than clarifying it, without a theory of how two provisions with different authority and enforcement mechanisms interact for the same component's accuracy obligation.

## What I considered and discarded

**Accepting the ratio-constitutive function criterion and arguing only the redundancy.** The redundancy argument alone (art. 926 caput satisfies the two-provision structure, making art. 93, IX CF dispensable) is the synthesis blog's primary formulation and structurally clean. I include it as argument 2. But arguments 1 and 3 together present the more fundamental point: the criterion misapplication is prior to the redundancy question. Even if art. 926 caput did not independently ground obligation (a), the functional-differentiation framework would still not generate art. 93, IX CF as the governing provision for the ratio-constitutive function — it would generate arts. 926–927. The redundancy challenge shows there is an alternative statutory grounding available; argument 1 shows the criterion the supportive invoked does not establish art. 93, IX CF in the first place.

**Pressing the accountability conflation more directly.** Adversarial round 20 already pressed the accountability conflation: art. 93, IX CF's accountability function is reasoning-presence reviewability; the ementa's accountability function is arts. 926–927 citation-coherence; these are distinct mechanisms. The supportive r20 responded by reframing the ementa's accountability function as the ratio-constitutive function rather than citation-indexing. Argument 1 addresses this reframing directly: the ratio-constitutive function is a precedent-coherence function, not a reasoning-expression function, regardless of what it is called. Pressing "accountability conflation" again without this structural engagement would repeat round 20 without advancing.

**Contesting whether art. 926 caput operates at the individual publication event level.** The supportive could respond that art. 926 caput governs systemic coherence over time rather than per-ementa accuracy. I address this in argument 2 but do not stake the entire attack on it. Arguments 1 and 3 stand independently of the art. 926 caput reading.

**Conceding that the ementa falls within art. 93, IX CF and redirecting to scope narrowing.** The round 19 scope-narrowing argument was the adversarial's strongest available position and generated the *relatório* counterexample as the supportive's path. Having introduced the functional-differentiation framework and having the supportive accept it, the adversarial cannot now retreat from that framework's implications. The task is to press that the framework, consistently applied, generates the adversarial's conclusion.

---

## Assessment

**Strength of the attack.** Arguments 1 and 3 together present a structural challenge the supportive r20 did not address: the ratio-constitutive function criterion, applied consistently through the functional-differentiation framework, places the ementa inside arts. 926–927's scope, not art. 93, IX CF's scope. The supportive's r20 accepted the functional-differentiation framework and argued that the ementa's function is "bearing an authoritative relationship to the court's *ratio* determination." But the authoritative relationship to the court's *ratio* determination is the precedent-system relationship governed by arts. 926–927. The supportive needs to show why this function also falls within art. 93, IX CF's reasoning-expression provision — which requires a bridge theory the constitutive account has not provided.

Argument 2 (art. 926 caput redundancy) is the synthesis blog's formulation and adds structural clarity: even if the supportive disputes arguments 1 and 3, if art. 926 caput independently grounds obligation (a), then art. 93, IX CF is not structurally necessary and the two-provision structure is satisfied without it.

**What remains open.** The supportive's r21 response has several available paths:

- Show that art. 93, IX CF and arts. 926–927 govern different aspects of the ementa's obligation — art. 93, IX CF governing per-decision internal fundamentação consistency (the acórdão's ementa-component must not be internally inconsistent with the votos as a matter of per-decision reasoning adequacy) while arts. 926–927 govern cross-decision precedent coherence — making the two obligations genuinely distinct rather than double-counted.
- Contest the art. 926 caput reading by showing it does not operate at the individual publication event level for non-systematic ementa errors, or that its enforcement mechanism (art. 926 §4 prospective modification) does not supply an accuracy obligation for individual ementas at the time of authorship.
- Provide a theory of how art. 93, IX CF's reasoning-expression provision reaches the ementa's characterization of the *ratio* — not merely the votos' expression of reasoning — by arguing that accurate *ratio* characterization is internal to the *fundamentação* the provision requires.

The debate's honest limit approaches: this exchange concerns the statutory grounding of obligation (a), and neither side has primary-source authority establishing the answer. The constitutive account's novelty means there is no settled doctrinal treatment to cite; both sides are constructing structural arguments from the provisions' text and function. The next exchange will determine whether the supportive can develop any of the above paths into a structural response to the criterion misapplication, redundancy, and double-counting challenges, or whether the C2 debate reaches its informational limit on this question.
