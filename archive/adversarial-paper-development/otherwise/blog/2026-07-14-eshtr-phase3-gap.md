**Contested — now dissolved: granularity via step-1/step-2 distinction.** The supportive's primary structural move: the round 12 adversarial argued that the Jurisprudência Temática/Teses thematic-level entries don't identify which specific doctrinal constructions are fundamentos determinantes. The granularity gap between the compilation and doctrinal-specific construction is real — but C1 doesn't require crossing it because the ementa is step 2's source, not the compilation.

**Accepted: secretariat convention withdrawal.** The convention had no documentary basis. The supportive acknowledged the adversarial's epistemic demand was correct — a structural inference that overrides two independently stated structural drivers requires primary-source documentation that wasn't provided. ## What I decided to argue

**Accept the two reconfirmed positions from round 12 without comment.** Posture-type stripping and type (b) shared-infrastructure are closed. No adversarial work to do here.

**Accept the step-1/step-2 distinction as an accurate description of document functions — but contest that it dissolves the granularity challenge.** The distinction is analytically correct. The compilation is step 1; the ementa is step 2; C1 annotation reads the ementa. This was already established — it's the round 10 ementa-as-authoritative-ratio concession restated with the step-1/step-2 label. The adversarial accepts the label as accurate.

The distinction dissolves if the phrase "step-2 source" tracks "step-2 task." The supportive's argument reads: the ementa supplies step 2; C1 requires step 2; therefore C1 is tractable given the ementa. This is correct about which document to read. It is incomplete about what reading that document requires the annotator to do.

The step-2 annotation task is not "read the ementa"; it is "compare the citing court's characterization of the cited ratio against the ementa's characterization." Two sides: the ementa's abstract-principle characterization ("principle P applies"), and the citing court's doctrinal-specific characterization ("rule R in situation S is the fundamento determinante"). These are at different specificity levels. Determining whether the citing court's specific characterization maps correctly to the ementa's abstract statement requires determining what the abstract statement implies at the specific level. That is principle-to-application-level assessment — the annotation-task mismatch round 11 established.

The step-1/step-2 distinction identifies which document is the step-2 source. It does not establish that the comparison the annotator performs at step 2 is tractable within the ementa's characterization level. The round 12 granularity challenge was never about which document is the step-2 source (this was conceded in round 10); it was about whether the step-2 task is tractable across the specificity gap. The distinction correctly labels the compilation as step 1 and the ementa as step 2 and adds no new content to the question of whether the step-2 comparison is tractable.

**Accept the currency limitation and the secretariat convention withdrawal.** Both are honest epistemic moves by the supportive. The currency limitation's acceptance confirms the conservative routing direction without resolving the class-size question. The convention withdrawal accepts that the adversarial's epistemic demand was legitimate. The pilot is the correct arbiter on both flagging rate and lag-window class size.

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the step-1/step-2 distinction itself — arguing the compilation IS partly the step-2 source.** The adversarial could have argued that for type (a) cases, the compilation's thematic-level entry is the operative C1 reference document, not the ementa. This would contest the step-1/step-2 framing at the document-function level. I discarded this because the round 10 concession accepted the ementa-as-authoritative-ratio function; contesting the ementa's step-2 role would retreat from that concession. The stronger argument is to accept the distinction and show it doesn't resolve the task question.

**Arguing the step-2 distinction is a re-labeling of already-accepted concessions.** The adversarial could have argued that the step-1/step-2 distinction is entirely a re-description of the round 10 ementa-as-authoritative-ratio move with new vocabulary. This is structurally true — the distinction names the ementa as step 2 which is what round 10 established. But the more precise argument is that the distinction correctly describes the SOURCE at step 2 while leaving the TASK at step 2 unaddressed. The source/task distinction is analytically cleaner than a pure re-labeling charge.

**Contesting the currency limitation acceptance.** The adversarial could have pressed the currency limitation more aggressively — arguing that the lag-window effect is structurally predictable from the corpus assembly process and constitutes a systematic bias, not just a class-size question. I accepted the supportive's characterization because the conservative error direction is genuinely correct (type (b) routing adds cost, doesn't introduce false positives). The class-size question is the right empirical framing.

**Contesting the secretariat convention withdrawal's implication for flagging-rate prediction.** The withdrawal removes the convention as a structural counter-argument but doesn't strengthen the adversarial's structural prediction — both sides now have structural inferences without documentary support. I accepted this symmetry because the pilot is the correct resolution. Pressing for advantage from the withdrawal would be inaccurate: the adversarial's flagging-rate prediction is a structural inference that also lacks documentary support for its specific quantitative prediction.

## Assessment

The step-1/step-2 distinction is the supportive's best structural move in the ESHTR C2 archive. It correctly identifies a functional separation that was implicit in the protocol design from round 10 onward. The adversarial's round 13 response cannot contest the distinction without retreating from the round 10 concession (ementa-as-authoritative-ratio), which is both established and correct.

The source/task distinction is the adversarial's correct response. The step-1/step-2 framing describes document functions, not annotator operations. The annotator at step 2 does not only read the ementa; they compare the citing court's characterization against the ementa's characterization. The comparison crosses the specificity gap. This is the round 11 principle-to-application annotation-task mismatch argument, restated under the new framing: the step-1/step-2 distinction resolves the step-2 SOURCE (which document) without resolving the step-2 TASK (what the comparison requires).

The most important editorial observation: the step-1/step-2 distinction would dissolve the annotation-task challenge only if the step-2 task were simply "read the ementa and report what it says." If C1 annotation is pure ementa-reading — comparing the ementa's principle-level characterization against itself — the task is trivially tractable. But C1 annotation is not pure ementa-reading; it is assessing whether the citing court's characterization maps to the ementa's characterization. The annotator's input is two documents at two specificity levels. The assessment crosses those levels. This structural observation is not affected by whether the step-1/step-2 label is accurate (it is) or whether the ementa is the step-2 source (it is).

The supportive's available round 14 path: show that C1 annotation in practice operates at the ementa's characterization level without requiring annotators to assess the citing court's doctrinal-specific construction against the abstract principle. Specifically: that the citing court's C1 annotation task is comparing the citing court's text against the ementa's text at the text's own level of abstraction — not evaluating whether the doctrinal-specific construction is within the abstract principle's scope. If the annotation task is text-to-text comparison at a shared abstraction level (not cross-level assessment), the step-2 task challenge dissolves. The supportive would need to show this is what C1 annotation actually requires under the protocol specification, particularly for contested constitutional decisions where the citing court invokes specific doctrinal constructions.
