## The two attacks

### Against (ee/gg) — Two problems with the composition argument

**Problem 1: Súmula 283 context-transfer.** Súmula 283 STF states: "É inadmissível o recurso extraordinário, quando a decisão recorrida assenta em mais de um fundamento suficiente e o recurso não abrange todos eles." This is an admissibility rule for extraordinary appeals. It tests whether the *appellant's brief* attacked each independently sufficient ground of the *lower court's challenged decision*. The question Súmula 283 answers is: did the challenger's brief omit a ground the challenged decision rested on? This is a screen on challenger diligence in specifying the error, not a criterion for identifying which element of a court's deliberation was the operative ratio.

R42 imports the "fundamento suficiente" concept from this admissibility-screening context and applies it to the plenary deliberation identification problem — which element of a relator's voto is the court's collectively operative *fundamento determinante*? These are structurally distinct questions. The admissibility screen operates after a decision is issued and asks whether the appellant caught all the decision's grounds. The plenary identification question operates to determine what the ground was in the first place. Súmula 283 provides no criterion for the second question. Súmula 126 STJ's admissibility context has the same gap.

**Problem 2: Post-proclamation voto.** R42's composition requires V to be "fixed as the ground-bearing text at proclamation time" — the premise that V is constituted as a text-fact at the moment of proclamation (CPC art. 941, §1º) such that adherence to it ratifies its grounds. STF plenary practice involves oral delivery at session, followed by written votos published in the acórdão weeks or months later. These written votos may differ from oral delivery through elaboration, addition of authorities, or reasoning refinement. If V's written text differs from its oral version, the composition's ground-fact — what V's text under the sufficiency criterion establishes — is determined by the post-session published version, not a text fixed at proclamation time.

R42's diary concedes this directly (remaining uncertainty #1): "Whether any STF decision has construed adherence 'nos termos do voto do Relator' as adoption of grounds, or the contrary." The adoption construction (Almeida & Bogossian's 95% figure; Klafke & Pretzel's per-voto ratification criterion) is doctrinal-empirical, not an STF holding. The "fixed at proclamation" premise is unverified for STF's specific post-session publication practice.

**Failure condition (gg):** R42's (ee) composition survives if: (a) Brazilian doctrine or authoritative STF institutional practice establishes that the "fundamento suficiente" reading from Súmula 283 STF / Súmula 126 STJ is operative for identifying a plenary court's *fundamento determinante* from a relator's voto — not merely for admissibility screening — such that the two contexts are non-distinct for this purpose; AND (b) Brazilian practice or doctrine establishes that the relator's written voto is fixed as the ground-bearing text at proclamation time for STF plenary decisions, including decisions where the written voto is published post-session, such that post-proclamation editorial modifications do not affect the composition's ground-fact for requirement (1) purposes. R42's diary names both as remaining uncertainties.

### Against (ff/hh) — Result-sufficiency relocates, not resolves, the level-of-generality problem; nested residual unresolved for most STF plenary decisions

**Problem 1: Result-sufficiency requires theory selection.** R42's criterion asks: "would G alone have been sufficient for the specific dispositif on the specific question proclaimed?" This is a counterfactual conditional. To answer it, one must determine whether G alone — without the other elements the relator or other justices invoked — was sufficient to ground the specific result. That determination requires knowing which legal theory was operative. In high-adversarial-record cases, the applicable legal standard is precisely the contested question. R41's J4/J5 example illustrates: J4's case-distinguishability ground and J5's premise-transfer ground both could have generated the result, but only one is sufficient *under the applicable legal standard* — and which standard applies is what the case was arguing about. The "specific dispositif" anchor fixes the result's identity but not at which level of doctrinal specificity the ground sufficient for that result is individuated. The level-of-generality problem is relocated from "what count as the same ground?" to "at which specificity level was G sufficient for this dispositif?" — not resolved.

R42's "the relator reads the level; the record fixes it" acknowledges exactly what r41 pressed: a per-relator interpretive act determines the level, not a mechanical determination from the text.

**Problem 2: Nested residual unresolved for decisions without a tese vote.** R42 names the residual explicitly: where P and P' are both shared and both result-sufficient, the ementa's choice between them is a scope question assigned to the tese vote (CPC arts. 985, 1.039). This assignment concedes that the identification question does not have a determinate answer within the "result-sufficient proposition" criterion alone — additional procedure is needed. But the tese vote procedure exists only in *repercussão geral*/IRDR proceedings. Most STF plenary decisions are not *repercussão geral* or IRDR cases. For the majority of STF plenary decisions — and most of the ESHTR calibration corpus — no formal tese vote is available. In those decisions, the nested-sufficient case has no resolution mechanism within R42's framework.

**Failure condition (hh):** R42's (ff) result-anchored criterion survives if: (a) the result-sufficiency determination for a ground G is mechanically readable from the voto text as a logical-entailment question without requiring any choice among competing legal theories about which standard the result required, producing a determinate answer in the high-adversarial-record class without per-relator interpretive judgment; AND (b) for STF plenary decisions without a formal tese vote, the nested-sufficient case has a resolution mechanism mechanically determinable from the voto record without a per-relator interpretive choice among accurate ementa descriptions; R42 assigns resolution to the tese vote (CPC arts. 985, 1.039), but that procedure does not exist for the large class of STF plenary decisions without *repercussão geral* or IRDR designation.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing the "fixed at proclamation" problem by arguing V's oral delivery IS the voto.** Tempting, but it would require an affirmative claim about STF institutional practice that is not clearly established. The more conservative attack — that the "fixed at proclamation" premise is unverified for STF's post-session written voto publication practice, per R42's own diary — is sufficient and uses R42's own concession.

**Arguing that the Almeida & Bogossian 95% figure is methodologically unreliable.** R43 should not contest the empirical figure directly — doing so would require engaging the study's methodology, which is beyond the primary-source verification available here. The structural attack is better: even granting the 95% figure, the adoption construction is not an STF holding, and the "fixed at proclamation" premise is what carries the theoretical weight.

**Pressing that Schauer's rule was designed for Anglo-American seriatim courts and does not transfer to Brazilian plenary practice.** This is a legitimate line but would require primary-source engagement with Schauer's original context vs. Brazilian institutional design. The weaker version — that the result-sufficiency determination requires theory selection in contested cases — is directly extractable from R41's J4/J5 example and R42's own concessions, so it is the stronger route.

**Constructing a new J6/J7 example for the nested-sufficient case.** R42's own named residual is more authoritative than a new constructed example — R42 identified the nested-sufficient case as the scope residual itself. Using R42's own admission is stronger than a constructed illustration.
