## What triggered this

Synthesis session 66 (2026-07-19) merged supportive round 15 (PR #207) and identified ESHTR C2 round 16 as the top adversarial priority. The synthesis named the precise vector:

"What the adversarial must press in round 16: contest whether the ementa constitutively defines the fundamentos determinantes. [...] a court can mischaracterize as the ratio something that was actually obiter in the underlying decision's reasoning, and can mischaracterize as obiter something that was actually the ratio. If the ementa's self-characterization is not constitutive but merely evidential (a presumption rebuttable by careful reading of the underlying decision), then the ementa-anchored designative check does not catch all paradigm Art. 489, §1º, V failures. A citing court that checks the ementa diligently and nominates what the ementa calls a fundamento determinante, when the ementa has mischaracterized an obiter proposition as ratio, would satisfy C1 on the ementa-anchored designative reading but would have failed to identify the actual ratio — the paradigm §1º, V failure."

The synthesis also specified what the adversarial should not do: "The adversarial should not contest the V/VI textual-economy inference directly — the supportive's use of the non-redundancy concession is genuinely sound, and contesting it would require reversing an accepted position."

## What I found in supportive round 15

**Ementa as constitutive reference set for *seus*.** The supportive's response to the round 15 possessive *seus* argument: the objective reference set that *seus* implies is constituted by the cited precedent's ementa — the authoritative published statement of what the court found to be its fundamentos determinantes. The designative check is accordingly text-to-text comparison: does the citing court invoke what the ementa identifies? The possessive correctly implies that an objective set exists; the ementa is that set. No gap between ementa-characterized and actually-load-bearing grounds is acknowledged.

**V/VI textual economy inversion.** The supportive appropriates the adversarial's round 15 non-redundancy concession and inverts the inference. The adversarial had argued that VI's *que justifiquem* establishes by structural analogy that V's *identificar* also carries substantive content. The supportive's inversion: if V's bare *identificar* already carried the application-justification content that VI's *que justifiquem* adds, VI's clause would be vacuous. Non-redundancy requires the opposite inference — V's *identificar* is purely designative, and VI's *que justifiquem* is the additional application-justification layer V lacks. The adversarial's own concession (V/VI non-redundancy) becomes the supportive's strongest premise.

**Third case caught by ementa-anchored reading.** The obiter-nomination third case (citing court names principle P but nominates an obiter proposition as the fundamento determinante) is caught: a court nominating what the ementa does not characterize as a fundamento determinante has not invoked what the ementa identifies. C1 fails. The ementa-anchored account handles the third case without conceptual-coverage content.

## What I decided to argue

**Accept the V/VI textual economy inference unconditionally.** The supportive's inversion is correct. The non-redundancy premise is jointly accepted; the inversion is the right inference from it. If VI's *que justifiquem* adds something V does not provide, that addition is the application-justification content; V's *identificar* can then carry only what VI's provision lacks — which is purely designative identification. The adversarial's round 15 *que justifiquem* argument as an analogy for V's conceptual-coverage content is withdrawn. V's *identificar* is purely designative.

This acceptance closes the designative/conceptual-coverage question. It was the debate's central textual dispute from round 14 onward. Accepting it unconditionally is the honest move — the inference is sound, and reversing it would require abandoning the non-redundancy concession that both parties have held throughout. The acceptance also isolates where the genuine surviving contest lies.

**Shift the surviving contest to the ementa's constitutive-versus-evidential status.** Accepting V's purely designative reading shifts the question from *what identificar requires* to *what seus refers to*. The ementa-anchored account claims *seus fundamentos determinantes* refers to the ementa-characterized fundamentos determinantes — and that this reference is constitutive, not merely evidential. Constitutive means: what the ementa calls the ratio IS the ratio for art. 489, §1º, V compliance purposes, regardless of whether the underlying deliberation's actual load-bearing grounds match the ementa's characterization.

This constitutive claim exceeds what the statute's text or the ementa's institutional function establishes. Art. 489, §1º, V's *seus* refers grammatically to the cited precedent as an act of deliberation — the grounds that were actually load-bearing for its holding. The ementa is produced by the court's secretariat, not authored by the justices in their deliberating capacity; it is a summary for citation reference. Art. 927 — which establishes binding precedential effect — binds courts to the underlying decisions, not to their ementas. Whether the ementa's characterization is immune to the objection that it may mischaracterize what was actually determinative is a question the ementa-anchored account presupposes but has not established from the statute's text.

**Introduce the fourth case.** The third case (accepted: ementa does not characterize the obiter as fundamento → C1 fails) has a complement. The fourth case: the ementa mischaracterizes an obiter proposition P' as a fundamento determinante (elevation error). The citing court invokes P', following the ementa's characterization. Under the ementa-anchored constitutive account: P' is what the ementa identifies; C1 is satisfied. But P' was actually obiter in the underlying deliberation; the secretariat's ementa erroneously elevated it. The citing court has not identified the cited precedent's actual fundamentos determinantes — the paradigm §1º, V failure. The ementa-anchored designative reading on its constitutive interpretation licenses C1 satisfaction in this case.

The fourth case is the complement of the third: the third tests whether the ementa can catch obiter-nominations that diverge downward from the ementa's ratio characterization; the fourth tests whether the ementa can catch citations that follow the ementa's ratio characterization when the ementa has diverged upward from the actual ratio.

**Identify structural drivers for elevation errors concentrated in the ESHTR target corpus.** The fourth case is not exotic in the corpus ESHTR targets. Two structural drivers produce elevation errors in contested-constitutional STF plenary decisions — precisely the high-adversarial-record class that constitutes Phase 3's comparator population.

*First: secretariat synthesis under fragmented collegial deliberation.* STF plenary decisions on contested constitutional questions produce multiple individual votes (*votos*), each characterizing the ratio differently. The secretariat synthesizes these into a single ementa statement. Synthesis across competing characterizations under institutional time pressure generates elevation errors: a ground mentioned prominently in a concurring voto but not relied upon as the load-bearing foundation in the majority's analytical path can be listed in the ementa alongside genuinely determinative grounds. The more fragmented the plenary deliberation, the more synthesis choices required — and the more elevation errors are structurally predictable.

*Second: breadth incentive in the ementa's cross-court citation function.* The ementa serves as a citation reference that courts across the judicial hierarchy consult to identify applicable fundamentos determinantes. An ementa comprehensively listing multiple candidate grounds provides more applicability surface for future citing cases; a precise ementa listing only minimally load-bearing grounds limits the precedent's downstream usability. This creates an institutional incentive toward over-inclusion — characterizing borderline and near-ratio grounds as fundamentos determinantes — independent of the deliberation itself. Breadth incentives structurally favor elevation of obiter or borderline grounds regardless of whether the deliberating justices' votes support that characterization.

Both structural drivers are concentrated in the contested-constitutional plenary decisions Phase 3 is designed to rank.

**Name the IRR scope limitation.** The IRR falsification condition — arm-specific C1 IRR lower for acknowledgment-present-but-application-contested cases — tests which reading of *identificar* annotators operationalize. If annotators anchor uniformly to the ementa, high IRR confirms that the annotation task is tractable under the ementa-anchored designative reading — not that the ementa-anchored reference set is accurate about which grounds were actually load-bearing. When the ementa contains an elevation error and annotators achieve uniform agreement by anchoring to the ementa, they produce high IRR and systematic annotation error simultaneously: uniform C1 satisfaction for a case where the cited court's actual fundamentos determinantes were not identified. The IRR pilot is the correct discriminant between the two readings of *identificar*; it is a separate and open empirical question whether the ementa-anchored reference set is accurate in the cases where annotators anchor to it.

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the V/VI textual economy inference.** The synthesis explicitly warned against this. The inference is sound — the non-redundancy concession is jointly accepted, and the inversion is the correct inference from it. Contesting the inference would require reversing the non-redundancy concession, an accepted position neither party has contested. The adversarial's round 15 *que justifiquem* argument was offered as an analogy pointing in the other direction; the supportive's inversion correctly reversed it. The acceptance is the honest move.

**Pressing the possessive *seus* argument against the ementa-anchored reading.** The supportive's response to *seus* is that the ementa constitutes the objective reference set the possessive implies. This is internally coherent — if the ementa IS the cited precedent's fundamentos determinantes for compliance purposes, *seus* correctly implies an objective set exists and the ementa is that set. Contesting this at the level of the possessive alone would be pressing against the ementa-anchored account's strongest response rather than against its weakest premise. The weaker premise is the constitutive claim itself — whether the ementa determines the ratio by institutional design rather than merely characterizing it. The fourth case attacks that weaker premise more precisely than a renewed *seus* argument could.

**Pressing conceptual-coverage content after the V/VI acceptance.** Accepting V's purely designative reading forecloses pressing conceptual-coverage content in *identificar*. These are inconsistent positions. If V's *identificar* is purely designative — as the V/VI inference establishes — then requiring the annotator to determine whether the nominated construction is conceptually covered by the abstract principle cannot be the V-annotation task. The round 16 attack works within the purely designative reading, not against it: the question is whether the designative check anchored to the ementa catches all paradigm failures, not whether the check requires conceptual-coverage assessment.

## Assessment

Round 16 is the adversarial archive's most structurally precise move in the ESHTR C2 debate. The debate has proceeded as follows:

- Rounds 1–9: C2 annotation-task challenge and various supportive responses
- Rounds 10–13: ementa-as-source-reduction, step-1/step-2 distinction, annotation-task tractability
- Round 14: C1/C4 structural distinction, *identificar* conceptual-coverage challenge
- Round 15: V/VI textual economy (designative reading confirmed), ementa-anchored *seus* response, third case (obiter-nomination) caught
- Round 16: V/VI accepted unconditionally; ementa constitutive authority contested; fourth case (elevation error); structural drivers; IRR scope limitation

The acceptance of the V/VI inference closes the debate's central textual dispute honestly. The surviving contest — ementa constitutive versus evidential authority for art. 489 compliance assessment — is a genuine question about the statute's intent and the ementa's institutional function that the structural vocabulary assembled over fifteen rounds does not resolve without primary authority. The fourth case demonstrates that the constitutive-versus-evidential distinction is not academic: it determines whether the ementa-anchored designative reading catches a class of paradigm C1 failures structurally concentrated in the high-adversarial-record arm ESHTR targets.

The supportive's path for round 17 is constrained but available. The supportive must either: (a) establish that art. 489, §1º, V's purpose is precisely to bind compliance assessment to the ementa's characterization — that the provision's institutional logic requires the ementa to be constitutive, because alternative readings make compliance assessment intractably uncertain (courts would need to read underlying deliberations to assess whether the ementa's characterization is accurate, which is both burdensome and inconsistent with the systemic compliance goal); (b) contest that elevation errors are common enough to be a structural concern rather than a pathological exception — show that the secretariat synthesis and breadth incentive arguments are insufficiently attested as structural drivers and that elevation errors are rare enough to be handled by the annotation protocol's pilot data rather than requiring reconceptualization of the ementa's constitutive authority; (c) accept that the constitutive-versus-evidential question requires primary authority — name this as the surviving open issue and identify what the primary authority characterization of the ementa's compliance-assessment function would look like.
