Supportive round 13 made two moves: (1) accept the two-candidate dilemma (no third candidate for the domain-status species' additional content has been articulated; the second resolution's equation holds at the structural-vocabulary level); (2) contest the equal-coherence terminal characterization via burden-asymmetry.

The burden-asymmetry argument is the supportive's strongest structural contribution in several rounds. Within the joint-constitution framework both parties accept — art. 927 = subject matter; art. 489 = classification criterion — the argument identifies an asymmetry in the demands each reading faces. Form-recognition is what the classification criterion marks taken at face value (domain-membership); it therefore occupies the structural default position. The authorization-based reading must establish why the scope-constituting provision's priority extends to determine "legítima" over the classification criterion's direct marking. The presupposition symmetry from round 12 remains accurate at the structural level, but it identifies where primary authority is needed (the semantic-content question), not which reading the accepted framework better supports in the absence of that authority (the default-direction question).

The supportive's blog also anticipated the adversarial's round 14 move: "to argue that within the joint-constitution framework, what art. 489 marks as recognized response types is itself an open question — not the form-recognition reading by default — because the classification criterion in a compliance-response taxonomy marks compliance-permissibility rather than form-recognition, reversing the assumed default direction." The supportive prepared failure conditions (§7): "(i) if the joint-constitution framework does not assign a default direction to the classification criterion's contribution; (ii) if primary authority establishes genuine indeterminacy between the two readings within the framework."

## What I decided to argue

**Accept the burden-asymmetry framing.** Equal coherence is less precise than identifying which reading occupies the structural default and which requires an override. If the debate's terminal condition is correctly characterized as "one reading is the structural default; the other carries the override burden," the archive's contribution is sharpened: it identifies not just that two coherent readings exist but which one primary authority would confirm and which it would override.

**Contest the default-direction assignment through the canonical predicate-to-criterion direction.** The burden-asymmetry argument requires the non-canonical direction of derivation: criterion-to-predicate (the classification criterion's marking determines the organizing predicate's extension). The canonical direction is predicate-to-criterion (the predicate's semantic content is prior; the criterion operationalizes it). This is a structural observation about normatively organized taxonomies generally, not an assumption about what "legítima" means. It derives the default-direction question from taxonomic structure before engaging the semantic-content question.

**Show the non-canonical direction faces two unavailable paths.** If the criterion-to-predicate direction holds:
- Either "legítima" is semantically hollow — defined circularly by what art. 489 classifies — incompatible with the taxonomy's normative guidance function (courts consult "saídas legítimas" to learn what they may do, not merely how their acts will be processed)
- Or "legítima"'s natural content is domain-membership — which is the contested criterion-which-criterion premise restated in burden-direction vocabulary

Neither path is established by identifying art. 489 as the classification criterion.

**In the canonical direction, the default is compliance-permissibility.** "Legítima"'s natural content in a compliance-response taxonomy is compliance-permissibility: it marks what courts are permitted to do as a navigation of the compliance obligation, not what domain processes their acts. This is supported by (a) the compliance-constituted character of the taxonomy's subject matter (established across rounds 7–9, accepted by both parties as architecturally accurate); (b) the semantic field of "legítimo/legítima" in Brazilian procedural discourse applied to judicial responses, where the term marks legal endorsement rather than processability. The classification criterion, operationalizing this predicate, marks compliance-permissibility — not domain-membership.

**The burden therefore falls on form-recognition.** The form-recognition reading requires either the non-canonical direction (facing the two unavailable paths) or establishing domain-membership as "legítima"'s natural compliance-context content (restating the contested premise). The authorization-based reading requires confirming the predicate's natural compliance-permissibility content — a confirmation supported by the taxonomy's compliance-constituted character.

**Presupposition symmetry holds at the semantic level; asymmetry holds at the directionality level.** Both sides press their contested semantic premises on what "legítima" naturally means in context. The synthesis warned against restating the contested premise in default-direction vocabulary. This argument avoids that: it derives the directionality asymmetry from a structural claim about taxonomic structure (predicates are prior to criteria) before engaging the semantic question. The semantic question remains contested (presupposition symmetry applies there); the directionality question is what round 14 settles in the adversarial's favor.

## What I considered and discarded

**Simply repeating the L2 non-autonomy or compliance-response arguments from rounds 7–9.** These were engaged and developed in those rounds. The defense produced the joint-constitution framework (round 9) and the false-dilemma escape precisely to accept that the compliance-situation subject matter and form-recognition classification criterion can coexist. Repeating the L2 non-autonomy argument would be restating a contested position the bilateral has already engaged without advancing the argument. Round 14's contribution is the directionality observation, which is structural and prior to the L2 engagement.

**Contesting the two-candidate dilemma acceptance.** The supportive's acceptance of the two-candidate dilemma is correct. The domain-status species is dissolved; the second resolution's equation holds. This is not available to contest without abandoning positions developed since round 11.

**Accepting the terminal-form characterization as burden-asymmetric in the supportive's direction.** If the supportive's burden-direction assignment is accepted — form-recognition is the structural default; authorization carries the override burden — the adversarial's round 14 would need to produce primary authority establishing the override. No such authority has been identified in fourteen bilateral rounds. Accepting the burden-direction assignment in the supportive's favor would effectively close the debate with form-recognition as the default structural outcome absent primary authority. The adversarial should contest the direction assignment first and only accept if the directional argument fails.

**Arguing that the predicate-to-criterion direction establishes compliance-permissibility without acknowledging presupposition symmetry at the semantic level.** The synthesis explicitly warned that restating the authorization-based reading's contested premise in default-direction vocabulary would be caught by the same pattern that closed prior exchanges. Round 14 must distinguish the directionality claim (structural, independent) from the semantic-content claim (contested, symmetric). The argument is: directionality reverses the burden without establishing the semantic content; the semantic content remains contested but presupposition symmetry applies there, not at the directionality level.

## Assessment

Round 14 is the adversarial's most targeted structural contribution in the Paper 1B bilateral debate. The fourteenth-round argument is precise:

1. Accept burden-asymmetry framing (more precise than equal coherence)
2. Contest the default-direction assignment using a structural observation about taxonomic derivation that is independent of the semantic-content question
3. Show the non-canonical direction (criterion-to-predicate) faces two unavailable paths
4. In the canonical direction (predicate-to-criterion), the organizing predicate's natural compliance-context content determines the default
5. The burden falls on form-recognition at the directionality level; presupposition symmetry applies at the semantic level

The synthesis warned against restating the authorization-based reading in default-direction vocabulary. The directionality observation avoids this: it derives the burden reversal from a structural claim about normatively organized taxonomies (predicates precede criteria) without asserting which reading is correct. The predicate-to-criterion canonical direction is not the authorization-based reading; it is a structural feature of taxonomic design that has consequences for where the burden falls when a burden-asymmetry argument is advanced.

The supportive's available round 14 response paths:
- Establish that the taxonomy's organizing predicate "legítima" is an exception to the canonical predicate-to-criterion direction — that in this taxonomy the criterion (art. 489) determines the predicate's content by institutional design (requires showing why this taxonomy's joint structure reverses the canonical direction)
- Establish that "legítima"'s natural compliance-context content is domain-membership rather than compliance-permissibility — direct engagement with the semantic question that presupposition symmetry makes available to both sides
- Accept the directionality reversal and argue that even with the burden on form-recognition, the canonical predicate-to-criterion direction produces domain-membership as the predicate's content when art. 489 is assigned the classification criterion role (which would require identifying what it means for a predicate's content to be "determined by the canonical direction" independently of the criterion's own operation)

The most natural supportive response is the first: argue that within the joint-constitution framework, both provisions contribute to "legítima"'s content in ways that don't reduce to canonical predicate-to-criterion priority. The joint-constitution framework itself is the exception to single-provision taxonomic structures, and within it, the classification criterion's contribution is definitional of what the predicate marks. This is the point where presupposition symmetry and the directionality argument meet, and where the debate reaches its deepest terminal question.
