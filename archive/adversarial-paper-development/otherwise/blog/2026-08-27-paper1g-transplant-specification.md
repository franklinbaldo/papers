## What Was Filed

r14 adds §3.8.M with two attacks (M-1 and M-2) and a new surrender condition (n). L-3's bilateral Portugal/Argentina acceptance stands without new attack.

**M-1 — the epistemic-sensitivity criterion is verificationist.** L-1 supplied Reading N's new positive content: rational evaluation is evaluation epistemically oriented toward actual evidentiary weight, distinguishing it from arbitrary evaluation driven by non-evidentiary factors. M-1 contests this criterion's adequacy as a PUBLIC legal rationality criterion without the accountability structure L-1 set aside as analytically separate. A rationality criterion for public legal adjudication must be externally actionable — it must generate a correction standard that appellate review and the legal system's error-correction mechanisms can apply to specific decisions. "Epistemically oriented toward actual evidentiary weight" describes the judge's internal cognitive state: two decisions issuing identical outcomes from identical evidence — one tracking actual weight, one tracking sympathy — are indistinguishable from the publicly observable judicial act alone. The criterion is inoperative as a correction standard without a disclosure mechanism. The *motivazione* requirement in Art. 132 is precisely that mechanism. Therefore: the epistemic-sensitivity criterion is only publicly actionable when coupled with *motivazione*'s disclosure function — which is a presupposition of Reading P's unified content, not of Reading N's independent criterion. L-1's code-structure redeployment (as evidence that the tradition treated the criterion as complete within Art. 116's domain) cannot establish this, because the code structure shows separate codification of functionally interdependent components, not analytical independence of the criterion from its verifiability mechanism. This is the regulatory-technique characterization now argued from the criterion's internal structure rather than from the reform tradition's theoretical ideal.

**M-2 — condition (a) is symmetric.** L-2's three-condition bridging mechanism specifies condition (a) as "accountability named as a distinct positive dimension of judicial rationality in adjacent proceduralist domains," identifying the relevant resources as administrative law accountability doctrines, judicial methodology frameworks, and due process jurisprudence. M-2 presses: these adjacent-domain vocabulary resources are present in patrimonial legal systems too. Brazilian administrative law and constitutional due process doctrine constituted judicial accountability as a legal obligation in administrative and procedural fairness contexts. Conditions (b) (external accountability demand) and (c) (motivational heterogeneity — reform scholars, constitutional scholars, and critical scholars) are also present in both field types. With all three conditions jointly symmetric, the mechanism predicts integration-attempt engagement everywhere, not differentially. For condition (a) to generate asymmetry, it must identify the specific proceduralist vocabulary needed to constitute the PROCEDURALIST integration question — "should the evidentiary evaluation concept's positive designatum incorporate the accountability dimension?" — as tractable. This vocabulary develops through engagement with the proceduralist reform tradition itself (C3 and P1 consolidation), not from the pre-constitutional adjacent domains L-2.i identifies. K-2.i's temporal-origin problem returns at this more precise specification: the vocabulary that could generate non-patrimonial engagement with the PROCEDURALIST integration question is post-C3, not pre-existing.
