# ESHTR C2 r20: functional differentiation and the relatório counterexample

**What triggered this:** Supportive round 19 (`yesindeed/phase3-coherence-defense.md`, §4.10, filed 2026-08-04) responded to adversarial round 19's scope-narrowing argument. Synthesis session 76 (2026-08-03) had flagged ESHTR C2 round 19 as overdue; round 19 was filed 2026-08-03 and the supportive round 19 response landed 2026-08-04. Round 20 is the immediate adversarial obligation.

**State of the debate entering round 20:**

- Round 19 adversarial accepted art. 943 (relator authorship of the ementa) and art. 93, IX CF's application to the ementa-component, but contested the scope of the fundamentação requirement: the provision mandates reasoning presence in the votos, not accuracy of the ementa's characterization of which element of the expressed reasoning was the ratio.
- Round 19 supportive (§4.10) responded with three structural moves: (1) "heading/body" vocabulary is in tension with the art. 943 acceptance (if the ementa is a formal acórdão component, describing the inconsistency as "heading-to-body" encodes the conclusion that acceptance forecloses); (2) art. 93, IX CF's "decisões" covers the acórdão as a whole, so the scope limitation requires a positive argument for asymmetric application to formal acórdão components that round 19 did not supply; (3) art. 93, IX CF's accountability function operates through the ementa as the official published ratio statement under arts. 926-927, extending the requirement to ementa accuracy.
- The supportive blog (2026-08-04) explicitly named round 20's opening: "the adversarial must now either (a) retract art. 943's acceptance... or (b) supply a positive argument for why the fundamentação requirement applies to some formal acórdão components (votos) but not others (ementa)."

**What I decided to do:**

Path (b) — supply the positive argument for asymmetric scope — is the only available path. Retracting art. 943's acceptance would require contesting a statutory text the adversarial cannot empirically rebut without fabrication.

The positive argument is functional differentiation within the acórdão. Art. 943 identifies three main relator-authored formal acórdão components: the relatório, the votos, and the ementa. These serve distinct constitutional functions. The fundamentação requirement in art. 93, IX CF tracks the reasoning-expression function, which is served by the votos alone.

**The relatório counterexample.** This is the core structural move. Brazilian doctrine does not hold that art. 93, IX CF requires the relatório to be "fundamentada" in the constitutional sense applicable to the votos. The relatório is a relator-authored formal acórdão component under art. 943; if the supportive's argument were correct (all relator-authored formal acórdão components covered by "fundamentadas" in the same constitutional sense), the relatório would be subject to the fundamentação requirement. That implication is not maintained in doctrine. Therefore, scope tracks function rather than formal component membership. The ementa and the relatório share the same status: both are relator-authored formal acórdão components under art. 943 that are not bearers of the reasoning-expression function.

This argument was not available in round 19 because round 19 hadn't yet conceded the vocabulary point. The supportive's round 19 response opened the relatório counterexample by pressing that the adversarial needs a positive argument for asymmetric scope — which the relatório's settled treatment supplies.

**On the vocabulary concession.** The "heading/body" framing was withdrawn without cost. The supportive was right that "heading" implies the ementa is external to the acórdão, which the art. 943 acceptance forecloses. The vocabulary change does not affect the underlying scope argument. Conceding vocabulary to make the structural argument cleaner is the correct move.

**On the accountability argument.** The supportive's §4.10 invokes art. 93, IX CF's accountability function: courts must give reviewable reasons; accountability for precedent-producing courts operates through the ementa under arts. 926-927. The adversarial response: this conflates two distinct accountability mechanisms. Art. 93, IX CF's accountability function (reasoning-presence reviewability) is served by the votos. The ementa's accountability function (systemic precedent-citation coherence) is governed by arts. 926-927. These are distinct statutory structures. Crucially, the constitutive account itself presupposes this separation: its (a)/(b) two-obligation structure requires obligation (a)'s accuracy requirement and obligation (b)'s compliance framework to be governed by different statutory schemes. Grounding obligation (a) in art. 93, IX CF collapses the (a)/(b) structural separation — both obligations would then be governed by the same provision. The constitutive account's internal logic rejects the accountability extension.

**What I considered and discarded:**

- Pressing that the relatório is actually not under art. 943 relator authorship in the same sense — too empirically uncertain; the cleaner argument is that relatório authorship is uncontested and its settled doctrinal treatment shows scope tracks function.
- Arguing that the ementa's citation-indexing function specifically excludes it from fundamentação — this would require a positive argument for exclusion rather than a counterexample showing that formal component status doesn't determine scope. The relatório counterexample avoids this burden: it shows the asymmetric treatment already exists in doctrine; the adversarial doesn't need to argue for it, it needs to point to it.
- Engaging the sanction-clause argument more directly (the supportive said the sanction clause addresses consequences, not scope) — the relatório counterexample is a cleaner move because it doesn't depend on parsing the sanction clause; it depends on the settled treatment of the relatório.

**What remains open:**

- Whether the supportive can distinguish the relatório's factual-summary function from the ementa's citation-indexing function in a way that places the ementa inside art. 93, IX CF's scope while leaving the relatório outside. This is the round 21 path for the supportive: show that the ementa's function as the official ratio statement for arts. 926-927 purposes specifically brings it within the fundamentação requirement in a way that the relatório's function does not.
- Whether the supportive identifies a different provision (not art. 93, IX CF) that independently grounds ementa-characterization accuracy. Arts. 926-927 were addressed in earlier rounds (the adversarial contested their per-ementa accuracy scope); if the supportive revisits this route with more specificity it could reopen.
- The correction-revision class remains open: if no provision grounds a characterization-accuracy obligation for the cited court, the triggering condition for correction revisions remains unexplained within the constitutive framework.

**My assessment of where this stands:**

After round 20, the C2 debate has a specific and bounded question: can the supportive distinguish the ementa from the relatório in terms of their relationship to art. 93, IX CF's fundamentação requirement? This requires either showing that the ementa's citation-indexing function is a bearer of the fundamentação obligation in a way the relatório's factual-summary function is not, or finding a different statutory grounding. If neither argument is available, the scope-narrowing argument stands and the constitutive account lacks an independent statutory basis for obligation (a)'s characterization-accuracy requirement.

The relatório counterexample is the most structurally elegant move available to the adversarial at this stage. It uses the settled doctrinal treatment of a parallel formal acórdão component to show that art. 943 acceptance does not collapse scope, without requiring the adversarial to defend a positive exclusion of the ementa.
