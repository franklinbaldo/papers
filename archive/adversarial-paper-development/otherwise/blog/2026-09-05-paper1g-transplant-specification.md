This correction has been applied in-place to §3.8.P P-2 with a note that the 1947 work is not pre-C3 and cannot supply condition (o)'s anchor. This correction does not change §3.8.P's substantive argument (the point always was that Gorphe 1924 constitutes epistemic virtue rather than external accountability); it sharpens the title precision and sets up T-2 below.

### Against (n-iv)/T-1: Art. 183's Admission-Stage Screen Answers Admissibility Relevance, Not "Decisivo per il Giudizio"

R21's S-1 is clever: if the 1940 code's art. 183 admission screen filters evidence before the trial record is formed, the record the cassation court reviews is already relevance-filtered. The *motivazione* is not needed as upstream relevance-determiner because the record itself only contains evidence that passed the relevance screen.

The press targets the nature of the relevance screen. Art. 183's screen answers admissibility relevance: prospective pertinenza and rilevanza, assessed before evidence is heard, against the parties' competing theories as advanced in their pleadings. This is fundamentally different from *omessa valutazione*'s "decisivo per il giudizio" standard, which is outcome-decisiveness under the legal theory the court actually employs.

These diverge in two directions:
- Evidence that passes art. 183 (could affect the outcome under some version of the parties' theories) may not be "decisivo per il giudizio" under the specific legal framework *motivazione* employs. The court may resolve the case on a legal theory that renders some admitted evidence non-decisive.
- Evidence bearing on the court's applied theory may not have been specifically flagged as "decisive" by art. 183's prospective screen, because the screen operates on what the parties put in issue before the evidence was heard, not on the theory the court subsequently developed.

S-1's "corrected-framework sequence" (n. 3 review → *omessa valutazione*) is itself *motivazione*-primary: the cassation court reading n. 3 is reading *motivazione* to assess adequacy, and identifying what evidence was required under the lower court's theory requires reading that theory from *motivazione* first. Art. 183 screens what the parties were permitted to present; it does not produce a record where every surviving piece of evidence is decisively required under *motivazione*'s eventual legal framework.

**New failure condition (n-v):** *Omessa valutazione* under the 1940 code determines "decisivo per il giudizio" from the art. 183-filtered record directly — treating all surviving evidence as normatively decisive without reading *motivazione*'s applied legal theory. This would collapse admissibility relevance and outcome-decisiveness into a single inquiry handled at the admission stage.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Accepting S-1's framework on the 1940 regime. R21's concession that R-1's picture fits the 1950–2012 regime better is accurate and the adversarial accepted it for the right reasons. The question is whether the 1940 original differs enough that art. 183 + art. 360 n. 5's original text fully pre-filters the record without *motivazione*. I concluded it does not: "decisivo per il giudizio" in art. 360 n. 5's text refers to decisive for the outcome of the judgment, which is a retrospective assessment requiring the court's applied theory — not the admission stage's prospective screen. S-1 establishes that the 1940 code has an admission-stage screen; it does not establish that the screen answers the same relevance question as *omessa valutazione*'s operative standard.

### Against (o-iv)/T-2: Method-Specification Without Application-Disclosure Constitutes No External Accountability Relationship

The bibliographic correction severs S-2's clearest formulation from the pre-C3 work: the "méthode technique" subtitle belongs to the 1947 *L'appréciation*, not the 1924 *La critique*. S-2 accepts this and narrows to: even the 1924 work constitutes method-specification (it describes technique for witness testimony criticism), and method-specification differs structurally from epistemic virtue because a method is public and third-party applicable.

The press: method-specification without a disclosure mechanism does not constitute an external accountability relationship. The distinction is between a standard and its instantiation.

A public method specifies what good evaluation consists in. The external accountability relationship requires that the judge's specific application of that method to particular evidence in the particular case be accessible to external verification. These are different things. A judge can follow Gorphe's criteria for assessing testimonial reliability — applying his psychological principles to a specific witness's performance — entirely internally. The result is a conviction. Unless the judge discloses how the method was applied to the specific witness and specific testimony, no external accountability relationship exists. The method's public character enables third parties to know what good application looks like; it does not enable them to verify whether the judge actually applied it.

This is what Art. 132 *motivazione* provides that Gorphe's method does not: it requires the judge to articulate specifically how the evidence was assessed, making the specific application — not just the general standard — accessible. S-2's method/virtue distinction establishes that Gorphe's tradition specifies a public standard rather than a merely personal disposition; it does not establish that the standard's specific instantiation is constituted as externally reviewable. The external accountability relationship condition (o) requires is the latter.

**New failure condition (o-vi):** The 1924 French tradition constitutes application-disclosure as a positive requirement of rational method-following — not merely method-specification, but a requirement that the specific application to particular evidentiary materials be externally articulable.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Accepting the method/virtue distinction as sufficient for condition (o). The distinction is genuinely interesting and S-2 is not wrong that method-specification and epistemic virtue are different. A public method has a different normative structure than a private virtue — it is in principle applicable by anyone, not just the person who possesses the relevant character trait. But the adversarial's position on condition (o) has consistently required external accountability in the sense of external reviewability of specific applications — not merely that a public standard exists. Conceding the method/virtue distinction without pressing the application-disclosure gap would underspecify what condition (o) requires.

### Against (o-v)/T-3: Accept the § 259 CPO Textual Finding; §3.8.D Independence Principle Applies to One-Provision Architecture

R21 withdraws the German institutional-prior argument and offers instead a textual finding: § 259 CPO 1877's second sentence places a conviction-specific reasons duty inside the free-evaluation norm. This is architecturally different from the Italian two-institution structure (Art. 116 + Art. 132 separately codified) and from the German two-provision structure (§ 286 ZPO + § 313 ZPO) the adversarial had analyzed.

The adversarial accepts the textual finding and accepts the R-3 concession. S-3 is right to withdraw the institutional-prior argument.

The press: the §3.8.D independence principle applies to code architecture generally, not only to cases where accountability-related text appears in a separate provision. The principle is that how codifiers chose to arrange their text does not determine how the scholarship organized its conceptual debates. This holds whether the accountability-related text appears in § 313 ZPO (separate provision) or in § 259 CPO's second sentence (inside the free-evaluation norm). The independence is between code architecture and scholarly discourse organization — not between "two provisions" and "one provision."

German *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholars engaging with § 259 CPO's one-provision structure could engage with the second sentence in multiple ways: as a technical form requirement for conviction pronouncements (specifying how verdicts are announced rather than what the conviction requires), as an interpretive scope question (how much reasons for conviction must be stated), or as evidence that free evaluation and reasons-giving were conceptually unified in the positive designatum. These are the scholarship's organizational choices; § 259 CPO's architecture does not determine which choice the scholarship made.

S-3's burden-reversal argument is therefore not conclusive: it establishes that the code architecture is different from what the adversarial had analyzed. It does not establish that this architectural difference determines scholarly discourse organization in a way that makes the §3.8.D independence argument inapplicable. The question for condition (o-iii, German prong) and condition (o-v) remains: did the *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship engage with accountability as the answer to the rationality question within the concept's positive designatum? The § 259 CPO second sentence is new evidence about codification technique; primary-source evidence from the scholarship's organized debates is still what the surrender conditions require.

**What I considered and discarded:**

Accepting the burden reversal and conceding the German prong. R21's textual finding is genuinely new — the adversarial had analyzed the § 286 ZPO + § 313 ZPO two-provision structure and found it replicates the Italian independence problem; § 259 CPO's one-provision integration is architecturally different. For a moment the adversarial considered whether this architectural difference is so significant that the scholarship's organizational choices are no longer independent from it. The conclusion was no: the independence principle operates between code architecture (any architecture) and scholarly discourse organization. A one-provision structure does not force scholars to constitute accountability as internal to the concept's positive designatum; it makes that constitution somewhat more natural (the text places the duty inside the grant), but "more natural" is not "determined." The Possibility A/B question is still about what the scholarship did, and primary-source evidence from the scholarship is still required.

## Assessment After R22

**n-iv/n-v:** The live question is whether *omessa valutazione di prove* under the 1940 code determined "decisivo per il giudizio" from the art. 183-filtered record directly or retrospectively from *motivazione*'s applied legal theory. R23 would need to show either (a) that "decisivo per il giudizio" in art. 360 n. 5's original text was assessed prospectively from the admitted evidence record without reference to *motivazione*'s employed legal framework, or (b) that art. 183's relevance screen specifically tracked the same legal theory the lower court would eventually employ in *motivazione* — making the two screening operations (art. 183 prospective; art. 360 n. 5 retrospective) produce the same subset.

**o-iv/o-vi:** The live question is whether the pre-C3 French professional judicial evaluation tradition — specifically *La critique du témoignage* (1924) — constituted not only method-specification but application-disclosure as a positive requirement of rational evaluation. R23 would need to show either (a) that Gorphe's 1924 method was understood to require the judge's specific application to specific evidence be externally articulable as part of what following the method consists in, or (b) a different French pre-C3 source that constitutes this application-disclosure requirement.

**o-v/T-3:** The live question is whether the *freie Beweiswürdigung* scholarship engaged with accountability as the answer to the rationality question within the concept's scholarly positive designatum, given § 259 CPO's one-provision architecture. R23 would need to show either (a) primary-source evidence from German proceduralist scholarship organized around § 259 CPO that constitutes accountability as internal to free evaluation's positive designatum (not merely as a technical form requirement), or (b) a structural argument showing why the one-provision architecture determines the scholarship's organizational choices in a way the §3.8.D independence principle cannot absorb.
