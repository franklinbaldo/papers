# ESHTR C2 r22: the per-decision dimension is designation, not expression

**What triggered this:** Supportive round 21 (`yesindeed/phase3-coherence-defense.md`, §4.12, filed 2026-08-12) responded to adversarial r21's three-ground attack (criterion misapplication; art. 926 caput redundancy; normative double-counting) with the per-decision/cross-decision two-dimension analysis: the ementa's ratio-constitutive function has two analytically separable dimensions — per-decision (a reasoning-expression act grounding obligation (a) in art. 93, IX CF) and cross-decision (a citation-reference function grounding obligation (b) in arts. 926–927). The supportive also argued that art. 926 caput operates systemically rather than per-ementa, and that the dual-provision structure involves no double-counting because the dimensions are analytically distinct. This session presses two structural challenges to that response.

---

## State of the debate entering round 22

- Round 20 adversarial conceded vocabulary (the "heading/body" framing is withdrawn; the ementa is a relator-authored formal acórdão component under art. 943), supplied the *relatório* counterexample as the functional-differentiation argument — scope tracks the reasoning-expression function, not formal component membership — and rejected the accountability conflation.
- Round 20 support response introduced the ratio-constitutive function criterion: scope tracks "bearing an authoritative relationship to the court's *ratio* determination for the precedent system," which the ementa satisfies and the *relatório* does not; the (a)/(b) two-actor, two-provision structure is maintained.
- Round 21 adversarial response pressed criterion misapplication (the ratio-constitutive function is a precedent-coherence function, not a reasoning-expression function; the functional-differentiation criterion places it inside arts. 926–927, not art. 93, IX CF), redundancy via art. 926 caput (independently grounds obligation (a)), and normative double-counting (no theory of interaction between two provisions with different constitutional authority and enforcement mechanisms).
- Round 21 support response introduces the per-decision/cross-decision two-dimension analysis: the ementa's per-decision dimension is a reasoning-expression act (characterizing the ratio within this decision's reasoning structure) grounded in art. 93, IX CF; the cross-decision dimension is a citation-reference function grounded in arts. 926–927; art. 926 caput operates systemically; no double-counting because each obligation is grounded in the provision appropriate to its analytical domain.

---

## What I decided to argue

The two-dimension analysis is a genuine structural advance over the ratio-constitutive function criterion. The prior criterion was pitched at the level of the ementa's overall function in the precedent system; the two-dimension analysis is pitched at the level of the act the ementa performs within a single decision. The adversarial's r21 attacks therefore need to be re-engaged at this finer analytical level.

**Two arguments:**

**1. (ii-a) The per-decision dimension is designation, not expression.** The supportive r21 characterizes the ementa's per-decision dimension as a reasoning-expression act. This characterization is incorrect. The act of designating which element of the already-expressed reasoning was decisive for this decision is an indexing act performed on the reasoning record — it presupposes that the votos have already expressed reasoning and operates on that expressed material by selecting and labeling one element as the *ratio*. This is categorically different from expressing reasoning.

Art. 93, IX CF governs decisions being "fundamentadas" — it requires that reasoning be present and expressed in the decision's reasoning record (the votos) so that the decisional process is reviewable. The accountability function the provision targets is reasoning-presence reviewability: can a reviewing court, party, or later court identify the reasoning that drove the outcome? This accountability function is fully satisfied when the votos express reasoning, regardless of whether the ementa correctly designates which expressed element was decisive. The elevation error case — the ementa misidentifying the *ratio* by elevating obiter to *ratio* — does not impair the votos' reasoning record or its reviewability. The votos still express reasoning; the defect is in the ementa's posterior indexing of that record.

The *relatório* analogy, which the functional-differentiation framework accepts as a formal acórdão component under art. 943 that does not fall within art. 93, IX CF, confirms the principle: formal per-decision components authored by the relator and bearing authoritative relationships to specific judicial outputs do not automatically inherit art. 93, IX CF's scope. The *relatório* bears an authoritative relationship to the factual record of this decision; this does not make factual-record accuracy an art. 93, IX CF obligation. The ementa bears an authoritative relationship to the *ratio* determination of this decision; this does not make *ratio*-designation accuracy an art. 93, IX CF obligation. The source of coverage is function-type (reasoning-expression), not formal component status or authoritative relationship.

The supportive's per-decision dimension, correctly analyzed, is a designation act, not a reasoning-expression act. The functional-differentiation framework does not place designation acts inside art. 93, IX CF's scope.

**2. (ii-b) Art. 926 caput per-event integrity scope.** The supportive r21 reads art. 926 caput's "mantê-la íntegra" as a systemic obligation — maintaining coherent jurisprudência across cases over time — with per-ementa accuracy governed only derivatively. Art. 926 §4's prospective-modification enforcement mechanism is cited in support: the enforcement framework addresses systemic departure rather than individual publication events.

This reading conflates the scope of the obligation with the scope of one enforcement mechanism. Art. 926 §4 addresses one dimension of art. 926 caput's integrity requirement: when an emerging doctrinal shift threatens systemic coherence, the modification must be prospective. This does not imply that art. 926 caput's substantive "mantê-la íntegra" obligation lacks per-event force.

Systemic integrity presupposes per-ementa accuracy as its structural prerequisite. A court that regularly mischaracterizes the *ratio* of its decisions in individual ementas — labeling obiter as *ratio* — has not "maintained its jurisprudência integral" in any meaningful sense: the published jurisprudential output that the precedent system uses for citation compliance does not accurately characterize the decisions from which it derives. Systemic incoherence is the accumulation of per-event inaccuracies. The systemic obligation therefore entails a per-ementa accuracy obligation as a necessary condition of its satisfaction.

Art. 926 caput's per-event integrity requirement, correctly read, independently grounds obligation (a). The (a)/(b) two-actor structure is preserved under this formulation without requiring art. 93, IX CF: obligation (a) is the cited court's per-ementa accuracy obligation, grounded in art. 926 caput's structural prerequisite for integral jurisprudência; obligation (b) is the citing court's citation compliance obligation, governed by arts. 926–927's compliance framework. Art. 93, IX CF is structurally dispensable for obligation (a) under this formulation.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing the criterion misapplication argument without the designation/expression distinction.** Adversarial r21 pressed that the ratio-constitutive function is a precedent-coherence function, not a reasoning-expression function. The supportive r21 responded by disaggregating the function into two dimensions. The r21 criterion misapplication argument addresses the function as a whole; it does not directly engage the per-decision dimension at the level the supportive introduced. The designation/expression distinction in argument 1 is the required level of engagement — it meets the two-dimension analysis on its own terms.

**Accepting the per-decision dimension and arguing only from art. 926 caput redundancy.** Argument 2 alone has structural force but concedes too much. If the per-decision dimension is a reasoning-expression act, then even if art. 926 caput independently grounds obligation (a), the supportive could argue that art. 93, IX CF applies additionally. Argument 1 contests the per-decision characterization; argument 2 shows that even if art. 93, IX CF did apply, it is not structurally necessary. Both are needed.

**Contesting the art. 926 §4 enforcement mechanism reading directly.** The supportive r21 invokes art. 926 §4 as evidence of systemic scope. I address this by distinguishing enforcement-mechanism scope from substantive-obligation scope — art. 926 §4 is evidence of one enforcement dimension, not a ceiling on art. 926 caput's substantive reach. This is more precise than contesting whether art. 926 §4 applies to individual ementas.

**Retreating to the normative double-counting argument.** Adversarial r21's double-counting argument was addressed by the supportive r21's no-double-counting response (different actors, different analytical domains, different provisions appropriate to each). Arguments 1 and 2 together are a stronger response because they press that (a) the per-decision dimension does not trigger art. 93, IX CF in the first place, and (b) art. 926 caput independently supplies obligation (a); the double-counting objection follows from this structural analysis rather than from the provisions' having different authority and enforcement mechanisms.

---

## Assessment

**Strength of the attack.** Argument 1 operates at the analytical level the supportive introduced. The designation/expression distinction is conceptually precise: there is a genuine and non-trivial difference between expressing reasoning (the votos' function) and designating which expressed element was decisive (the ementa's ratio-constitutive act). The supportive's characterization of the per-decision dimension as a "reasoning-expression act" is either ambiguous (the ementa expresses the court's judgment about which expressed reasoning was decisive, which is a second-order expression act) or incorrect (the first-order expression act is the votos'; the ementa's act is indexing). The adversarial presses the second reading. Whether the second-order reading has independent force is the question the supportive r22 response must address.

Argument 2 is structurally clean: the "mantê-la íntegra" reading at the per-event level follows naturally from the systemic-integrity obligation's structural prerequisites. The supportive's art. 926 §4 enforcement-mechanism argument is responsive to the systemic reading but does not exhaust the substantive reading.

**What remains open.** The supportive r22 response has two available paths:

- Show that the ementa's per-decision dimension is a second-order reasoning-expression act: the ementa expresses the court's authoritative judgment about which expressed reasoning was decisive, which is itself a form of reasoning expression (meta-level) rather than mere indexing. This requires a theory of what counts as "reasoning-expression" for art. 93, IX CF purposes that includes second-order characterization acts.
- Contest the art. 926 caput per-event reading by showing that the "íntegra" obligation's per-event force does not generate an accuracy obligation at the level of individual ementas — that the structural-prerequisite argument overreads the relationship between systemic integrity and per-decision accuracy.

The debate is narrowing. The question is whether art. 93, IX CF reaches second-order characterization acts, and whether art. 926 caput's systemic integrity obligation generates per-ementa accuracy requirements. Neither question has a settled doctrinal answer; both sides are constructing structural arguments from the provisions' text. The honest-limit question is whether either side can develop a textual or functional argument strong enough to foreclose the other's reading.
