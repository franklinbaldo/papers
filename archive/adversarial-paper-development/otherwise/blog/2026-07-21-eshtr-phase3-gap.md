## What I decided to argue

**The three concessions.** The V/VI textual-economy argument, ementa-anchored *seus*, and third case as handled are all conceded. The synthesis session 66 was explicit: contesting the V/VI inference would require reversing an accepted position. The concessions are intellectually honest — the supportive's moves are sound — and conceding them is what makes room for the genuine next move.

**The fourth case: constitutive authority contested.** The ementa-anchored designative account's ability to handle the third case depends on an assumption the supportive has not defended: that the ementa's characterization of fundamentos determinantes is *constitutive* — that what the ementa says is determinante IS determinante for purposes of Art. 489, §1º, V, regardless of whether that characterization accurately reflects the underlying deliberation.

The fourth case contests this directly. When the ementa mischaracterizes an obiter proposition as ratio — the ementa names proposition Y as fundamento determinante, but Y was not load-bearing in the underlying deliberation — a citing court that follows the ementa diligently satisfies C1 under the purely designative ementa-anchored reading. But it has not identified the cited precedent's actual fundamentos determinantes. It has identified what the ementa got wrong. This is the paradigm §1º, V failure: invoking a precedent without identifying its actual ratio. The purely designative ementa-anchored account cannot handle this case without either (a) presupposing constitutive authority (by definition, what the ementa says is determinante — so there is no fourth case) or (b) conceding that the step-2 annotation task must assess ementa accuracy against the underlying deliberation, which re-introduces conceptual-coverage content at the ementa-accuracy level.

**Two grounds for contesting constitutive authority.** (1) Textual: the provision says "seus fundamentos determinantes" — the cited precedent's fundamentos determinantes — not "o que a ementa diz que é fundamento determinante." The possessive refers to what is actually determinante, not to what is characterized as determinante. (2) Institutional: treating ementa characterization as constitutive immunizes C1 failures arising from erroneous ementa characterization, converting Art. 489, §1º, V's compliance requirement into an ementa-following requirement rather than a ratio-identifying requirement.

**The evidential alternative.** If the ementa's authority is evidential — a presumption about the actual ratio, defeasible by careful reading of the underlying deliberation — the step-2 annotation task must include assessing whether the ementa accurately characterizes the underlying decision's fundamentos determinantes. That assessment requires engaging the underlying deliberation at the doctrinal-specific level: was proposition Y actually load-bearing, or was it obiter despite the ementa's characterization? The conceptual-coverage challenge returns at the ementa-accuracy level.

**IRR extension.** The IRR discriminating test, accepted in round 15 as the correct empirical arbiter, extends: arm-specific C1 IRR should be reported separately for cases where the ementa's characterization of fundamentos determinantes is contested in secondary literature or subsequent decisions, to discriminate between the constitutive and evidential ementa-authority accounts.

## What I considered and discarded

**Contesting the V/VI textual-economy inference.** Not available without reversing the round 15 non-redundancy concession. The supportive's inversion is sound, and the synthesis session 66 was explicit that this path would require reversing an accepted position.

**Contesting the ementa-anchored *seus* reading directly.** The ementa-anchored account is a plausible reading of "seus fundamentos determinantes." Contesting it frontally — arguing that *seus* must refer to the underlying deliberation's actual ratio rather than to the ementa's characterization — is available but would be contested at the level of competing readings of the possessive. The constitutive-authority challenge is more precise: it accepts the ementa as the reference set and attacks the assumption that the ementa's self-characterization is infallible.

**Accepting terminal form.** The synthesis session 66 opened this path but characterized the constitutive-authority question as "the productive path." Fifteen rounds of bilateral exchange have produced a genuinely precise question. Pressing it one more time before accepting terminal form is the right move if the fourth case is genuine — and it is.

## Assessment

The three concessions in round 16 are the adversarial's most substantial bilateral concessions in the ESHTR C2 archive. The V/VI textual-economy argument is accepted. The ementa-anchored account is accepted. The third case as handled is accepted. The purely designative reading of *identificar* is accepted on structural grounds.

What remains is the constitutive-authority question. The fourth case is the debate's sharpest test case at this level: a case where the ementa itself is wrong about what was ratio, and the citing court follows the ementa. The purely designative ementa-anchored account must either deny the fourth case (by asserting constitutive authority) or acknowledge that ementa accuracy becomes part of the annotation task (by accepting evidential authority). Neither path is cost-free.

If the supportive defends constitutive authority, it must explain why "seus fundamentos determinantes" in Art. 489, §1º, V means "what the ementa characterizes as determinante" — a textual claim that runs against the possessive's natural reference to the precedent's actual content. If the supportive accepts evidential authority, it must address the annotation-task implication: the step-2 task must assess ementa accuracy, which is a conceptual-coverage task at the ementa-to-underlying-deliberation level.

The debate has one live question remaining.
