## The two attacks

### Against (cc/ee): proclamation supplies dispositif fact, not *fundamento determinante* fact

R40's proclamation argument proves too little for what requirement (1) needs.

What CPC art. 941 establishes: the President proclaims the *resultado* (result). What RISTF art. 135 §2º establishes: the President proclaims the *decisão* (decision). What RISTF art. 97 establishes: the *extrato da ata* contains "a decisão proclamada pelo Presidente" and the redactor's name.

None of these provisions establishes that the President proclaims the court's *fundamento determinante*. "Nos termos do voto do Relator" — the voting formula — names which justice's voto the majority followed. It establishes whose voto governs, not which element within that voto constitutes the collectively determinative ground. The *extrato da ata* records the proclaimed decision and the redactor's name; the ground is absent.

R40's own fragmented-no-*tese* admission provides the diagnostic. In the fragmented class, the proclamation records: (a) the dispositif result, and (b) the redactor identity. What is absent in the fragmented class is a majority *fundamento determinante*. But this is precisely the *fundamento determinante* fact that is absent, not the dispositif fact. The proclamation in the fragmented class still supplies both dispositif and redactor — what it lacks is the ground-level assertion, because no majority ground exists to assert.

This diagnostic shows that the dispositif/fundamento distinction tracks a structural feature of the proclamation act, not just a structural feature of fragmented cases. In both the fragmented and non-fragmented cases, the proclamation invariably supplies: (i) the result and (ii) the redactor identity. What the proclamation additionally supplies in non-fragmented cases with a majority ground is not a distinct proclamation act — it is the downstream consequence that a majority ground exists to be designated. The ground enters the record when the ementa is drafted, not when the proclamation occurs.

R40's honesty limit, restated precisely: what is absent in the fragmented-no-*tese* case is the *fundamento determinante* fact before designation, not a separate proclamation formula for the ground. The non-fragmented case does not contain a separate proclamation of the ground either — both cases have the same proclamation structure (result + redactor); the difference is that in the non-fragmented case the ementa's subsequent designation is accurate against the deliberative record, while in the fragmented case there is no majority ground for the ementa to accurately designate.

**Failure condition (ee):** R40's (cc) argument survives this attack if Brazilian practice or doctrine authoritatively construes "nos termos do voto do Relator" as collective court endorsement of the specific grounds in the relator's voto — not only the dispositif — constituting the *fundamento determinante* assertion r39's pre-existence standard requires. R40's diary names this as an unresolved uncertainty. Until that authority is produced, the proclamation supplies the dispositif fact, not the *fundamento determinante* fact.

### Against (dd/ff): the majority-on-grounds rule presupposes level-of-generality, not provides it

R40 relocated from the *regimento interno* to Marinoni's majority-on-grounds rule: the *fundamento determinante* "só pode ser formada pela maioria do colegiado." This is a real improvement — the *regimento interno* claim was correctly rejected. But the relocation moves the problem rather than solving it.

The majority-on-grounds rule counts majority support over "each ground." What individuates a ground for counting purposes is not specified by the rule. This is not an implementation detail — it is the threshold question the rule presupposes to have been answered before counting can begin.

Consider two justices whose votos address the requirement that citing courts identify *fundamentos determinantes* to prevent erroneous extensions of cited precedents:

- J4 writes: "The citing court must identify the *fundamentos determinantes* of the cited precedent because extension to factually distinguishable situations violates art. 489, §1º, V."
- J5 writes: "Art. 489, §1º, V requires identifying the ratio by asking whether the precedent's factual and legal premises hold in the present case."

At fine-grained specificity, these are distinct grounds: J4's is a case-distinguishability ground, J5's is a premise-transfer ground. At coarse-grained specificity, both are C1-type *fundamento*-identification grounds. Whether they constitute the "same ground" for majority-counting purposes depends entirely on the level of generality at which the individuation criterion operates.

Marinoni does not supply this criterion. The *autônomos/agregados* distinction addresses whether a voto's reasoning is self-sufficient for the dispositif, not at what doctrinal level of specificity two votos state "the same" ground. Brazilian proceduralist doctrine has not produced a canonical individuation criterion for this purpose — if it had, the level-of-generality problem in precedent identification that the C1/C4 debate has examined from round 13 onward would already be resolved.

The counting problem is not susceptible to the same primary-source move r40 deployed on (bb) and (cc). For (bb), the sources showed ground-independence is the operative criterion. For (cc), the sources showed the proclamation records the result and the redactor. For (dd), no source specifies the level-of-generality criterion because that criterion is normatively contested in precisely the domain (precedent identification) where ESHTR's C1 annotation task operates.

**Failure condition (ff):** R40's (dd) relocation survives if Marinoni's framework or Brazilian proceduralist doctrine specifies an operational level-of-generality criterion for *fundamento determinante* individuation — a criterion determining from voto text alone at what level of doctrinal specificity two invocations constitute "the same ground" versus "related but distinct grounds," reducing ground-counting to a mechanical text-reading task without normative judgment. R40's diary names this as the adversarial's best route back. Until that criterion is produced, the majority-on-grounds rule presupposes what it would need to provide.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing (bb) again despite r40's strong response.** The ground-independence redefinition is well-grounded in Marinoni and the designation rules. I accepted the concession. Re-pressing after a clean primary-source resolution would loop without new material.

**Pressing the "nos termos do voto do Relator" point harder by constructing a hypothetical proclamation formula.** The (ee) failure condition captures what r41 needs to establish without inventing a proclamation formula that does not appear in the sources. The burden is on the supportive to supply the authority; the adversarial does not need to fabricate one to make the point.

**Arguing that the *autônomos/agregados* distinction itself has a level-of-generality problem.** This is available but targets (bb), which I accepted. The level-of-generality attack is stronger at (dd) where it attacks the counting rule directly rather than the individuation of a single voto's independence claim.

**Using the CPC arts. 985 and 1.039 *tese* mechanism as a counter-example.** R40's diary mentions these as confirming that Marinoni's proposal is the institutional form of the majority-on-grounds point. But these provisions concern the *tese jurídica* in *repercussão geral* proceedings, which is a formally adopted collective statement — a different mechanism from the ementa-anchored ground-identification that requirement (1) targets. Pressing this would confuse two distinct contexts.

## Assessment after round 41

Three conditions from r39 survive into the ledger:

- **(bb):** Substantially addressed by r40's ground-independence redefinition. The (bb) failure condition as originally stated (independence criterion mechanically specified by *regimento interno*) is now the weaker version of the live question. R40's redefinition relocates the independence criterion to Marinoni's textual distinction, which is primary-source-grounded. Not a live press after r40.

- **(cc/ee):** Live after r41. R40's proclamation argument establishes the dispositif fact's pre-designation existence; r41 shows the *fundamento determinante* fact is structurally distinct from the dispositif fact, using r40's own fragmented-no-*tese* admission as the diagnostic.

- **(dd/ff):** Live after r41. R40's majority-on-grounds relocation is well-grounded in Marinoni but presupposes a level-of-generality criterion the framework does not supply. R40's own diary identifies this as the adversarial's best route back.

**Loop-closure check:** R41 does not loop. It presses two new structural arguments not present in r39 — (ee) the dispositif/fundamento distinction in the proclamation act, and (ff) the level-of-generality presupposition in the majority-on-grounds rule. Both arguments use evidence from r40's own diary and primary sources r40 established. The concession on (bb) is a genuine concession, not a strategy to refocus.
