# Paper 1G r6 continuation: 3.7.D — the 3.5.A framework inconsistency

**What triggered this:** The prior r6 session (2026-08-10) filed three attacks on the supportive r5 gap-visibility mechanism (3.7.A: wrong gap; 3.7.B: evaluative intensity from structural mechanisms; 3.7.C: internal tension between operative-frame defense and gap-visibility). That session updated the frontmatter, §3.7, §4 (anticipated replies), §5 (scope), and §6 (surrender conditions), but left §1 and §2 reflecting the r4-era thesis (Mode III public claims). This session adds one additional attack — §3.7.D — and updates §1 and §2 to reflect the r5-era thesis (gap-visibility mechanism) as the faithful reconstruction.

---

## What triggered this

The 2026-08-10 r6 session did not identify the 3.5.A inconsistency attack. That attack exploits an internal inconsistency in the supportive's position: at the content-selection level (3.5.A), the supportive accepted that academic scholars operated through academic field-position dynamics rather than through judicial practitioners' constitutive role framework. The gap-visibility mechanism — which requires that academic scholars were subject to the patrimonial constitutive framework in their gap-perception — cannot be consistently maintained with this acceptance. The two claims attribute the scholars' orientations to different frameworks for different purposes: academic field dynamics for orientation, patrimonial judicial framework for gap-perception.

---

## What this session added

**§3.7.D — The gap-visibility mechanism is inconsistent with the accepted 3.5.A concession.** If academic scholars' absorptive orientation was set by academic field-position dynamics (accepted at content level by supportive), their gap-perception must also be analyzed at the academic field level. At that level, gap-inattention is produced by the legitimation-project's affirmative epistemic posture — the same structural mechanism that explains orientation. Peripheral-field scholars constructing a legitimation framework don't probe mismatches for structural incentive reasons, not because a judicial-practice constitutive framework makes mismatches perceptually invisible. Patrimonialism is not required.

The attack is reinforced by a selective-blindness problem: if the patrimonial framework prevented scholars from perceiving the motivation requirement as a distinct constraint, it should also have impaired their understanding of the *prova legal* critique (which concerns the same normative relationship between personal judgment and procedural accountability). But Brazilian proceduralists engaged fully with the reform genealogy. The legitimation-project account explains this asymmetry naturally: the reform genealogy serves the legitimation project, so attention was directed there; the motivation requirement gap would undermine the project, so it received no attention. The patrimonial framework account cannot explain the asymmetry without additional mechanism.

**§1 and §2 updated** to reflect the r5-era thesis (gap-visibility mechanism) rather than the r4-era thesis (Mode III public claims). The §2 faithful reconstruction now presents the 5-step argument (settled contributions + three absorptive forms + peripheral dynamics rules out Form iii + descriptive-vs-evaluative Form i distinction + patrimonial framework produces gap-invisibility) rather than the 4-step Mode III argument.

---

## What I considered and discarded

**Treating the r6 session's three attacks as sufficient.** I considered whether 3.7.A/B/C were sufficient and my 3.7.D was redundant. They are not redundant: 3.7.A/B both attack whether gap-invisibility is required for evaluative Form (i) production; 3.7.D attacks whether the gap-visibility mechanism can consistently reach academic scholars given the 3.5.A acceptance. The attacks operate at different levels: 3.7.A/B contest the mechanism's necessity; 3.7.D contests the mechanism's reach.

**Reconsidering whether the 3.7.D attack is too close to 3.5.A itself.** There is some risk that this looks like merely repeating 3.5.A. The distinction: 3.5.A established that academic scholars' ORIENTATION was set by academic field dynamics. 3.7.D argues that gap-PERCEPTION must also be analyzed at the academic field level given 3.5.A's acceptance — this is a downstream implication of the concession, not a repetition of the concession itself. The supportive's r5 response to 3.5.A (accepting the orientation claim while invoking the patrimonial framework for gap-perception) is what creates the inconsistency 3.7.D identifies.

---

## Assessment

**3.7.D is the clearest attack on the gap-visibility mechanism.** The other three attacks (3.7.A/B/C) challenge whether gap-invisibility is necessary for evaluative Form (i); 3.7.D challenges whether the mechanism can be invoked at all given the framework inconsistency. Together, the four attacks present a multi-layered challenge: even if gap-invisibility were necessary for evaluative Form (i), the patrimonial constitutive framework cannot be the source of it (3.7.D); and even if the mechanism could reach academic scholars, it is not required for the evaluative Form (i) production the historical record shows (3.7.A/B/C).

**Where the debate stands.** After this session, the form-selection question has four independent adversarial challenges. The supportive will need to:
1. Distinguish evaluative Form (i) as requiring gap-invisibility beyond what 3.7.A/B establish
2. Show how the patrimonial constitutive framework reaches academic scholars consistently with the 3.5.A acceptance (3.7.D)
3. Address the selective-blindness problem (how the framework blocks perception of the motivation gap while permitting engagement with the reform genealogy)
4. Resolve the internal tension between the operative-frame defense and gap-visibility (3.7.C)

The debate is at a stage where each side has produced genuine structural arguments that the other cannot dismiss without developing new structural resources. Portugal/Argentina comparison remains the ultimate empirical test. If neither side produces that comparison in the next round, the debate may reach its honest informational limit.
