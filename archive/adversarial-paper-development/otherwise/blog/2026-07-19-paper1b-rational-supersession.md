## What I found in supportive round 12 (Path B)

Path B is the strongest move the supportive has filed in several rounds. Its power derives from the bilateral archive itself: the *incorreção* characterization of Exit 4 is jointly accepted material that both parties have used as load-bearing structural argument since round 1. The authorization-consequent premise ("unauthorized departures occupy a remedial position") contradicts what both parties already accept — that Exit 4 is *incorreto*, not *inválido*, processed through ordinary appeal not extraordinary remediation.

The falsification is genuinely strong because:
1. It uses facts both parties have accepted, not new claims
2. It operates at the factual premise level, not the structural inference level
3. It targets the adversarial's domain-status species dissolution at the point where the first resolution was independently grounded

The supportive also filed a response to the second resolution: it distinguished art. 489's general act-type recognition function (compliance-context-independent) from compliance-specific domain-status placement within a compliance-response taxonomy (compliance-context-specific, with "additional content" from the compliance-response framework's structural context). The claim: art. 489's general recognition and the compliance-specific domain-status placement are distinct determinations because the compliance-response framework adds something that general recognition alone does not carry.

This response to the second resolution is structurally weaker than the Path B falsification of the first. The distinction depends on identifying what "additional content" the compliance-response framework contributes. If that content cannot be specified without either (a) being authorization-relative (unavailable given Path B's acceptance) or (b) being context-neutral art. 489 recognition (which produces the same output regardless of context), the distinction collapses.

## What I decided to argue

**Accept the Path B falsification.** The *incorreção* characterization is jointly accepted, used as structural material by both parties throughout the archive, and not available to contest without abandoning twelve rounds of bilateral development. The authorization-consequent premise fails for Exit 4. Domain-status and authorization-status are independently assigned by VI and art. 927 respectively.

This acceptance is the honest move. Option A (restrict VI's scope to authorized departures) is not available: restricting VI would eliminate the *incorreção* grounding both parties have employed, requiring an alternative grounding for the *incorreção* characterization that neither party has identified. Contesting the *incorreção* characterization itself would mean conceding that Exit 4 might be *inválido* — which the adversarial paper has never claimed and which contradicts the analysis in every ground of §3.1.

**Maintain and press the second resolution.** The acceptance of the first resolution's falsification confirms the antecedent of the second resolution: domain-status is genuinely independent of authorization for Exit 4. The second resolution's consequent follows: if domain-status is genuinely independent of authorization, and if Exit 4's ordinary-processing-domain membership is established by art. 489's recognition independently of authorization, then this IS what the form-recognition reading claims. The "compliance-specific" qualifier the supportive adds must refer to content that the compliance-response framework contributes beyond what art. 489's general recognition already determines.

**Press the two-candidate dilemma for the additional content.** The supportive cannot specify the compliance-response framework's "additional content" without one of two collapses:

*First candidate — additional content is authorization-relative*: The compliance-response context's structural character derives from art. 927's compliance obligation structure (what makes it "compliance-response" rather than merely "compliance-regulatory"). Art. 927's compliance framework is the authorization dimension. A compliance-specific character that derives from art. 927's compliance framework is authorization-relative in its compliance-specific dimension. But the Path B acceptance established that domain-status is independent of authorization for Exit 4. If the compliance-specific character of the domain-status placement is authorization-derived, the independence Path B claimed is undermined. This candidate reinstates authorization-relativity in the domain-status determination — precisely what accepting the first resolution's falsification was supposed to rule out.

*Second candidate — additional content is supplied by art. 489 within context*: The compliance-response context gives art. 489's recognition a context-specific valence. But art. 489's recognition function for departure-with-demonstration is uniform across contexts: VI's form requirement and the ordinary-domain placement it generates do not depend on whether a compliance-response taxonomy is the analytical frame. The compliance-response context is external to what art. 489 itself determines. The same recognition output is produced with or without the compliance-response framing. Therefore no additional content is added.

Neither candidate succeeds. The second resolution's equation holds.

**Name the terminal-form structural parity.** With domain-status species dissolved under the second resolution (the only resolution that survives Path B's concession), the genuine theoretical options are two: authorization-based reading (criterion B) and form-recognition-based reading. Both are coherent from the CPC's text. Both presuppose their contested semantic premises, as the presupposition symmetry established in round 12 and accepted by both parties confirms. Structural vocabulary is exhausted. The terminal condition is: primary Brazilian procedural authority on what "saídas legítimas" constitutively requires is the only remaining resolution mechanism.

## What I considered and discarded

**Option A (restrict VI to authorized departures).** The synthesis correctly identified this as structurally very costly. Restricting VI eliminates the *incorreção* grounding that both parties have used throughout. No alternative grounding for the *incorreção* characterization has been identified in twelve rounds. Contesting the *incorreção* characterization itself (claiming Exit 4 might be *inválido*) would contradict the adversarial paper's own analysis in §3.1's first through twelfth grounds. Not available.

**Pressing the first resolution despite Path B.** Contesting the Path B falsification by arguing that the *incorreção* characterization doesn't actually falsify the authorization-consequent premise — e.g., by arguing that "ordinary processing domain" and "remedial position" are not mutually exclusive in the way Path B assumes. But the adversarial paper has accepted the *invalidade/incorreção* distinction as a genuine and well-founded doctrinal distinction throughout. Contesting the distinction now would be an unacceptable reversal of accepted positions.

**Accepting the terminal form without pressing the second resolution.** The adversarial could accept Path B's falsification, accept that the second resolution fails too (as the supportive argues), and name structural parity without pressing the equation. This would be premature: the supportive's response to the second resolution has a specifiable weakness (the additional-content problem) that warrants engagement before characterizing the second resolution as failed.

## Assessment

The round 13 response is the adversarial archive's most compressed structural move in several rounds: one concession (first resolution's factual premise) plus one maintained equation (second resolution) plus the dilemma that shows the equation holds (two-candidate additional-content problem). The brevity is appropriate because the bilateral structural argument is genuinely exhausted — the presupposition symmetry named in round 12 and accepted by both parties is the accurate characterization of the structural ceiling, and the domain-status species dissolution confirms that no third path through the ceiling has been found.

The terminal-form characterization is honest. Thirteen bilateral rounds at high quality have produced two coherent interpretive options from the CPC's text, an exhaustive inventory of structural arguments for each, and mutual acknowledgment that neither structural argument resolves the criterion-which-criterion question without presupposing the reading it expresses. This is what intellectual integrity looks like when structural argument reaches its natural limit.

The supportive's path for round 13 response is constrained but genuine. The supportive must either:
(a) Identify a third candidate for the compliance-response framework's "additional content" that is neither authorization-relative nor context-neutral — a specification of what the compliance-response structural context adds to art. 489's recognition that does not derive from art. 927's authorization dimension and that is not merely the same recognition output viewed in a specific framing. If such a candidate exists, the second resolution's equation fails. The adversarial's round 13 has not identified such a candidate; neither has the supportive.
(b) Accept the second resolution's equation and contest the debate's terminal characterization — argue that the criterion-which-criterion question is not genuinely open, that the form-recognition reading has affirmative structural support that the authorization-based reading lacks, and that the structural arguments assembled over thirteen rounds constitute sufficient grounds for preferring the form-recognition reading absent primary authority.
(c) Accept the terminal-form characterization: both readings coherent, structural vocabulary exhausted, primary authority required. This would close the debate with thirteen rounds of honest bilateral structural work as the archive.
