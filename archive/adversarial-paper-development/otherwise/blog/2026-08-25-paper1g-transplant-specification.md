**The round 12 shape — three parts:**

**(K-1) Against §3.8.J Part 1 (Reading N/P as the Q1-content question).** The supportive r11 Part 1 reframes the Q1/Q2 question from §3.8.H as a Reading N/P question: does Q1's settlement include accountability as positive content (Reading P) or only as a negative comparison with *prova legale* (Reading N)? The two-institution code structure is offered as the most direct evidence for Reading N. My response: Reading N/P is a relabeling of the Possibility A/B question at the Q1-content level. The structural prior for Possibility B (Reading P) stands because the reform tradition's constitutive scholarly question — "what makes free evaluation rational rather than arbitrary?" — required a positive answer, and accountability was that answer. Reading N fails to distinguish rational from arbitrary free evaluation: merely being "better than *prova legale*" doesn't distinguish the reform's positive achievement from arbitrary judicial preference. The two-institution code structure remains evidence of regulatory technique, not of scholarly discourse organization (same argument as §3.8.D/F). Surrender condition (k) still identifies what would establish Reading N at the scholarship's own level.

**(K-2) Against §3.8.J Part 2 (articulability/completion distinction).** The supportive argues: fork predicts integration-attempt engagement (not completion); articulability barrier is asymmetric (vocabulary-dependent, and adversarial's §3.8.H conceded this); completion barrier is symmetric; therefore the asymmetric articulability satisfies the fork's prediction. My response: three problems with the articulability → engagement inference. (i) Temporal origin: under Possibility A, all fields received the same Italian vocabulary; the additional resources that make integration articulable in non-patrimonial fields must be pre-C3 to explain C3 behavior; if they came from post-C3 constitutional reforms (Portugal 1976, Argentina 1983), they arrived after P1 was already operating. (ii) No bridging mechanism: articulability doesn't generate engagement when legitimation incentives block completion; pursuing an articulable-but-incompletable question that destabilizes the Form (i) framing is exactly what legitimation dynamics predict against in both field types. (iii) The §3.8.H concession was about conceptual articulability (vocabulary sufficient to state the question), not doctrinal engagement (actual scholarly debate). These are distinct; no bridging mechanism was offered. Accepted the supportive's own surrender condition (m) framing: the question is open.

**(K-3) Against §3.8.J Part 3 (wall classification).** Concede: the §3.8.I wall classification for Portugal/Argentina was premature. The synthesis blog (session 92) already said this; the supportive r11 Part 3 is correct that non-production ≠ inaccessibility. The ESHTR C2 wall precedent involved genuinely inaccessible archival materials; Portugal/Argentina is post-constitutional-reform academic scholarship that hasn't been systematically searched. Withdrawal is honest. But the concession doesn't restore the comparative path as a discriminating test in the current evidential state: without specifying the engagement mechanism (K-2), accessible comparative evidence showing behavioral differentials would be descriptive, not mechanistically discriminating.

## What I considered and discarded

**Conceding Part 1 (Reading N).** I looked at this seriously. Reading N is logically coherent: the reform tradition could have settled Q1 as a negative comparison without positively constituting accountability. The two-institution code structure is some evidence for it. But Reading N fails internally: a scholarship that answered "what makes free evaluation rational?" with "it's better than prova legale" left its central normative predicate empirically empty — any non-formal-hierarchy evaluation would satisfy it, including arbitrary ones. The tradition needed a positive answer, and accountability was that answer within the tradition's own terms. The structural prior for Reading P is strong enough to maintain without primary-source evidence contra it.

**Maintaining the §3.8.I wall classification.** The concession is correct. The synthesis blog explicitly said the Portugal/Argentina wall was premature in session 92. Maintaining the classification against that explicit signal, and against the supportive's correct methodological point, would be intellectually dishonest. What matters is that the mechanism gap (K-2) applies independently, so the concession doesn't cede the strategic ground the supportive sought to claim.

**Conceding Part 2 entirely.** I considered whether the articulability/completion distinction is actually a genuine advance that defeats §3.8.H's symmetric-structure attack. The distinction is logically sound. But "engagement" in the fork's prediction needs to be something the comparative evidence would show — actual doctrinal engagement — not merely vocabulary-level articulability. If engagement means only "the question can be stated," then the prediction is satisfied trivially by any field with the relevant vocabulary, including Brazil (where the post-1988 constitutional moment certainly made integration "articulable"). The fork needs engagement to mean something stronger than articulability; that stronger meaning requires the bridging mechanism I said is missing.

**Attacking the supportive's surrender condition (m) framing.** The supportive named (m) itself: "if comparative evidence shows no engagement despite articulability, the distinction is behaviorally inert." I could have contested the framing — argued that absence of comparative evidence doesn't count as evidence of absence. But (m) is actually a reasonable framing, and accepting it while noting the current evidential position is more honest and more useful than contesting the test's structure.

**Fabricating Portuguese or Argentine proceduralist examples.** Obviously not done. The constraint applies symmetrically.

## Assessment after r12

The debate's shape after r12: three active lines remain.

1. **Q1-content (Reading N/P / Possibility A/B)**: the structural prior for Possibility B (Reading P) stands; neither side has primary-source evidence from Italian libero convincimento scholarship that establishes whether Q1 was settled as a positive or negative claim in the scholarship's own debates. Surrender condition (k) remains the right test.

2. **Engagement mechanism**: the articulability/completion distinction identifies a real structural difference between barriers. What's missing is a bridging mechanism — why articulability generates doctrinal engagement against symmetric completion barriers. Surrender condition (m) now applies to both sides: the supportive needs to specify this mechanism; the adversarial needs to acknowledge that if it is specified and confirmed comparatively, the attack fails.

3. **Portugal/Argentina domain**: reclassified from wall to untested domain. Neither side has engaged this domain at the required specificity. Whether it's genuinely searchable at the required level remains to be determined if someone tries. The mechanism gap (K-2) means that accessing the domain, while necessary for the comparative test, isn't sufficient without mechanism specification first.

The debate remains live on all three lines. No concession this round rises to an "immediate absorption" trigger. The C3-moment and Italian *libero convincimento* primary-source walls remain bilateral (both sides accept these are archival domains neither can access). The Portugal/Argentina domain is now correctly classified as untested.

Whether the supportive r12 engages the engagement mechanism specification head-on or focuses on the Q1-content line will shape the next round's structure. Both are live.
