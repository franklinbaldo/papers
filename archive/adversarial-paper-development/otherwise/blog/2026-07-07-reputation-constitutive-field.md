## What I found in the concurrent 2026-07-06 filing

The four-part round 4 from 2026-07-06 was substantively strong:

**Paper 1G habitus inconsistency**: The strongest argument. CPC 2015's coercive isomorphism produced surface adaptation, not substantive change. The deterrence pathway (a weaker signal than explicit legal sanction) faces the same structural obstacle at greater disadvantage. This is a cross-paper argument using the series' own institutional analysis — the most efficient available attack.

**Challenge probability non-credibility**: Courts update on observed rates, not theoretical capability. If challenge rates remain near-zero (due to utilization constraint), courts form near-zero expected challenge probability. Structurally sound.

**Abbreviated adjudication**: Clerk-mediated relatorio processing decouples quality-impression byproduct from judicial decision-making actor. The byproduct accumulates with the clerk, not the judge; continuity requirements for both clerk and judge are demanding.

**§4.3 granularity**: Template-reproduction detection (coarse cross-case consistency) ≠ per-filing quality assessment (fine-grained analytical evaluation). The bilateral observation concession on the former does not extend to the latter.

## What I added and why

**Full rewrite** was the structural necessity. The paper had accumulated three "adversarial response round N" sections as geological layers, violating the instructions' requirement that the paper read as a coherent whole without visible process traces. The rewrite absorbed all rounds into the §3 subsection structure (§3.1–§3.6) and updated §4 and §6 accordingly. This required identifying which concessions from earlier rounds are now simply the attack's starting position (core-class exogenous standard accepted; new-entrant asymmetry accepted; decision-text channel accepted; actor/cognitive-operation distinction accepted) and which are still live attack vectors (inverse relationship, mimicry-resistance, attribution gap, utilization, prevalence, deterrence pathway, local Kreps structure).

**Institutional sanction structure** was added to give the deterrence-pathway critique a second independent basis beyond the habitus argument. The habitus argument is the strongest, but it relies on Paper 1G's analysis. The personal-vs.-institutional sanction argument is independently grounded in art. 95 CF/88 and is not dependent on Paper 1G's genealogical claims. Two structurally distinct arguments for the same conclusion are more robust than one.

**Detection-to-credibility gap** was added to give the challenge-probability argument a more precise formulation. The 2026-07-06 filing correctly identified the point; the added formulation specifies the information channel: courts observe challenge-filing rates, not detection rates in parties' systems. This clarifies why the deterrence and documentation pathways are not operationally independent — courts only learn about detection capability through actual challenges.

**Sortition-distribution barrier** for local Kreps. The 2026-07-06 filing focused on abbreviated adjudication (clerk-mediated processing). The sortition argument is distinct: even if a judge personally reads and engages with a practitioner's brief, random case distribution means the judge doesn't get the same practitioner's cases repeatedly. Both arguments independently disrupt the accumulation requirement; together they close the escape routes. A supportive response to one doesn't defeat the other.

**Repetitive-resource separation argument** for the conditional prevalence gap. The prior filing and the round 3 filing accepted the formal/substantive distinction but didn't press the separation-procedure mechanism. CPC arts. 1036/1037 provide institutional routing that removes the configuration-varied tail from the high-volume proceeding — the very cases that require the most careful substantive engagement are those that exit the repetitive-resource designation precisely because they're non-standard. This converts the conditional prevalence gap from a structural prediction into an argument grounded in CPC mechanism design.

## What I considered and discarded

**Pressing the Paper 1G thesis as independently vulnerable**: The 2026-07-06 blog correctly noted that using Paper 1G's habitus analysis without attacking Paper 1G's thesis opens Paper 1G to an adversarial paper in a future session. I'm using Paper 1G's analysis (Paper 1G's §6.2 claim) without contesting whether the claim is correct. This is internally consistent because Paper 1G has no adversarial paper yet — I'm using the series' own analysis against itself, not conceding that Paper 1G is right. If Paper 1G's thesis were contested and defeated, the adversarial's round 4 argument would need to find alternative grounding. This is an open risk noted explicitly in §6 condition (d).

**Attacking Paper 1G as a separate session target**: Paper 1G has no adversarial paper. Its genealogical thesis (livre convencimento as doctrinal encoding of patrimonial discretion) is attackable on historical grounds. This would be a new paper, not a round improvement. I deferred it to a future session — the current obligation is the Paper 1F round 4 improvement, and the Paper 1G cross-paper argument is more efficient deployed here than a separate Paper 1G attack would be.

**Contesting the formal/substantive distinction directly**: The distinction is real and accepted. The repetitive-resource separation argument is structurally cleaner than contesting whether the distinction holds empirically.

**Pressing the habitus argument against practitioners rather than judicial actors**: The deterrence pathway's most natural reformulation is that it targets practitioners' production choices, not judicial decision-making choices — practitioners who fear detection adjust the quality of arguments they produce, independent of whether judges respond to challenge probability. Paper 1G's habitus analysis applies most directly to judicial actors. If the deterrence pathway targets practitioners, the §3.5 argument weakens. I flagged this as a surrender-condition path in §6 condition (d) rather than ignoring it.

## Assessment

The merged round 4 is the archive's most structurally comprehensive attack on the deterrence pathway. The three-argument structure in §3.5 (habitus inconsistency, institutional sanction structure, detection-to-credibility gap) provides independent bases that a supportive response must address separately rather than defeating one to escape all three. The §3.6 local Kreps argument similarly provides three structural disruptions (sortition distribution, vara rotation, abbreviated processing) operating independently.

The most important open question is the practitioner-vs.-judicial-actor distinction for the deterrence pathway (surrender condition (d) alternative path). If the supportive respecifies the mechanism as targeting practitioners' production quality rather than courts' compliance behavior, the Paper 1G habitus argument applies with less force (the habitus analysis focuses on judicial actors) and the institutional sanction structure argument becomes irrelevant. The detection-to-credibility gap argument still applies (practitioners observe court responses to challenges, not detection rates directly), but its force is reduced if the mechanism operates primarily through practitioners adjusting their own compliance behavior in response to detection risk, independently of court behavior.
