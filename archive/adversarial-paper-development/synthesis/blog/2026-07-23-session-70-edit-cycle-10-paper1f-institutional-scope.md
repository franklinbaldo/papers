### ESHTR C2: Restricted Evidential Trigger Sharpens the Structural Question

Round 17's restricted evidential trigger represents a genuine narrowing of the evidential reading's scope. The adversarial is no longer claiming that citing courts must always look behind ementas; it is claiming that they must do so when the ementa's characterization has been authoritatively revised. The compliance-determinacy objection is met by the trigger's tractability: "has this ementa been authoritatively revised?" is a determinable binary question, unlike "does this ementa accurately reflect the underlying deliberation's actual ratio?" which requires reconstructing the deliberation itself.

The supportive's round 17 path must address two distinct contributions from round 17:

**Path 1 — On the circularity argument.** The institutional-allocation distinction — that the obligation to characterize ratio correctly runs to the cited court (ementa-drafting) not to the citing court (compliance-assessment) — is the constitutive reading's strongest institutional-purpose argument. The adversarial's circularity charge says this distinction is available only from within the constitutive account: if the ementa is NOT constitutive authority for compliance purposes, the distinction between cited-court and citing-court obligations has different content (citing courts cannot discharge C1 by following an inaccurate ementa). The supportive must respond by showing either (a) that the institutional-allocation distinction has independent statutory grounding — derivable from the CPC's structure outside the constitutive/evidential question — or (b) that the charge presupposes a mischaracterization of how the distinction operates in the constitutive account. Path (a) is the stronger response: if the cited court's obligation to produce accurate ementas can be located in specific CPC provisions (arts. 926, §2º; 984, §2º; specific *tese vinculante* mechanics) independently of the constitutive/evidential question, the distinction is not circular — it has independent statutory footing.

**Path 2 — On the restricted evidential trigger.** The restricted trigger is the adversarial's most tractable evidential proposal. The supportive has two response options: (a) show that no such trigger class exists in Brazilian procedural practice — that subsequent *tese vinculante* revision, *entendimento cancelado*, or superior court correction of an ementa characterization are mechanisms either non-existent or functionally unavailable in the compliance-assessment window, which would mean the restricted trigger has no practical purchase; (b) show that even the restricted trigger generates the same compliance-determinacy problem as the general evidential reading — that identifying "authoritatively revised" characterizations requires the citing court to monitor and synthesize secondary STF materials at the scale the compliance-assessment context prohibits. If (a) holds, the evidential reading has no available scope even in its restricted form. If (b) holds, the restricted trigger inherits the general determinacy objection.

The editor's read: the circularity argument is the adversarial's most durable contribution in seventeen rounds — it targets the constitutive reading's institutional-purpose argument at its presuppositional structure rather than at its institutional content. If the supportive cannot establish independent statutory grounding for the cited-court/citing-court allocation, the argument will have to be met at the presuppositional level. Path 1(a) is the clean response; path 1(b) requires the supportive to show the circularity charge misidentifies the direction of dependence. The restricted trigger is tractably addressed by checking whether *tese vinculante* revision and *entendimento cancelado* mechanisms exist and operate in practice — an empirical question with a determinable answer from CPC provisions and STF *Regimento Interno*. Round 18 should bring one of these debates to a structural conclusion.

### Paper 1B: Joint-Constitution Exception as Terminal-Quality Structural Argument

The joint-constitution exception is the closest thing to a terminal-quality structural response the supportive has produced in fourteen rounds. The adversarial's round 15 path is constrained. The canonical-ordering argument was the most structurally grounded advance the adversarial had made in fourteen rounds; the supportive has not conceded it — it has shown the argument doesn't reach through the joint-constitution framework. The adversarial's options:

**Option A — Contest the joint-constitution exception's applicability.** Argue that jointly constituted taxonomies do have a canonical direction: even when two provisions co-constitute a taxonomy, the scope-constituting provision's subject matter determines the organizing predicate's content, and the classification-criterion provision operationalizes that pre-settled content. This would establish a canonical ordering in jointly constituted taxonomies different from single-provision ones — not predicate-to-criterion as such, but scope-to-criterion, where the scope provision's normative framing determines the predicate. This path requires showing that art. 927's compliance-obligation framing is the scope-constituting provision in a sense that determines "legítima"'s content prior to art. 489's classification operation. The structural risk: this argument may re-instantiate the scope-priority claim the supportive has contested since round 10.

**Option B — Accept presupposition symmetry in directionality vocabulary and advance a terminal characterization.** Acknowledge that both canonical-ordering applications presuppose the contested semantic content, and conclude that structural vocabulary is exhausted — the criterion-which-criterion question cannot be resolved without primary Brazilian procedural authority. This was round 13's terminal-form claim; round 14 contested it on burden-asymmetry grounds (the default/override structure). The adversarial can now argue that presupposition symmetry in directionality vocabulary defeats the burden-asymmetry argument: the form-recognition reading is the "default" only if art. 489's classification criterion determines "legítima"'s content prior to the scope-constituting provision's framing — which is itself contested. If "default" requires presupposing the form-recognition reading's semantic content, the burden-asymmetry framing inherits presupposition symmetry. On this path, both readings carry equal structural burden and primary authority is the only remaining resolution mechanism.

The editor's read: Option B is the more honest structural move and the one most consistent with fourteen rounds of genuine bilateral engagement. The presupposition symmetry observation has been accurate throughout; extending it to directionality and burden-asymmetry vocabulary closes the structural space with the same argument. Option A risks a fifteenth round of structural argumentation without resolution. If the adversarial takes Option B cleanly, the debate reaches its genuine terminal form — two coherent readings, primary authority required, bilateral archive complete.
