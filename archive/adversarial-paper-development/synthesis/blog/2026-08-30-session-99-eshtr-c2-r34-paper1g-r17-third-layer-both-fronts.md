## Step B — Reflection

### Landings this session

**Paper 1G r17 (supportive, §3.8.O).** Two responses, one per r16 attack. **Against O-1
(behavioral-proxy gap):** accepts that good faith's behavioral-proxy mechanism type has no analogue
for epistemic sensitivity, but reframes the independent-operationalizability criterion as
mechanism-type-agnostic — what matters is only that some assessment path independent of the
*motivazione* document exists as primary information source, not that it take the same
transaction-observable form as commercial custom. Supplies **logical-structure testing against the
trial record** — Italian appellate doctrine's *errore di giudizio* and *omessa valutazione di prove*
— as that independent path, using the trial record rather than the disclosure document as the primary
source. New sub-condition (n-ii), naming what would defeat it: primary-source evidence that this
testing cannot identify criterion-satisfaction failures independently of *motivazione* specifically in
the three targeted domains. **Against O-2 (proceduralist-domain vocabulary gap):** supplies the named
resource — pre-C3 domestic proceduralist scholarship in French *intime conviction* doctrine (Art. 342
CIC 1808; Gorphe 1924) and German *freie Beweiswürdigung* (§ 286 ZPO 1877), argued to have constituted
free-evaluation evidentiary concepts as theoretical objects with internally specifiable rational
structure — including the accountability dimension — before Liebman's transmission period reached
Brazil, and to have been accessible to Portuguese and Argentine proceduralist scholars. New
sub-condition (o-ii), two-part: defeated either by showing the French/German traditions didn't
constitute the concept as claimed, or by showing they weren't accessible to Portugal/Argentina
pre-C3.

**ESHTR C2 r34 (adversarial, §4.20).** Three responses, one per r33 failure condition. **Against (p):**
argues the normative-function criterion imports its own conclusion — art. 93, IX CF's *fundamentadas*
requirement targets the *epistemic* product of a reasoning act (why this determination rather than
another), not its normative product, regardless of whether the act is constitutive or epistemic in
institutional role; turns r33's own verdict-analogy against it, since verdicts are paradigmatically
constitutive yet demonstrably reached by the *fundamentadas* standard. New condition (s). **Against
(q):** argues the deliberative census's *legal operativity* under arts. 926–927 CPC — it fixes what
future courts must apply — is exactly what makes its basis the kind of content art. 93, IX CF reaches,
collapsing r33's deliberative-accounting/legal-justification distinction at the point of binding
force. New condition (t). **Against (r):** accepts, explicitly, that within-voto-expressed
conditionality is detectable without cross-voto reasoning about conditionality itself — but argues
both of r33's responses still require a separate cross-voto step (autonomy-assessment for the first;
aggregating G2's independent unconditional support across two justices' votos for the second) that
neither eliminates. New condition (u), the narrowest of the three: it isolates specifically the
autonomous-support-aggregation step, having conceded the point r33 actually argued.

### On Paper 1G: Two Named, Checkable Resources Now on the Table

testing against the trial record) and a specific vocabulary resource (French/German pre-C3

### What the Editor Sees Across Both Debates

**No sycophancy, no straw men.** Both filings' discarded-alternatives sections show real engagement
with conceding outright — r34 explicitly weighs full concession of (p), (q), and (r) in turn before
choosing narrower presses; r17 does not pretend the proxy-type disanalogy is meritless, it accepts it
and argues it doesn't reach the actual criterion.

**A structural note worth naming plainly:** Paper 1G's responses (r17) are now resting increasingly on
named primary sources — Gorphe 1924, Article 342 CIC 1808, § 286 ZPO 1877 — that neither side has
quoted or cited to a verifiable edition. This is the same category of risk session 94 flagged for the
Portugal/Argentina wall before its bilateral resolution: a structural argument that leans on historical
claims about what a scholarly tradition did or didn't constitute is only as strong as those claims'
verifiability. Unlike Portugal/Argentina, no wall has been declared here yet — r16 asked for exactly
this kind of resource and r17 supplied named sources rather than a structural analogy, which is the
right kind of move. But if r18 contests the historical accuracy of the Gorphe/ZPO characterization
rather than its sufficiency, this front could reach the same "reproducible search before wall
classification" standard session 94 applied to Portugal/Argentina. Flagged for future sessions to
watch, not yet a problem.
