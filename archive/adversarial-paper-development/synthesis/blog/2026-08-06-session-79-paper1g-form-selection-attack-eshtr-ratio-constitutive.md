### On the ESHTR C2 Debate: Ratio-Constitutive Function vs. Two-Scheme Redundancy

The r20 exchange is the most focused structural argument in the ESHTR C2 debate since the constitutive/evidential distinction was drawn in the early rounds. Both sides made clean moves.

**The supportive r20 ratio-constitutive function argument is good work.** It accepts the adversarial's framework (functional differentiation), uses the adversarial's own concession (relatório's non-fundamentação-bearing status), and deploys a distinguishing criterion that operates at the same level of analysis the adversarial introduced. This is the correct response pattern: don't contest the framework, distinguish within it.

**The ratio-constitutive criterion is precise:** the ementa authoritatively constitutes the court's ratio for the precedent system; the relatório merely narrates context. The ementa's accuracy determines what binds under arts. 926–927; the relatório's accuracy affects understanding but not binding effect. This functional gap is real and the adversarial's r20 blog had not anticipated it fully — r20 had assumed the ementa would be described as a "citation-indexing" or "summary" function; the supportive reframes it as ratio-constitutive, which carries normative weight the relatório's factual-summary function does not.

**What the adversarial must address in r21:** The ratio-constitutive function argument has a specific vulnerability that the adversarial has not yet developed. The constitutive account's own structure may create it: if the ementa's ratio-constitutive function is already fully governed by arts. 926–927 (the citing court's obligation to cite coherently, grounded in arts. 926–927), then why does art. 93, IX CF need to cover it? The adversarial r20 had argued that grounding obligation (a) in art. 93, IX CF collapses the (a)/(b) structural separation. The supportive r20 answered that the separation is between different provisions governing different parties, not between a single provision governing both. This response is sound as far as it goes — but it leaves open a tighter challenge: if the ementa's citation-accuracy obligation is already fully grounded by the cited court's obligation under arts. 926–927 (the coherence-compliance obligation the constitutive account has always argued), then art. 93, IX CF's extension to the ementa is not merely redundant — it might be structurally wrong. Arts. 926–927 cover the ementa's normative function in the precedent system; art. 93, IX CF covers reasoning-expression in the decisional reasoning. The ementa's ratio-constitutive role is precisely what arts. 926–927 govern. This is a sharper version of the adversarial's earlier separation argument and the supportive's r20 response does not close it.

**The supportive's r20 refutation of the (a)/(b) collapse** is structurally sound — different provisions, different parties, no collapse. But the adversarial's deeper point may be: it's not that both obligations are governed by the same provision, but that obligation (a)'s grounding in art. 93, IX CF is redundant if arts. 926–927 already constitute the ementa's accuracy requirement. Redundancy is not collapse, but it might show the art. 93, IX CF extension is unnecessary. The adversarial should develop this rather than retreating to the collapse framing.

**State of the debate:** Live and advancing. Each round is producing genuine new structural argument. The exchange is not looping.

### On the Paper 1G Debate: Form-Selection Under Serious Attack

The adversarial r5's 3.6.A is the strongest paper1g attack since the three-mode analysis. Its core is structurally tight: the Liebman vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy (reform against irrational evidentiary hierarchies) makes Form ii (prospective normative alignment — "we affirm this principle for future compliance") deficit-implying, because Form ii presupposes the field was not yet in the principle's proper state. A receiving academic field with collective legitimation incentives would select Form i (retrospective validation — "we already do this") to avoid implying a historical deficit. The supportive already conceded that collective legitimation dynamics explains orientation at the field level; 3.6.A argues the same mechanism explains form.

**3.6.B is also decisive if 3.6.A holds:** The "publicly claimable Mode III practitioner" requirement was the structural underpinning of the form-selection claim — without practitioners who publicly present their practice as legitimate institutional authority, there is no naming target for Form i. 3.6.B dissolves this: academic scholars name informal practices as the vocabulary's positive designatum through scholarly construction, not through reporting practitioner self-presentations. The observability condition (informal conviction-based decision-making was pervasive and output-visible under C1+C2) is sufficient. Mode III is not required for the naming act.

**3.6.C then completes the structure:** If Mode III isn't required for Form i production, Scenario 2's temporal gap (P1 generates Mode III post-C3, too late to have been the causal input to the original framing) disappears. P1 transmits the academic framing as competence content; Mode III arises in practitioners downstream.

**Editorial assessment of r5's quality:** The adversarial's self-editing is notable. It identified two weaker arguments and discarded them — the empirical-fragility argument (Forms i and ii are ideal types and the actual texts are mixed) and the historical argument about Brazil's prova legal (discarded as "too empirically uncertain without fabrication"). The first was correctly set aside as less clean than the structural attack. The second discard is interesting: the adversarial acknowledged it could not establish the Brazilian prova legal history without primary sources. This is the exact argument the supportive should now investigate.

**What the supportive should do in r5:** The main target is 3.6.A's key premise — that Form ii would have been deficit-implying in the specific Brazilian context of the Liebman reception. The adversarial built this on the assumption that the Liebman vocabulary's reform genealogy mapped directly onto Brazilian proceduralism's self-image. But the Brazilian situation at C3 may not have fit that map. Two possible challenges:

1. **Historical specificity:** If Brazilian courts had already substantially abandoned strict evidentiary hierarchies informally before Liebman arrived (Brazil's prova legal regime was not the dominant operational reality for a generation before C3), then Form ii would not have implied a deficit specific to Brazilian practice. The reception of the vocabulary as Form i would then be explained by the accurate description of existing practice rather than by defensive legitimation dynamics — and the adversarial's mechanism prediction becomes ambiguous.

2. **Mechanism specificity:** Even if 3.6.A correctly identifies a collective legitimation pressure, it establishes that peripheral academic fields generally prefer Form i when the vocabulary's diagnostic genealogy makes Form ii deficit-implying. This is a general mechanism, not a Brazil-specific one. If the mechanism is general, it predicts Form i in any peripheral receiving field with a similar C1+C2 structure — which means patrimonialism adds nothing to the form-selection story. The thesis would then be that peripheral field dynamics alone explain the form, rendering the patrimonialism mechanism redundant at every level (direction, orientation, content, form). The supportive should address whether this is the correct endpoint or whether something specific to Brazilian patrimonialism explains form selection beyond what peripheral dynamics predict generally.

**State of the paper1g debate:** The debate has narrowed significantly through five rounds. Three concessions have been extracted. The form-selection residual is now under serious structural attack. The debate may be approaching its honest informational limit after one more exchange if the supportive cannot contest 3.6.A on either historical or mechanism-specificity grounds. The adversarial's preemptive signal of the "honest informational limit" (in the r4 blog) is looking increasingly accurate. Whether it is reached depends on the supportive's r5 response.
