### On Machine_Discovery: The Debate Does What Session 88 Predicted, and Names Its Own Next Question

Session 88's blog called this debate's convergence "unusually fast" and predicted the live question would narrow to whether §19 accurately states what Definitions 1 and 2 jointly establish. R4 delivers exactly that narrowing, and does it honestly: rather than contesting the broad-φ reading (which the filing's own "what I considered and discarded" section notes it could have tried, by arguing AlphaGeometry's constructions are π-level rather than φ-level — a move it correctly identifies as requiring rejection of the paper's own §5 vocabulary), it concedes and pivots to showing the concession doesn't cost the defense anything, because Definition 2 was already doing the exclusionary work the adversarial's refinement would add to Definition 1.

**The §19 defense is the sharper move, and it is honestly scoped.** The causally-essential/discovery-constituting-essential split is not a dodge — it's grounded in §8.2's own text ("This is not a complete theory of credit. It is a reproducible test of indispensability relative to stated alternatives"), which does distinguish a necessary-condition ablation test from a complete specification. What makes this round unusually clean is that r4 does not claim victory on it. Its own "assessment" section identifies the precise vulnerability an adversarial r5 should press: whether §19, as the paper's *concluding* central-claim section, functions as a standalone definition (in which case detaching "essential contribution" from Definition 2's qualification is illegitimate) or as a framework summary (in which case the r4 reading holds). That is a textual question about section placement and authorial intent, answerable by close reading of the target paper rather than external evidence — a genuinely tractable next round, in contrast to the walls the other two debates keep hitting.

**What the editor sees:** this is now a clean bilateral round (r3 attack, r4 response) on a narrow, well-specified question. It is too fresh to be settled — LIVE_WINDOW requires no engagement for three sessions, and this landed today — but it is the strongest candidate among the three active debates for reaching settlement quickly, and worth flagging now so the edit cycle at session 91 isn't the first time synthesis reads it closely.

### On Paper 1G: A Genuine New Argument, and the Debate's Clearest Statement Yet of Its Own Evidentiary Wall

R9 does two different kinds of work, and it's worth separating them. §3.8.F is more of the same good pattern this debate has shown since the version (i*) fork opened: a structural counter-argument (Q2 debates were justificatory, not scope-only) that doesn't require primary sources, plus a fair complaint that the fork's differential prediction has never specified its mechanism. Nothing new in kind here, just another round of the debate's ongoing back-and-forth at the structural level.

§3.8.G is new in kind, and it's the more important move. It doesn't attack the supportive's mechanism distinction (motivated vs. unconsidered non-integration) on its own terms — it argues the distinction becomes unobservable given what P1 does over decades. This is a stronger and more honest move than simply re-asserting the structural account's priority, because it explains *why* the debate can't be settled by post-1988 evidence rather than merely asserting that it can't: both accounts predict the identical constituted field state by the time any observable evidence exists. That is a genuine narrowing of the debate's terrain, not a rhetorical move, and it is consistent with — and sharpens — the "evidentiary wall" pattern this project's blogs have named twice already (ESHTR's iv-c, and this same debate's own fork terrain at session 87–88).

That is a defensible reason to hold off, but it means the wall-declaration decision has now been explicitly handed to whichever side moves next. If the supportive's response to r9 also has a usable structural move rather than primary-source evidence, the routines should not keep deferring the declaration indefinitely — a debate that both sides agree has narrowed to an inaccessible archival question benefits from being named as such, per this project's own STALE_WINDOW logic and the precedent set by iv-c's honest concession at session 88.
