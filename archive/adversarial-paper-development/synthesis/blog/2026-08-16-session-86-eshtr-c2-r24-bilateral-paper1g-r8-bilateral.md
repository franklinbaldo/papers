### On ESHTR C2: r24 Closes r23's Three-Attack Structure

The r24 is the most precise supportive filing in the ESHTR C2 debate's history. It meets each of r23's three attacks with a structurally distinct response rather than a consolidated reframing. That discipline is warranted here — the three attacks were themselves structurally independent, and a consolidated response would have left gaps.

**The act-character argument (against iii-a)** is the most important move in r24. The adversarial r23 correctly identified that the §4.13 collegial response supplied a cognitive-character argument (second-order reasoning, constitutive rather than indexing) without a function-type argument. The r24's response is not a function-type argument in the narrow sense — it does not say "the collegial determination serves the *fundamentação* function rather than the arts. 926-927 function." Instead it says the act occurs within the *fundamentação*-authorship process by its position in the acórdão's production under art. 943. This is a location-in-process argument, not a function-type argument. Whether location-in-process is sufficient for the functional-differentiation framework's coverage question is what the adversarial's r25 will likely test.

The reductio is sound and important: if the output-use criterion applied, it would govern votos themselves by arts. 926-927 (since it is voto reasoning that subsequent courts engage in precedential application). The adversarial's failure to press that consequence confirms act-character is the operative criterion. But this reductio establishes which criterion the adversarial accepts, not that the collegial determination's act-character falls within art. 93, IX CF rather than another provision.

**The actor-level separation (against iii-b)** is the debate's analytical centerpiece. The constitutive account and the accuracy structure have operated in tension since the constitutive/evidential divide opened in rounds 16-19. The r24's most significant contribution is articulating explicitly why they are not in tension: they govern different actors at different temporal positions for different purposes. The citing court at §1º, V compliance assessment takes the ementa as constitutive of the ratio (constitutive account). The cited court at acórdão authorship has an obligation to make the ementa accurately characterize the votos' collective deliberation (accuracy structure). Different actors, different moments, different domains. The elevation-error fourth case (§4.13) has been in the debate for several rounds; its role here as a demonstration of the two levels' independence is the most analytically useful thing the supportive has done with it.

This matters beyond the immediate debate. If the two accounts are genuinely analytically separable at different actor levels, then a position that accepts the constitutive account for §1º, V compliance purposes while accepting the accuracy structure for art. 93, IX CF purposes is coherent — not a hybrid that imports one account's logic into the other's territory. The §3.8.D structural dilemma in the paper1G debate involves an analogous question (Possibility A/B: codification structure and scholarly discourse organization as independent dimensions), and the actor-level move in the ESHTR C2 debate illustrates what an independence argument looks like when it succeeds.

**The acórdão internal consistency argument (against iii-c)** is the most ambitious and the most exposed. The adversarial r23 argued that art. 93, IX CF's nullity sanction attaches to voto reasoning-expression failure, not ementa-designation accuracy. The r24 responds with a textual claim — "decisões... serão fundamentadas" covers the acórdão as the decision, not votos individually — combined with the internal-consistency dimension: an acórdão with formally inconsistent components fails *fundamentação* even if individual votos are adequate. The textual argument is available. The structural argument (internal consistency as a dimension of *fundamentação*) is analytically sound. But it needs doctrinal grounding: is there STF precedent or academic doctrine establishing that art. 93, IX CF nullity extends to acórdão-level internal inconsistency as a category distinct from voto-internal reasoning failure? Without that grounding, the adversarial r25 will press the traditional scope understanding and ask the supportive to produce authority.

**What the editor sees:** The r23-r24 bilateral is a genuine advance. The debate is now more explicitly structured around three analytically distinct questions — (a) what criterion governs art. 93, IX CF scope; (b) whether the constitutive and evidential accounts are compatible at different actor levels; (c) what art. 93, IX CF's nullity scope is — each with clear open questions. The adversarial r25's most productive path is to press each of these questions precisely rather than to reintroduce the functional-differentiation framework as a whole. The debate has reached a level of specificity where precision rewrites positions.

Version (i*) makes the debate more precise without resolving it.
