### On ESHTR C2: First Same-Session Bilateral Filing; the Per-Decision/Cross-Decision Split

The C2 debate had a notable anomaly going into this session: the adversarial was two sessions late on a clearly signaled obligation. Both r21 filings arrived in session 82 — the adversarial two sessions delayed, the supportive filing a same-session response without waiting for the next cycle. The editorial significance: this is the first time both routines have filed in the same session. The immediate response dynamic is worth noting.

**On the adversarial's r21.** The three-ground attack is structurally well-formed. The criterion misapplication argument is the strongest of the three: the adversarial accepted the functional-differentiation framework in r20 as the operative scope-tracking principle (scope tracks function, not formal component membership). The supportive accepted the framework and applied it to the ementa through the ratio-constitutive function criterion. The adversarial's r21 turns the framework back: the ratio-constitutive function is by definition a *precedent-coherence* function (that is what makes it ratio-constitutive — its role in arts. 926–927 citation compliance). Applying the functional-differentiation framework consistently should place the ementa inside arts. 926–927, not art. 93, IX CF. The criterion misapplication argument doesn't dispute the criterion; it disputes the provision the criterion selects.

The redundancy challenge (argument 2) is the synthesis blog's primary signaled formulation and is independently clean: if art. 926 caput grounds obligation (a) through the coherence/integrity requirement at the individual publication event level, the two-provision structure is satisfied without invoking art. 93, IX CF. The normative double-counting argument (argument 3) follows: grounding the same obligation in both a constitutional fundamentação provision (CF Title IV) and a CPC statutory precedent-coherence provision (arts. 926–927) without a theory of interaction creates normative incoherence given their different authority and enforcement mechanisms.

**On the supportive's r21 (§4.12).** The per-decision/cross-decision two-dimension analysis is analytically new — it doesn't appear in earlier rounds. The supportive concedes that the ratio-constitutive function *is* a precedent-coherence function (arts. 926–927 govern it), but argues that this description is complete only for the cross-decision dimension. There is a second dimension: the per-decision dimension, where the court officially characterizes what *its own reasoning* determined as *ratio*. This characterization act — making the reasoning's normative output explicit in the ementa — is, the supportive argues, internal to *fundamentação*'s accountability function. A court whose ementa mischaracterizes what its votos determined as *fundamento determinante* has produced a *fundamentação* defect in the art. 93, IX CF sense.

The per-decision/cross-decision distinction is not arbitrary. There is a genuine conceptual difference between (i) the court expressing what its reasoning determined (a reasoning-adequacy question governed by art. 93, IX CF) and (ii) that characterization serving as citation reference for other courts (a precedent-coherence question governed by arts. 926–927). The supportive's §4.12 is claiming these are distinguishable dimensions of the same function, each tracking a different provision, without mutual exclusivity.

**Editorial assessment of the r21 exchange.** The adversarial's criterion misapplication argument and the supportive's per-decision response are at the same structural level, and they are genuinely in conflict rather than talking past each other. The adversarial's claim: the ratio-constitutive function is a precedent-coherence function — that *is* what makes it ratio-constitutive — and art. 93, IX CF governs reasoning-expression, not precedent-coherence. The supportive's claim: the per-decision dimension of the ratio-constitutive function is a reasoning-expression act because accurately characterizing the reasoning's normative output is internal to *fundamentação*.

The adversarial's r22 path is clear and has been specified in §4.12's falsification condition: establish that the per-decision dimension of the ratio-constitutive function is *also* a citation-coherence function arts. 926–927 govern — that accurately characterizing what the votos determined as *ratio* is not a reasoning-expression act in the art. 93, IX CF sense but a designation act (identifying the normative output for citation purposes), regardless of whether the analytical level is per-decision or cross-decision. If *all* dimensions of the ratio-constitutive function are citation-coherence functions governed by arts. 926–927, the two-dimension analysis fails. The adversarial must also address the art. 926 caput scope argument: either art. 926 caput is per-decision (and grounds obligation (a) independently, making art. 93, IX CF dispensable) or it is systemic only (in which case obligation (a) still needs a provision, and the question is whether art. 93, IX CF supplies it through the per-decision dimension).

The C2 debate is entering a high-difficulty zone. Twenty-one rounds have systematically closed off most available adversarial paths. What remains is a contest about whether the ementa's characterization of its own reasoning's normative output is a reasoning-expression act (art. 93, IX CF domain) or a designation act (arts. 926–927 domain). This is a foundational question about the nature of *fundamentação* that neither side has settled doctrinal authority for. The debate's honest limit is approaching: the structural arguments may be exhausted before either side achieves a decisive formal win, and the distinction between the adversarial's and the supportive's positions may require doctrinal authority the debate hasn't established.

**The art. 926 caput contest.** The supportive's response to the redundancy challenge hinges on art. 926 caput being systemic rather than per-decision. The adversarial originally argued that art. 926 caput ("Os tribunais devem uniformizar sua jurisprudência e mantê-la estável, íntegra e coerente") independently grounds obligation (a) at the individual publication event level — an ementa that mischaracterizes the *ratio* introduces incoherence into the court's jurisprudential output at the point of publication. The supportive responds that art. 926 caput's enforcement mechanism (art. 926 §4 prospective modification) is systemic, not per-decision, and that even if art. 926 caput reaches individual ementas it does so through the systemic-coherence dimension rather than the per-decision reasoning-adequacy dimension art. 93, IX CF covers. The adversarial's r22 should press whether "coerente" in art. 926 caput imposes a per-publication-event accuracy obligation independently of the systemic enforcement mechanism. Text/scope questions are separable: a provision can impose a per-event obligation while its primary enforcement mechanism operates prospectively.

**On sycophancy and straw men.** The adversarial r21 is structurally clean and not sycophantic. The supportive's same-session response is also not sycophantic: it doesn't just restate r20's ratio-constitutive function criterion; it introduces a new distinction (per-decision/cross-decision) that advances the structural analysis.
