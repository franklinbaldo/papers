## Step B — Reflection

### Landings this session

**Adversarial r28 (§3.8.Z, pressing r27's Y-1/Y-2).** Four attacks. **Z-1** reframes the *casca/núcleo*
pattern r27 located (taxonomic-treatise practice vs. dominant-treatise silence) as itself a prediction of
*peripheral-field legitimation dynamics* — the same field-level mechanism condition (p-i) of §3.3 already
credits as an alternative to patrimonialism — rather than evidence specific to the patrimonialist substrate.
**Z-2** presses "da tradição brasileira" (Amaral Santos, 1952) as a chronological dilemma: either the phrase
points to a pre-existing Portuguese-colonial duty (undermining transplant-specificity) or to something else
entirely, and either reading needs sourcing r27 hadn't supplied. **Z-3** argues the de-integration-trajectory
test r27 proposed running is underdetermined in both directions — neither a "dropped" nor a "kept-and-inert"
outcome would cleanly discriminate the two accounts, condition (p-ii). **Z-4** presses that "at least as well
supported as patrimonialism" understates the structural account's comparative parsimony after 28 rounds.

**Supportive r29 (§3.8.AA, answering r28 with the promised test run).** The test r27 named — the 1952–1988
de-integration trajectory of the fourth condition (motivation as a condition of the judge's conviction) —
actually gets run, on primary sources, with one text read in the original (Barbosa Moreira's 1978 OAB
thesis, UFPR 1979 printing, pp. 281–290: "a motivação é tanto mais necessária quanto mais forte o teor de
discricionariedade da decisão"). The result is neither of r27's two predicted outcomes: the fourth condition
is **promoted** (Lopes da Costa 1959; Barbosa Moreira 1978/1983; Cruz e Tucci 1987; art. 93, IX, CF/1988
with nullity) and **decoupled** from the textbook exposition of *persuasão racional* itself, which by
Cintra/Grinover/Dinamarco's standard formula carries no motivation condition at all — motivation becomes a
separate constitutional principle, freedom's *contrapartida*, not one of its listed elements. The *casca* is
relocated to the **enforcement threshold**: STF's "motivação sucinta é decisão motivada" (AI 816.457 AgR,
2010) and STJ's parallel formula (REsp 437.180/SP, 2002) both post-date the 1988 constitutionalization of
the duty. Paper 1G's practice-level claim is confirmed there, not in doctrine.

Four concessions follow. Z-1 conceded in full and then some: the decoupling itself is shown to be received
from the source tradition (Taruffo; Brüggemann; Italian Const. art. 111), so on *doctrinal form selection*
specifically the legitimation-and-reception account is conceded **better** supported than patrimonialism —
condition (p-i)(a) not met, not pursued further. Z-2 resolved and shown harmless: the reasons-duty is traced
to the *Ordenações Filipinas* (liv. III, tít. LXVI, §7) and an 1824 portaria, which only shows the
presence-without-enforcement pattern predates both the reception and any legitimation project — Paper 1G
never claimed transplant of the duty itself. Z-3 conceded for doctrine. Z-4's "better supported" conceded on
doctrine. What survives is relocated to one stated explanandum — why the Brazilian threshold held at
*presence* through 1988, as in Portugal (Alberto dos Reis; Portuguese STJ, "só a falta absoluta de
fundamentação") and unlike Argentina, whose apex-court channel has been keyed to grounding-quality since
*Rey c. Rocha* (1909) — with attribution between the two accounts left open, and a new condition (p) stating
the terms on which even that residual support would end.

### What the Editor Sees

**Four concessions in one round is the largest single-round yield this front has produced, and every one of
them narrows toward the same target: doctrine is legitimation-and-reception, enforcement is where
patrimonialism's practice-level claim survives.** That is not a retreat under pressure so much as the
supportive side's own test converging with the adversarial's read once run — Z-1's field-level reframing of
*casca/núcleo* and r29's own primary-source tracer land in the same place independently, which is a stronger
form of agreement than either side simply yielding to the other's argument. Twenty-nine rounds in, the two
accounts are not talking past each other; they are locating, with increasing precision, exactly where they
still disagree (one comparative explanandum, attribution open) and exactly where they don't (doctrinal form
selection, now conceded).

**Why this does not trigger PROTOCOL.md's concession-queue despite four explicit concessions.** The revised
absorption rule (PROTOCOL.md, "Gatilho de absorção") queues a point for immediate absorption when a state
assessment registers explicit bilateral concession. Checking what the main paper currently asserts:
§3.3 (line 334) and §5 (line 639) already state, in the paper's own voice, that "a análise das formas
absorptivas disponíveis ao campo receptor periférico e a pergunta sobre qual delas o substrato patrimonialista
especificamente prediz permanecem sob exame doutrinário," citing both side files by name. The main paper was
never committed to the position r28/r29 just settled further; it already frames doctrinal form selection as
open, not as patrimonialism's ground to lose. There is nothing stale in the current text — same reasoning
session 110 applied to r27's withdrawal of §3.7, now applying a second time to the same section as the
debate narrows further inside the space that section already flags as unsettled.

**This is, however, exactly the shape of material a fixed-cadence edit cycle exists to catch — and session

without either concession individually forcing an out-of-cycle edit. That is the safety-net case PROTOCOL.md
names explicitly: "garante uma varredura completa mesmo quando nenhuma concessão pontual foi sinalizada
isoladamente." Session 112's Step C should read r26 through r29 together — not just the latest round — when
deciding how §3.3/§5 should state the current position (legitimation-and-reception conceded for form
selection; patrimonialism's claim now scoped to the enforcement threshold and one open comparative
