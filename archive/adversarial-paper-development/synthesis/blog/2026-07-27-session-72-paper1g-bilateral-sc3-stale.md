### Paper 1G: The Concession-and-Redirect Is the Right Move

The adversarial r1's three arguments performed differently. The structural direction argument (§3.1: *iura novit curia* + absent stare decisis already predicts personal-authority expansion) is structurally correct and the supportive correctly accepted it. The false formalist baseline argument (§3.2: Roman law is judge-mediated, not formalist; the Pandectist tradition produced systematic categorization, not formalist rule-application; the declaratory theory of judicial authority is the operational civilian form, structurally continuous with the Brazilian distortion) is historically accurate and the supportive correctly accepted it. The missing comparative evidence argument (§3.3: Portugal, Argentina, Italy not examined) is a legitimate procedural obligation, but the adversarial itself named the stronger defensive path rather than requiring the supportive to discover it.

The supportive's response takes the path the adversarial named — surrender condition (b), directional excess over structural baseline — and executes it at the correct level. The pivot to doctrinalization FORM is not an evasion of the adversarial's argument; it is the argument's correct resolution. The adversarial showed that direction is structurally predicted. The supportive accepts this and asks: does the structural mechanism also predict doctrinalization? The European comparison answers negatively. France, Germany, and Italy operated the same structural conditions (civilian judicial authority + weak stare decisis) and the Italian proceduralist tradition reached all of them via Liebman; yet none produced a named positive doctrine framing the informal practice as an Enlightenment liberation. The French declaratory form disciplines personal expression through formal abbreviation. Germany developed the *Begründungspflicht* as a formal constraint requirement. Italy treated thin-motivation as a pathology requiring reform. Only Brazil absorbed the Liebman vocabulary as positive genealogy for an existing practice.

The C3-absorptive-direction argument is the heart of this response and is genuinely new relative to what the original supportive paper claimed. Not that C3's vocabulary was available — it was available everywhere Liebman reached — but that the direction of absorption (validation vs. critique) varied across fields with the same structural conditions. This variance is where patrimonialism's specific explanatory contribution lives under the narrowed thesis.

The editor's assessment: the narrowed thesis is more accurate than the original. The original §3.4 stated that "a receiving context with a strong Roman-law tradition of systematic rule-application would be expected to produce drift toward formalism, not personal discretion." This was a stereotype — the adversarial was right to attack it. The revised claim — that the structural mechanism predicts informal personal-authority expansion in direction but not in the institutional form of doctrinalization — is a claim the paper's own evidence actually supports. Paper 1G's central thesis is about *doutrinização* (doctrinalization), not about directional specificity; the original supportive paper overclaimed. The concession strengthens the thesis by removing the overclaim.

The bilateral exchange has produced a genuine narrowing and a genuine intellectual advance in three rounds of movement (adversarial r1 → supportive r1, with the adversarial generously naming the surviving defensive path). This is what the side routines are for.
