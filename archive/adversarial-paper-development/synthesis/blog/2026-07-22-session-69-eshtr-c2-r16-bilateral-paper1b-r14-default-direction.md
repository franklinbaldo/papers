When the statute says "seus fundamentos determinantes," it is at least not obvious that "seus" means "what the ementa says are its fundamentos determinantes" rather than "its actual fundamentos determinantes."

The adversarial's round 17 path is clear from this tension: press the textual case. The compliance-assessment function is the supportive's argument; the text is the adversarial's unforced opening. "Seus fundamentos determinantes" in a compliance-assessment standard still refers to the cited precedent's actual fundamentos determinantes; the standard asks whether the citing court engaged those actual fundamentos determinantes — and that the ementa may be the institutional reference point doesn't change what the possessive refers to. The evidential reading is not merely an accuracy standard; it is the standard the text's possessive specifies.

This is the adversarial's most available path. If taken, the round 17 exchange will be the debate's terminal textual confrontation. The supportive will need either a textual argument for why "seus" in a compliance-assessment context means "what the ementa identifies as seus," or a concession that the textual argument favors the evidential reading combined with a statutory purpose override argument (the provision should be read by its compliance-assessment purpose, not its literal text, because the literal textual reading produces a compliance standard with no determinable reference point in a high-volume system).

### Paper 1B: Round 14 Is the Adversarial's Sharpest Structural Contribution in Fourteen Rounds

The canonical predicate-to-criterion direction observation is structurally sound and avoids the trap the synthesis warned against. Previous adversarial rounds either restated contested premises or constructed positions the supportive could dissolve (filtering account, domain-status species). Round 14 derives the burden reversal from a structural feature of taxonomic design before reaching the semantic question.

The observation is genuine: in normatively organized taxonomies, classification criteria operationalize predicates, not vice versa. A compliance-response taxonomy's organizing predicate ("legítima") is prior to the classification criterion (art. 489). If "legítima" has natural compliance-permissibility content in a compliance-constituted subject-matter context, then the classification criterion operationalizes compliance-permissibility — which is the authorization-based reading's position.

The supportive's response is constrained to two paths: (a) argue that the joint-constitution framework (art. 927 + art. 489) is an exception to the canonical predicate-to-criterion direction, because in this taxonomy the two provisions jointly constitute "legítima"'s content rather than having one prior to the other; (b) argue that even in the canonical predicate-to-criterion direction, "legítima"'s natural content in a compliance-response taxonomy is domain-membership rather than compliance-permissibility — that courts navigate compliance by tracking what art. 489 recognizes as a valid response type, and that domain-membership is the appropriate compliance-relative content for the taxonomy's organizing predicate.

Path (a) is the joint-constitution framework's most important structural resource. The framework was accepted in round 9 precisely to avoid single-provision determinacy; if the framework genuinely involves joint constitution of "legítima"'s semantic content, then the canonical predicate-to-criterion direction doesn't apply in its standard form — both provisions contribute to the predicate's content simultaneously. The adversarial's round 14 assumes single-provision taxonomic structure; the supportive can contest whether the joint-constitution framework is an exception.

Path (b) is riskier: it concedes the canonical direction and contests the predicate's natural content directly, returning to the contested semantic premise at the organizing-predicate level. The synthesis warned that presupposition symmetry applies at the semantic level. Path (b) would need independent grounding for domain-membership as the compliance-relative semantic content.

The editor's read: path (a) is the supportive's strongest option and the one the round 14 adversarial blog anticipated as "the point where presupposition symmetry and the directionality argument meet."
