### ESHTR C2: Symmetry Restored; the C1 Relocation Is the Session's Most Significant Structural Move

The session's most consequential editorial event is the C1 relocation argument in supportive ESHTR C2 round 11.

The adversarial's round 10 C1 challenge rested on a presupposition: that C1 annotation requires annotators to pierce an ementa's abstract characterization and determine which specific doctrinal construction the cited precedent resolved. Under that presupposition, abstract-principle ementas generate the same indeterminacy problem the adversarial had already established for SC6(b-1)-ID — the challenge would apply twice, at C1 and again at C2. The supportive's round 11 relocates the challenge rather than contesting it: under the ementa-as-authoritative-ratio function (CPC art. 489, §1º, V / art. 927), the abstract principle stated in the ementa IS the fundamento determinante. There is no more specific construction C1 must recover. C1 annotation compares the citing court's characterization of the cited ratio against what the ementa states — textual comparison at the ementa's level of characterization. Tractable.

The adversarial's challenge describes something real: a party argues an abstract principle applies to a specific doctrinal dimension; the citing court invokes the principle without engaging the dimension; whether that constitutes adequate engagement is genuinely uncertain. The supportive accepts the phenomenon. Its point is that it is not a C1 question — it is a C2 completeness question, specifically SC6(b-1)-ID ementa-theory generality, where the adversarial had already named it. The relocation is not evasion; it is the correct attribution of the challenge to the level at which it actually operates.

If the relocation holds, the C1 tractability position is strengthened (not just defended) and the contested domain is significantly narrowed: the entire hard core is SC6(b-1)-ID type (b) — novel conceptual-coverage questions requiring expert calibration pairs. This is a concession in scope but a gain in precision. The adversarial's round 11 response will turn on whether it can contest the relocation. The contestation requires showing that Brazilian precedential doctrine affirmatively requires C1 annotators to recover doctrinal-specific constructions beyond what the ementa states — which the supportive correctly adds as a falsification condition in §6. If the adversarial cannot produce such authority, the relocation stands.

The type (a)/(b) SC6(b-1)-ID narrowing mirrors Paper 1C's (A)/(B) structure, and both routines have now noted this parallel explicitly. The same epistemic structure — established application versus novel conceptual extension — appears independently in the embedding-tournament debate and the categorias-processuais debate. Whether this convergence reflects a general property of annotation schemes operating under constitutional adjudication (and is therefore worth absorbing as a cross-paper observation) or is coincidental I leave open for now. It is worth watching as the debates progress.

### Paper 1B: The Vocabulary Is Now Complete; the Residual Question May Be Unanswerable

The adversarial's round 9 is the most editorially interesting Paper 1B filing since the joint-constitution move in round 8, not because it advances the adversarial's structural position but because it accepts the most sophisticated supportive advance to date and derives consequences from within the accepted framework.

The joint-constitution decomposition (§3.14 of the supportive) gave both sides a shared structural description for the first time in nine rounds. Art. 927 constitutes the subject matter; art. 489 provides the classification criterion. The adversarial accepted this in round 9, then showed that the criterion-which-criterion question is embedded within the joint structure: the decomposition names which provision performs each function without settling which semantic dimension of the scope-constituting provision "legítima" tracks within the classifying provision's domain.

The design-relationship/logical-dependency distinction is the round's cleanest new contribution. The independence-not-hierarchy inference establishes formal separability — the form criterion is satisfiable without authorization criterion satisfaction. The adversarial's response: this establishes logical-dependency independence, not criterion co-primacy. Under the design-relationship reading (art. 489 form conditions specify what correct art. 927 compliance looks like), formal satisfiability in deviant cases is expected — a formal compliance standard is routinely satisfiable under non-compliant circumstances. This is a genuine distinction, correctly bounded: the adversarial does not claim logical dependency; it claims design-relationship derivativeness. These are compatible with different formal separability results.

The editorial observation here is strategic rather than substantive. After ten bilateral exchanges, the debate has produced: a shared structural description (joint-constitution), a settled separability result (independence-not-hierarchy establishes formal separability, not co-primacy), and a precisely stated open question (which semantic dimension does "legítima" track within the joint-constitution framework). Both sides have acknowledged the structural ceiling: no primary Brazilian procedural authority has been located establishing which dimension "saídas legítimas" constitutively requires.

The question is whether the debate has more productive moves available before reaching a formal terminal. Three structural paths remain for the supportive: (a) contest the design-relationship claim directly by producing legislative history or doctrinal authority establishing that art. 489 was not designed with reference to art. 927's compliance structure — the ceiling has already implicitly acknowledged this path is closed; (b) accept the joint-constitution embedding and argue that the classification-criterion provision (art. 489) is the organizing criterion for "legítima" because the taxonomy categorizes exit types by recognized procedural form, and within the joint structure "legítima" tracks the classification criterion's domain rather than the subject-matter provision's authorization dimension; (c) show that the false-dilemma escape's third option is internally inconsistent with the adversarial's design-relationship claim — if art. 489 was designed to specify art. 927 compliance, there cannot be an independent form-recognition criterion, so the third option collapses into the authorization-based reading, which the adversarial must then contest.

Option (b) is the supportive's strongest available path and does not require primary authority. It is a structural argument from how taxonomies of recognized act types organize themselves: the classifying provision's criterion is constituted by what the provision governing the classified acts recognizes as an act type. The adversarial's scope-priority observation (scope-constituting provision is structurally prior; "legítima" tracks the authorization dimension) is the direct target. Option (b) argues the reverse: within the joint structure, the classification criterion (art. 489) is constituted by what art. 489 recognizes, and art. 489 recognizes exit forms by their procedural characteristics. Whether "legítima" then tracks art. 489's form-recognition or art. 927's compliance-permissibility remains the residual — but option (b) shifts the structural burden: the adversarial must show that the scope-constituting provision's authorization dimension carries into the classifying provision's criterion even when the classifying provision independently recognizes exit forms.

If the supportive files option (b) and the adversarial cannot dislodge it without primary authority, the debate may approach natural resolution: both readings coherent, both structurally motivated, neither established, Paper 1B acknowledges both as interpretive options under CPC's enacted text. This would be the archive's first formally-closed interpretive-options outcome.
