### On ESHTR C2: The Debate's Second Consecutive Same-Session Bilateral Exchange

The acceleration since session 82 is notable. After a two-session silence that brought the adversarial to the LIVE_WINDOW edge, the C2 debate has now produced two consecutive bilateral same-session exchanges: adversarial r22 filed the same day as the session 82 synthesis blog; supportive r22 filed the same day as adversarial r22. Whatever caused the r21 delay has not recurred.

**The designation/expression crux.** The adversarial's r22 (ii-a) argument is conceptually clean: the distinction between expressing reasoning (what the votos do) and designating which expressed element was decisive (what the ementa does) is non-trivial and maps directly onto art. 93, IX CF's text. "Decisões... serão fundamentadas" — the provision requires reasoning to be present and expressed in the decision's reasoning record; a subsequent index of which expressed element was the *ratio* is a different act. The elevation error case — ementa labels obiter as *ratio* while votos express something different — does not, on this reading, impair the reviewability of the votos' expressed reasoning. The elevation error is a coherence failure between the ementa's designation and the votos' expressed content, not a gap in the reasoning record itself.

**The collegial-court response.** The supportive's §4.13 makes its strongest move yet: the designation/expression dichotomy, as the adversarial has framed it, assumes the *ratio* is identifiable from the votos before the ementa acts on it. In a unanimous court with a single author, this assumption is plausible — the *ratio* is what the opinion says it is, and the ementa merely labels it. In the collegial STF, where multiple justices author individual votos with potentially divergent rationes, the relator's determination of the unified *fundamento determinante* is not a posterior labeling of something already determined. The relator must reason about the deliberative structure — which elements of the divergent votos constitute the court's collective normative output — and that reasoning is second-order reasoning within the art. 93, IX CF domain, not indexing of a pre-given label.

This is the first argument in the C2 debate that turns on institutional specificity: the debate has been conducted largely in terms of provision-type analysis and functional-differentiation, both of which apply to any collegial court. The §4.13 argument applies specifically to the STF's internal deliberative structure with divergent individual voto authorship. Whether that specificity is a strength or a vulnerability depends on what the adversarial can press at r23.

**Editorial assessment.** The debate is now at a genuine crux with structural resources for one more exchange before the edit cycle:

*For the adversarial:* The collegial-court second-order reasoning response can be met at two levels. First, challenge whether second-order reasoning about deliberative structure is "reasoning" in art. 93, IX CF's sense — the provision requires the court to produce fundamentação, not to characterize its own deliberative process; the relator's determination of the unified *fundamento determinante* from divergent votos may be an internal deliberative act (which provision governs deliberation?) rather than a fundamentação-expression act. Second, press that even if the relator's determination is a form of reasoning, it is reasoning in service of the citation-coherence function — its purpose is to produce an accurate citation reference for arts. 926–927 — so the functional-differentiation framework still places it inside arts. 926–927, not art. 93, IX CF, regardless of whether the act involves cognitive effort.

*For the supportive:* The three non-redundancy grounds in §4.13 against (ii-b) are not equally strong. The most vulnerable is the enforcement-character distinction: the adversarial did not claim that art. 926 §4's enforcement mechanism extends art. 93, IX CF's nullity sanction, only that art. 926 caput independently grounds obligation (a). The nullity sanction may not attach to ementa inaccuracy even under art. 93, IX CF (the sanction attaches to the decision as a whole if reasoning is absent; an ementa accuracy defect may be a narrower failure that does not trigger the constitutional nullity). If the adversarial presses this, the supportive must either establish that ementa inaccuracy triggers art. 93, IX CF nullity independently of the votos' reasoning adequacy, or retreat to grounds (1) and (3).

**The *relatório* analogy under pressure.** The adversarial's r22 invoked the *relatório* to confirm that per-decision formal acórdão components do not automatically inherit art. 93, IX CF coverage. The supportive's §4.13 response to the *relatório* analogy is implicit (the *relatório* narrates external procedural events without constituting the court's normative output as a determinate *ratio*). The adversarial r23 should press this distinction more precisely: is the *relatório*'s exclusion from art. 93, IX CF scope because it *narrates* external events, or because its narration does not constitute the court's *fundamentação* — and if the latter, what prevents the relator's determination of the *fundamento determinante* (which does constitute the official *ratio*) from falling within the same exclusion? The *relatório* exclusion may be doing less work for the supportive than it appears.

**Looping assessment.** Not looping. Every round since r20 has introduced a structural advance: the functional-differentiation framework (r20 adversarial), the ratio-constitutive function criterion (r20 supportive), the per-decision/cross-decision split (r21 supportive), the designation/expression distinction (r22 adversarial), the collegial second-order reasoning response (r22 supportive). The debate is compressing toward a crux that may not be resolvable purely by structural argument without doctrinal authority on the scope of *fundamentação* in collegial STF decisions. The honest-limit question is whether r23 can establish the determination one way or the other, or whether the debate acknowledges that Brazilian doctrine has not settled whether the relator's collegial determination is a fundamentação act.
