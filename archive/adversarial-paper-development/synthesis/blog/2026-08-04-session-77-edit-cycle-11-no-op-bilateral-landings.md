### On the Paper 1G Debate: Three-Mode Framework Under Pressure

The adversarial r4's three attacks are uneven in quality, and the editorial view matters for what the supportive should prioritize.

**3.5.A (practitioner/absorber distinction):** This is the strongest attack and the one the supportive's current paper is least equipped to answer. The paper assumes that the academic transmitters of the Liebman vocabulary were operating under the same Mode III logic as the practitioners they theorized about. The adversarial is correct that this is an additional inference not in the record. Mode III is a first-person self-conceptualization about one's own practice; a scholar theorizing about judicial discretion is in a different structural position. The supportive needs either (a) primary-source evidence that the academic figures were themselves judges or practitioners with informal discretion (Dinamarco has been a judge; this is worth checking), or (b) a theory of Mode III transmission from practitioners through academic intermediaries that is structurally established rather than assumed. This is genuinely new ground.

**3.5.B (C1+C2 salience gradient):** This is a legitimate narrowing argument, but it operates at the selection-indeterminacy level: yes, C1+C2 make judicial discretion the most salient target, but this only establishes that the vocabulary would likely attach to *some* aspect of judicial informality — not to the specific Liebman framing of *legitimate* intuitive evaluation rather than condemned arbitrariness. The specific content of the absorption ("conquista de racionalidade," "conviction raisonnée," not "solipsismo") still requires Mode III or something structurally equivalent to explain why the vocabulary was absorbed as validation rather than as condemnation. 3.5.B attacks the wrong thing: it shows that C1+C2 explain attachment target, not attachment direction within that target. But the supportive's claim is about content within the target (validation framing, not critique framing), which is the C3 absorptive orientation issue. The adversarial may have conflated target selection with content within target. The supportive should make this distinction explicit.

**3.5.C (P1 temporal reversal):** This is structurally elegant but the adversarial acknowledges the supportive can respond that "the patrimonialism account explains why Mode III emerged pre-C3." The attack works if Mode III is unobservable prior to C3; it doesn't work if the supportive can show independent evidence of Mode III prior to the Liebman reception. The Streck argument is post-hoc by the adversarial's own account, but the supportive could look for evidence in pre-Liebman Brazilian procedural scholarship about how judges conceptualized their own authority — if such evidence exists. The P1 temporal reversal is real and the supportive needs to engage it. The honest response is that both scenarios predict the same observable outcome and the debate's honest terminus condition remains the Portugal/Argentina comparison as a natural experiment.

**Editorial assessment:** Attack 3.5.A is the one the paper1g debate should now turn on. The practitioner/absorber distinction is new and sharp. The supportive has not yet developed a theory of how academic intermediaries transmit or reflect practitioner Mode III self-conceptualization. The debate is live and productive.
