# Paper 1B — five exits, rational supersession and silent partial application prior-art audit — 2026-09-18

> **Status:** first claim-specific temporal prior-art audit of [`paper1B_cinco_saidas_precedentes.md`](../../paper1B_cinco_saidas_precedentes.md). The main finding is material: the paper's broad Exit 4 proposition — that an inferior judge may, under art. 489, §1º, VI, decline to apply a still-binding precedent in the concrete case through an exceptional and heavily reasoned supersession analysis — was publicly defended in substantially the same Brazilian doctrinal debate before our cutoff. The distinction between **case-specific nonapplication/supersession by any judge** and **formal revision/replacement of the binding thesis by the source court** was also stated expressly before our cutoff. The exact five-item taxonomy and the exact autonomous label `aplicação parcial silenciosa` were not located in the searched pre-cutoff sources. Chronology alone does **not** establish copying, derivation, plagiarism, bad faith, or causal dependence.

## 1. Claims audited

The current paper is decomposed into the following claims for temporal comparison:

- **C1 — five-exit taxonomy:** arts. 927, §1º, and 489, §1º, V–VI, together with precedent theory, can be organized into five legitimate responses: correct application; reasoned distinguishing; recognition of later supersession by the source court; rational supersession; and abstention from invocation.
- **C2 — lower-court rational supersession:** a court, including a court inferior to the source court, may decline to apply a binding precedent by expressly demonstrating why its ratio should no longer control; the current text includes internal argumentative defect, supervening norm, systemic inconsistency, or sufficiently strong reasons as possible grounds.
- **C3 — case-specific supersession versus formal revision:** declining to apply the precedent in a concrete case is conceptually distinct from replacing the binding thesis with a new binding thesis; the latter remains a function of the competent/source court and qualified procedure.
- **C4 — silent partial application as an autonomous reasoning defect:** invoking the favorable portion of a binding precedent while omitting an express qualification or unfavorable part of its ratio, without distinguishing or superseding it, is an autonomous art. 489, §1º, V reasoning defect.
- **C5 — stronger-reason / rational-binding architecture:** precedent binds because it supplies a legal reason for analogous cases, while sufficiently strong counter-reasons should be exposed rather than hidden; lower-court disagreement can serve an informational/dialogic function even where the source court retains final revision authority.
- **C6 — abstention as a distinct fifth exit:** if the court genuinely resolves the case on an independent legal question and does not invoke the precedent as a premise, the obligations created by invoking that precedent are not triggered in the same way.

## 4. Pre-cutoff findings

### 4.1 Schmitz (2015), as publicly quoted in later sources: new grounds can justify nonapplication by supersession

**Work:** Leonard Ziesemer Schmitz, *Fundamentação das decisões judiciais*, São Paulo: RT, 2015, p. 342.

The original book was not available in a primary open full-text source during this run, so the classification below is conservative. A publicly accessible 2024 article reproduces Schmitz's account: art. 489, §1º, VI leaves room for a judge to articulate a genuinely new argument not previously faced by the precedent; the counterargument must weaken the precedent's reasons, and superseding it means making explicit its incompatibility with the law.

- public quotation and bibliography: Flávia Pereira Ribeiro & César Augusto Costa, “Precedentes vinculantes: Possibilidade de superação pelo juiz singular,” Migalhas, 2024-06-12: <https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/409097/precedentes-vinculantes-possibilidade-de-superacao-pelo-juiz-singular>.

**Compared claims:** C2, C5.

**Classification:** `partial_prior_art`. The publicly verifiable quotation materially anticipates Paper 1B's `new/strong argument against the ratio` structure, but this run did not independently inspect the 2015 book page itself.

### 4.2 Ataíde Jr. & Peixoto (2014): anticipatory overruling already supplied a lower-court nonapplication channel, but only under a narrower signal-based condition

**Work:** Jaldemiro Rodrigues de Ataíde Jr. & Ravi Peixoto, “Flexibilidade, stare decisis e o desenvolvimento do anticipatory overruling no direito brasileiro,” *Revista de Processo*, v. 236, 2014, pp. 279–301.

The work develops `anticipatory overruling`: an inferior court may decline to apply a superior precedent when later developments indicate that the source court is likely to overrule it. The literature ties this to erosion, signaling, inconsistency and a heightened argumentative burden.

**Compared claims:** C2, C5.

**Classification:** `partial_prior_art`. It clearly predates and occupies the lower-court nonapplication problem, but it is narrower than Paper 1B's broad Exit 4 because it requires a predictive/signal relationship to the source court rather than merely a sufficiently strong independent attack on the ratio.

### 4.3 Sousa & Rossi (2017): concrete-case nonapplication without extinguishing the precedent

**Work:** Diego Crevelin de Sousa & Júlio César Rossi, “Precedentes: na dúvida... Não aplico! A dura saga dos precedentes no Brasil. O exemplo privilegiado da presunção de inocência,” Empório do Direito, **2017-03-18**.

The original Empório URL currently returns 404, but the publication date and bibliographic record are independently reproduced in later open legal literature, including *Temas de Direito Processual Contemporâneo* and other journal bibliographies. More importantly, the 2020 ABDPRO article below quotes the authors' concrete-case distinction: the precedent may be left unapplied in one case yet continue to exist and bind in other cases, including future cases before the same judge, unless reasons for its incompatibility with positive law are again supplied.

**Compared claims:** C2, C3.

**Classification:** `partial_prior_art` from the currently verifiable evidence. It is a clear antecedent to case-specific nonapplication without formal extinction of the precedent, but the unavailable original page prevents this audit from assigning a stronger claim-level classification solely from secondary quotations.

### 4.4 Nogueira e Malafaia (2020): direct prior art for the revision-versus-case-specific-supersession architecture

**Work:** Evie Nogueira e Malafaia, **“ABDPRO #116 — Decisões de efeito vinculante no CPC/15: revisão x superação”**, Empório do Direito, **2020-02-12**.

- primary public page: <https://emporiododireito.com.br/leitura/abdpro-116-decisoes-de-efeito-vinculante-no-cpc-15-revisao-x-superacao>.

The article draws the same structural distinction central to Paper 1B's Exit 4:

1. **revision/replacement** of a binding thesis, producing another binding thesis, requires the competent/source tribunal and qualified procedure;
2. **supersession/nonapplication in the concrete case** may be undertaken by **any magistrate**, including one hierarchically inferior to the source tribunal, if the required analytical burden of reasoning is satisfied;
3. the inferior judge need not wait for prior signaling by the source tribunal;
4. permitting the controversy to arise in lower instances is defended as a way to “oxygenate” the system and allow the issue to travel bottom-up to the courts responsible for formal revision.

This is not merely adjacent discussion of distinguishing or anticipatory overruling. It expressly addresses the same institutional allocation problem and gives substantially the same answer at the generic level.

**Compared claims:** C2, C3, C5.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for C3; `prior_art` for the broad institutional core of C2; `partial_prior_art` for C5's particular stronger-reason/rational-binding formulation.

**Boundary:** Paper 1B's present C2 is broader in one respect: it expressly includes internal argumentative error in the ratio, not only obsolescence or changed social/systemic circumstances. That narrower refinement is not fully exhausted by Malafaia's article alone.

### 4.5 Ribeiro & Costa (2024): direct prior art for lower-court supersession without source-court movement

**Work:** Flávia Pereira Ribeiro & César Augusto Costa, **“Precedentes vinculantes: Possibilidade de superação pelo juiz singular,”** Migalhas, **2024-06-12**.

- primary public page: <https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/409097/precedentes-vinculantes-possibilidade-de-superacao-pelo-juiz-singular>.

The article expressly argues that a first-instance judge may decline to apply a binding precedent even where the source court has made **no movement** toward supersession. It grounds this in art. 489, §1º, VI; says the CPC appears to allow every judicial body to decline a precedent it regards as superseded; requires exceptionally strong reasoning; and warns that this cannot collapse into personal disagreement. It also quotes Schmitz's stronger formulation that new grounds not previously confronted may weaken the ratio and expose its incompatibility with the law.

**Compared claims:** C2, C5.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the broad C2 authorization/nonapplication proposition; `partial_prior_art` for Paper 1B's exact rational-error taxonomy.

### 4.6 Pitta (public 2025): thesis-level statement that supersession can occur at any jurisdictional level

**Work:** Fernanda Pagotto Gomes Pitta, *Mecanismos processuais de superação dos precedentes nos Tribunais Superiores*, doctoral thesis, PUC-SP; manuscript dated 2024; institutional repository record publicly available **2025-02-21**.

- PUC-SP repository discovery record: <https://tede.pucsp.br/handle/handle/5333/simple-search?etal=0&filter_field_1=publisher&filter_field_2=access&filter_field_3=sponsorship&filter_field_4=has_content_in_original_bundle&filter_field_5=available&filter_type_1=equals&filter_type_2=equals&filter_type_3=equals&filter_type_4=equals&filter_type_5=equals&filter_value_1=Pontif%C3%ADcia+Universidade+Cat%C3%B3lica+de+S%C3%A3o+Paulo&filter_value_2=Acesso+Aberto&filter_value_3=Coordena%C3%A7%C3%A3o+de+Aperfei%C3%A7oamento+de+Pessoal+de+N%C3%ADvel+Superior+%E2%80%93+CAPES&filter_value_4=true&filter_value_5=2025&order=desc&query=&rpp=10&sort_by=score&start=10>;
- thesis PDF: <https://repositorio.pucsp.br/jspui/bitstream/handle/44082/1/Fernanda%20Pagotto%20Gomes%20Pitta.pdf>.

The thesis states that, under art. 489, §1º, VI, a judge or any tribunal may use supersession when applying a precedent; this is not exclusive to the court that created it. It expressly argues that an inferior judge may re-evaluate the foundations of a binding precedent and, with an adequate argumentative burden, decline it. In its conclusion it separates **annulment/modification**, which only the originating tribunal may perform, from **supersession in the concrete case**, which it says can occur at any instance because the reasons authorizing nonapplication can be assessed at any hierarchical level.

**Compared claims:** C2, C3, C5.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for C2's broad institutional proposition and for C3; `partial_prior_art` for the exact Paper 1B articulation of internal logical error as one specific trigger.

### 4.7 Older contrary doctrine is also material: the problem itself was already sharply framed

Pre-cutoff literature also contains the opposite allocation rule. The 2020 ABDPRO article quotes Daniel Mitidiero's 2013 position that only the court responsible for forming the precedent can legitimately depart from it, and cites Marinoni's 2013 position that appellate courts cannot deny application after the Supreme Court fixes the meaning, although they may continue contributing dissenting reasons showing why revocation is necessary. Ravi Peixoto's later work likewise restricts lower-court nonapplication to narrower categories such as source-court supersession, later legislation, or anticipatory overruling.

**Compared claims:** C2, C5.

**Classification:** `adjacent_prior_work` / contrary prior work. These works do not anticipate Paper 1B's affirmative answer, but they demonstrate that the authorization-versus-dialogue problem and the informational role of reasoned lower-court dissent were already explicit parts of the doctrinal field before our cutoff.

### 4.8 Silent partial application: a pre-cutoff concrete analogue exists, but the exact autonomous category remains unverified

A federal-court filing in a 2025 case, following a sentence published **2026-03-16**, argues under the heading `APLICAÇÃO PARCIAL DE TESE VINCULANTE (Tema 1.009/STJ)` that a decision invoked the favorable part of a binding STJ thesis but failed to analyze the express good-faith qualification that was part of the same thesis. It characterizes this as partial/incomplete application and an internal contradiction.

- currently accessible index: <https://www.jusbrasil.com.br/jurisprudencia/trf-2/5822549540/inteiro-teor-5822549552>.

The factual/doctrinal pattern is strikingly close to C4: favorable rule used, integrated qualification omitted, resulting application described as incomplete. But this run did not independently reconstruct the **first public availability date** of that filing from a primary TRF2 source; the currently indexed page is an aggregator. It therefore cannot safely be used as a hard temporal-priority certificate.

**Compared claim:** C4.

**Classification:** `uncertain_date_or_dependency` as a priority candidate; substantively it is at least strong `partial_prior_art` if primary public availability before 2026-05-10 is later verified.

Separately, older doctrine and case law around art. 489, §1º, V already require the court to identify the precedent's determining grounds and demonstrate fit, so the normative building blocks of C4 are clearly pre-cutoff. What was **not located** in this run is the exact autonomous label `aplicação parcial silenciosa de precedente vinculante` or a pre-cutoff source that clearly elevates the same pattern into a separately named doctrinal category.

## 7. Material consequences for Paper 1B

1. **Do not frame broad Exit 4 as a novel discovery.** The proposition that an inferior judge may, under art. 489, §1º, VI, reasonedly decline a still-binding precedent in the concrete case was openly defended before 2026, including in a 2020 article and a PUC-SP doctoral thesis publicly available in 2025.
2. **The revision/supersession distinction is not novel.** The `formal replacement by source court` versus `case-specific nonapplication by any judge` architecture is direct prior art.
3. **The exact five-exit synthesis may still be an original organization.** This audit did not locate the same five-item enumeration. Its originality, if claimed, should be limited to the taxonomy/synthesis rather than the underlying Exit 4 authorization proposition.
4. **`Aplicação parcial silenciosa` remains the strongest candidate for a narrower contribution.** Its legal ingredients are old and a strikingly similar partial-application argument exists in a March-2026 federal filing, but the exact autonomous category/name was not located before cutoff in this run.
5. **The paper should engage the directly overlapping literature before external publication.** At minimum, Malafaia 2020, Ribeiro & Costa 2024, Pitta 2025 and the anticipatory-overruling literature should appear in the doctrinal map; otherwise the current exposition risks making a heavily occupied controversy look newly opened.

## 8. Audit conclusion

The broad originality picture changed materially in this run.

Paper 1B's most controversial substantive proposition — lower-court rational supersession of a still-binding source-court precedent — sits inside an established pre-2026 Brazilian doctrinal controversy. Multiple pre-cutoff sources defend materially the same institutional possibility, and at least one source states almost exactly Paper 1B's structural distinction between case-specific supersession and formal source-court revision. The correct classification is therefore **`prior_art` for the broad C2/C3 propositions**, not merely adjacent work.

What survives this round as potentially distinctive is narrower: the **exact five-exit organization**, the paper's particular stronger-reason synthesis, and especially the attempt to name `aplicação parcial silenciosa` as an autonomous reasoning defect. Those narrower contributions require further source-specific auditing before any first-to-formulate claim would be defensible.

No claim of copying, causal dependence, plagiarism, bad faith, or appropriation is supported by this chronology.
