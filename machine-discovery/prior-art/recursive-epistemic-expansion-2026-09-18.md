# Machine Discovery and Recursive Epistemic Expansion prior-art audit — 2026-09-18

> **Status:** first claim-specific reproducible prior-art audit of the central novelty boundary in [`machine_discovery.md`](../../machine_discovery.md). This audit materially narrows the paper's generic novelty claims. It does **not** establish exhaustive novelty, patent novelty, independent invention, copying, plagiarism, or causal dependence.

## 1. Claims audited

The paper contains several separable propositions that must be dated and evaluated independently rather than inheriting novelty from the title or from one another:

- **C1 — discovery as certified epistemic transition:** the scientifically relevant unit is not a machine output in isolation but a transition in which a candidate artifact survives appropriate certification and changes an accepted/public epistemic state;
- **C2 — explicit acceptance decomposition:** correctness/certification, novelty relative to a prior state, provenance/machine contribution, public uptake, significance, and downstream usefulness are distinct properties rather than one binary label;
- **C3 — frozen-snapshot novelty:** novelty is a bounded, auditable negative search relative to a frozen knowledge snapshot, an equivalence standard, and a declared novelty-search budget;
- **C4 — public-state representation:** accepted knowledge is represented as a versioned state containing language/ontology, admissible rules, accepted claims, certificates, provenance, and an audit/acceptance policy;
- **C5 — recursive curriculum expansion:** once a new accepted artifact becomes reusable knowledge, later learners face a changed informational environment; recursive machine discovery is the case in which this accepted enlargement measurably improves later learning or discovery;
- **C6 — resource-accounted recursive productivity:** recursive benefit should be established on held-out downstream tasks while charging retrieval, storage, verification, interpretation, and other relevant resource costs, rather than by observing library growth alone;
- **C7 — formal-mathematics operationalization:** a clean benchmark freezes a formal library/corpus, distinguishes rediscovery from novelty, independently verifies candidates, records provenance, audits novelty, admits accepted artifacts, and then tests controlled downstream reuse.

The later wording change in §19 that made the `machine-originated` / `machine-assisted` restriction explicit is not assigned the 1 August cutoff merely because it lives in the same paper. That textual refinement was incorporated later and would require its own later cutoff if a priority dispute turned on that exact wording.

## 4. Pre-cutoff findings

### 4.1 Automated scientific discovery itself is decades-old prior art

**Work:** Ross D. King et al., **“Functional genomic hypothesis generation and experimentation by a robot scientist.”** *Nature* 427, 247–252.

- public publication: **2004-01-15**;
- DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02236>;
- primary page: <https://www.nature.com/articles/nature02236>.

**Compared claims:** background premise behind C1 and C7.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for closed-loop machine scientific discovery; `adjacent_prior_work` / `partial_prior_art` for the paper's public-epistemic-state criterion.

King et al. describe a physically implemented AI system that automatically originates hypotheses, designs experiments, executes them, interprets results, rejects inconsistent hypotheses, and repeats the cycle. Earlier computational-discovery literature cited by the paper reaches even further back. Consequently, neither “machine scientific discovery” nor autonomous hypothesis→experiment→analysis cycling can carry novelty here.

**Difference:** the Robot Scientist work demonstrates automated scientific method; it does not define discovery by a frozen novelty audit plus provenance plus public-state admission and downstream curriculum effects.

### 4.2 The epistemic conception of scientific progress already identifies progress with accumulation of knowledge

**Works:** Alexander Bird, **“What is Scientific Progress?”** *Noûs* 41(1), 2007; and **“Scientific progress as accumulation of knowledge: a reply to Rowbottom.”** *Studies in History and Philosophy of Science* 39(2), 2008.

- 2007 DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0068.2007.00638.x>;
- 2008 DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2008.03.019>.

**Compared claims:** C1 and C4.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the generic epistemic thesis that scientific progress consists in accumulation/enlargement of knowledge; `adjacent_prior_work` for the machine-specific audit architecture.

Bird's epistemic account explicitly characterizes scientific progress by accumulation of knowledge. This substantially precedes the paper's broad philosophical move from isolated outputs toward change in an epistemic body. The present paper's potentially distinctive content must therefore lie in the **operational machine-discovery gate**, versioning, provenance, bounded novelty audit, and recursive-use criterion—not in the general idea that progress is an enlargement of knowledge.

### 4.3 AI-generated research had already crossed an external peer-review gate

**Work/system:** Sakana AI, **The AI Scientist-v2** peer-review experiment.

- public announcement: **2025-03-12**;
- technical report released April 2025;
- primary announcement: <https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist-first-publication/>;
- report: <https://pub.sakana.ai/ai-scientist-v2/paper/paper.pdf>.

**Compared claims:** C1–C2.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the generic distinction between internally generated research and research that passes an external institutional acceptance gate; `partial_prior_art` for the complete accepted-discovery definition.

The experiment submitted fully AI-generated manuscripts to a real workshop review process; one scored above the human acceptance threshold. This is a concrete pre-cutoff case in which external review is treated as a meaningful boundary after artifact generation.

**Difference:** passing peer review is not identical to the paper's full public-state admission criterion. The experiment was conducted under a study protocol, and “peer-review threshold crossed” does not by itself establish frozen-snapshot novelty, durable public uptake, provenance completeness, or recursive downstream fertility. It nevertheless eliminates any novelty claim based merely on “AI output becomes scientifically relevant only after independent external acceptance.”

### 4.4 Self-Revising Discovery Systems is a close pre-cutoff conceptual antecedent to C1/C2

**Work:** Fiona Y. Wang & Markus J. Buehler, **“Self-Revising Discovery Systems for Science: A Categorical Framework for Agentic Artificial Intelligence.”**

- arXiv v1: **2026-05-31 20:29:43 UTC**;
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01444>.

**Compared claims:** C1, C2, C4, partially C7.

**Classification:** `partial_prior_art` for the full machine-discovery framework; `prior_art` for the generic claim that discovery is a **verified state/regime transition rather than answer generation**.

This is the most important conceptual antecedent found in the run. Wang & Buehler state that scientific discovery is “not only answer generation” but revision of the representational regime. Their system state is formally represented; provenance is first-class; discovery is a **verified regime transition**; old artifacts are transported across the transition and residual content identifies what is genuinely new; CategoryScienceClaw combines typed artifacts, open needs, workflow mutation, gates, stress tests, provenance, and public discourse into a proof-carrying knowledge-computation graph.

That material predates our cutoff by two months. Therefore C1 cannot be presented as novel merely because it replaces “artifact” with “verified transition.” C2's generic decomposition into generation, validation/gating, provenance, and novelty also has material antecedents here.

**Difference:** the two frameworks cut the boundary differently. Wang & Buehler's decisive novelty is a **representational-regime/schema transition**: discovery changes what types and operations the system can represent. `machine_discovery.md` allows an accepted discovery to add a theorem, algorithm, dataset, or empirical result **without requiring a schema transition**, and makes public acceptance plus snapshot-relative novelty part of the discovery event. It additionally distinguishes extensional knowledge growth from resource-bounded reach and defines recursive discovery by measurable downstream reuse. Thus the categorical paper materially narrows C1/C2 but does not anticipate the whole conjunction.

### 4.5 Frozen, time-relative novelty measurement was already operationalized before our cutoff

**Work:** Neel Somani, **“PriorProof: A Point-in-Time Measure of Technique Novelty for Formal Proofs.”**

- arXiv v1: **2026-07-18 23:10:00 UTC**;
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16997>.

**Compared claims:** C3 and C7.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the component “novelty must be time-relative and scored only against an earlier frozen formal-library snapshot”; `partial_prior_art` for the paper's broader snapshot-novel discovery audit.

PriorProof evaluates proof-route nonstandardness using a prior built **only from an earlier quarterly snapshot of Mathlib**. The object and objective differ from ours—it measures technique nonstandardness rather than statement novelty/public discovery—but it directly occupies the methodological idea of a point-in-time novelty signal insulated from later knowledge.

**Difference:** `machine_discovery.md` defines novelty as a negative equivalence search over an admitted prior state and an explicit search budget, and treats novelty as one gate in a broader discovery event. PriorProof is a calibrated quantitative technique-novelty measure, not a public-acceptance/provenance framework. Still, “freeze the earlier formal corpus so later knowledge cannot leak into novelty assessment” is not a new component.

### 4.6 Growing verified lemma libraries had already been proposed as reusable learner-created knowledge

**Work:** Haiming Wang et al., **“LEGO-Prover: Neural Theorem Proving with Growing Libraries.”**

- arXiv v1: **2023-10-01 12:47:59 UTC**;
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00656>.

**Compared claims:** C5 and C7.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the generic mechanism in which an LLM/prover creates verified reusable lemmas, adds them to a persistent library, and later proof search consumes that enlarged library; `partial_prior_art` for recursive machine discovery.

LEGO-Prover explicitly contrasts fixed theorem libraries with a growing skill library containing verified lemmas. The prover creates new skills during proof search, stores them, retrieves them later, and originally reported downstream pass-rate improvement from the enlarged library.

This means the core causal skeleton

`learner/prover creates reusable result → verified result enters persistent library → later solving uses the enlarged library`

predates C5 by years.

**Critical correction:** Ian Berlot-Attwell, Frank Rudzicz & Xujie Si, **“LLM Library Learning Fails: A LEGO-Prover Case Study”** (arXiv v1 **2025-04-03**) re-examined that mechanism and found no evidence of direct lemma reuse, evidence against soft reuse, and that the reported accuracy gain disappeared when computational cost was accounted for. That critique is itself pre-cutoff and is highly relevant to C6. It shows why “the library grew and aggregate performance rose” is not sufficient evidence that learned artifacts caused recursive productivity.

### 4.7 Self-Supervised Theorem Discovery anticipates most of the formal recursive-curriculum mechanism

**Work:** Kazuki Ota, Takayuki Osa & Tatsuya Harada, **“Self-Supervised Theorem Discovery in a Formal Axiomatic System.”**

- arXiv v1: **2026-06-27 05:55:18 UTC**;
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28747>.

**Compared claims:** C5 and C7.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the formal-mathematics component “autonomously discovered formally valid theorems are accumulated in a library, reused as lemmas, and improve later proof performance”; `partial_prior_art` for C5's complete public-epistemic-state formulation.

This work is already cited in `machine_discovery.md`, but the present audit changes its **priority classification**. The system starts from axioms and inference rules, alternates proof search with useful-theorem extraction, grows a theorem library, reuses entries as lemmas, and reports that discovered theorems improve LLM proof performance when supplied as prompt lemmas. The paper explicitly describes the resulting theorems as formally verifiable external knowledge for later reasoning.

This is very close to “learner changes curriculum.” Therefore C5 cannot be claimed as a new mechanism at the level of growing/reusing formal knowledge.

**Difference:** the Ota et al. mechanism does not require literature novelty relative to a frozen public snapshot, provenance adequate for machine-contribution attribution, public-community acceptance, or a distinction between extensional addition and resource-accounted practical reach. Its theorem library is an experimental artifact, not the paper's richer public epistemic state.

### 4.8 AlphaEvolve already links machine-discovered artifacts to downstream operational reuse, including improvement of its own substrate

**Work:** Alexander Novikov et al., **“AlphaEvolve: A coding agent for scientific and algorithmic discovery.”**

- arXiv v1: **2025-06-16 06:37:18 UTC**;
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131>.

**Compared claims:** C1, C5, C7.

**Classification:** `partial_prior_art`.

AlphaEvolve uses automated evaluators in an iterative evolutionary loop and reports novel, provably correct algorithms surpassing prior best solutions. It also reports operational deployment of improvements in Google's computational stack and that one result accelerated training of the LLM underpinning AlphaEvolve itself.

This predates our cutoff and gives a real example of machine-generated candidates that pass objective evaluators, establish performance novelty, and are reused downstream—including a weak form of recursive substrate improvement.

**Difference:** successful evaluator scores/deployment do not establish the paper's public novelty audit or public epistemic-state admission, and acceleration of the underlying LLM's training is not the same as later learners consuming a newly accepted theorem/result as public curriculum.

### 4.9 Cost-matched evaluation of recursive library learning is itself prior work

**Work:** Ian Berlot-Attwell, Frank Rudzicz & Xujie Si, **“LLM Library Learning Fails: A LEGO-Prover Case Study.”**

- arXiv v1: **2025-04-03 21:53:51 UTC**;
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03048>.

**Compared claim:** C6.

**Classification:** `prior_art` for the principle that library-learning/self-improvement claims require behavioral evidence and equal computational budgets; `partial_prior_art` for the full resource ledger in C6.

The paper's strongest methodological result is that LEGO-Prover's apparent gains vanish after computational cost is accounted for and that stronger evaluation standards must include behavioral analysis and equal compute for baselines. This directly anticipates the paper's insistence that reusable knowledge must demonstrate downstream gain after resource costs rather than merely coincide with a larger library.

**Difference:** C6 proposes a broader ledger—retrieval, storage, verification, interpretation, and downstream held-out reach—rather than compute parity alone. The generic “charge the extra resources before calling library growth self-improvement” idea is nevertheless prior art.

## 7. Material change to the novelty boundary

The prior paper wording in §3 says that what remains conceptually under-specified is the transition from “system emitted an artifact” to “public epistemic state expanded,” and then from public expansion to “recursive improvement of later learners.” After this audit, that sentence is too broad if read as a priority claim.

The evidence above establishes that, **before our 2026-08-01 cutoff**:

1. scientific progress had long been modeled philosophically as accumulation of knowledge;
2. automated machine discovery and closed-loop scientific experimentation already existed;
3. external human peer review had already been used to test AI-generated research beyond self-assertion;
4. scientific discovery had explicitly been formalized as a **verified regime transition**, with typed state, provenance, gates, stress tests, and public discourse;
5. point-in-time novelty against earlier frozen formal-library snapshots was already operationalized;
6. verified machine-created lemmas/theorems had already been placed in growing libraries and reused in later proof search;
7. a June 2026 theorem-discovery system had already reported that discovered, formally verifiable theorems improved subsequent LLM proving;
8. compute-accounted criticism of recursive library learning had already established the need for matched resource budgets.

Accordingly, the novelty candidate is **not** any one of the phrases “discovery is a state transition,” “verify before accepting,” “freeze the prior corpus,” “grow a theorem library,” or “later learners benefit from machine-created knowledge.” Those components are materially occupied.

### Surviving narrower conjunction

After the searches above, no pre-cutoff work was located that combines all of the following in one operational definition:

- a **public/community** epistemic state rather than only an internal model/library/schema;
- domain-relative certification with explicit common-mode/audit-dependence accounting;
- novelty as a **bounded negative equivalence search** against a frozen prior public snapshot and declared search budget;
- provenance sufficient to separate machine-originated, machine-assisted, verification-only, retrieval-only, and human contributions;
- **public uptake/admission** as a condition distinct from correctness and novelty;
- explicit separation of extensional accepted-claim growth from resource-bounded practical reach;
- artifact-level downstream **fertility** and controlled later-learner reuse;
- a definition of **recursive machine discovery** that requires the accepted public-state enlargement itself to cause measurable held-out improvement after retrieval/storage/verification/interpretation costs.

This is a bounded negative-search result, not a statement that the combination is globally unprecedented.

The nearest conceptual predecessor found is Wang & Buehler's verified regime-transition framework. The nearest empirical/formal predecessor for recursive reuse is Ota et al.'s self-supervised theorem discovery. The paper's strongest remaining contribution therefore lies in **joining public epistemic acceptance, time-bounded novelty/provenance audit, and causally demonstrated resource-accounted downstream fertility into one discovery event model**.

## 8. Consequences for experiments

The current experimental programme should not ask merely whether a machine can produce a verified new theorem or whether an enlarged theorem library correlates with better downstream proof scores. Those have strong antecedents and known evaluation failure modes.

A discriminating evaluation should include at least these controls:

1. **Ota-style growing verified library:** formal validity + reuse, without public novelty/provenance/uptake gates;
2. **LEGO-Prover-style library growth:** persistent reusable lemmas, evaluated with equal compute and direct-reuse tracing;
3. **PriorProof-style frozen point-in-time novelty:** earlier-snapshot novelty signal without the full discovery-event definition;
4. **verified-regime-transition control:** explicit state/schema change with provenance/gates, but without public uptake and recursive fertility;
5. **public acceptance without fertility:** certified + novel + provenance-audited + admitted artifact that produces no later gain;
6. **fertility without novelty:** known/rediscovered but useful artifact that improves later proving;
7. **full framework:** certified, snapshot-novel, provenance-audited, publicly admitted artifact whose later causal reuse produces held-out resource-accounted gain.

The strongest falsification test is causal: remove or withhold the accepted artifact while holding total compute, retrieval opportunities, and training exposure fixed. If downstream gain survives, the “recursive discovery” interpretation has failed for that artifact.

## 9. Search negatives and unresolved questions

No pre-cutoff source was located that exactly matches the full conjunction in §7. The searches did not establish that none exists. Specific unresolved areas for future runs include:

- science-of-science and scholarly-communication models in which “acceptance into public knowledge” is formalized as a state transition;
- provenance ontologies for scientific claims that might already combine certification, novelty, contribution attribution, and uptake;
- older automated-discovery systems that write accepted rules/hypotheses back into persistent shared knowledge bases and quantify subsequent task improvement;
- theorem-library governance systems outside Mathlib/Lean where machine-generated certified claims enter a community-reviewed corpus;
- patent prior art around autonomous invention systems with novelty checking and knowledge-base update loops;
- post-cutoff systems that may explicitly cite or closely reproduce the complete `certification → snapshot novelty → provenance → public uptake → downstream fertility` conjunction.

Searches for the exact paper title, `Franklin Baldo` plus machine discovery, `certified epistemic transition`, and `recursive expansion of verifiable knowledge` did not locate a later paper claiming dependency on our work in this run.

## 10. Bottom line

The audit materially narrows the originality boundary.

- **Not novel as components:** automated machine discovery; progress as knowledge accumulation; external review of AI-generated science; discovery-as-verified-transition in the generic sense; growing verified theorem libraries; reuse of discovered lemmas; frozen point-in-time formal novelty; and compute-matched evaluation of library learning.
- **Still unresolved after this bounded search:** the complete operational conjunction of **public epistemic-state admission + bounded snapshot novelty + provenance/credit decomposition + certification independence + extensional-versus-practical expansion + causally demonstrated, resource-accounted fertility for later learners**.

That narrower conjunction is the claim the paper should defend experimentally. The audit does not support an unqualified “first” claim.
